# African music canon, 2000s–2020s: sources and notes

Built from what critics, award bodies and best-of lists say, not from what Fandome already holds. 100 artists: tier 1 = 30, tier 2 = 49, tier 3 = 21. Data is in `canon.csv` (columns as specified, plus `years_active`, `reason`, `source_count`).

Method: each source was opened and its entries noted. `source_count` counts distinct sources per artist. Tiers weigh that count, how strong the sources are (decade lists, Rolling Stone, Grammys, Headies and SAMA albums of the year count for more than a single year list) and the artist's role in their scene, so a region with few lists (East Africa, francophone pop) is not erased by Nigeria's denser press. Where only one source supports an artist, the reason column says why that source is enough.

## Notes
- **Catalogue overlap** (src/data/catalog.ts): only Tyla (`tyla`), Youssou N'Dour (`youssoundour`), Femi Kuti (`femikuti`, missing *Legacy+*), Tony Allen (`tonyallen`, missing *Tomorrow Comes the Harvest*) and Die Antwoord (`dieantwoord`). Fela Kuti, King Sunny Adé, Mahmoud Guinia, Lura, Dino D'Santiago and Buraka Som Sistema are in the catalogue but predate this canon or sit outside it. The other 95 artists are missing entirely.
- French diaspora acts that the African lists mention (GIMS, *Le Fléau*, in Afrocritik 2021; Aya Nakamura, *Aya*, in PAM 2020; Dadju & Tayc, *Héritage*, in the OkayAfrica reader poll) are part of the French scene and already in the catalogue. They are not counted here.
- **Album years** come from general knowledge where the source gave none. Two are unverified and flagged in the CSV: Koffi Olomide's *V12* and Eddy Kenzo's *Biology*. Pulse gives Timaya's 2007 debut as both *True Story* and *My Story*. Tumi and the Volume (dropped) and Oumou Sangaré's *Oumou* (2003) are a live album and a compilation, and the owner has deprioritised both kinds.
- **Possibly important but in no opened source** (so not ranked here; research next): Shatta Wale, Mr Eazi, Kojo Antwi, Uncle Waffles, Master KG, DBN Gogo (named only in the Evening Standard guide), Babes Wodumo and Distruction Boyz (gqom; DJ Mag could not be opened), Zuchu, Harmonize, Alikiba, Teddy Afro, Mulatu Astatke, Didi B, Toofan, Sidiki Diabaté, Wally Seck, Staff Benda Bilili, Songhoy Blues, Wegz and Marwan Pablo (Egypt).
- **Trimmed to stay near 100** (each has 1–2 sources; they can come back as tier 3): Baloji, DJ Black Low, Mayra Andrade, Habib Koité, Juliani, Jupiter & Okwess, Kenneth Mugabi, King Ayisoba, Malcolm Jiyane, MC Yallah, Nakhane, Noura Mint Seymali, Oliver Mtukudzi, Pure Akan, R2Bees, Shekhinah, Sékouba Bambino, KiDi, Sampa the Great, Jesse Jagz, Prettyboy D-O, KMRU, Tumi and the Volume, Moonchild Sanelly, Blinky Bill, Daara J, Lady Donli, Styl-Plus, 9ice, Kwesi Arthur, Deena Abdelwahed, Bongeziwe Mabandla, Samthing Soweto, KOKOKO!, WurlD, Muthoni Drummer Queen, Barnaba Classic, Cheikh Lô, Tamikrest, Sona Jobarteh.
- **Language and discovery rule**: several South African rappers (AKA, Cassper Nyovest, Nasty C) and some Nigerian alté acts sing or rap largely in English or Pidgin. Under `isLocalArtist` they are still local scenes (not US/UK), but whether a given album is sung mainly in its scene's language should be checked album by album.
- **Coverage bias**: Nigeria has the densest press (Pulse, Zikoko, Native), so Nigerian artists collect more sources. Francophone and East African pop stars (Fally Ipupa, DJ Arafat, Diamond Platnumz, Sauti Sol) were tiered on their role in their scene and the strength of fewer sources.
