# Scenes section: research (October 2026)

Goal: a new section where a fan explores a music scene they don't know yet (Italy, Brazil, Spain and Latin America, France, Afro, Korea), step by step like Duolingo, and sees how far they have got. This note collects what learning apps, music psychology and EU rules tell us, and what it means for Fandome. Design: [`scenes-design.md`](scenes-design.md).

## 1. How Duolingo builds a path

- In 2022 Duolingo replaced its free "skill tree" with **one linear path**: units made of small levels, with practice and spaced review built into the path, and topics mixed on purpose (interleaving). Duolingo says learners on the path did better than on the tree (company study, no published method). [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/duolingos-update-redesign-luis-von-ahn-interview-rcna44655)
- Criticism: the path is very long and the loss of choice felt restrictive to many users. [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/down-the-wrong-path-the-disaster-of-the-latest-duolingo-ui-update-a4cdd1e6ea1c), [PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2022/10/17/duolingo-app-update-with-path-ui-faces-backlash-from-users/)
- **Streaks and leagues** are its strongest engagement tools, and the most criticised: people keep a streak out of fear of losing it, streak freezes can be bought, leagues reward XP farming more than learning. [Duolingo on streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit), [study on Duolingo's gamification](https://tfulib.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/794/files/p87-99%20Jonathan%20Isaacson.pdf), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/reasons-give-up-duolingo-streak-3543009)

**For Fandome:** take the clear path of small steps and the mixing of topics; keep the possibility of skipping ahead (choice); leave out streaks, leagues and anything that punishes a pause.

## 2. Levels you can understand: the CEFR model

- Language levels (A1 to C2) are described with **"can do" statements** ("I can understand…"), used for self-assessment; the levels are broad bands, not points, and different skills can grow at different speeds. [Council of Europe](https://rm.coe.int/16802fc1cc), [self-assessment grid](https://rm.coe.int/168045b15f)

**For Fandome:** a scene level made of a few broad bands, each explained in plain words of what you know ("You know Italian pop from the 1960s to today"), with separate "skills" (breadth of artists, eras, sub-scenes, depth of albums) shown as progress, not one opaque number.

## 3. What music psychology says about discovery

- **Familiarity and liking** follow an inverted U: we like music more as it becomes familiar, up to a point; pleasure comes from moderate novelty (Berlyne). Recommendations should mix familiar and new. [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00147/full), [arXiv 2025, streaming data](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03568v1)
- **Curiosity and tolerance for uncertainty** predict how much people appreciate unfamiliar (e.g. non-Western) music. [Goldsmiths, Galvan & Omigie](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/32028/1/Galvan%20%26%20Omigie_Manuscript.pdf)
- **Openness** to unfamiliar music changes with age (lower in adolescence, higher in young adulthood; a hypothesis, not settled). [Iușcă, summary of LeBlanc/Hargreaves](https://artes-iasi.ro/index.php/ajm/article/download/128/141/424)
- **Musical sophistication** is not only training: the Goldsmiths index (Gold-MSI) has an "Active engagement" dimension (time, money, attention given to music). [Gold-MSI](https://www.gold.ac.uk/music-mind-brain/gold-msi/), [Müllensiefen et al. 2014](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/10160/1/journal.pone.0089642.pdf)
- **Learning design**: the zone of proximal development (what you can do with a little help) and scaffolding that is removed as you grow; interleaving as a "desirable difficulty". [ZPD and scaffolding](https://www.skillshub.com/blog/vgotskys-zone-proximal-development-scaffolding/), [desirable difficulties](https://ictineducation.org/home-page/quick-look-desirable-difficulties-in-action)

**For Fandome:** enter each scene through an artist close to what you already love (our Bridge), then alternate close and stretching steps (two close, one further); give more explanation at the start and less later; measure engagement (artists explored, albums rated, eras, sub-scenes), never "talent".

## 4. Gamification: when it helps, when it hurts

- On average small positive effects on learning and motivation, very dependent on design. [meta-analysis summary](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/139776), [SDT and gamification](https://icenet.blog/2025/06/17/align-the-game-to-your-aim-considering-gamification-through-the-lens-of-self-determination-theory/)
- **Self-determination theory**: rewards help when they support competence, autonomy and relatedness; they hurt when they feel controlling or unrelated to the activity (**overjustification**: external rewards can lower intrinsic motivation). [CHI 2022 workshop](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~veltk101/publications/art/chi2022-sdtws.pdf)

**For Fandome:** the reward is the music and the feeling of knowing a scene; levels describe what you know, they are not currency; no points for time spent.

## 5. EU rules on addictive design

- The Commission's DSA guidelines on minors (July 2025, art. 28) ask platforms to switch off by default for minors features that drive excessive use, such as streaks, autoplay and push notifications; the Digital Fairness Act consultation lists "penalties for disengagement, such as breaking a streak" among addictive designs. [Scalevise summary](https://scalevise.com/resources/eu-dsa-guidelines-addictive-design-minors/), [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/digital-fairness-act-unpacked-addictive-design), [vzbv report](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/2026-09/26-06-19_vzbv_Report_DSA_Art%2028.pdf)

**For Fandome (users are 14+):** no streaks, no loss of progress, no notifications to bring you back, no public rankings of people; progress private by default.

## 6. Maps and guides of genres

- Every Noise at Once (Glenn McDonald, Echo Nest/Spotify) mapped thousands of genres on two axes (organic ↔ electric, dense ↔ bouncy), each with representative artists. [Spotify for Artists](https://artists.spotify.com/de/blog/how-spotify-discovers-the-genres-of-tomorrow), [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/every-noise-at-once-echo-nest)
- Streaming editorial guides are mostly per artist ("Essentials" ≈ best of) rather than per scene. [Apple Community](https://communities.apple.com/fr/thread/254963265)

**For Fandome:** there is room for a guided, explained path through a scene, made with fans; a visual map of a scene's sub-genres can come later (our genre families already give the structure).
