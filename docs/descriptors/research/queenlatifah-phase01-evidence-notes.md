# Queen Latifah — album evidence and editorial decisions, Fandome phase 01

Research cut-off: 29 October 2026. All proposed values are editorial assessments, **not** borrowed numerical scores. Sources named below are defined with exact URLs in `queenlatifah_phase01_sources.csv`. Album descriptors are `draft` for second-person review.

## `queenlatifah-allhailthequeen` — All Hail the Queen (1989)

- **Genres / priority:** Library of Congress National Recording Registry essay expressly names hip-hop, house, reggae and jazz. AllMusic critic Alex Henderson names the **hip-house** single **Come Into My House** and the reggae-based **Princess of the Posse** and **The Pros**; do not classify the whole album as a house album. `dancehall` is secondary to broader reggae. Critics also place it in the Native Tongues/Flavor Unit orbit. AllMusic's album-level Golden Age style is an era/style, not automatically synonymous with `oldschoolrap`, so it is not substituted.
- **Energy 4 / mood 4 / theatrical 3 / tempo 4 / groove 4:** lively confrontational and celebration tracks plus club material and slower dialogue songs. The record is not uniformly ecstatic; tough rhymes and political subjects limit mood. Christian Science Monitor's 1989 review, AllMusic, Library of Congress and Wax Poetics describe different facets.
- **Electronic 3 / experimental 3 / polish 3:** sample-derived beats and rhythmic club experiments are documented, but no explicit model of drum machine/sampler was identified. No invented SP-1200, 808 or dedicated keyboard model.
- **Voice `rap|clear|powerful`:** Library of Congress confirms rap *and singing* on the album, but Fandome's voice taxonomy lacks a general `sung` label. Assertive melodic/clear singing and rap are addressed by `clear`, while `rap` stays primary.
- **Instruments `sampler|bass`:** Wax Poetics describes the sampled-recording construction; AllMusic explicitly credits Shane Faber on bass. `sampler` refers to the album's sampled musical material, **not** a verified hardware model. Physical drums, strings and piano are not added as performed instruments without a direct credit. This is a deliberately conservative list.
- **Production `sampling`:** documented by Wax Poetics; avoid `drumprogramming` and undocumented machine brands. **Producer credits:** 45 King, Prince Paul, Daddy-O, KRS-One, Louie Vega, Queen Latifah. AllMusic lists Latifah as a producer, while the shorter 1989 credits sometimes list five without her; MusicBrainz confirms 45 King/Queen co-production on Come Into My House. An original 12-song sequence is distinct from bonus remixes appearing on many CD/digital editions; bonus remixers must not automatically become original-album producers.
- **Lyrics:** empowering female self-determination and race/sex/social issues on Ladies First, Evil That Men Do; boisterous battle rap and party/house material also present. **Sound similarity:** De La Soul and Jungle Brothers are editorial Native Tongues sonic analogues; De La Soul appear as guests, so **not** placed under `influenced_by`. **Album influenced_by empty:** none of the verified artist-wide influence declarations explicitly says that person shaped this particular album.
- **Landmark 1:** Library of Congress 2023 National Recording Registry selection, plus Wax Poetics historical analysis. **Release date conflict:** public pages give 7 November and 28 November 1989; Fandome stores only the agreed year 1989.

## `queenlatifah-natureofasista` — Nature of a Sista' (1991)

- **Genres:** AllMusic `Pop-Rap`; Albumism (Mark Chappelle, 1 September 2026) explicitly highlights R&B, dance, funk and reggae in the album's sound. `jazz` is **selected-track** texture via piano groove of Tania Maria on How Do I Love Thee and horn vamps on Fly Girl. Gil Griffin's Washington Post review identifies dancehall on Sexy Fancy, not a whole-album pure dancehall record.
- **Energy 3 / mood 4 / theatrical 2 / experimental 3 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 4:** shift toward melodic R&B and lighter grooves while retaining hard single Latifah's Had It Up 2 Here. Her own account in a JazzTimes interview establishes the conscious mixing of rap, toasts and melody. Contemporary Washington Post is markedly less favorable than 2026 Albumism and AllMusic; a critical disagreement on *quality* does not invalidate the instrumentation or genres.
- **Voice `rap|clear|soft`:** two plainly sung R&B songs Give Me Your Love and Love Again, in addition to rapping. This is actual album vocal diversity rather than inferred from a later jazz career.
- **Instruments `keyboards|saxophone|sampler`:** AllMusic explicitly credits David Bellochio, Doctor Illington and Dr Shane Faber for keyboards; Paul Shapiro for saxophone. The studio uses documented sampled passages, such as James Brown's The Payback on If You Don't Know (see album's liner-note transcription), without asserting that the sample artist played on the album. **Production `sampling`:** avoid a named model or generic drum-programming guess.
- **Producers:** Naughty by Nature, K-Cut, Soulshock, Cutfather, Louie Vega, Nevelle Hodge and Queen Latifah. Some releases credit joint `Soulpower Productions` for the Danish duo; identify individual members rather than treat 'Soulpower Productions' as an additional producer. **Do NOT assign DJ Mark the 45 King**: he receives *concept* credit, not production on the album; Albumism explicitly contrasts this with the debut.
- **Lyrics:** identity, autonomy, love and desire, party and social treatment of women. **Landmark 0:** notable artistic expansion but not as consistently treated as a defining Latifah LP as the debut or Black Reign. **Album influenced_by empty:** Tania Maria is a sampled/quoted composer on How Do I Love Thee, not a documented producer-named **album-level** formative influence.

## `queenlatifah-blackreign` — Black Reign (1993)

- **Genres:** core hip-hop, East Coast movement, AllMusic's `Pop-Rap` and `Alternative Rap` (exact **new** `alternativerap` token). Jazz-rap applies especially to U.N.I.T.Y., reggae to Weekend Love and jazz to Winki's Theme: the 1994 Washington Post review explicitly verifies each track's style, and these are long-tail genres.
- **Energy 4 / mood 2 / theatrical 3 / experimental 2 / polish 3 / tempo 3 / groove 4:** forceful messages, challenges to sexism, urban injustice and mourning; even approachable hooks carry serious topics. Compare AllMusic review with Rock Hall artist profile. Mood 2 accommodates both the serious core and warmer tracks.
- **Instruments `piano|trumpet|trombone|bass|drums|sampler`:** AllMusic credits Gordon Chambers (piano), Kevin Batchelor (trumpet), Clark Gayton (trombone), Kim Clark (bass), Kendu Dickens (drums). `sampler` means sourced recorded material heard on the LP, particularly the Crusaders in U.N.I.T.Y.; **Joe Sample/Crusaders are a sampled original, not hired session performers on Black Reign**.
- **Production:** sample use is confirmed by MusicBrainz and Glide; `hornarrangements` corresponds to AllMusic's named horn-arrangement credits. No specific sampling keyboard brand stated. Producer **S.I.D. Reynolds**, **Tony Dofat**, **Kay Gee**, **Mufi** (on U.N.I.T.Y.) and **Queen Latifah** (Winki's Theme). AllMusic also lists generic Sid aliases; do not duplicate them.
- **Lyrics:** social inequality, misogyny/domestic abuse, interpersonal tension, storytelling and personal loss. **Landmark 1:** Grammy-winning U.N.I.T.Y. and 1993 album's historic role (Rock Hall). **Album influenced_by empty:** The Crusaders supplied a *sample*, prohibited from this column; KRS-One / Treach / Tony Rebel are guests, not automatically influences.

## `queenlatifah-orderinthecourt` — Order in the Court (1998)

- **Genres:** AllMusic expressly gives Pop-Rap and **Alternative Rap**; the latter is proposed as a **new exact token `alternativerap`** instead of being silently collapsed to the existing `alternativehiphop`, per owner instruction. Album's contemporary soul and R&B overlay is described directly by Stephen Thomas Erlewine. `soul` applies mainly to singing/arrangements, not a full soul LP.
- **Energy 3 / mood 3 / theatrical 3 / experimental 2 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** the menacing rhythmic opening Bananas contrasts with soft Paper, lush duets and midtempo R&B. Albumism offers a warmer modern reassessment; Los Angeles Times contemporary Soren Baker review attacks pop gloss and reduced hard rap; *retain disagreement*. `confidence=medium` because stylistic taxonomy and extended producer list need second-person verification despite strong personnel evidence.
- **Voice `rap|clear|soft`:** AllMusic review and Apple Music repertoire identify a mix of rapped and sung pieces. **Instruments `bass|keyboards|guitar|drums|turntables`:** AllMusic credits Jerry Duplessis bass/drums, Warryn Campbell bass/guitar/keyboards, Pras keyboards, Josh Weinstein guitar and DJ Butta Fingers for scratching. `turntables` denotes documented scratching, not an invented machine model.
- **Production `drumprogramming|sampling`:** AllMusic credits Kendu Isaacs for drum programming; album analysis describes samples on Bananas, plus known borrowed recordings. **Important:** Paper *interpolates* I Heard It Through the Grapevine; the original song copyright says interpolation, not sampled audio. Never use the Paper interpolation as proof of sampling. Explicit scratching is a performance credit, not a separate listed production token.
- **Producers:** compiled from AllMusic album personnel and Qobuz song-specific credits, excluding writers/sampled artists, engineers, mixers only, vocal arrangers and guests. Normalize Chad Elliott's `Dr. Ceuss`/`Dr. Seuss` conflicting printed spelling as `Chad "Dr. Ceuss" Elliott` from distributor credits. Al West and Mary Brown have producer credit in AllMusic; team split varies across editions and may be revised after liner-note verification. Confirm each producer before marking `reviewed`. **Sound comparison:** AllMusic biography reports contemporary Missy Elliott comparisons; not a stated influence. **Album influenced_by intentionally empty.**

## Shared classification and ID caveats

- `src/data/catalog.ts` and `src/data/seed.ts` have **no Queen Latifah** as checked on 29 October 2026; proposed artist ID `queenlatifah`, album IDs constructed as `queenlatifah-` + title stripped to lowercase alphanumerics. Validator will reject these until the artist and four records are added. No need to modify existing IDs.
- `Queen Latifah and the Original Flavor Unit` (1996) is often misrepresented as an eighth studio album by generic databases, but official Spotify calls it **Various Artists / Compilation**, and it contains performances chiefly by Flavor Unit members; exclude even though some stores label it an album under Queen Latifah.
- No live records, greatest hits, generic remixes, guest appearances or albums by other artists included. Only `alternativerap` appears to require a new genre token (two albums); existing `hiphouse` is used for the debut.
- `sounds_like` only claims critical/editorial resemblance, `influenced_by` only direct statements expressly tied to a *given album*. Do not turn collaborations, sampled works, labels or national scenes into album influences.
