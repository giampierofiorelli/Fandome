# Ennio Morricone — phase 02 evidence notes

Dated **26 October 2026**. Independent, strictly incremental research based on the original album configurations where they could be established. The evidence is graded for the **recording**, not inherited from Morricone's reputation. `mood=0` means extremely bleak, **not** bad quality; a blank scale means no defensible recording-specific measure. The `year` refers to first evidenced long-play album, not the film or first 7-inch. Original-label conflicts are recorded rather than normalized away.

## La resa dei conti — `morricone-laresadeiconti` (1967; medium)

- **Date and version.** An archived original Italian Eureka-Parade EPL 2891(S) LP seller explicitly dates the first pressing to **1967**; independent Musik-Sammler lists original release 1967. The specialty discography identifies the same EPL 2891 13-cue album and the separate U.S. 1968 *The Big Gundown* LP (UA UAS 5190). Conversely Contempo's later double-LP reissue describes the original as 1968 and inaccurately gives the film as 1968; this conflicts with the director/composer film chronology. The original Italian version has two **different-language** title performances: English *Run Man Run* and Italian *Corri uomo corri*. The 1968 US LP omits that exact Italian-album track sequence. A 1967 single is also documented, but it is not the LP. We privilege the 1967 physical-copy dating **provisionally**; do not suppress the conflict. Sources: [Eureka-Parade copy](https://www.popsike.com/ENNIO-MORRICONE-LA-RESA-DEI-CONTI-LP-Eureka-Parade-EPL-2891S-Italia1967/220891733767.html), [Music-Sammler](https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/ennio-morricone-la-resa-dei-conti-cd-223027/), [SoundtrackCollector](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/21013/Resa%2BDei%2BConti%2C%2BLa), [Contempo](https://contemporecords.it/en/prodotto/ennio-morricone-la-resa-dei-conti-2/).
- **Genres and the 0–5 judgments.** `spaghettiwestern` is established by the album-specific critic's description and the original film-music idiom. `avantgarde` is a **texture**, not the dominant style: Contempo explicitly calls parts of the score avant-garde. `soundtrack` marks function, last. Energy **4** reflects tumultuous title and chase passages separated by soft interludes; electronic **1** indicates predominantly orchestral/acoustic sources with amplified guitar; mood **2** is dark, tense with occasional lyrical tenderness; theatrical **4** for operatic vocal climaxes, massed strings and brass; experimental **3** for sharp timbral dislocations; tempo **3** mixed urgent/slow; groove **2** for some rhythmic action cues not dance tracks. `polish` is intentionally **empty**: reviewers describe writing and sound but provide no solid original-release fidelity assessment. Primary listening account [Head Heritage](https://www.headheritage.co.uk/unsung/the-book-of-seth/ennio-morricone-the-big-gundown).
- **Voice, lyrics and language.** Mostly `instrumental`; Christy (Maria Cristina Brancucci) has a strongly projected theatrical English main song and Italian version, giving `powerful`, and Alessandroni's choral voices support climaxes. `en|it` is **limited to those original LP songs**. `storytelling|social` describes those chase/freedom vocal texts, not the whole instrumental score. Morricone's title theme for *Per qualche dollaro in più* is an **entirely different** work with the same title *La resa dei conti*; never merge recordings or evidence. [Original LP copy](https://www.popsike.com/ENNIO-MORRICONE-LA-RESA-DEI-CONTI-LP-Eureka-Parade-EPL-2891S-Italia1967/220891733767.html), [Dramatic Vocalise Database](https://www.philipnauman.com/diss/film_by-comp-b.php?movie_id=79).
- **Instruments:** original LP sleeve description establishes Morricone's orchestra directed by Bruno Nicolai, Bruno Battisti D'Amario guitar, Michele Lacerenza trumpet, and vocal ensemble; independent Head Heritage album review specifically hears guitar, timpani, strings, percussion and a wind solo; `ocarina` is documented in more recent musicological research *Ennio Morricone's Film Music in the Western Genre* (University of Nottingham public abstract/search text, p. 179), but the full redirect could not be retrieved here, so verify the exact page before treating that token as final. List is conservative and avoids attributing Alessandroni's **other** instruments (piano, mandolin) merely because he could play them. `timpani` is independent of plain `percussion`.
- **Production:** left **empty**. One album review uses 'reverbed' for an action cue but neither exact processing device nor **heavy** reverb is established; film sound-effects are not independently documented as multitrack processing. No named record producer of this 1967 pressing confirmed. A **2001/2012 remaster producer** must not migrate into the original `producers` cell. Nicolai is conductor, not evidenced record producer.
- **Agreement / disagreement:** The label and record copy agree on featured soloists and score palette, differ on record year. Head Heritage emphasizes near-operatic emotional shifts, while Contempo highlights avant-garde juxtapositions; these coexist, not a majority vote. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` blank: having the same performers as Leone's earlier scores is not proof of a named album-specific influence; the film's predecessor is not a separate artist.

## Il grande silenzio — `morricone-ilgrandesilenzio` (1968; high)

- **Edition.** Italian Parade **FPR 317, 1968**, 13 cues. MusicBrainz and SwissCharts/Hitparade independently list the historical LP. Later Beat and other reissues sometimes use a different track order, alternate cue spellings (*Voci nel vento* vs *Voci nel deserto*) and bonus material. Do not import reissue additions or the 1995 paired *Un bellissimo novembre* material. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/66e853d6-1c31-4d53-94af-4e4a936f28eb), [Hitparade](https://hitparade.ch/album/Soundtrack-_-Ennio-Morricone/Il-grande-silenzio-299030).
- **Genres and scales.** Album review and music critics classify this as a bleak western soundtrack, but deliberately **less showy than the Leone records**. `spaghettiwestern` captures the genre of musical scoring, `classical` the distinctly orchestral, Bach-like string-adagio language noted by specialist reviewer Joep de Bruijn (not a Bach-influence claim); `experimental` is limited to the dissonant, unnerving textures recognized by Mark Lager. Source [Movie Wave](https://www.movie-wave.net/il-grande-silenzio/), [Maintitles](https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/il-grande-silenzio/), [CineAction](https://cineaction.ca/issue-101/ennio-morricones-elegiac-and-powerful-soundtracks-of-1968/). Energy **2**: pastoral and elegiac material outweighs fierce chase music. Electronic **1**: essentially chamber/orchestral with documented electric guitar. Mood **0**: the reviews emphasize extreme hopelessness and tragedy. Theatrical **4**: high-contrast choral and string climaxes despite otherwise reserved scoring. Experimental **3**: sitar/tabla and dissonant, hard-edged action cues. Tempo **2**, groove **0**: almost all cues function as dramatic material, not dancing. `polish` unscored rather than estimating tape quality.
- **Voice and instruments.** `instrumental|choral` because the vocals are largely a **wordless** Cantori Moderni choir, **not** operatic lead singing or lyrics. Main instruments `strings|guitar|violin|electricguitar|woodwinds|trumpet|piano|celesta|flute|cello|sitar|tabla|percussion` each tied to particular album cues. The pastoral main-title score features strings, guitar, choir; the theme *Invito all'amore* has solo violin and sometimes cello; *Passaggi nel tempo* brings sharper electric guitar, trumpet, sitar and tabla; author Mark Lager names celesta and flute. `sitar` and `tabla` come from Joep de Bruijn's unusually detailed cue review and may be less certain than the main strings, so those instrument-level items should be verified independently if an import strictness gate is applied. [Maintitles](https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/il-grande-silenzio/), [CineAction](https://cineaction.ca/issue-101/ennio-morricones-elegiac-and-powerful-soundtracks-of-1968/), [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2020/09/19/ennio-morricone-reviews-part-vi/).
- **Production and producers:** no original recording engineer/machine/mix technique securely evidenced; **blank**. Do not read 'echo' in a critical description as documented hardware. Names of chorus director / music director should not populate original-record `producers`.
- **Critical nuance:** James Southall calls the music comparatively conventional (relative to Morricone's flamboyant Leone Westerns), while Mark Lager and de Bruijn note exceptionally experimental and dissonant *sequences*. The row assigns experimental **3**, not 5, giving the lyrical majority priority. Neither source names a specific **composer** influencing this album, so `influenced_by` remains empty. Album's `sounds_like` left empty rather than making unlicensed comparisons.

## Il mercenario — `morricone-ilmercenario` (1969; medium)

- **Release chronology.** *Il mercenario* is a 1968 film; United Artists **UA 3153** is a **1968 single** (*Il mercenario*/*Paco*). The first independently itemized full LP found is **UAS 29 005** in **France, 1969** (*El Mercenario*, 15 cues). A specialist record seller describes a German first issue as **1968**, while a different discography dates German UAS 29005 I to **1975**. Apple Music labels a **16-track digital** album December 1968. Those claims do not establish the same **1968 first 12-inch** in primary label records. Put **1969** as first firmly documented LP date, and keep confidence medium until label scans settle it. Sources: [SoundtrackCollector](https://soundtrackcollector.com/title/21411/Mercenario%2C%2BIl), [Tiliqua](https://www.tiliqua-records.com/rare/reco_O-P.html), [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/il-mercenario-original-motion-picture-soundtrack/204246898).
- **Genres and scales.** Jonathan Broxton identifies the overall spaghetti-western music idiom while also calling the main titles a galloping Mexican march. Specialist writing at Movie Music International specifically describes the **samba-infused** *Bamba Vivace*, mariachi *Fiesta* moments and a Viennese **waltz** episode. The first musical genre receives highest weight; `mariachi|samba|march|waltz` are all partial and are ordered by prominence, **not** asserted as the whole recording. [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2020/08/02/ennio-morricone-reviews-part-v/), [Movie Music International](https://jonman492000.wordpress.com/2020/07/17/il-maestro-ennio-morricone-music-memories-and-creating-a-musical-genre/comment-page-1/). Energy **4** and tempo **4** emphasize the galloping call-and-response fanfares; mood **3** because triumphal/revolutionary themes offset threat; theatrical **5** because brasses, voices, percussion and orchestral crescendos are integral; experimental **3** for contrasting whistle, organ, chants and orchestral textures; groove **3** from rhythmic samba/fiesta cuts, not a dance-album claim. Electronic **1** from amplified electric guitar over acoustic ensemble. `polish` left blank.
- **Voice, instruments and lyrics.** Spanish-language songs occur in the 1969 15-cue LP as `Mexican Dream (vocal)` and `Song to My Land (vocal)`, so `language=es`. `choral` reflects the Spanish ensembles; most of the program is instrumental. `political|storytelling` refer **only** to sung revolutionary narrative and historical context, not wordless cues. The critic directly identifies `brass|trumpet|strings|electricguitar|guitar|organ|percussion|whistle|castanets|orchestra`. Avoid `snaredrum`: the well-known film cue *L'Arena* is included in some later 16-track issues but the itemized **first French LP** listing does not name that 4:44 cue (its `Sand or Arena` is a different short listing); the snare is cited for *L'Arena*, so import-level album inclusion is unproven. Likewise do not claim all expansions have the 1969 LP track sequence. [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2020/08/02/ennio-morricone-reviews-part-v/), [SoundtrackCollector](https://soundtrackcollector.com/title/21411/Mercenario%2C%2BIl).
- **Credits / producers.** Original compositions and orchestration credited to **Ennio Morricone**, with **Bruno Nicolai conducting** the reissue-documented performance. A specialist essay uses the looser phrase “collaboration between Morricone and Nicolai”, but that is not an independently substantiated composer / sound-production co-credit for the first LP. `producers` stays blank pending original sleeve or session sheets. No specific signal processing or recording gear confirmed, so `production` blank. [Reissue credit](https://www.traumbaum.de/en/releases/165030.html), [MMI assessment](https://jonman492000.wordpress.com/2020/07/17/il-maestro-ennio-morricone-music-memories-and-creating-a-musical-genre/comment-page-1/).
- **Influences:** No direct album-specific named musical artist influence. Mexican styles are genre/style descriptors, not musicians. The reuse of *L'Arena* by Quentin Tarantino is a later synchronization, **not** evidence that Tarantino influenced the recording.

## C’era una volta il West — `morricone-ceraunavoltailwest` (1969; high for sounds, medium for date)

- **Catalog identity and dates.** Fandome already indexes `C’era una volta il West` with **1968**, corresponding to the film, and the exact ID must be **preserved**. In the Phase 01 first-original-LP methodology the early full 13-cue RCA record is best assigned **1969**, supported by a **1969 French** pressing and at least one Italian collector record. MusicBrainz and another discography date the **Italian RCA OLS 3** issue **1970**. Thus exact *Italian* first press remains unsettled; as soon as the importer standardizes album-year rather than film-year, review a catalogue-year correction to 1969, avoiding silent automatic edits. Sources: [SoundtrackCollector](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/1367/C%27era%2BUna%2BVolta%2BIl%2BWest), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/06d095ae-88a6-309b-8e88-e87bd99d4fb0), [1969 Italian collector entry](https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/ennio-morricone-cera-una-volta-il-west-il-lp-618299/).
- **Genres.** The score's main identity is `spaghettiwestern` in the **musical** tradition of Morricone's Leone recordings; `classical` refers to extended orchestral classical-motif form (musicologist Guido Heldt); `folk|country` refer the American traditional-music idioms named by Mark Lager, notably Cheyenne's theme. Lager also explicitly describes a mixture of **Italian opera** in the scoring: `opera` is a **texture**, not an operatic libretto. `soundtrack` is format only. BFI says Second Viennese School admiration but that does not license names of individual composers in `influenced_by`. Sources: [BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/lists/ennio-morricone-10-scores), [CineAction](https://cineaction.ca/issue-101/ennio-morricones-elegiac-and-powerful-soundtracks-of-1968/), [Heldt, pp.201–203](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/42/64/68/2020426468.pdf).
- **Scales.** Energy **3** as a balance of tense violent ostinati and gentle expansive themes; electronic **1** with acoustic orchestra plus electric guitar (musicologist mentions Cheyenne's electronic timbres, no named synthesizer); mood **2** given longing, violence, occasional wry relaxation; theatrical **5** for the vast arcs and prominent solo voice; experimental **4** for extreme differences between character leitmotifs including abrasive electric-guitar timbres and unusual instrumental contrasts; tempo **2** mainly slow or moderately paced; groove **1** because Cheyenne has a lilt, but the album is not dance-focused. `polish` blank: orchestral richness does not establish engineering polish.
- **Voice / lyrics.** Mostly instrumental, but wordless soprano **Edda Dell’Orso** (opera-trained singing) and **I Cantori Moderni di Alessandroni** make `instrumental|operatic|choral` appropriate. No sung language, so **leave language blank**. Wordless syllables are not meaningful words: leave lyrics blank. BFI and original-recording credits distinguish soprano from chorus.
- **Instrument-level provenance.** **Guido Heldt (2013)** ties Jill’s material to `harpsichord|celesta|strings|brass` (French horns) and Dell’Orso's voice; Harmonica's instrument `harmonica`; Frank's `electricguitar`; Cheyenne's `banjo`; Morton's `piano`. BFI independently gives **flute** and Edda's voice for Jill, electric guitar and harmonica for antagonists. Craig Lysy describes harp/woodblock accompaniment for Cheyenne, a `mallets`-class vibraphone with harpsichord and cello for Jill, and orchestra and woodwinds. `percussion` for clopping effects in Cheyenne cues; `whistle` for his relaxed loping theme. All cited instruments are supported *for this score*, and major recurring motifs are on original 13-track RCA set. **BFI describes a “dulcimer” for Jill but Heldt hears harpsichord/celesta**; to avoid an ambiguous identification, we did **not** add `dulcimer` or `hammereddulcimer` without a verified specific player/instrument credit.
- **Production:** `distortion` narrowly justified by Heldt's detailed description of **Frank's distorted electric guitar** (not a claim of any pedal or rack model). Film opening sound design (windmill, insects, water, or silence) is discussed in some critical accounts, but film-only effects or on-set sound **must not** become `fieldrecordings`, `analogtape`, `heavyreverb` or other record production tokens. The album does not carry enough evidence to populate `producers`: Morricone composer/arranger/conductor is a different category from credited **sound record producer**. Source [Heldt, p.202](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/42/64/68/2020426468.pdf) independently **viewed in the source PDF screenshot**.
- **Critic variance:** BFI describes Jill's lead timbre as a dulcimer, musicologist Heldt distinguishes keyboard timbres, and Craig Lysy specifies vibraphone; the proposed row retains named, corroborated instrument categories and flags the disputed dulcimer. A 1988 remastered CD lists digital production personnel: these people must **not** be inserted as 1969 original LP producers. Comparison labels `sounds_like` and album-specific `influenced_by` are **empty** since assigning arbitrary other composers undermines recommendation explainability.

## Shared import cautions

1. The *format* tag `soundtrack` is included last. Distinguish film genre, recorded music genre and full original **album** repertoire in `genre_evidence.csv`; `mariachi` is the only **new** phase-02 genre not already introduced for phase 01.
2. **No `producers` were invented.** Composer, orchestrator, conductor, film producer, sound mixer, music publisher, and reissue producer are different credit categories.
3. Only documented instruments and process tags are used. Do not backfill technology from album style or inferred era. `distortion` is the sole phase-02 production token, tied to a specific analysis of Frank's guitar on West.
4. Fandome is still missing `morricone.csv` on the public `descriptors` branch when checked. This is an **import handoff**, not a GitHub commit.
