# Alanis Morissette — Phase 01: album-by-album evidence notes

**Scope:** 25 October 2026. The Fandome `descriptors` branch had **no artist entry for Alanis Morissette** and no `docs/descriptors/alanis.csv`. `alanis` is a **proposed new ID**, not a verified existing ID. The catalogue must be extended (and the new ID approved) before importing. Do not confuse the catalogue with MusicBrainz. The earliest two releases were originally **billed as Alanis** even though we normalize the Fandome artist field to the full name. The CSV uses original studio albums only, not compilations, live releases, anniversary extras, or unreleased projects.

All scales are Fandome **editorial judgments** anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`, never measured acoustic properties. Genres are separately referenced and track-limited sounds are deliberately last. Source links and caveats are provided below. A missing production technique reflects unavailable evidence, not absence of that technique.

## `alanis-alanis` — *Alanis* (1991)

- **Release / performers / production:** [MusicBrainz original MCA issue](https://musicbrainz.org/release/b5f74014-e32f-40b3-8068-bde71d209ebe) and [original-cover credit transcription](https://alanismorissette.fandom.com/wiki/Alanis_%28album%29) support producer **Leslie Howe**; guitar, keyboards, and **drum programming**. The source lists Serge Côté as keyboard player. Howe's extra keyboards are specifically credited. String **arrangement** credited to Frank Levin for *On My Own* explains the production token `stringarrangements`, but does not certify an actual orchestral/string-section recording, so `strings` is not in instruments.
- **Producer caveat:** Some secondary listings call Frank Levin a producer of *Human Touch*, while an attributed sleeve transcript uses the nonstandard label “mastermind.” For the album-level list only Leslie Howe is clearly confirmed. Claude should check actual sleeve if considering Levin as a track-level producer; songwriting does **not** imply production.
- **Genres / voice:** [Canadian Music Hall of Fame](https://canadianmusichalloffame.ca/inductee/alanis-morissette/) and [Daily Vault, Christopher Thelen](https://dailyvault.com/toc.php5?review=3261) explicitly describe a dance-pop debut. Thelen distinguishes bouncy club numbers from standout ballad *On My Own*, and likens the commercial format to Tiffany. Vocals `clear|powerful` are interpretive within reviews rather than measured range.
- **Scales:** Energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 4 from dominant upbeat tracks but with ballad exception; electronic 3 from credited drum programming and keys combined with guitar; mood 4 from chiefly effervescent treatment; theatrical 3 from commercial lead singing; experimental 0 from conventional song structures; polish 3 from fully produced dance-pop. **Do not apply the 1995 rock persona retroactively.**
- **Lyrics:** `love|heartbreak|party|identity` tracks and contemporary descriptions. The least securely supported is `identity`; if requiring song-by-song lyric transcription, downgrade or leave it empty. `sounds_like` is editorial sonic similarity, *not* influence. `influenced_by` left blank.
- **Critical dispute:** Thelen thought some hooks worked, but judged later songs generic; industry biography emphasizes early commercial success. Neither should erase the stylistic facts. Confidence `medium` because accessible 1991 liner and critical evidence are scarcer.

## `alanis-nowisthetime` — *Now Is the Time* (1992)

- **Release / producer:** [AllMusic discography](https://www.allmusic.com/album/now-is-the-time-mw0000386186), [AllMusic artist biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/alanis-morissette-mn0000932665) and [secondary original-sleeve transcription](https://alanismorissette.fandom.com/wiki/Now_Is_the_Time) corroborate the 1992 release and **Leslie Howe as producer**. Serge Côté appears repeatedly as *songwriter*, not credited co-producer: he must not be added to `producers` on that basis.
- **Instruments / production:** [Album credits transcription](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_Is_the_Time) names keyboards (Côté) and guitar (Howe); guitar type is unknown, hence use broad existing repo token `guitar`, not invented acoustic/electric specificity. Although Howe programmed drums on *Alanis*, no independently corroborated documentation establishes **drum programming on this 1992 album**; the production column is intentionally empty. No synth brand, sampler, or software is assumed.
- **Genre boundary:** [1992 album source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_Is_the_Time) names `pop`, `teenpop`, `dancepop`. [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/37284-alanis-morissette-now-is-the-time.php) also lists `newjackswing`, but its supporting scope is **low-confidence** and must remain low-ranked and a *texture* until independent verification. [AllMusic biographer](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/morissette-mn0000932665) calls the follow-up more stripped-back and ballad-centred than the debut. Do not misclassify as 1995 alternative rock.
- **Scales:** Energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2 distinguish fuller ballad weight from debut; electronic 2 for the documented keyboards next to guitar (not a drum machine claim); mood 3 mixed; theatrical 2, experimental 0 and polish 3 reflect conventional, controlled commercial pop arrangements. These are constrained **judgments**, not source-quantified properties.
- **Lyrics / tone:** `love|heartbreak|introspection|identity`; limited extant serious criticism means `medium` confidence. `sounds_like` comparisons are sonic judgments based on early commercial pop context; no documented influence was asserted.

## `alanis-jaggedlittlepill` — *Jagged Little Pill* (1995)

- **Identity and genre:** [Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/news/feature-alanis-morissettes-jagged-little-pill-record/) explicitly identifies the third album as alternative rock. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000644720) assigns Alternative Pop/Rock, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock, Contemporary Pop/Rock and Post-Grunge. Thus `alternativerock` and `alternativepoprock` coexist, not interchangeable; pop-rock labels represent crossover accessibility. These genre tags classify the *album*, though aggressive electric-guitar numbers and acoustic passages vary substantially.
- **Producer / instrumental sources:** [MusicBrainz original 1995 release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/3b58a643-6152-4785-bc35-7bb7a08f8dad) and [AllMusic liner credits](https://www.allmusic.com/cg/amg.dll?p=amg&sql=10%3Aw9foxquhldhe~T0) confirm **Glen Ballard** as sole producer; Morissette is a writer and performer, **not credited as co-producer on the original album**. Electric guitars and acoustic sections are documented but original personnel credit mostly unqualified `guitar`, so retain `guitar` broad. Credited drum kit, bass guitar, keys, organ, harmonica and percussion justify the instrument ordering. Flea (bass), Dave Navarro (guitar) and Benmont Tench (organ) are musicians, never producers or `influenced_by` values.
- **Technical recording:** [1997 first-person Sound on Sound interview with engineer Christopher Fogel](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/christopher-fogel-alanis-morissettes-jagged-little-pill) is unusually specific: early tracks started with loops/guitar/vocals; live drums were added to five tracks; organ appears across the record; lead vocals were commonly early takes; overdubs selective; ADAT multitrack tracked the project, while analogue tape was used on later drum transfers and several master sources; some other tracks mastered from DAT. Hence `adatrecording|onetake|overdubbing|layeredvocals|sampling|analogtape`, with **layeredvocals** based on Fogel's example of twelve backing voices on *All I Really Want*, and **sampling** limited to explicit sampled rhythmic material in MusicBrainz. These are **not** claims that every track used every process.
- **Recording caveat:** Although Sound on Sound's 1997 profile mentions Pro Tools and Cubase, those products concern plans for a *subsequent album*. **Do not label this record `protools` or `cubase`**. An unqualified loop does not prove any hardware sampler model. `analogtape` is not the album's main original recording format.
- **Scale reasoning:** Energy 4 for driving rock/rhythm tracks alongside some quieter cuts; electronic 1 for loops and programming but primarily guitar-band source; mood 2 because confessional anger, anxiety and heartbreak dominate despite bright melodies; theatrical 4 for volatile projected vocals; experimental 1 because verse-chorus songs remain highly accessible; polish 3 because polished craft retains rough first-take performances; tempo 3 / groove 2 for a varied rock set not built for dancing. `voice=powerful|raspy|clear|soft` reflects forceful high-register belting, distinctive timbre, and softer acoustic passages.
- **Themes / comparison:** `heartbreak|identity|introspection|love|social|irony`, involving rejection, pressure, expectations, independence and narrative irony in *Ironic*. All `sounds_like` are **editorial** sonic similarities; no specific cited influence was compelling enough to populate `influenced_by`. The album is a defining `landmark=1`.
- **Critical differences:** [Morissette's own retrospective](https://medium.com/cuepoint/jagged-little-pill-an-essay-c77a67c7daed) emphasizes imperfect spontaneous takes, contrasting later pop perfection narratives; [PRX oral history](https://theworld.org/stories/2015/12/17/you-oughta-know-oral-history-alanis-morissettes-jagged-little-pill) provides corroboration. The album's pop songwriting is deliberate, even where marketed as alternative rock.

## `alanis-supposedformerinfatuationjunkie` — *Supposed Former Infatuation Junkie* (1998)

- **Release / genre:** The 17-track original 1998 edition is described by [Stephen Thomas Erlewine at AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000600813) with multiple alternative pop/rock styles; [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/supposed-former-infatuation-junkie-alanis-morissette/0093624709466) broadly categorizes it as Pop. [eonmusic retrospective](https://www.eonmusic.co.uk/reviews/25-years-on-alanis-morissettes-supposed-former-infatuation-junkie-still-has-bite) explicitly identifies **Bristol trip-hop touch** on *I Was Hoping* and **bhangra beat** on *Front Row*. Hence `triphop` only on some tracks and **new token `bhangra`** tagged as texture, at end of genre list. It is **not a uniform trip-hop or bhangra album**.
- **Production / personnel:** [MusicBrainz original CD](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0907197a-155e-33ff-af01-f40ec660c74c) and [Qobuz label-supplied credits](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/supposed-former-infatuation-junkie-alanis-morissette/0093624709466) independently credit **Alanis Morissette and Glen Ballard** as producers. Morissette flute, harmonica, piano; Ballard guitar, piano, synthesizer, programming; Benmont Tench organ and **Chamberlin**, Chris Chaney bass, Gary Novak drum kit/percussion, Joel Shearer and Nick Lashley guitars. `cello` comes from eonmusic's cello-led *Your Congratulations*. String-arrangement credits for David Campbell/Ballard justify `stringarrangements`; *Thank U*'s sampled beat supports `sampling`. No identified device/software package claimed.
- **Disputed instrument identification:** Qobuz describes Benmont Tench's keyboard as a **Mellotron**; AllMusic and original MusicBrainz credit **Chamberlin**. Use `chamberlin` for now (two aligned sources), document conflict instead of silently listing both as distinct definite instruments.
- **Influence attribution:** [Richard Harrington, Washington Post, 3 Nov 1998](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1998/11/04/alanis-morissette-rhythms-of-the-soul/59f50989-e0e1-466e-8997-2f85304b397b/) explicitly says Morissette had long cited Tori Amos's *Little Earthquakes*, and identifies vocal qawwali ornamentation associated with **Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan**. These are the two properly sourced `influenced_by` entries, with **Nusrat** a critic-linked sonic influence (not a direct claim Morissette named him). Portishead is a **sonic resemblance**, not confirmed artist-acknowledged influence. The India journey did affect the music; the Cuba journey cannot be used to claim Cuban rhythmic influences without evidence.
- **Scales:** Energy 3 (mix of urgent and subdued songs), electronic 2 (programmed beats, synth with band instrumentation), mood 2 (brooding but redemptive), theatrical 3 (controlled vocals with eruptions), experimental 3 (17-song nonlinear lyrical streams, imported rhythmic/ornamental textures), polish 3 (deliberately restrained rather than big radio gloss), tempo 2 and groove 2 (pensive long-form songs outrank dance moments). This is Fandome editorial calibration. `voice` softer/more controlled relative to 1995 without erasing raspy belt.
- **Themes / critical disagreement:** Spiritual searching, self-acceptance, personal relationships and self-criticism supported in 1998 Washington Post review. [The Independent's Tim Perry](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/album-reviews-1183407.html) found the prolonged 72-minute inward gaze tiresome; [AllMusic's Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000600813) values ambition and sonic expansion. Both are recorded, so its defining-album `landmark=1` is a recommendation judgment, not unanimity about quality.

## Corpus exclusions and forthcoming title

- 1987 *Fate Stay with Me* is a **single**, not an album. The compilation *Her First Two Albums* is a repackaging and not a new studio work.
- *MTV Unplugged* (1999) is live: consider a **separate landmark exception** after studio phases; do not include automatically.
- *Jagged Little Pill Acoustic* (2005) is a **studio re-recording** of 1995 material and should receive a separate, non-duplicating value-of-remake assessment later.
