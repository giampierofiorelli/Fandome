# Beyoncé — Phase 01: album-level evidence notes

Research basis: original standard-edition studio albums, not anniversary reissues, remixes, live albums or greatest-hits sets. All ratings are independent editorial estimates using the Fandome anchors. `confidence` rates source coverage, not whether a subjective score has a scientific ground truth. Source keys/URLs appear in the sibling CSVs.

## 2003 — Dangerously in Love

- **Genres**: AllMusic applies contemporary R&B, dance-pop and pop to the record; The Guardian emphasizes balladry alongside dance songs. The Los Angeles Times identifies funk on “Crazy in Love,” disco styling on “Naughty Girl,” and reggae/house accents on “Baby Boy.” Genre evidence separates album-wide styles and individual tracks. Dancehall is supported by the Sean Paul collaboration and “Baby Boy” context; this must remain local rather than overriding the R&B backbone.
- **Scores**: Energy 3 and tempo 3 balance aggressive hits with numerous long ballads; groove 3 reflects a mixed record. Electronic 2 is appropriate for sample-based studio R&B mixed with live instrumentation, not an electronic-first album. Mood 3 contains excitement and heartbreak; theatrical 3 reflects vocal projection but otherwise conventional song frameworks; experimental 1 and polish 5 reflect a commercial, highly refined pop debut. These are review-led judgments, not inferred from the singer’s general style.
- **Voice and lyrics**: LA Times discusses low, soft phrases as well as high/forceful singing; themes of attraction, commitment, disappointment are widespread.
- **Instrumentation**: MusicBrainz release credits name guitar on “Dangerously in Love 2”, and bass/drums/keyboards plus violin/viola/cello/strings on the standard-release “The Closer I Get to You” performance. A horn *sample* on “Crazy in Love” is NOT counted as a live horn player; likewise sampled Donna Summer does not enter `influenced_by`.
- **Production**: Sampling is documented for “Crazy in Love” (Chi-Lites), “Naughty Girl” (Donna Summer), “Be With You” (Shuggie Otis); string arranging is credited for “The Closer I Get to You.” No drum-machine model identified.
- **Producers**: MusicBrainz credits Beyoncé with Rich Harrison, Scott Storch, Focus…, Missy Elliott, Craig Brockman, Nisan Stewart, Bryce Wilson, Erroll McCalla Jr. and Mark Batson across standard tracks. Brockman and Stewart have *co-producer* roles; “additional producer” alone is insufficient for Big Boi. Mark Batson is a main producer, unlike a mere strings arranger. Catalog row may be expanded after full booklet review.
- **Disagreement**: Guardian's verdict is harsh on cohesion, while retrospective acclaim emphasizes landmark singles. Landmark describes career impact, not uniform critical praise. `influenced_by` stays empty: the credited samples are not personal inspirations.

## 2006 — B’Day

- **Genres**: AllMusic and GRAMMY classify it as R&B/pop with substantial funk. Original Pitchfork review says comparatively little balladry and intense, kinetic grooves. Retrospective Pitchfork explicitly locates DC go-go feel in “Freakum Dress”, so new `gogo` is scoped to some tracks, not the album as a whole.
- **Scores**: Energy 4, tempo 4 and groove 4 reflect the dominance of urgent uptempo percussion compared with its predecessor. Mood 4 and theatrical 4 account for celebratory/assertive performances despite darker “Ring the Alarm” and “Resentment.” Electronic 2 balances programmed beats with horns, bass and live playing; experimental 2 for fractured chants/more disruptive grooves. Polish 4 is professional but intentionally more raw/urgent than the debut.
- **Instruments**: MusicBrainz credits live bass (Jon Jon), horns and arrangements (Rodney Jerkins), and guitar on tracks/editions; source-supported keyboards/drums/percussion appear in session testimony. No analogue synthesizer model is inferred. Brass is the main reusable token; trumpet and trombone are not asserted on the main row without stronger coverage of the original edition.
- **Production**: `hornarrangements` is directly credited by MusicBrainz. `drumprogramming` and `layeredvocals` have producer/session testimony in the 2026 oral-history reprint; `sampling` is evident from “Suga Mama” and other documented sampled grooves. An engineer discusses an “808” in the “Déjà Vu” production, but that does not establish a specific Roland TR-808 hardware unit; `tr808` is *not* entered. MPC-mentioned sessions are track-based and not safely assigned to the album’s recurring sound, so MPC remains out.
- **Producers**: Beyoncé, Rodney Jerkins, Swizz Beatz, Rich Harrison, Sean Garrett, The Neptunes, StarGate, Walter Millsap III, Candice Nelson and Cameron Wallace appear in production accounts. Ne-Yo is included only where an explicit co-producer credit is supplied, not for songwriting. Do not turn Makeba Riddick's songwriting/vocal direction into a general producer credit. Deluxe-only contributions should not overwrite standard-edition credits.
- **Influence**: Producer Rodney Jerkins recounts listening to Michael Jackson’s *Off the Wall* when imagining “Déjà Vu.” This supports Michael Jackson as a **producer-documented album influence**, unlike the Chi-Lites sample. The oral history is a reprint; use medium strength for machinery specifics.
- **Edition caveat**: 2007 deluxe includes “Beautiful Liar” with Shakira and Spanish-language tracks. These are not treated as the album's standard sonic centre; `language=en`, no `es`, and no Shakira co-billing for the album itself.

## 2008 — I Am… Sasha Fierce

- **Genres**: AllMusic's contemporary R&B/pop/adult contemporary is album-level. Guardian distinctly describes the *I Am…* half as ballads/MOR rock and *Sasha Fierce* as electronic R&B, with *Radio* called synthpop and *Diva* unusually skeletal. Synthpop and soft rock belong to **some tracks** rather than the entire double-album.
- **Scores**: Energy 3, tempo 3 and groove 3 average the subdued first half against the assertive second half; scoring either disc alone would be misleading. Electronic 3 balances guitars and live drums on “If I Were a Boy” with electronic, synth-centered club productions. Mood 3 spans vulnerability and exuberance. Theatrical 4 is supported by the dual persona, intense vocals and anthems; experimental 2 for tracks like “Diva” but mostly familiar structures; polish 5 is glossy crossover pop.
- **Instrumentation**: MusicBrainz explicitly credits live and additional drums plus guitar on “If I Were a Boy,” guitar on “Ave Maria,” and unspecified instruments on other tracks. Unspecified instrumentation is not promoted to synth or keyboards merely because a review says synthpop.
- **Production**: Production is intentionally empty: electronic arrangements in reviews do not prove a specific drum-programming technique, autotune, vocoder or machine model. Leave unknown until a technical credit is identified.
- **Producers**: Standard-edition MusicBrainz credits include Beyoncé, Toby Gad, Ryan Tedder, StarGate, Amanda Ghost, Ian Dench, Dave McCracken, Tricky Stewart, The-Dream, Jim Jonsin, Rico Love, Sean Garrett and Bangladesh. In `producers`, Amanda Ghost/Ian Dench/Dave McCracken are specifically credited as producers (not merely songwriters). Credits vary by edition; do not infer all extra musicians from the deluxe edition.
- **Influences**: No specific musical artist is sufficiently tied to this precise studio album in the reviewed sources; the artist-level Tina Turner/Mariah Carey influences should not be copied to this row.
- **Disagreement**: Guardian's two reviews sharply question the 'true self' conceit and the ballad disc, while confirming the electronic-versus-acoustic contrast. This is a sound descriptor, not a critical endorsement.

## 2011 — 4

- **Genres**: AllMusic lists contemporary R&B, adult contemporary R&B, pop and dance-pop, while Guardian reviews describe pervasive retro soul and 1980s R&B. “Countdown” explicitly carries *Afrobeat brass* and “Run the World (Girls)” a *dancehall rhythm*: both are carefully designated **some tracks**. Afrobeat must not be treated as album-wide merely because Beyoncé cites Fela Kuti.
- **Scores**: Energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 because several first-half tracks are slow, despite climactic “Countdown” and “End of Time.” Electronic 2: guitars, live drums, horns, bass and piano support a more organic band texture than *Sasha Fierce*, although “Run the World” is electronic. Mood 3 combines heartbreak/anger, optimism and affection; theatrical 4 from demanding vocal performances and brass climaxes; experimental 2 for unusual song structures/“Run the World”; polish 4 for pristine multitrack production.
- **Instrumentation**: MusicBrainz standard 2011 edition separately credits Nikki Glaspie on drums, Jeff Bhasker on guitar/keys, Superpower Horns brass, Drew Sayers and Nick Videen saxophones, Cole Kamen-Green/Josiah Woodson on trumpet, Alex Asher on trombone, Jack Daley on bass, and Brent Kutzle/Ryan Tedder on piano and cello. These are not genre-to-instrument guesses. Guardian hears steel-drum colours in “Countdown”, but the MusicBrainz personnel do not separately credit played steel drums; leave `steeldrums` out rather than conflate the sonic timbre with an instrument credit.
- **Production**: Horn and strings arrangement credited; Jeff Bhasker credited drum programming; “Countdown” directly samples Boyz II Men; Beyoncé explicitly describes using live-performance vocal grit and belting, while Guardian describes falsetto turns. Machine-specific plugins and drum units remain unknown.
- **Producers**: Source release lists credited producer/co-producer roles across tracks for Beyoncé, Shea Taylor, The-Dream, Tricky Stewart, Jeff Bhasker, Babyface, Antonio Dixon, Caleb McCampbell, S1, Kanye West, Luke Steele, Ryan Tedder, Brent Kutzle, Kuk Harrell and Switch. Some sources call Switch 'additional producer' only for “End of Time” but he is producer on “Run the World”; do not infer credit from the 'additional production' role. Frank Ocean wrote “I Miss You” but was not credited as producer and is excluded. Diplo's sample on “Run the World” alone is not a producer credit.
- **Influences**: Beyoncé names Fela Kuti, Earth Wind & Fire, DeBarge, Lionel Richie, Teena Marie, Jackson 5, New Edition, Adele, Florence + the Machine, Prince explicitly in a 2011 interview about writing *4*. Every named act has this direct interview source. Do not enter sampled Boyz II Men as an album influence on that basis alone.
- **Disagreement**: Guardian describes an uneven retro focus, while AllMusic discusses an intentionally non-commercial sequence. The conflicting valuations do not negate shared sonic evidence.
