# Mina — Phase 02: evidence and decisions

**Scope.** Four editorially important original albums (1966, 1967, 1968 and 1971), not a complete Mina discography. Verified against the Fandome `descriptors` branch on 25 October 2026. Four earlier Phase 01 entries already exist: Tintarella di luna, Il cielo in una stanza, Mina (1964), Studio Uno (1965). **Never duplicate or replace these.** Source identifiers below are prefixes in `mina_phase02_sources.csv` `what_it_supports`.

## `mina-studiouno66` — *Studio Uno '66* (1966)
- **Original identity**: Ri-Fi RFL LP 14016, twelve songs [S-OFF, S-LP]. 1965 *Studio Uno* is a separate predecessor. The album draws on Mina's TV appearance, not a live concert album. The catalogue's title could retain the official typographic apostrophe; generated slug remains `studiouno66`.
- **Main genres**: melodic Italian Pop / Euro-Pop / Adult Contemporary from AllMusic [S-AM]; TV-based musica leggera with orchestration [S-OFF]; US theatrical standards *Ebb Tide* and *Lontanissimo (Somewhere)* are **track subset only**, not enough to say the LP is predominantly jazz [S-JAZZ]. The `jazz` tag deliberately very weak and should be reviewed.
- **0–5 judgments**: `electronic=0` reflects orchestral instruments and no documented synth- or drum-machine-led tracks; `energy=3, groove=2, tempo=3` because the record combines some rhythmic pop with big slow ballads, **not** a dance record [S-OFF,S-AM]. `mood=3` balances cheerier Ta-ra-ta-ta with heartbreak / yearning songs. `theatrical=4` reflects elaborate TV-variety vocal climaxes and dramatic song arrangements. `polish=4` for arranged Ri-Fi studio recordings; `experimental=1` for conventional song structures despite one exceptionally inventive arrangement [S-MOR]. All numeric values remain editorial judgments, not reviewer-scored measurements.
- **Personnel**: Wladimiro Albera is documented as *producer* in sleeve-derived metadata [S-WIKI]; Morricone is composer and arranger of one track only [S-OFF,S-MOR]. Other orchestral conductors/arrangers must **not** automatically enter `producers`.
- **Influences**: Morricone describes *the recording* and praises Mina but does not establish an upstream artist inspiring this LP [S-MOR]. `influenced_by` therefore blank. References to Dusty Springfield, Shirley Bassey and Petula Clark appear in `sounds_like` as editorial comparisons only.
- **Language**: predominantly Italian; *Ebb Tide* is an English-language standard in its original repertoire. The LP's specific recorded language for this song is not established as securely as the title; `it|en` provisional. Reduce to `it` if the original performance is shown to be Italian.

## `mina-dedicatoamiopadre` — *Dedicato a mio padre* (1967)
- **Importance**: original first PDU studio album, founded with father Giacomo Mazzini; Italian critic Giorgio Moltisanti in *Rolling Stone Italia* identifies a rare artistic peak, mixing American balladry, English-language standards, and De André's *La canzone di Marinella* [D-RS,D-OFF,D-EN]. Original 1967 LP distinguished from 1970 repackaging [D-IT].
- **Genres**: jazz/vocal jazz/standards substantial; `folk` concerns *La canzone di Marinella* and regional narrative material only, `latin` concerns Spanish repertoire, `bossanova` only *So*, `swing` an arrangement characteristic, not strong all-LP category [D-OFF,D-JAZZ,D-RS]. Other pop family labels reflect AllMusic broad catalogue taxonomy, not evidence the album lacks jazz [D-AM].
- **Scales**: `electronic=0` grounded in acoustic orchestra, brass, drums, strings as described by the Italian critic. `energy=2, tempo=2, groove=2` weighted toward measured standards; `mood=2` subdued late-night emotional repertoire; `theatrical=3` balances intimate song readings with show-standard vocal projection; `experimental=2` because shifting language and repertoire depart from earlier TV pop, though arrangements remain conventional. `polish=4` is clean orchestral studio recording with reportedly near-live-take performance [D-RS].
- **Voice and lyrics**: predominantly clear and powerful with softer phrasing, narratives/romantic longing and nostalgic standards. Songs are in English, Italian (including dialect), Spanish; the Brazilian *So* is an Italian reworking, not proof of album-wide Portuguese singing [D-IT,D-OFF].
- **Crediting**: Augusto Martelli **arranges and conducts**, not proven to have a formal producer credit [D-IT,D-OFF]; producer cell blank. Interpreting De André does not demonstrate that he was a documented influence on Mina's whole record; `influenced_by` blank.

## `mina-canzonissima68` — *Canzonissima '68* (1968)
- **Original identity**: PDU PLD A 5005 first pressing, twelve studio tracks; themed to the widely followed RAI television program. *Zum zum zum* and *Vorrei che fosse amore* are themes; it is **not** a captured live programme [C-LP,C-TRACK,C-IT].
- **Genre scope**: Italian pop, musica leggera / romantic orchestral songs dominate. *Niente di niente* adapts the Delfonics' soul/R&B song, and *Sacumdì sacumdà* adapts Brazilian *Nem Vem Que Não Tem*. They support separate `soul`, `rnb`, and `bossanova` as **limited-scope** genre signals; retail soul tag alone is weak [C-BIO,C-MERCH,C-TRACK]. Italian discography supplies `folk`, weak and not representative of most tracks [C-IT]. No categorical genre caps.
- **Scales**: `electronic=0` (period orchestra, not synthesis); `energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3` due to playful, syncopated TV songs amidst dramatic ballads; `mood=3` for light and dark cuts; `theatrical=4` prime-time variety with full-orchestra delivery; `polish=4`; `experimental=1` for mainstream arrangement approach. These are informed editorial averages for the **original twelve** tracks, not later television reprises.
- **Producers**: Bruno Canfora, Augusto Martelli and Luis Bacalov documented as **arrangers / orchestra conductors**, not verified LP producers [C-SLEEVE,C-WIKI]. Producer deliberately blank; sources disagree or incompletely list Bacalov, note track B2 only. `influenced_by` blank; covers / writers are not necessarily influence attributions.
- **Voice / language**: powerful-clear Italian lead, Italian adaptations and originals. The *Treccani* study documents Mina's cultural impact with *La voce del silenzio*; do not mistake it for Mina composing the song [C-TRE].

## `mina-mina1971` — *Mina* (November 1971), distinguished as *Mina (1971)* in Fandome
- **Critical duplicate-ID hazard**: A 1964 album already titled *Mina* holds `mina-mina`. This *different original album* was issued in November **1971**, PDU PLD A 5037, known as the monkey-cover album (twelve songs) [M-LP,M-IT,M-OFF]. **To preserve both**, store the later catalogue tuple and descriptor title as `Mina (1971)`, generating valid Fandome ID `mina-mina1971`. The physical release title is simply *Mina*, not a different album with a new official title. Claude must append the disambiguation to the catalog title; **never overwrite `mina-mina`.**
- **Signature tracks and musical shape**: *E penso a te*, *Grande, grande, grande*, *Amor mio*, *Alfie*, *Something* [M-LP,M-AM]. The record balances dramatic Italian romantic ballads with some international pop/jazz repertoire. Expert taxonomy Pop/Rock, Euro-Pop, Italian Pop, Adult Contemporary [M-AM]; some secondary sources label blue-eyed soul [M-EN] which is **weak retrospective** evidence, not proof it is uniformly R&B.
- **Genre addition**: `blueeyedsoul` absent from present scenes, families and validator. Claude should add the token in all three only if this weak specific categorisation is approved; other supplied tokens already exist. `jazz` and `standards` denote a small subset (*Alfie*), not the dominant aesthetic.
- **Production**: Pino Presti (E penso a te, Grande grande grande), Augusto Martelli (Le farfalle nella notte, Sentimentale), Gian Piero Reverberi (Capirò, Amor mio, Mi fai sentire così strana), Mario Robbiani (other songs) are explicitly **arrangers/conductors**, not documented formal producers [M-MBASE,M-IT]. Vittorio Buffoli is *coordinator*, Nuccio Rinaldis/Abramo Pesatori are engineers. All **excluded** from `producers` until a distinct production credit exists.
- **0–5**: `electronic=0` for documented conventional instrumental orchestra, not digital/synthetic. `energy=2, tempo=2, groove=2` because romantic ballads dominate; `mood=2` poignant affection and longing, mixed with brighter moments; `theatrical=4` from full-bodied dramatic climaxes; `polish=4` from elaborate orchestration; `experimental=1` conventional forms despite cross-language covers. Reflect album overall, not the hit *Grande, grande, grande* alone.
- **Voice / lyrics**: `powerful|clear|soft` for striking dynamics from controlled verses to soaring choruses; Italian plus English covers; `love|heartbreak|introspection` supported by album repertoire and romantic lyric content. Influenced_by blank: covering Beatles and Burt Bacharach standards or featuring Battisti songs **does not prove influence from those artists**.

## Uncertainties / constraints
- These are **editorial descriptors**, not measured tempo or acoustic tests. Specific thinly supported secondary genre tags have low evidence strength and should not be elevated above the main ones by Claude.
- This phase **selects landmark discs only**; it does not claim no other Mina album exists 1966–1971. *Mina 2*, *Mina alla Bussola dal vivo*, *Mina canta o Brasil*, and *…quando tu mi spiavi in cima a un batticuore…* need separate editorial selection rather than automatic inclusion.
- Next-phase provisional: *Frutta e verdura* (1973), *Amanti di valore* (1973), *Baby Gate* (1974), *Minacantalucio* (1975) **only after verifying release identity**; instead prefer original 1975 *La Mina*, *Minacantalucio* is likely a later compilation. The next four should be chosen by critical significance, not quota.
