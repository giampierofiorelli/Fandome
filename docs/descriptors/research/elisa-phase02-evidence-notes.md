# Elisa — Phase 02: album-specific evidence and calibration

**Scope:** October 24, 2026. Only *Lotus* (2003), *Pearl Days* (2004), *Heart* (2009). Research-derived draft interpretations; numerical scales are not direct measurements. Keep earlier Elisa phase 01 and every other artist intact.

**Source hierarchy:** dated specialist reviews (Rockol, SUONO, AllMusic), contemporary artist interviews, release-credit metadata MusicBrainz/Qobuz. Promotional materials are supporting evidence only, never equal to independent review. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` are intentionally empty without attributable direct artist-level documentation; artists covered or collaborators are not automatically influences. 25-column CSV holds all major genre/style labels attested for these albums; `genre_evidence.csv` specifies dominant vs minor categories. Empty claims are preferable to fictional detail.

## 1. Lotus — 2003, `elisa-lotus`
- **Edition/identity/type:** Original sixteen-track November 2003 Sugar studio album, although all-acoustic live-like room feel. A hybrid of new songs, re-recorded older songs and covers. Rockol calls it fourth album; contemporary SUONO explicitly says tracked in studio. Therefore `studio`, *not* `live`, *not* a pure retrospective `compilation` [L03_SUONO,L03_ROCKOL]. AllMusic regional 2004 database year does NOT replace Italian 2003 first issue [L03_AM].
- **Genres:** Rock and pop/pop-rock appear in SUONO and AllMusic classification, but **acoustic** describes actual instrumentation, not a newly invented genre. The AllMusic `alt`, `indierock`, `adultalternativepoprock` are taxonomy styles, not an assertion of loud amplified rock. This is a critical qualifier for site similarity [L03_SUONO,L03_AM].
- **Production:** Elisa Toffoli and **Pasquale Minieri** both listed as producers [L03_MB], corroborated by contemporary interview [L03_SUONO_INTERVIEW]. Minieri also engineered acoustic-room ambience. Andrea Rigonat is arranger/guitarist, **not** automatically a producer on this record.
- **Energy 1:** subdued, organic, rehearsed-live acoustic performances with sparse voice, acoustic guitars, organ and room sounds [L03_SUONO,L03_MB]. **Electronic 0:** sonic centerpiece acoustic; credits include limited keyboard/synth but this is not electronica; 0 is shorthand for overwhelmingly acoustic, not literal absence of keys. **Mood 3:** intimate warm, alternates reflective and affirming. **Theatrical 1:** close vocal/instrument presentation, despite strong vocal peaks. **Experimental 2:** new rearrangements, acoustic reinterpretations and cover selection create moderate departures, not radical sound design. **Polish 3:** professional studio capture but deliberately raw organic room sound. **Tempo 2:** generally measured and gentle. **Groove 1:** no sustained dance-beat orientation.
- **Voice:** `clear|soft|powerful` indicates both intimate singing and the ability to project, supported by SUONO praise, not vocal timbre derived just from Elisa's fame. **Lyrics:** broad themes `love|introspection|spiritual|heartbreak` apply across originals and diverse covers; spiritual dimension partly cover-specific ('Hallelujah'), downweight if review policy requires only dominant original songs. **Language:** English-dominant with an Italian-language cover (*Almeno tu nell’universo*). **Landmark 1:** first self-produced concept album, documented career turning point. **Comparisons/influences:** Rockol naming Leonard Cohen/Velvet Underground as *covered writers* is not direct evidence of influence, so leave `influenced_by` blank. Confidence high for production/recording and central sound; subjective lyrical range remains reviewable.

## 2. Pearl Days — 2004, `elisa-pearldays`
- **Edition/type:** original October 15 2004 ten-track studio album, NOT 2005 eleven-track reissue with later Italian-language *Una poesia anche per te* [P04_ROCKOL2,P04_MB]. Original lead language `en`, no `it` simply because reissue added a bonus song.
- **Genres:** `rock|poprock|pop` established by Giulio Nannini's Rockol review and contemporaneous SUONO. `electronic` appears as a secondary production element in original label announcement, not the album's main genre [P04_ROCKOL_PRESS]; Claude may reject that weakly grounded minor label after review.
- **Production:** producer credits **Glen Ballard and Elisa Toffoli** recorded by MusicBrainz album relation [P04_MB]. Ballard central in contemporaneous journalism [P04_ROCKOL1,P04_SUONO]. Do NOT include guitarist Tim Pierce, drummer Matt Chamberlain, mixing/recording engineers as producers.
- **Energy 4:** critics emphasize electric guitars and forceful radio rock, versus quieter Lotus. **Electronic 2:** core is live rock instrumentalists with supporting programming/electronic textures (MusicBrainz + label press release). **Mood 3:** catchy rock optimism juxtaposed with separation and pain. **Theatrical 3:** choruses and prominent vocals but not stage-theater music. **Experimental 1:** conventional international pop-rock songwriting rather than production experimentation. **Polish 5:** deliberately immaculate and radio-ready Ballard-produced Los Angeles sessions (Rockol), despite some lyrical/rock rawness. **Tempo 3:** moderately lively with ballad interludes. **Groove 2:** propulsive drum/backbeat rather than dancefloor-oriented.
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear|soft` critic-supported high presence with restrained ballad passages. **Lyrics:** `love|heartbreak|introspection|social` album tackles loved ones, distance, interpersonal separation, with 'Together' explicitly discussed by Elisa as antiwar but with personal text [P04_SUONO,P04_ROCKOL_PRESS]. `social` is *track-level* and should be reviewed; do not label whole record political. **No `sounds_like`:** Rockol notes Glen Ballard's work with Alanis Morissette as a *producer résumé*; this does not prove direct sonic similarity or documented influence. **Landmark 0:** well-documented contrast to Lotus, but defining status less certain than first self-produced acoustic reinvention. Confidence high for overall sound.

## 3. Heart — 2009, `elisa-heart`
- **Edition/type:** original fourteen tracks released November 13 2009; not a later deluxe edition [H09_ROCKOL,H09_SOUNDSBLOG]. Missing in Fandome album catalog currently; Claude needs a new catalog row while maintaining this ID.
- **Genres:** AllMusic reviewer Mariano Prunes expressly says *contemporary alt-rock and electronica* and its genre is Pop/Rock [H09_AM]. Album of the Year metadata also uses `alternativerock`, `poprock`, while Rockol hears guitars and ballads [H09_AOTY,H09_ROCKOL]. `electronic` is the broad genre complement to **electronica**, an influence more than the instrument majority. Do not automatically assign dance, synthpop, or experimental genres by association.
- **Production:** Elisa Toffoli and Andrea Rigonat as two credited producers; supported independently by Rockol and song-level Qobuz metadata [H09_ROCKOL,H09_QOBUZ]. David Frazer mixed but is *not* a producer. Giuliano Sangiorgi and Antony Hegarty are guests, not producers or assumed influences.
- **Energy 3:** substantial electric alt-rock guitar tracks balance ballads. **Electronic 2:** electronics part of sound but guitars/voice central, not a programming-dominated project. **Mood 2:** emotive, reflective and dramatic, darker than the accessible Pearl Days hooks. **Theatrical 3:** anthemic intensity plus intimate ballads. **Experimental 2:** broad stylistic palette and altered cover, still accessible rock forms. **Polish 4:** detailed domestic studio production with ample rock dynamics, not pristine Ballard gloss. **Tempo 3:** overall mid-tempo with rock numbers and slower ballads. **Groove 2:** rock backbeat and occasional electronics, not dancefloor-led.
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear|soft` changing performances across guitar-led songs and ballads. **Lyrics:** `love|introspection|heartbreak|identity` editorial interpretation from period interviews/reviews of relationship and reflection; no claim of exclusively motherhood themes. **Language:** original 14 songs: 12 English, 2 Italian (*Ti vorrei sollevare*; *Anche se non trovi le parole*) [H09_ROCKOL]. **Landmark 0:** substantial milestone of self-production, but not automatically one of career's defining iconic records. **Influences** left blank: a cover of Tears for Fears and guests do not establish explicit influence [H09_ROCKOL].
- **Critic disagreement:** AllMusic praises independence and sonic execution but finds fewer distinctive songwriting hooks, while Rockol describes strong maturation without that specific reservation [H09_AM,H09_ROCKOL]. Source agreement is on sound and production, **not on artistic value**.
