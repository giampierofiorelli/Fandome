# Queen — Phase 04: evidence notes and conservative interpretation

Research cut-off: 2026-10-26. Original release configurations are the reference, not deluxe remixes, concert re-recordings, or later bonus tracks. Bracket codes resolve to the phase sources CSV. The numeric scales are **editorial album-wide assessments** anchored in the Fandome guide, not facts claimed by the cited publications. Most genre tokens follow actual critic/tag wording; one-track styles are last.

## queen-themiracle — The Miracle (1989)

**Release, production and instrumentation.** The band and David Richards produced this record. Richards' engineering and keyboard/programming work and Brian Zellis' computer programming are evidenced independently [M1,M4,M5]. The Queen players supply lead vocals/piano/keyboards (Mercury), guitar (May), bass and some rhythm guitar/keyboards (Deacon), drums/percussion and some keyboard work (Taylor) [M4,M5]. MusicBrainz ties both **Korg M1** and **Roland D-50** to Freddie's synthesizer parts on the title track; the former requires the new `korgm1` production token; the latter is already permitted [M4]. Piano and synth are not interchanged. MusicBrainz distinguishes drum-set programming (e.g., Party) from actual Taylor kit playing: both `drumprogramming` and `drums` are justified, with programmable parts only on selected songs [M4]. `sampler` comes from a song-specific Richards credit on Scandal [M7], and its prominence is low. Timpani are credited on Was It All Worth It [M4]. `liverecording` refers to documented preserved first-take/studio band playing, **not** a stage-recorded live album [M2]. Dense choral layering is evident in the recorded backing vocals on multiple original tracks as documented in MusicBrainz [M4]; it is not an unsubstantiated model of a particular harmonizer.

**Genres and relative weight.** AllMusic labels the record album rock, art rock, contemporary pop/rock, hard rock, dance-rock and arena rock [M3]. The AOTY genre panel adds Pop Rock, Synthpop and AOR, weaker descriptors because of aggregated/editorial tagging [M6]. Hard guitar anthems (I Want It All, Khashoggi's Ship) sit beside synth-led pop and rhythmic/keyboard singles (The Invisible Man). `synthpop` is only a subsidiary ingredient rather than claiming the whole record is electronic. `rock` is a licensed broad category; do not replace specific genres with it. **No new genre tokens.**

**Scales.** `energy=4`: emphatic rock anthems balanced by less forceful pop. `electronic=3`: authentic guitar/bass/drums coexist with synths, M1/D-50 parts and programmed rhythms [M4]. `mood=3`: buoyant Party/Breakthru vs criticism/retrospection in Scandal and Was It All Worth It [M2,M3]. `theatrical=4`: layered voices and arena choruses. `experimental=2`: mostly short formal pop/rock writing despite some multi-part segments. `polish=4`: meticulous Richards-era studio record, but preserved band first takes [M2]. `tempo=3` and `groove=2`: mostly mid/up-tempo with some dance-rock, not a full dance album. Some singles are energetically exceptional; these scores reflect the whole original LP.

**Voices, subjects, resemblance and caution.** Mercury's dominant full-force, clear, occasionally rasp-edged leads plus band choral harmonies [M2,M4]. Themes: social commentary on the title song, intrusive press in Scandal, reflective Was It All Worth It, romantic Breakthru, celebratory Party [M2,M3]. `sounds_like` are editorial reference points and do not imply influence. The group-wide Beatles/Zep heritage is *not* copied into `influenced_by`: no album-specific first-hand causal claim verified for this phase. `landmark=1` because of the shared songwriting-credit pivot and late-era studio identity [M1,M2], not because acclaim was universal. **Critics' disagreement:** promotional official sources praise a triumph; AOTY aggregate lists mixed critic reactions. Neither cancels a specific credited instrument.

## queen-innuendo — Innuendo (1991)

**Credits.** Queen and David Richards produced the album [I1,I4]. Mercury sang and played keyboards, May guitar/keyboards, Deacon bass/keyboards, Taylor drums/percussion/keyboards; Mike Moran supplied keyboards on All God's People; **Steve Howe** plays Spanish guitar in the title track, not on every album track [I4,I5,I9]. David Richards' computer programming supports `programming` [I5]. Drum machines/programmable percussion appear on specific tracks [I10]; live drums remain primary. MusicBrainz credits timpani on All God's People, hence it appears last among instruments [I9]. The title track's synthetic orchestral passage is commonly attributed to a **Korg M1 workstation**, not a confirmed live orchestra [I10]. `korgm1` is flagged for Claude because it was already supported on The Miracle. **Do not add `orchestra`** from a keyboard emulation. Slide guitar is a track-level timbre credit; low importance [I10].

**Genres.** AllMusic identifies album rock, art rock, hard rock, arena rock, dance-rock and contemporary pop/rock [I3]. Aggregated AOTY tags add Pop Rock, Progressive Rock and Synthpop [I6]. `prog` is the Fandome token for source-described progressive rock; changing sections/odd metres of Innuendo justify it but **not** applying an album-wide flamenco classification. The **flamenco** section and Howe contribution were specifically described by Brian May, giving `flamenco` only passage scope [I7]. The concise heavy Headlong and The Hitman show why `hardrock` outranks the subsidiary synth elements [I2]. `sounds_like` are only editorial comparisons.

**Scales.** `energy=4`: emphatic riffs and Show Must Go On climax coexist with contemplative mid-tempo pieces. `electronic=3`: live rock core alongside programmed drums and synth orchestrations [I4,I9,I10]. `mood=1`: unusually sombre/reflective sequence (These Are the Days of Our Lives, Don't Try So Hard, Show Must Go On), with Delilah as contrasting humour [I1,I2]. `theatrical=5`: multiphase Innuendo, dramatic high-register vocals and large final ballad [I7,I8]. `experimental=4`: flamenco bridge, asymmetrical movements and conspicuous genre/style shifts [I7,I10]. `polish=4`: multi-studio, layered production [I1]. `tempo=3`, `groove=2`: variable pace, primarily directed towards narrative/rock listening. `belting` refers to Mercury's **recorded** full-power final take on The Show Must Go On as explained by Brian May [I8], not May's own falsetto demo (which does not belong to the album). `landmark=1` for the artist-defining closing statement released during Mercury's lifetime. Critics differ over consistency and relative merits against The Miracle; the album's track textures are still documented [I1,I2].

## queen-madeinheaven — Made in Heaven (1995)

**Original-release and posthumous context.** Released 6 November 1995, years after Mercury's 1991 death. Official Queen history states surviving members used remaining Freddie vocal recordings alongside material repurposed from the 1980s solo career [H1]. Queen are credited as primary producers; David Richards, Justin Shirley-Smith and Joshua J. Macrae are explicitly **co-producers** on the original album sleeve and official history [H1,H2], so all four names/groups are included in `producers`. Mack's separate credit for recording additional material is **not a co-production credit** and is therefore not in the producer field [H2]. The frequent confusion of recording date with release date must be avoided: some source vocals are from earlier than 1991 and later Queen instruments were recorded in 1993–95 [H1,H6,H9]. The CD includes short Yeah and extended untitled Track 13, but these are **not independent Queen albums**. Ambient receives low-importance genre scope because of the extended atmospheric piece and AOTY album-style tag [H5,H10]. Do not let that single track determine typical album tempo.

**Sounds and credited techniques.** The original sleeve identifies Mercury (vocals, keyboards/piano), May (guitar and vocals), Deacon (bass, keyboards), Taylor (drums, percussion, keyboards and vocals), plus extra Let Me Live backing singers [H2,H3]. The gospel choir on Let Me Live is clearly track-specific: `gospel` is a minor genre, `choral` a vocal character [H8]. Mercury's source recordings were reworked with fresh Queen accompaniment; `overdubbing`, `sonicmanipulation`, and `layeredvocals` refer to this documented late construction and choral layering rather than imaginary machines [H1,H6,H9]. `sampling` is narrowly supported by the *Goin' Back* excerpt and other sound snippets assembled into Mother Love [H2,H6]; this **does not** turn every song into sample-based electronic music. Do not insert a sampler **instrument** solely from the presence of prerecorded vocals. On Mother Love, Mercury's final vocals coexist with May's concluding vocal; Rod Stewart was involved in an early predecessor of Let Me Live, **not** in the released core album credits [H6,H8].

**Genres/scales.** Pop-rock/album-rock melodic songwriting with hard-rock guitars is described by AllMusic; AOTY adds ambient and AOR [H4,H5]. Dance-rock is a low-strength, explicitly source-labelled genre for the original You Don't Fool Me album track [H11]; keep last and exclude later club remixes. `energy=3`, `electronic=2`, `mood=2`: elegiac/mixed performances foreground organic band instruments, with some studio soundscapes and synthesizer sound sources. `theatrical=4`, `polish=5`: deliberately completed layered, lush closing record [H1,H4,H9]. `experimental=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2`: largely melodic rock and reflective ballads, one groove-led number. **Critics disagree** whether reconstruction yielded cohesion or over-extended slight material; this uncertainty affects artistic assessment, not credited producers [H4,H5]. `sounds_like=Freddie Mercury|Brian May` means their separately released records share source performances and/or voices, **not documented influence**. `landmark=1` as Freddie's posthumous final Queen album.

## queen-thecosmosrocks — The Cosmos Rocks (2008)

**Billing and separation from Queen with Freddie.** Label's 2008 press release bills **Queen + Paul Rodgers**; already registered under exact album ID in `coBilled.ts` [C1]. This is an album of newly recorded material, not an archival Queen release. John Deacon and Freddie Mercury **do not perform**. Co-production credits: May, Taylor and Rodgers are the three main producers; Joshua J. Macrae, Justin Shirley-Smith and Kris Fredriksson are explicitly billed as co-producers (the latter three are included precisely because co-production, not merely engineering, is credited) [C1,C6]. May and Rodgers split bass parts; Taylor drums, May guitars, Rodgers lead vocals, with varied keyboard/piano, acoustic guitar and harmonica parts documented in album credits and interviews [C1,C2,C6,C9]. Exact synth machine models are undocumented; **leave them out**. Official press does not establish a drum machine, so there is none in the row.

**Recording techniques and stylistic contrast.** The original band interview describes an acoustic-guitar jam captured almost as played and other tracks built with overdubs and large vocal harmonies [C2]. Therefore `liverecording` means **live studio performance** for one song only, whereas `overdubbing|layeredvocals` describe different songs and are corroborated by the Guardian [C2,C4]. Paul Rodgers has a substantially more gritty blues-rooted tone than Mercury, hence `raspy` first rather than mechanically copying earlier Queen vocal entries. `sounds_like` Free/Bad Company is independently supported by Guardian's comparison [C4]. Crucially, **Brian May himself** describes strong elements of Free and Bad Company in the new recording [C2], permitting those two as documented `influenced_by` / carried musical heritage; they are not guests on this album. The Beatles and Jimi Hendrix were named as broad musical teachers in label publicity, but that is not direct album-specific causal evidence; intentionally excluded [C1].

**Genre decisions.** AllMusic supplies Album Rock, Art Rock, Contemporary Pop/Rock, Hard Rock, Arena Rock [C3]; 'blues rock' is an explicit album descriptor [C8] and independently coherent with the contemporary band interview about blues passages [C2,C7]. `blues` remains lower because only parts of the record are so described. An Observer reviewer calls Cosmos Rockin boogie (single-song scope only) [C5]. `rockandroll` is the musicians' own phrase in the recording-context interview [C2]. `energy=4`, `electronic=1`, `mood=3`, `theatrical=3`, `experimental=1`, `polish=4`, `tempo=3`, `groove=2`: predominantly straightforward loud studio guitar rock with some tender blues ballads, competent layered recording and few electronic elements [C2-C5]. `landmark=0`: historically the one-off Queen + Paul Rodgers studio album, but not one of the most defining **core Queen** albums. Critics broadly find it closer to Rodgers' earlier blues rock than original Queen opera-rock; mixed to unfavourable evaluations are recorded, without judging facts by review scores [C3-C5].

## Cross-album decisions

- All scale numbers are 0–5 **judgments** with anchors from the Fandome guide, not pseudo-objective critic ratings. Ambiguous individual hardware is excluded where the sources don't substantiate it.
- Main-first instrument ordering reflects coverage, not every-song incidence. Dedicated per-genre evidence differentiates entire album, subgroup and one track.
- No live or greatest-hits releases, 2011 remasters, expanded 2022 sessions, Queen + Paul Rodgers stage recordings, or recycled deluxe extras are added.
- No source establishes a directly album-specific Beatles/Hendrix role for this phase; broad band influences belong in Phase 01 ARTIST INFLUENCES, which is not duplicated here.
- The last four Queen-related original studio/soundtrack records are now described across Phases 01–04; no Phase 05 studio albums currently remain in the specified canon through 26 October 2026.
