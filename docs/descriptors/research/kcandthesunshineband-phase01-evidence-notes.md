# KC and the Sunshine Band — phase 01 — research and audit

Research date: 28 October 2026. The 27-column descriptors are *draft editorial judgments* anchored to qualified reviews and documented credits, not measured lab scores. Four original studio albums, **1974–1976**; no reissues, live albums or compilations. All four are absent from Fandome `catalog.ts` and `seed.ts` at the time of lookup; `docs/descriptors/kcandthesunshineband.csv` also not found. Proposed artist ID `kcandthesunshineband`. All sources have album-specific entries in `*_sources.csv`.

## Do It Good (1974) — `kcandthesunshineband-doitgood`

- **Classification:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r1970924/review) lists Funk, Disco, Dance-Pop and R&B across the original album. Funk comes first because the debut is more raw and rhythm-driven than the following disco records. The genre `dancepop` is a retrospective stylistic label; it is not a claim that 1974 marketing used that phrase.
- **Credits:** [Rhino/Qobuz original track-level credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/do-it-good-kc-the-sunshine-band/0081227842468) identify **Richard Finch alone** as original producer. [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/) places the album at TK Studios, with Harry Wayne Casey on keyboards/lead vocal; Finch bass/drums/percussion, Smith guitar, Goytisolo and Brown percussion, trumpets and saxophone, and female backing singers.
- **Voice, energy, lyrics:** Original English-language party/funk songs such as *Queen of Clubs*, *Sound Your Funky Horn* and *I'm a Pushover* support `party|love|sensuality`. The rhythmic propulsion and upbeat character support `energy=4`, `mood=4`, `groove=5`. This is an editorial scale judgment, not a quotation from the sources. `electronic=0` means a predominantly acoustic/electric band; no album-specific synthesizer/drum machine is confirmed.
- **Production field deliberately blank:** No specific machine or recording process is securely tied by album-level sources. Horn instruments are documented; named `hornarrangements` as a production *technique* would require precise original album credit. Casey's later description of Junkanoo is not proof that steel drums, cowbells or whistles were used on every track. [Casey, Grammy](https://www.grammy.com/news/harry-wayne-casey-the-boogie-man-returns/).
- **Reception:** [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=KC+and+the+Sunshine+Band) assigns B+ and highlights the distinctly Miami Latin-influenced rhythmic character; AllMusic is positive about its UK club appeal. `landmark=0` compares the debut with the following major breakthrough, without implying it is negligible.

## KC and the Sunshine Band (1975) — `kcandthesunshineband-kcandthesunshineband`

- **Release and production:** Original 1975 second LP, co-produced by **Harry Wayne Casey and Richard Finch** ([JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/); [original personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC_and_the_Sunshine_Band_%28album%29)). An album with *Get Down Tonight*, *That's the Way (I Like It)* and *Boogie Shoes*, not the same as the later greatest-hits sets. Its title exactly matches the artist: do not confuse this with a duplicate artist room.
- **Genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/kc-and-the-sunshine-band-mw0000627024) calls it disco/dance-pop with R&B framing, with funk also identified by the [album discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC_and_the_Sunshine_Band_%28album%29). The album’s distinct soul qualities are discussed by the [Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/news/harry-wayne-casey-the-boogie-man-returns/). No modern electronic-dance genre is added on the strength of its recording technology.
- **Arrangement/production:** **Specific evidence** from Casey’s [MusicRadar first-person account](https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/classic-tracks-harry-wayne-casey-on-kc-and-the-sunshine-bands-get-down-tonight-603572) of *Get Down Tonight*: speed-changed guitar solo (`varispeed`), multitrack horn overdubs (`overdubbing`, `hornarrangements`), backing vocals doubled (`layeredvocals`), and a delay used to thicken the lead (`delay`). These are **track-level** production signatures, not every-track claims. [KMUW interview](https://www.kmuw.org/music/2024-09-19/kc-and-the-sunshine-band-brings-positive-energy-to-kansas-through-disco-and-funk-music?_amp=true) independently confirms the tape-speed technique. No digital-synth/machine model is invented; `electronic=0` reflects the record’s primarily live rhythm, brass and keyboards (not a statement that no electronic signal processing occurred).
- **Instrumentation:** Lead keyboard, guitar, bass/drums/percussion, trumpet/saxophone and backing vocals appear on the original personnel roster. Handclaps on *Get Down Tonight* are described by [Grammy](https://www.grammy.com/news/harry-wayne-casey-the-boogie-man-returns/). `polish=4` reflects a polished studio arrangement against 1974's harder-edged early singles; `experimental=2` reflects selective tape effects rather than an avant-garde LP.
- **Reception/importance:** [Christgau A−](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=KC+and+the+Sunshine+Band) describes greater brightness and pop accessibility than the debut; the two number-one US hits establish `landmark=1`. Similarities to Motown or James Brown in contemporary disco discourse belong in editorial comparisons, not unsourced per-album `influenced_by` fields.

## The Sound of Sunshine (1975) — `kcandthesunshineband-thesoundofsunshine`

- **Crucial artist-billing exception:** Original 1975 LP credits **The Sunshine Band** rather than *KC and the Sunshine Band*, but label [Rhino](https://www.rhino.com/article/happy-45th-the-sunshine-band-the-sound-of-sunshine) explicitly places it as the third studio LP in the same sequence. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/artist/76a5a114-ed6a-4eb6-8a81-ad5768d4b41c) preserves the original artist attribution. Thus it is filed as an album in the KC and the Sunshine Band catalog **with the printed alias flagged in the audit and import guide**, not silently overwritten or assigned to an invented second artist room. Claude may store a precise original-credit alias where the data model permits.
- **Not live or compilation:** Original 1975 studio LP of instrumental tracks; reworking *Rock Your Baby* as an instrumental within a newly issued studio album does not make the whole album a compilation. **Voice `instrumental`**, `lyrics` empty and `language` empty: credits of backing singers on some databases are contradicted by Rhino’s explicit instrumental description; sparse vocal shouts do not establish a main sung language.
- **Sound/genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-sound-of-sunshine-mw0000348623) identifies disco, dance-pop and R&B; [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/) describes funk grooves, interlocking guitar, sax-led melodies and horns. `energy=3`, `groove=4` versus the vocal singles’ higher drive: editorial judgment about album-wide pace and purpose, not an implication this is ambient music.
- **Instruments/production:** Casey keyboards, Finch bass/drums, Smith guitar, Goytisolo percussion, trumpet and tenor/baritone saxophone players in [credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sound_of_Sunshine); no supported precise drum machine or synth model. Keep `production` blank rather than transferring the sped-up guitar method from the prior album.
- **Reception:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-sound-of-sunshine-mw0000348623) is more generous than [Christgau's separate Sunshine Band review](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=KC+and+the+Sunshine+Band) might be; the more restrained `landmark=0` reflects absence of the vocal chart-defining singles. Do not copy Christgau's KC page grade to this alias record.

## Part 3 (1976) — `kcandthesunshineband-part3`

- **Release:** Fourth original LP, October 1976, following *The Sound of Sunshine*. Producers **Casey and Finch** ([original album credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_3_%28KC_and_the_Sunshine_Band_album%29); [TK studio study](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/)). Original tracks include *(Shake, Shake, Shake) Shake Your Booty*, *I Like to Do It*, *I'm Your Boogie Man* and *Keep It Comin' Love*.
- **Genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/part-3-r67108) explicitly classifies it as disco, dance-pop, R&B and in the professional review specifically as funk/soul/disco. All five genre tokens are supported; funk, soul and R&B are not inferred simply because the record uses horns.
- **Personnel:** Casey keyboards and lead voice; Smith guitar, Finch bass/drums/percussion, Johnson drums, Goytisolo and Brown percussion, brass/saxes by Faulk, Tanno, Lewis, Sidener. [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/) specifically mentions tambourine on *I'm Your Boogie Man* and handclaps on *I Like to Do It*. Hence `tambourine|handclaps` are album instruments but **track-specific**, not all-track arrangements.
- **Production:** `overdubbing` conservatively reflects Casey's [first-person description](https://www.grammy.com/news/harry-wayne-casey-the-boogie-man-returns/) of 1970s group multitracking; exact technical process for this LP is less directly documented than the 1975 breakthrough and should be rechecked with label multitrack notes before treating it as firm. No speculative digital tools or drum-machine models.
- **Scores / reception:** Intense direct-dance arrangements justify `energy=5`, `groove=5`, `mood=5`, `theatrical=4`; all are 0–5 editorial anchor judgments. [Christgau B+](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=KC+and+the+Sunshine+Band) finds the album more predictable than the debut, while [AllMusic's Alex Henderson](https://www.allmusic.com/album/part-3-r67108) stresses how the grooves have aged well. This explains `experimental=1` with `landmark=1`; different views are documented instead of averaged into an ungrounded universal assessment.

## Cross-album caveats

1. **Artist-wide influences are not album-level influences.** In his [Music Feeds interview (2017)](https://musicfeeds.com.au/features/kc-sunshine-band-dangers-fame-exciting-new-music/), Casey directly identifies Stevie Wonder, Marvin Gaye, Supremes, Temptations, Gladys Knight, Joe Cocker, James Brown, Chicago and Blood Sweat & Tears, among others. Record these under ARTIST INFLUENCES in the import guide; leave each album's `influenced_by` empty without album-specific testimony. Motown is a label/style, not a single artist.
2. [Casey’s Grammy interview](https://www.grammy.com/news/harry-wayne-casey-the-boogie-man-returns/) describes Junkanoo percussion and a wish for Caribbean-inflected party music, but it does **not** prove actual steel drums, cowbells or a named machine on a specific LP. Do not put those in the instrument or production CSV simply because the inspiration existed.
3. `sounds_like` remains empty for all four albums: critics mostly compare KC's place in 1970s pop or mention producer collaborators (George McCrae), not exact sonic correspondences linked to a particular record. None is made up to fill the field.
4. `The Sound of Sunshine` has an unusual sleeve credit and no lead vocals. Explicit label evidence takes precedence over automatic inferencing from identical band personnel or automated platform artist mappings.
5. Retrospective *Dance-Pop* labels are sourced to AllMusic but represent contemporary cataloguing, not proof that the expression was used at the 1974/75 date of recording. Modern album remixes are not used as 1970s evidence.
