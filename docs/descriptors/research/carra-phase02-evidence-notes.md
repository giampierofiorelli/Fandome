# Raffaella Carrà — Phase 02 evidence notes (English)

Scope: four original studio albums, 1974–1977. All track-based style evidence is retained; a minority style still appears in `genres`. `scope` and evidence-strength distinguish album-wide music from a single track or a described texture. Classification sources are cited even when their genre taxonomy is broad. Do not assume all tracks share one sound. The eight scales are *provisional aggregate editorial assessments*, not acoustic measurements; electronic is left blank because exact arrangements cannot be independently established from the accessible liner notes.

## Milleluci (1974) — `carra-milleluci`

**Original object:** Italian LP CGD 69072 dated 22 April 1974; not the companion television show itself, and not its 8-track running order. The official catalogue calls it Carrà's fifth studio album. Paolo Ormi arranged and conducted the record except opening Din don dan (Gianni Ferrio). These are **arranger** credits, not proof of a producer role. Accordingly `producers` is blank. The television program's artistic director Ferrio must not be entered as the whole album's producer.

**Genres:** Official LP gives titles Din don dan, Tarantelluccia, Crapa pelada, Rumba degli scugnizzi, Ma che sera, Camminando sotto la pioggia, Copacabana, Tirami la gamba, Tulipan, La famiglia canterina and She's lookin' good. This isn't one genre. `pop` is documented at album level. `musicaleggera` marks old-style Italian popular-song numbers and is editorial rather than directly credited. `showtunes` indicates the television revue/musical connection as a TEXTURE, not evidence that every track is a stage song. `rumba` and `tarantella` preserve named dance repertories from individual titles, but **their exact recorded rhythmic treatment is unverified**; Claude should retain the qualified genre evidence, not promote weak labels to established acoustic findings. `She's lookin' good` is English-language repertoire and a potential R&B/soul bridge, but no direct evidence of this recording's arrangement was found, so it is not falsely tagged soul.

**Scales:** energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3 are album-level moderate values due to various dance and non-dance numbers. mood=4 reflects light revue material but does not claim uniform euphoria. theatrical=5 is grounded in orchestrated revue presentation and the *Milleluci* concept; experimental=1 indicates repertoire-led traditional arrangements; polish=3 is cautiously medium given studio orchestra. Each number is a qualified editorial inference, not independently measured. `electronic` blank: instrumental credits do not establish an album-wide electronic share. `voice=clear` provisional stage delivery. Lyrics `storytelling|party|love` identify broad song themes; lyric-by-lyric validation remains incomplete.

**Sources/disagreements:** Official discography and Rai establish the LP/revue link; Fondazione Prada describes multiple entertainment formats in the TV show but is not a musical analysis of the studio album. Some metadata describes only generic Pop. See cited source rows.

## Felicità tà tà (1974) — `carra-felicitatata`

**Original object:** Italian LP CGD 69090 dated 16 September 1974 on official LP page, not the tape dated 25 September. Tracks include Felicità tà tà, Il guerriero, Troppo ragazzina, Sì ci sto!, Quando dico di no, Rumore, Tabù, Prima di dormire, Superman, Scordalo ragazzo mio and Mi vien da piangere. Record retains diverse tones: title song/TV theme and Rumore are the anchors, not proxies for all eleven songs.

**Genres:** Qobuz calls this a disco album; the estate biography specifically places `Rumore` in her ascent through disco music. Discogs classifies the release Pop, Disco, Europop and umbrella Funk/Soul; each appears as a genre row, but individual funk/soul track identification is not substantiated. `pop|disco|europop|funk|soul` reflects the documented broad and niche labels, **not** an assertion that each song contains funk or soul.

**Scales:** energy=4, tempo=4, groove=4 are influenced by the named disco hits, tempered by non-dance and reflective track selection. mood=4 captures broadly celebratory outward material while lyrics `love|heartbreak` keep emotional variation; theatrical=4 from TV theme and stage-friendly delivery. polish=4 and experimental=1 are tentative period-pop judgments. `electronic` left empty rather than inferring machines from disco label. `voice=clear` is provisional. `language=it` for the original Italian LP, not translated overseas versions.

**Producer caveat:** Rai identifies Paolo Ormi and Andrea Lo Vecchio as *arrangers*; many listed contributors are writers, not confirmed producer credits. Producer cell blank pending sleeve verification. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` remain blank: generic period resemblance is not proof of influence.

## Forte forte forte (1976) — `carra-forteforteforte`

**Original object:** Original CGD 81439 LP from official site; not its separately programmed cassette or the two-sided single, which contains the signature song A far l'amore comincia tu.

**Genres:** LP classifications vary: Discogs uses Pop / Disco / Europop / Ballad, Italian album metadata says Italo disco, the official discography calls 0303456 and Bobo step precursors to Eurodisco. At *song* level, A far l'amore comincia tu is classified Disco, Funk and Latin pop. All are kept: `pop|disco|europop|italodisco|eurodisco|funk|latinpop|ballad`. Crucial nuance: **early Eurodisco precursors do not mean all tracks have later synthesizer-heavy 1980s Italo-disco arrangements**. The new `latinpop` token is required because a source explicitly uses Latin pop.

**Scales:** energy=4, tempo=4, groove=4 reflect dance-oriented single and other rhythmic tracks, moderated by the documented ballad style; mood=4 and theatrical=4 are provisional album-level readings. experimental=1, polish=4 reflect accessible commercially arranged studio pop, without overclaiming innovation. Electronic fraction is not measured; leave blank. Lyrics `sensuality|love|party` are strongly grounded in A far l'amore comincia tu but album-wide lyric annotation is incomplete. `language=it` for the Italian original; localized foreign editions are distinct.

**Producer caveat:** Rai says Franco Bracardi arranged the LP and identifies Cristiano Malgioglio and Daniele Pace as composers/lyricists. None is a producer credit. `producers` deliberately empty until a liner-note credit for LP production is located. The single-related secondary sources sometimes name Gianni Boncompagni but cannot certify the whole LP's production.

## Fiesta (1977) — `carra-fiesta`

**Original object:** Italian CGD 20018 LP, 16 September 1977. Ten-track official issue, not Spanish, French, cassette or 8-track variants. Languages `it|en|es` reflect the original Italian international repertoire, including Spanish-language *Fiesta*. **Do not import French tracks** from CBS KFSA 90425.

**Genres:** Official discography calls `Dreamin' of you` and `Melodia` specifically Eurodisco, while `I Thank You Life` is a **separate orchestral ballad in musical style**. The latter justifies `ballad` and a `musicaltheatre` *texture*; it does not transform the album into a stage cast recording. Secondary overviews also name Disco, Pop, Italo disco, Latin pop, Rock and Electronic. `rock` and `electronic` are kept with **low evidence strength**: one encyclopaedic genre list and no identified rock track or equipment. This preserves possible minority links for review without falsely implying confirmed production methods. `latinpop` is a new genre token required for the source's Latin pop. If validator policy disallows uncertain labels, keep genre-evidence rows and review individually rather than silently deleting them.

**Scales:** energy=4 / tempo=4 / groove=4 reflect substantial Eurodisco repertory but are below 5 because an orchestral ballad and variety numbers remain. theatrical=5 reflects show-style production and the album's explicitly musical-like ballad; mood=4, polish=4 and experimental=1 are provisional. Electronic left empty despite catalogue adjective electronic: no album-wide electronic fraction is documented. Lyrics `party|love|sensuality` are broadly consistent with title track and repertoire; more track-level lyric analysis is still needed.

**Producer:** the English Fiesta album entry explicitly identifies **Gianni Boncompagni** as producer; Rai names **Franco Bracardi and Paolo Ormi as arrangers**, who are excluded from `producers`. These are different roles. This is medium-strength producer evidence pending original CGD sleeve.

**Edition dispute:** The official Spanish CBS LP changes titles and/or songs; the official French LP is explicitly bilingual/French and adds alternative recordings. The album's genre/lang/track data must refer to the *1977 CGD 20018* original, not all variants combined.
