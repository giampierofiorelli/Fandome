# Evidence notes — Laura Pausini, phase 02 (2000–2006)

**Research completed:** 29 October 2026. Source-level support is listed in CSV. Scales are original comparative judgments using Fandome's 0–5 anchors; they do not purport to be independently measured acoustic features. `confidence=medium` acknowledges edition and production ambiguities. All rows are `draft`.

## 1. Tra te e il mare (2000) — `pausini-trateeilmare`

**Source and chronology.** The original CGD East West Italian [2000 CD](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/8573843962) was issued September 15, 2000. [Muziekweb's original 2000 copy](https://www.muziekweb.nl/en/Link/JK103176/Tra-te-e-il-mare) lists 14 tracks, ending in “The Extra Mile” (English). Later [Warner/Qobuz stream](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/tra-te-e-il-mare-laura-pausini/0825646630875) has 13 songs, omitting it. We describe 14-track physical original, hence `it|en`. The sister Spanish CD [pressing](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/843972) is a guide to shared instrumentation, not a second original album.

**Genre placement.** AllMusic calls it Euro-Pop, Italian Pop, Latin Pop. [Original 45cat CD](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/8573843962) categorizes Soft Rock and Easy Listening as well as Pop. [Muziekweb critic](https://www.muziekweb.nl/en/Link/JK103176/Tra-te-e-il-mare) identifies strongly produced soft pop/rock. The pop/Italian tags come first; Latin Pop is retained *last* as a classification of market crossover rather than evidence of Latin rhythms. Do not invent `latin` instrumentation.

**Instruments.** The [original track title on Apple/Warner](https://music.apple.com/us/song/478345911) credits Dado Parisini keyboards/programming, Andrea Braido/Gabriele Fersini/Riccardo Galardini guitar, Cesare Chiodo bass, Massimo Pacciani drums, Christopher Warren-Green violin and London Chamber Orchestra. [2000 sleeve of shared language sessions](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/843972) names electronic drums, synth bass, saxophone, flute, piano/keys, full orchestra and guitars. Some exist only on part of the album; no specific synth brand is proved. `stringarrangements` is directly documented on “Anche se non mi vuoi” [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/tra-te-e-il-mare-laura-pausini/0825646630875); `programming` comes from Apple and sleeve. Do not infer e.g. autotune.

**Production.** [Apple/Warner](https://music.apple.com/us/song/478345911) labels Pausini, Dado Parisini and Alfredo Cerruti as track producers. However [sleeve credit](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/843972) calls Cerruti executive, so he is excluded from album producer list to avoid an executive-only false credit. Pausini and Parisini are consistent. Celso Valli and KC Porter have artistic-production and/or selected-track roles on album resources but exact per-track `Producer` credit is weaker; the importer should check the original full sleeve. No composer is placed in `influenced_by` simply because they wrote a song.

**Scales.** Mostly mid-tempo with larger choruses, occasional bright radio-pop and reflective ballads: energy 3, mood 2, tempo 3 and groove 2. Studio and orchestra arrangements are highly polished (5); electronic sources are present but acoustic band predominant (2). Theatric 3 for swelling vocal/strings; experimental 0 for conventional verse/chorus. Romantic and personal texts plus “Per vivere” social material. `sounds_like` comparisons are editorial only.

## 2. From the Inside (2002) — `pausini-fromtheinside`

**Source.** [Original 2002 Atlantic CD transcript](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/0927486552), [AllMusic 2002 original album](https://www.allmusic.com/album/from-the-inside-mw0000662499). 12 tracks, including a piano-vocal reprise. 2003 bonus editions are explicitly excluded. Entire original album is sung in English, distinct from Italian or Spanish translations of other albums.

**Genres.** AllMusic's album metadata: Euro-Pop, Italian Pop and Latin Pop; [contemporary AllMusic critic Christina Fuoco](https://www.allmusic.com/album/from-the-inside-mw0000662499) describes mainstream easy-listening pop, Europop radio singles, a dance-oriented “Surrender” and a funkier ballad “Do I Dare”. The original [45cat CD](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/0927486552) tags Pop, Soft Rock and Easy Listening. The [Musicbase pressing listing](https://musicbase.net/cs/from-the-inside-1z57q4m) adds Pop Rock. Dance-pop is [also the genre assigned in an album reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Inside_%28Laura_Pausini_album%29), but the source of the *specific* audible dance sound is the single; do not characterize the complete record as a dance LP. Italian Pop/Latin Pop are international industry labels **not language evidence**, both placed near the tail.

**Instrumentation/production.** AllMusic explicitly names Michael Landau electric/acoustic guitars and sitar (particular tracks), John Robinson/Curt Bisquera/Vinnie Colaiuta drums, Leland Sklar/Nathan East bass, Patrick Leonard piano and keys, Dado Parisini synth-bass, several keyboard programmers and string-arranger David Campbell. Original sleeve names Vinnie Colaiuta, Lee Sklar and others. `programming`, `stringarrangements` and `digitalediting` are explicitly credited; no undocumented digital workstation or pitch correction is asserted.

**Producer breadth caveat.** AllMusic aggregates a broad international list: the CSV carries all named `Producer` persons, rather than only high-profile album producers. Original [45cat sleeve](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/0927486552) specifically distinguishes executive credits for Pausini, KC Porter, Cerruti, Craig Kallman, Ron Shapiro and Ruby Marchand, with separate track production credits for other figures. AllMusic additionally calls Pausini, KC Porter and Cerruti producers, hence their inclusion despite executive dual-role; **review individual tracks to settle their precise production involvement**. Mark Taylor, Jimmy Bralower, Eric Kupper and others may be one-song names; they are still included when genuinely labelled Producer. Peter Zizzo is *vocal producer only*, not included. No sampled songs, guests or reviewer's Dion comparisons are `influenced_by`.

**Comparisons.** AllMusic names Céline Dion and easy-listening sphere of Michael Bolton/Backstreet Boys; `sounds_like` is editorial and not a verified artist-declared influence. Synths and programming stronger than 2000 (electronic 3), midrange rhythm energy 3, groove 3 (not 5), high polish 5, conventional structures experimental 0. Voice power/clarity and romantic themes grounded in critic review and track titles.

## 3. Resta in ascolto (2004) — `pausini-restainascolto`

**Editions.** Original 11-track Warner CD released [October 22, 2004](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/v-2615/laura-pausini-resta-in-ascolto); an AllMusic specific album page showing 2006 represents a later issue. Spanish *Escucha* is a parallel-language studio release, not a distinct new composition album. [Original CD+DVD sleeve](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/5050467563421) makes clear who is executive producer (Gabriele Parisi) and artistic producer (Dado Parisini, Celso Valli, Rick Nowels, Alfredo Cerruti). Warner Qobuz similarly splits roles. Original album credited as produced by Pausini; AllMusic lists Pausini, Parisini, Valli, Nowels, Cesare Chiodo and John Themis as producers. Because AllMusic **does not tag Cerruti as producer** on the 2004 Italian album and Qobuz tags him artistic, omit Cerruti. These cross-database distinctions should be rechecked against printed liner notes.

**Track lyrics.** A [September 2004 Rockol report](https://www.rockol.it/news-65650/nel-nuovo-album-di-laura-pausini-una-canzone-scritta-con-madonna) quotes Pausini saying that “Mi abbandono a te” retained an English chorus in an otherwise Italian adaptation of a Madonna/Rick Nowels song. Use `it|en` on the Italian original; English is secondary and short. Madonna is a **songwriter, not a duet guest and not album-specific documented influence**. Additional tracks include Vasco Rossi/Gaetano Curreri's “Benedetta passione”, Biagio Antonacci's “Vivimi” and a war-related “Dove l'aria è polvere”; writing credits do not make them producers. [Rockol's contemporary review](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/v-2615/laura-pausini-resta-in-ascolto) records the album's darker themes.

**Style.** [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/59322-laura-pausini-resta-in-ascolto.php) classifies Pop Rock and Italo Pop; [original CD cataloguing](https://musicbase.net/resta-in-ascolto) uses Soft Rock and Ballad; AllMusic critic discusses its adult-contemporary sensibility. `latinpop` is a weak cross-market secondary classification: consider dropping it after strict token review rather than asserting Latin rhythmic substance. Scoring: mood 1 (separation/war), dramatic intensity theatrical 4, strong electric guitars and choruses energy 3, polished production 5, mostly acoustic rhythm section plus digital programming electronic 2, tempo 3 and groove 2.

**Instruments and techniques.** [AllMusic Italian credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/resta-in-ascolto-mw0002606795) document Valli piano/keys/strings, Dado Parisini brass arrangements and programming, guitarists Emiliano Fantuzzi, Gabriele Fersini, Massimo Varini, bassists Cesare Chiodo/Paolo Costa, drummers Alfredo Golino/Ian Thomas, saxophone Stefano Cantini, flugelhorn Marco Tamburini, trombone Roberto Rossi, percussion Luis Conte. `stringarrangements`, `hornarrangements`, `programming`, `drumprogramming` are credited, not inferred.

**Disagreement.** One [Rockol reviewer](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/2843/laura-pausini-resta-in-ascolto) found the performances overly forceful while another contemporary descriptive account stressed the developing lyric maturity. The theatrical score represents intensity of presentation rather than a universal positive evaluation.

## 4. Io canto (2006) — `pausini-iocanto`

**Scope:** not a retrospective Pausini compilation. These are 2006 **new studio recordings** of Italian pop and rock compositions; [AllMusic's album review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/io-canto-mw0000735774) directly describes the album's premise and dense guitar/bass/drums/string/synth production. Original Italian [Atlantic CD 5051011729027](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/5051011729027) has 16 songs as recorded in the [collector discography](https://www.laurapausinidiscografia.it/2006---io-canto.html). Modern streaming [e.g. 19-track digital](https://open.spotify.com/album/2DkzL30yoYI5sS0JgCXQxc) adds alternative solo takes and bonus songs; different French and Spanish country editions also exist. Do not add `fr|es` to original Italian album. Juanes “Il mio canto libero”, Johnny Hallyday “Come il sole all'improvviso”, Tiziano Ferro “Non me lo so spiegare” are track-level guests, not album co-billing.

**Genres and sounds.** AllMusic calls it Euro-Pop, Italian Pop and Latin Pop; [original CD styles](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/laura-pausini/io-canto-album_ffgfgr.html) explicitly Soft Rock, Pop Rock and Ballad. Albums's poprock is anchored in new recordings such as “La mia banda suona il rock”; more symphonic ballads anchor the rest. `latinpop` is a retail categorization not proof that the 2006 Italian version is Spanish. The voice is expressive and forceful, supported by AllMusic review, with a wide range of affection, separation and freedom themes (Fossati, Bersani, Baglioni, Battisti etc.). `sounds_like` editorial only; `influenced_by` **blank** because writing, appearing on, or covering an artist doesn't prove the singer explicitly named them as musical influences for this record.

**Specific recording evidence.** [AllMusic musicians](https://www.allmusic.com/album/io-canto-mw0000735774) list Vinnie Colaiuta/Alfredo Golino drums; Celso Valli piano, drum programming, synth bass and string arrangements; Dado Parisini electric piano Fender Rhodes and keys; guitars by Greg Howe, Michael Landau, Daniel Vuletic, Riccardo Galardini etc.; bass by Nathan East/Paul Bushnell/Roberto Gallinelli; John Beasley piano; Paolo Carta organ; Raphael Padilla percussion; Oversea Orchestra on selected songs. [CD credit transcription](https://musicbase.net/io-canto) corroborates guitar and rhythm band; [original pressing song credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/laura-pausini/io-canto-album_ffgfgr.html) corroborate Fender Rhodes on “Scrivimi” and drum programming on “Stella gemella”. Producer split is directly given by [Warner/Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/io-canto-laura-pausini/u50od9vp48lnc): Celso Valli, Dado Parisini, Pausini and Daniel Vuletic per different songs. Executives and arrangers without the producer title excluded.

**Scales and confidence.** The choruses rise dramatically and band arrangements can rock, but a substantial share is piano-driven: energy 3, electronic 2, theatrical 4, groove 2; very polished 5, little structural experimentation 0. Its emotional mood moves between melancholy and resolve, yielding mood 2, and a largely mid-tempo profile gives tempo 3. Medium confidence recognizes some detailed credits are gathered across shared Spanish releases as well as Italian pressing, albeit mostly the same sessions.
