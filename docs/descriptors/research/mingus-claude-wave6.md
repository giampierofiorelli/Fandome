## brubeck-timeout: Time Out (1959)
**Sources**
- [HDTT] HDTT booklet, which reprints the AllMusic review: https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0778/3319/files/Brubeck_Time_Out_Booklet.pdf
- [JF] Jazzfuel: https://jazzfuel.com/time-out-dave-brubeck/
- [AB] The Audio Beat, Marc Mickelson, Aug 16 2012: https://www.theaudiobeat.com/music/dave_brubeck_time_out_lp.htm
- [SDPB] SDPB, Karl Gehrke, Dec 21 2020: https://www.sdpb.org/blogs/behind-the-beat/dave-brubeck-time-outtakes
- [PBS] Ira Gitler on PBS: https://www.pbs.org/brubeck/theMusic/criticIraGitler.htm
- [TG] Ted Gioia on "Take Five", jazz.com: https://www.jazz.com/music/dave-brubeck-take-five

**Cells**
- **genres:**
  - jazz: every source.
  - cooljazz: [JF] calls Desmond "A light-toned altoist in the West Coast cool style".
  - classical: [HDTT/AllMusic] says "Blue Rondo à la Turk" "blends jazz with classical form and Turkish folk rhythms". That is one track, so it goes last.
  - The cooljazz support is weak: it describes the altoist's style, and [AB] says Brubeck's music was "something beyond 'cool'". Medium confidence.
- **energy 2, tempo 3, groove 2:**
  - [HDTT] "never stops swinging".
  - [JF] "a swinging pulse from bass and drums".
  - [AB] "this rousing Time Out".
  - Odd meters keep the groove from being dance music.
- **mood 3:** [TG] "Brubeck made the experiment fun". "Strange Meadow Lark" and "Kathy's Waltz" are lyrical.
- **theatrical 2:** a quartet. [AB] "languid tone and abundant lyricism".
- **experimental 2:** the meters are new, but [HDTT] says the challenges are "not jarring" and the record is "amazingly accessible for all its advanced thinking".
- **polish 3:** a normal Columbia studio record, 30th Street Studio ([HDTT] track credits).
- **voice:** instrumental.
- **instruments:** from MusicBrainz and [JF]: piano (Brubeck), alto sax (Desmond), double bass (Wright), drums (Morello).
- **production:** empty. No source describes the recording technique.
- **scene:** [AB] places Brubeck in the "West Coast jazz movement". The studio is from [HDTT].
- **producers:** Teo Macero (MusicBrainz).
- **sounds_like:** empty.
- **influenced_by:** empty. The 9/8 idea came from Turkish street musicians Brubeck heard on the 1958 State Department tour ([HDTT], [SDPB]). They are not a named artist.
- **landmark 1:** [HDTT/AllMusic] "Dave Brubeck's defining masterpiece".
- **Reception:**
  - Panned at release: [HDTT] "many critics initially roasted him", and Gitler [PBS] was "very mixed".
  - Huge sales: [SDPB] says it was the first jazz album to sell a million, per Billboard.
  - Acclaim is 4, not 5, because of the mixed reception at release.
  - Mainstream 3: [HDTT] says the label "wasn't keen on releasing such an arty project", and [JF] says Columbia first wanted the conventional *Gone With the Wind*.
- **Legacy 4:** [AB] "led to a string of similarly themed 'time' albums"; [HDTT/AllMusic] "Countless other musicians built on its pioneering experiments". It is not 5 because neither source names a lasting stylistic school.

## mingus-mingusahum: Mingus Ah Um (1959)
**Sources**
- [RS] RS 500 (2020), #380: https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-albums-of-all-time-32520/charles-mingus-mingus-ah-um-2-32641/
- [JJ] Jazz Journal, Wif Stenger, May 5 2020: https://jazzjournal.co.uk/2020/05/05/charles-mingus-mingus-ah-um-2/
- [SM] Sean Murphy, Kinds of Blue (Substack), 2025, reprinting his 2009 piece: https://murphlaw.substack.com/p/mingus-ah-um
- [SSB] Sounding Out! "Mingus Ah Um at 60" (Earl H. Brooks, Brittnay L. Proctor): https://soundstudiesblog.com/category/mingus-ah-um-at-60/page/2/

**Cells**
- **genres:**
  - bop: [RS] "hard-driving bop"; [JJ] "swing and early bop".
  - blues: [SM] blues fused with "gospel and folk"; [SSB] (Brooks) on "Fables": "bluesy, bebop instrumental solos".
  - gospel: [RS] "a torrid, gospel inspired rave-up ('Better Git It in Your Soul')"; [SSB] (Brooks) "Holiness and Sanctified" church inflections. Gospel is one track, but that track is the opener and best known, so it sits 4th.
  - swing: [JJ] "swing and early bop".
- **energy 3:**
  - [JJ] "rambunctious, razzle-dazzle" ("Better Git It"); [RS] "torrid".
  - Against that, "silky" ("Pork Pie Hat") and [SM] "delicately understated tone poem".
- **mood 3:** joyous gospel shouts and [JJ] "his most fun album", against [RS] "a mournful elegy".
- **theatrical 3:** [JJ] "razzle-dazzle"; [JJ] "jokey" and "tongue-in-cheek" ("Jelly Roll").
- **experimental 2:** [JJ] sees a "Janus-faced look back" and a move toward the "emerging 'new thing'". The forms are still tune-based.
- **polish 3:** [SM] "Macero's production throughout is impeccable".
- **tempo 3:** [SM] says "Fables" has Richmond "urging the band into double time", alongside ballads.
- **groove 2:** [SSB] (Proctor) says "Open Letter to Duke" has a bounce that recalls jazz as dance music. [JJ] calls "Boogie Stop Shuffle" a "playful cop-show-theme rumble".
- **voice:** instrumental. There are shouts on one track but no lead vocal.
- **instruments:** from MusicBrainz:
  - double bass (Mingus);
  - alto and tenor saxes (Handy, Hadi, Ervin);
  - trombone (Knepper, Dennis);
  - piano (Parlan);
  - drums (Richmond);
  - clarinet (Handy, one track).
- **production tapesplicing:** [SM] the originals were "edited due to LP time constraints".
- **lyrics, language:** empty. The album is instrumental. "Fables of Faubus" is a protest, but its lyrics are not on this LP.
- **scene:** [JJ] Columbia's 30th Street Studio.
- **producers:** Teo Macero (MusicBrainz; [SM]).
- **sounds_like Duke Ellington:** [RS] "filtered the vibrancy and romance of his hero Duke Ellington's big-band orchestrations"; [JJ] Ellingtonian elegance.
- **influenced_by Duke Ellington [1/9], Jelly Roll Morton [1/9]: uncertain, please check.**
  - These come from Mingus's own homage titles, "Open Letter to Duke" and "Jelly Roll". [SSB] (Proctor) hears Morton's "left-hand bass figure and leisurely tempo" in "Jelly Roll".
  - No opened source quotes Mingus naming them as the album's influence. [SM] says he "worshipped Duke Ellington" (a critic).
  - Drop both if homage titles don't count as the artist's statement.
  - Lester Young ("Goodbye Pork Pie Hat") is an elegy, not a stylistic model, so I left him out.
- **landmark 1:** [JJ] "masterpiece"; [RS] "the place to hear why Mingus deserves a place in any survey of America's greatest composers".
- **Reception:** RS 500 #380, Grammy Hall of Fame 2013, [JJ] "masterpiece". Mainstream 2: auteur jazz.
- **No legacy row.** [SM] says "Goodbye Pork Pie Hat" "became an immediate standard and has been much-covered", and [JJ] names later versions by McLaughlin and Joni Mitchell. That is one song being covered, not a statement that the album influenced later music.

**Sources**

- [SP] Sputnikmusic user review "Fort23" (weak): https://www.sputnikmusic.com/album/16438/Thelonius-Monk-Brilliant-Corners

**Cells**
- **genres:**

- **energy 3:** [JJ61] Rollins "strident but exciting"; "a restless composition". Against that, a celesta ballad and a solo-piano standard.
- **mood 3:** [JJ61] "Pannonica" is "nostalgic to a surprising degree", and the title track is "restless".
- **theatrical 2:** small group.
- **experimental 3:**
  - [TG] the title track "speeds up, it slows down, it shifts course abruptly"; the band needed 25 takes.
  - [JJ61] "curious angular phrasing"; the theme is "written in varying tempos".
- **polish 3:** a normal studio record. [TG] calls it "gorgeously flawed".
- **tempo 3:** varying tempos [JJ61].
- **groove 2:** "Bemsha Swing" and the blues, but the meters shift.
- **instruments:** from MusicBrainz and [CR]:

  - trumpet (Clark Terry);
  - timpani ([JJ61], Roach plays "tympani" on "Bemsha").
- **production tapesplicing:** [TG] "a patchwork spliced together from the various takes".
- **scene:** [CR] Reeves Sound Studio, New York.
- **producers:** Orrin Keepnews (MusicBrainz; [CR]).
- **sounds_like, influenced_by:** empty.
- **landmark 1:** [JJ61] "the yardstick of Monk's work"; [UD] one of three LPs "pivotal" to his reputation. Craft's own "pivotal" is a label value judgment and not used.
- **Reception:**
  - [CR] Hentoff in Down Beat: "Riverside's most important modern jazz LP to date"; Down Beat later called it "1957's most praised LP".
  - National Recording Registry 2003 [CR]; Grammy Hall of Fame 1999.
  - [JJ61] "desert island disc".
  - Importance 5: the comeback album ([CR] cabaret card restored months after release).
- **No legacy row.**
- **Could not open:** the Guardian (John Fordham, "My favourite album", 2011). Christgau's Monk page only mentions the album in passing ("less complex and demanding than, say, Brilliant Corners").

  - avantgardejazz: [OTR/AllMusic] "a watershed event in the genesis of avant-garde jazz".
  - blues and gospel: [RS] "grounded in the cathartic release of the gospel and blues of his native Texas". Williams: "a deep and personal feeling for the blues".
- **energy 3:** [PM] "unabashedly intense"; Gleason "startling abruptness and discontinuity". "Lonely Woman" and "Peace" are slow.
- **mood 2:** [TG] the "haunting" melody; Wilson hears a wail "strikingly similar to the New Orleans dirges"; lament.
- **theatrical 2:** quartet. [PM] "nakedly emotional".
- **experimental 4:** [RS] "still a jarring sound to encounter for the first time"; Wilson "not easy music for the listener". It is not 5 because the themes are tune-based ([RS] "as lyrical as it is demanding").

- **voice:** instrumental.

- **producers:** Nesuhi Ertegun (MusicBrainz, Atlantic SD 1317).
- **sounds_like:**
  - Charlie Parker: Balliett (The New Yorker, 1959), "Charlie Parker supplies the central heating for Coleman's style".

- **influenced_by:** empty at album level. Red Connor is an artist-level influence; see artists.md.
- **landmark 1:** [RS] says on this album he "pioneered free jazz"; [OTR/AllMusic] "watershed".
- **Reception:** RS 500 #417, Grammy Hall of Fame 2015, Down Beat 4 stars. Mainstream 1: Billboard 1959, "won't have mass appeal".
- **Legacy 5:** [PM] Coltrane's mid-'60s Impulse records "grow directly out of the opening salvo fired by Coleman in '59"; [OTR/AllMusic] "watershed event in the genesis of avant-garde jazz". Martin Williams's "will affect the whole character of jazz music" is in the label's own liner notes, so it is not used.

## milesdavis-sketchesofspain: Sketches of Spain (1960), a new album for milesdavis
**Sources**
- [MD] milesdavis.com, the official site, which speaks for the artist: https://www.milesdavis.com/albums/sketches-of-spain/
- [JJ] Jazz Journal, Derek Ansell, Jun 6 2021: https://jazzjournal.co.uk/2021/06/06/miles-davis-sketches-of-spain/

**Cells**
- **genres:**
  - flamenco: [JJ] "a little gem of jazz and flamenco"; "Solea" is a flamenco lament that swings "in jazz format".
  - blues: [JJ] "the Spanish style fused with the blues"; Davis on "Solea" in [MD]: "It's close to the American black feeling in the blues."
  - No opened source says "third stream" or "classical", so neither is added.
- **energy 1, tempo 1, mood 1:** [JJ] "a haunting, sombre trumpet solo throughout"; [MD] "pinched, lonely sound". The exception is "Will O' The Wisp", in an "almost jubilant mood" [JJ].
- **theatrical 4:** a full orchestra conducted by Gil Evans, with dramatic swells. This is my judgment from the scoring in [MD] and [JJ].
- **experimental 3:** Iberian folk and concert material recast as orchestral jazz ([MD] "timeless tapestry of Iberian sounds").
- **polish 4:** [MD] "a Herculean effort that lasted from November 1959 to March"; many takes; Evans demanded precision.
- **groove 1.**
- **instruments:** from MusicBrainz: trumpet and flugelhorn (Davis), French horns, tuba, trombones, trumpets, flutes, oboe, bassoon, bass clarinet, clarinet, harp (Janet Putnam), double bass (Chambers), drums (Cobb), percussion (Elvin Jones, José Mangual). [JJ] "oboe interjections", "melancholy notes from tuba", "chime-like harp".
- **production hornarrangements:** Gil Evans arranged and conducted (MusicBrainz; [JJ]).
- **scene:** follows the Kind of Blue row (New York, Columbia).
- **producers:** Teo Macero and Irving Townsend ([MD]: "Teo Macero produced; Irving Townsend is also credited as producer"). Engineer Fred Plaut. Gil Evans is credited as arranger and conductor, not producer, so he is not listed.
- **influenced_by:** empty.
  - Rodrigo (Concierto de Aranjuez) and Falla (El Amor Brujo, for "Will O' The Wisp") are composers whose pieces are adapted, so they count as covers.
  - The Alan Lomax field recordings are source material for the adapted folk pieces.
  - "Flamenco Sketches" is Davis's own earlier work, so it is excluded as a self-link.
- **landmark 0:** the only "breakthrough" wording is the estate's ([MD]: "brought him mainstream recognition"), which is a value judgment. No independent source opened calls it defining.
- **Reception:** Grammy Hall of Fame 1997; [JJ] positive. Acclaim 4, because I could not open AllMusic, Penguin or NPR. Mainstream 3: a lush, accessible orchestral record, but auteur-made.
- **No legacy row.**

## armstrong-louisarmstrongplayswchandy: Louis Armstrong Plays W.C. Handy (1954)
**Sources**
- [LAHM] Louis Armstrong House Museum virtual exhibit, Ricky Riccardi (Director of Research Collections), "They're My Tops": https://virtualexhibits.louisarmstronghouse.org/2021/04/27/theyre-my-tops-the-making-of-louis-armstrong-plays-w-c-handy/
- MusicBrainz: Columbia CL 591.

**Cells**
- **genres:**
  - jazz: Armstrong in [LAHM], "Jazz music—that's the way we express ourselves"; he also says "I wouldn't call them Dixieland", so dixieland is not used.
  - blues: Handy is "The Father of the Blues", and the songs are his blues ("St. Louis Blues", "Atlanta Blues", "Hesitating Blues", "Ole Miss Blues").
- **energy 3:** the band "caught fire" on the first session; "St. Louis Blues" is relaxed and 8:50 long.
- **mood 3:** laughter and ad-libbed comic stanzas with Velma Middleton, alongside "wailing" blues choruses.
- **theatrical 3:** Middleton duets, and a chorus sung by studio guests on "Long Gone".
- **experimental 1.**
- **polish 3:** Avakian "edited and spliced" the masters.
- **tempo, groove:** empty. No source describes them.
- **voice:** empty. [LAHM] does not describe his voice on this record. The Satch Plays Fats row uses raspy, from a different source.
- **instruments:** from MusicBrainz and [LAHM]: trumpet (Armstrong), trombone (Trummy Young), clarinet (Barney Bigard), piano (Billy Kyle), bass (Arvell Shaw), drums (Barrett Deems).
- **production:**
  - tapesplicing: Avakian spliced the masters.
  - overdubbing: on "Atlanta Blues", Armstrong overdubbed trumpet and vocal obbligatos.
- **language:** en.
- **scene:** follows the Satch Plays Fats row. Recorded in Chicago (Wrigley Building, probably WBBM's studio).
- **producers:** George Avakian (MusicBrainz; [LAHM]).
- **influenced_by:** empty. Handy wrote the songs being covered, so he is excluded.
- **landmark 1:** [LAHM] "Armstrong's landmark recording", "a true masterpiece", "a definitive portrait". Riccardi speaks for the museum, not the label. This matches how wave 3 used LAHM.
- **Reception:** Billboard rave, Dec 4 1954; Hentoff in Down Beat, 5 stars; Grammy Hall of Fame 2010. Wave 3 also notes that Hentoff called Satch Plays Fats "not equal in quality" to this album. Importance 4: his first Columbia album.
- **No legacy row.**
- **Thin row:** tempo, groove and voice are empty, and confidence is medium.
