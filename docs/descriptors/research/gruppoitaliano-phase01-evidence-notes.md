# Gruppo Italiano — phase 01: editorial evidence and scoring notes

Scope: three original studio albums; references in `gruppoitaliano_phase01_sources.csv` and 13 corresponding genre rows in `gruppoitaliano_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Only well-supported instruments or production techniques were filled. Genre taxonomies from later discographic sources are not substitutes for contemporaneous criticism; ratings are provisional. The eight numerical scales follow the Fandome 0–5 anchors; empty means unknown, not zero.

## Maccherock (1982) — `gruppoitaliano-maccherock`

- **Edition**: the original LP on Mara&C/Dischi Ricordi, catalogue SMRL 6288, 13 songs. The original contains a side of 5 original songs and 8 adapted covers (Salerno's retrospective label account). The prior band Randa is not a separate Gruppo Italiano LP; the 1982 release carries the Gruppo Italiano name. No *Tropicana* on this album.
- **Genres**: `poprock` and `italodisco` are given by the album's discography. A contemporaneous Hit Parade Italia feature identifies a distinct rockabilly flavour in the first album (not the tropical hit), justifying `rockabilly` high in the ordered list. `pop` describes the accessible arrangements of adapted songs and originals. This is NOT automatically `newwave` or `synthpop`: neither is supported for the full LP by the available sources.
- **Instrumentation**: credited lineup guitar (Chicco Santulli), bass (Gigi Folino), drums and percussion (Roberto/Bozo Del Bo); Patrizia Di Malta and Raffaella Riva provide lead/backing singing. No reliable album-level lists of keyboards, electronic drum machines, named synths, tape effects or recording software were located. The final row uses only `guitar|bass|drums|percussion`; the credited guitar type is unspecified, so do not automatically add `electricguitar`. Blank `production` and `electronic` reflect evidence limits.
- **Producer**: Oscar Prudente is attributed by the album discography and its arranger history, whereas the Salerno/Maionchi pair own the releasing label. Their roles are not interchangeable.
- **Scales**: energy 3 and tempo 3 for brisk comic-pop and retro numbers; mood 3 (bright irony alongside satirical topics); theatrical 3 due to mannered vocal and staged comic presentation; experimental 2 for unusual adaptations/wordplay; polish 3 for professional early-eighties studio arrangements; groove 2 for dance accents but not predominantly dance-floor songs. Electronic left unscored. `voice=clear|choral` reflects prominent two-voice ensemble rather than an opera chorus. Texts rely on playful paraphrases, media satires and storytelling; these are editor-classified, not an analysis of every lyric.
- **Reviews / conflicts**: Some music journalism dates the debut to 1981; the original Ricordi catalogue and retrospective Salerno account give 1982. Use 1982. One writer mentions Frank Zappa as a preference of the earlier Randa but does not offer a direct band quote; `influenced_by` is empty.

## Tapioca manioca (1984) — `gruppoitaliano-tapiocamanioca`

- **Edition**: original ten-song Ricordi LP SMRL 6310; features *Halloween*, *Fotografia*, *Il treno del caffè*, *Cha cha cha!*, *Boulevard*, *Anni ruggenti*, *Warrnambool*, *Santo Domingo*, *Sotto le conchiglie*, *Dammi un sabato nite*. Importantly, **not** *Tropicana*, even though that was their previous hit. Distinguish original LP from later retrospective playlists.
- **Genres**: original LP explicitly designated `italodisco|pop`; *Anni ruggenti* original single confirms Italo disco. Retrospective reviews describe 1930s retro dance-pop staging and lightly electronic arrangement, supporting `dancepop` and a `disco` parent. A biographical discography speaks of exotic sonorities and *Cha cha cha! / Santo Domingo* are tropical/Latin-styled track titles. Use `latin` last at *selected tracks* scope, not as the album's defining sound. Avoid `synthpop` (a specific genre) without a source explicitly naming it.
- **Instrumentation / production**: guitars, bass, drums, percussion and two singers reliably described by the LP's personnel. *Anni ruggenti* was arranged in a retro-electronic manner, but no source names the instrument or effect generating the electronic layer. It does not justify synth, Fairlight, DX7, synthprogramming, drumprogramming or similar production tokens. Production blank. Oscar Prudente given as album producer.
- **Scales**: energy 3; electronic 2 (documented on the lead festival song; no whole-LP electronics list); mood 4 (playful dance pop); theatrical 4 (costumed, designed performance); experimental 1; polish 4; tempo 3; groove 4 (dance-led style, album numbers blend festival-pop and Latin rhythmic details). The electronic value is estimated from a *track-level* arrangement, not verified for all ten tracks.
- **Compositions and influences**: contemporary review draws attention to a resemblance of *Anni ruggenti* to *When I'm Sixty-Four* by the Beatles. This does **not** establish The Beatles as cited artistic influences, much less a whole-album influence. Do not add the band or song authors to `influenced_by`.
- **Critical divergence**: reviewers place the album between lighthearted novelty pop and weak follow-up to a strong single; disagreement concerns reception, not the basic 1984 original identity.

## Surf in Italy (1985) — `gruppoitaliano-surfinitaly`

- **Edition**: original 9-track Ricordi LP SMRL 6343. Includes *Cuando calienta el sol*, *Sono bugiarda*, *Con te sulla spiaggia*, *Ti dirò*, *Che colpa abbiamo noi*, *Datemi un martello*, *A chi*, *Tropicana*, *L'estate sta finendo*. Eight older/crossover covers surround the 1983 original *Tropicana*. Because the 1985 LP has newly produced group performances and a distinct cover-revival concept, it is `studio`, **not** the later 2002 retrospective compilation.
- **Genres**: the album's discography assigns four: `pop`, `rock`, `funk`, and `disco`. These are the entire set; no invented exact synth era or Latin subgenre. `pop` is first as most inclusive style; `disco|funk|rock` follow as recorded dance, guitar and rhythmic stylistic components. *Datemi un martello* and *Sono bugiarda* come from older rock/pop material, but source genre designations refer to this album's recorded interpretations rather than the original recordings.
- **Producer**: a group history explicitly states the 1985 artistic production was taken over by guitarist/arranger **Chicco Santulli**; prior LP producer Oscar Prudente should not be automatically retained here. Without sleeve confirmation, `producers=Chicco Santulli` remains medium confidence and subject to original-label verification.
- **Instruments and studio**: only the documented band's guitars, bass, drums and percussion; two women sing with occasional Gigi Folino vocals. No source confirms a specific keyboard, sequencer, drum machine, loop technique or mixer, so `production` and the `electronic` scale are blank rather than guessed. It is **unknown** whether the 1985 *Tropicana* is a reuse of its 1983 master or a re-recording.
- **Scales**: energy 3, mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 0, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3, acoustic/electronic blank. Recreational revival material and dance-pop interpretations make it relatively bright. Retrospective lyrical topics can include social satire through *Tropicana*, but that does not make the record a political concept album.
- **Caveat**: the description “raccolta di brani editi” refers to covers of previously published **other artists' songs**. Distinguish this kind of studio covers set from a greatest-hits collection of old Gruppo Italiano recordings. *Tropicana* itself is a reused older composition, with master status uncertain.

## Sources and musical influences

See the separate `sources.csv` for the 18 album-specific references. Documented artist-declared influences were not established. Contemporary writers compare the earlier Randa to Frank Zappa and reviewers draw a Beatles resemblance in *Anni ruggenti*, but neither qualifies under Fandome's strict causal influence rule. Never convert cover songwriters, original cover performers, guest players, journalists' analogies, or studio producers into `influenced_by` entries.
