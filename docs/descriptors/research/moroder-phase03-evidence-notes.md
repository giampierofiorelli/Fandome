# Giorgio Moroder — Phase 03 / evidenti per album

**Research frozen 2026-10-25.** Sources referenced by the key at the start of each item in `moroder_phase03_sources.csv`. The 25-column records make **album-wide editorial judgements**; stylistic subgenres on a single track are annotated separately in `genre_evidence.csv`. Score anchors are the repository guide, not numerical measurements.

## 1. `moroder-lovesinyoulovesinme` — *Love's in You, Love's in Me* (1978)

- **Identification and authorship.** The 23 June 1978 original Casablanca record is attributed to **Giorgio & Chris**, Moroder with American singer **Chris Bennett** [L-OFF; L-ORIG]. Treat the release as a documented, substantive Moroder collaboration (not as a Moroder-only performance); album-type `studio` and artist room `Giorgio Moroder` are project indexing decisions. Moroder and **Pete Bellotte** have explicit album-level producer credits [L-OFF; L-ORIG]; other players (Forsey, Faltermeyer, Wyman, Koppers) have instrumental/programming/engineering credits and should not be auto-added to producer field.
- **Genres.** A contemporary reviewer calls it comparatively gentle **disco**, while acknowledging a real dancefloor pulse and connected side-one tracks [L-REVIEW]. A specialist original-LP appraisal explicitly uses **Euro Disco** [L-CLUB]. The Repertoire historical account describes the production as a more **pop-oriented** approach with Moog/vocoder accents on specific tracks [L-REISSUE]. `electronic` must **not** imply an all-electronic record. `rnb` and `soul` are distant database/retail umbrellas [L-AM; L-REISSUE]: lower-priority and lower-strength, not independently established R&B-dominant tracks.
- **Scales.** `energy=3` / `tempo=3`: softer moderately paced dance record, not *From Here to Eternity*'s driving synthesized suite [L-REVIEW; L-REISSUE]. `electronic=3`: prominent synth/programming, but live band, backing choir and star singer also prominently credited [L-ORIG; L-REISSUE]. `mood=3` reconciles affectionate warmth with `Love Now, Hurt Later` and unresolved yearning. `theatrical=2` because Bennett's vocal intimacy is foregrounded over stage drama [L-REVIEW]. `experimental=2` because side A mixes as a suite and there is some vocoder texture, but the songs remain fairly accessible [L-REVIEW; L-REISSUE]. `polish=4` supported by studio personnel and favorable 1978 recording assessment [L-REVIEW]. `groove=4`: club-capable, but not uniformly maximum-intensity disco [L-REVIEW].
- **Voice / language / lyric content.** Bennett's singing is foregrounded with Moroder sharing vocals [L-REVIEW; L-ORIG]. `soft|clear` is a qualified editorial reading of the lightly delivered, melodic female-led songs; voice documentation is weaker than producer documentation. English language and `love|heartbreak` supported by original titles/record; lyrics have weaker thematic scope than an annotated lyrics source. Omit unsupported `party` and `identity`.
- **Resemblance and influence.** Nearby Moroder-created studio records *Munich Machine* and contemporaneous Donna Summer / Roberta Kelly tracks belong in `sounds_like` as **editorial sonic-neighbors**, not demonstrated influences on this collaboration. `influenced_by` stays **empty**.
- **Landmark = 0, confidence = medium.** Real original collaboration and strong credits; however there is sparse sustained critical discussion of the full album, and the 2026 edition truncates/absorbs the separate original `Reprise` track (6 vs 7 entries) [L-OFF; L-REISSUE]. Always judge 1978 seven-track LP.

## 2. `moroder-midnightexpress` — *Midnight Express* (1978)

- **Identity / film status.** This is the actual **1978 Alan Parker movie soundtrack**, not a space-inspired derivative or a compilation [M-OFF; M-MB]. Moroder won the **Best Original Score** Oscar [M-OFF]. **Moroder alone is named as LP producer** [M-SLEEVE; M-FR]; Alan Marshall and David Puttnam, often displayed nearby as film producers, must **not** appear in the music `producers` field. **Harold Faltermeyer** is credited electronic instrument player/arranger, not LP co-producer [M-SLEEVE].
- **Track/edition caution.** Official site prints `Istanbul Opening` twice, with different times [M-OFF]. The original 1978 LP and library identify `Istanbul Blues` with David Castle on B1, `Istanbul Opening` instrumental on B3, and Chris Bennett on vocal finale [M-MUSIK; M-FR]. Do not base the music solely on the disco single **Chase** or a later reordered 2015 repress [M-AM].
- **Genres.** `soundtrack|electronic` apply widely; `electrodisco|disco` most strongly describe *Chase* and some other rhythmic passages, not *The Wheel*, *Istanbul Opening* or *Cacaphoney* [M-AM; M-PITCH; M-AOTY]. AllMusic places some romantic/pop elements in `dancepop`, and its broad R&B taxonomy is weak; `trance` is **the critic's sequencer-trance analogy for Chase**, **not** a historically contemporaneous 1990s trance LP [M-AM].
- **Scales.** `energy=3`: driving opener but extended eerie / slow scene-setting. `electronic=4`: synthetic focus with **credited real drums and percussion**, so not a fully electronic Kraftwerk 5 [M-SLEEVE]. `mood=1`: prison-film tension, disorientation, menace predominate, despite the warmer love theme [M-AM]. `theatrical=4`: filmic dramatic cues serving story. `experimental=4`: non-standard, abstract sound design (*Wheel*, *Cacaphoney*) highlighted by John Bush [M-AM]. `polish=4` professionally produced multi-studio score and meticulous arrangement [M-SLEEVE]. `tempo=3` / `groove=3`: alternation of famous extended disco number with many non-dance cues. **Don't treat `Chase` as whole album**.
- **Voice / lyrics / language.** Primarily **instrumental**, with only two vocal tracks: David Castle's *Istanbul Blues*, Bennett on vocal theme [M-AM; M-MUSIK]. Leave unsubstantiated singers' *vocal timbres* out of the list; `instrumental` is dominant. `storytelling` applies to the sung prison narrative, `love` to the romantic theme (both **minority track content**). English vocals, no second-language vocal evidence. Scene scores are separate from lyrics.
- **Influences.** Moroder expressly recounts Parker **asking for music inspired by the sonic atmosphere of Donna Summer's *I Feel Love***, which Moroder produced; `Donna Summer` here is a qualified *project cue*, not a suggestion she performed on the soundtrack [M-INTERVIEW]. John Bush compares its sequenced tracks with **Tangerine Dream**; comparison goes exclusively into `sounds_like` [M-AM]. It is never valid to infer influence just from later 1980s soundtracks emulating Chase.
- **Landmark = 1, high.** Oscar-winning first Moroder film score, with a track recognized as foundational electronic cinema music [M-OFF; M-PITCH]. High confidence on release/credits and broad sound, lower on the dance metadata applied to full album.

## 3. `moroder-musicfrombattlestargalacticaandotheroriginalcompositions` — *Music From "Battlestar Galactica" and Other Original Compositions* (1978)

- **Critical classification correction.** Original US Casablanca **NBLP 7126**, documented **December 11, 1978** [B-COPY]. Giorgio Moroder himself says he **did not write the original television music** and that this was **not the programme's original soundtrack** but reinterpretations plus a Moroder original [B-MORODER; B-KNACK]. Classify `type=studio` for these newly recorded album performances, **while keeping `soundtrack` as a genre with limited TV-adaptation scope**. This conflicts with record-shop soundtracks catalogue sections; document explicitly. Source original alternate title sometimes includes **“From Giorgio Moroder”**; retain title shown in phase plan as canonical Fandome title and list alternate here.
- **Two-sided shape.** Eight short linked *Battlestar* arrangements on side A, with Moog, real **brass, string section, choir, drumkit, bass** [B-SLEEVE]; one Moroder-composed ~15m **Evolution** on side B [B-QOBUZ]. A critic describes live drummer, guitar and tambourine on Evolution [B-NPR]; don't misstate the entire album as machine-only. Moroder and **Harold Faltermeyer** are both original LP producers; **Reinhold Mack is engineer** [B-SLEEVE]. **Stu Phillips** wrote several TV themes but didn't produce this LP.
- **Genres.** Original is classified disco, electronic, Funk/Soul, and an independent genre aggregator calls it `electrodisco|spacedisco` [B-RECORD; B-AOTY]. Qobuz explicitly says `dance` [B-QOBUZ]. `funk` is especially supported for *Evolution* by Guardian critic [B-GUARDIAN]. `soul` = **weak original label broad 'Funk/Soul' catalogue umbrella**, not album-wide soul singing (see genre evidence). `soundtrack` refers only to repurposed TV *themes*, not audio commissioned/used as original TV score [B-MORODER].
- **Scales.** `energy=3` moderately active themes and extended restrained Evolution; `electronic=3` since machines coexist with string/brass rhythm section [B-SLEEVE]. `mood=3`: heroic themes versus sinister drifting instrumental passages, not unrelieved gloom. `theatrical=4`: brass, strings and TV sci-fi grandeur. `experimental=3`: effect-laden extended Evolution balances standard theme adaptations. `polish=4`: multiple layers and orchestrated mixes. `tempo=3`, `groove=4`: many side-A grooves, with the more psychedelic meander of Evolution preventing maximum dancefloor consistency [B-NPR; B-GUARDIAN].
- **Voice/lyrics/language.** `instrumental` is album-dominant; choir credited on side A does not automatically qualify as lead `choral` voice. A short *It's Love, Love, Love* vocal performance exists but **no reliable whole-album lyric programme or language designation** has been established; leave lyrics/language empty rather than invent broad album themes.
- **Influences.** **Stu Phillips** wrote principal original TV themes explicitly adapted on the first side [B-QOBUZ; B-SLEEVE], qualifying a direct documented source for `influenced_by`. Note this is **compositional adaptation**, not an assertion Moroder cites Phillips as an aesthetic role model. Critical Pink Floyd analogy and shared-studio Munich Machine are `sounds_like` not influences [B-NPR].
- **Landmark = 0, confidence = medium.** The original track *Evolution* is critically beloved [B-GUARDIAN; B-NPR], but artist's own account is dismissive of the album [B-KNACK]; neither whole LP nor TV side merits automatic 1. Track and provenance verified strongly, holistic album scales less so.

## 4. `moroder-emc` — *E=MC²* (1979)

- **Catalog and year, critical.** The branch `descriptors` already includes title *E=MC²*, year **1979**. Its actual auto-generated `album_id` is **`moroder-emc`**, because the TS slug normalises accents with NFD and then strips everything outside `[a-z0-9]`, so superscript **² disappears**. Do not create `moroder-emc2` and do not rename title [E-OFF]. Initial release **27 August 1979**, six songs only; some AllMusic region listings say **1980** and some reissues add *Evolution* or *Love's in You* as bonuses; exclude bonuses [E-OFF; E-REISSUE].
- **Producers discrepancy.** Giorgio's official website lists **only Giorgio Moroder** [E-OFF], while original LP sleeve in some territories and independent AllMusic list **Giorgio Moroder and Harold Faltermeyer** as producers [E-SLEEVE; E-ALLMUSIC]. Explicit co-producer evidence from original LP justifies preserving **both** but **flag for confirmation against main US NBLP 7169 sleeve**; `high` overall, specific credit disagreement kept visible. Soundstream recordists, computerized editors Bloomenthal/Rothaar and songwriter Pete Bellotte are *not* automatically producers.
- **Crucial electronic 4 versus 5.** AllMusic's Alex Henderson calls the record an entirely electronic album [E-ALLMUSIC], but **Moroder's own first-person testimony** describes **four or five piano players and live drums** on digital master, with some programming [E-SOS]. Faltermeyer explains mechanically actuated piano and clavinet triggered by the MicroComposer [E-REDBULL]. `electronic=4`: synthesizers central, but some real acoustic/electromechanical instruments. **Live-to-digital is a recording pathway, not evidence of fully electronic sound sources**. Reverb identifies Roland System 700, MC-8, Moog vocoder and Polymoog on *Baby Blue* specifically [E-REVERB]. Do not extrapolate these named models to every track.
- **Genres.** `electrodisco|eurodisco` explicitly called out in AOTY taxonomy; `disco|electronic` corroborated by AllMusic. `dancepop|pop` from accessible conventional disco tunes and strong hooks; genres are **not** techno, house or 1980s industrial just because the album preceded/influenced them [E-AOTY; E-ALLMUSIC; E-FACT]. `synthpop` isn't asserted in source as an album-wide genre and is omitted.
- **Scales.** `energy=4`, `groove=5`: overt, continuous dancefloor material [E-FACT; E-AOTY]. `mood=4`: FACT specifically distinguishes brighter, cheekier, accessible tones from darker previous album [E-FACT]. `theatrical=3`: layered studio voices and title-track meta-credits, but not a full theatrical concept narrative [E-REDBULL]. `experimental=3`: pioneering technical recording and vocoders, otherwise traditional pop/disco structures; **recording engineering novelty should not by itself inflate composition experimentalism** [E-FACT; E-ALLMUSIC]. `polish=5`: sophisticated studio/equipment and detailed digital production [E-SOS; E-DAZED]. `tempo=4`: largely uptempo disco, not a metronomic BPM measurement.
- **Voice and lyrics.** Moroder sings lead; vocoder modifies some phrases, especially chorus and title track [E-REDBULL; E-SPIN]. Mark `soft` as relatively restrained/non-belting singer, a **qualified editorial** descriptor; do not add `spoken` for sung final credits. `language=en`, with English-language original song titles. Broad love/romantic and flirtatious party context on six vocal songs; `love|sensuality|party` weaker than factual production values, and not intended as every-track lyrical transcript.
- **Resemblance versus influence.** `Donna Summer|Munich Machine|Kraftwerk` only **sonic/editorial neighbors**—not their actual performance credits or certified named sources. FACT's signed specialist critic explicitly frames this LP as indebted to orchestral disco and **Cerrone** [E-FACT]; put `Cerrone` in `influenced_by` with the caveat that this is a **critic's attribution**, not a Moroder artist quote. Important distinction: a future artist being influenced by this album is not a preceding influence.
- **Landmark = 1, high.** A recognized milestone in experimental studio recording; significance comes from **historically unusual digital production** plus a full LP of club-pop songs, not falsely claiming to have invented all-electronic music [E-OFF; E-REISSUE; E-SOS].

## Selection and exclusions

- Phase 03 is incremental after 1973–1977 Phase 02 and 1969–1973 Phase 01: **only these four titles**. `moroder.csv` already has previous phases; append/merge on `album_id`, never overwrite it.
- *Thank God It's Friday* (1978), *Munich Machine Introducing Chris Bennett: A Whiter Shade of Pale* (1978), *Munich Machine: Body Shine* (1979), Donna Summer productions and *No. 1 in Heaven* (1979) by Sparks are **related Moroder-associated productions, not all Moroder studio albums**. Flag for later separate collaboration-impact audit if needed; do not silently misattribute them as this artist's four rows.
- The 1979 E=MC² title is **not** 1980 original despite AllMusic and some national issue calendars listing 1980.
- No new genre tokens in this phase: `electrodisco`, `eurodisco`, `spacedisco`, `trance`, `dancepop` and others are **already in the updated branch Genre union** after Phase 02.
