# Riccardo Cocciante — Fase 03: prove, scale, identità delle edizioni

**Artist ID:** `cocciante`; branch `descriptors`. Four original Italian studio LPs: *Cocciante* (1982), *Sincerità* (1983), *Il mare dei papaveri* (1985), *La grande avventura* (1987). Phase 01 covers 1972–75, phase 02 covers 1976–80. **None of those rows appear here.** Scores on 0–5 scale are editorial judgments calibrated to Fandome guide, not source quotations. A track-level genre is weighted toward the end; no covered song or band member counts as influenced_by.

## 1. `cocciante-cocciante` — *Cocciante* (1982; 8 tracks)

**Original edition:** RCA Italiana PL 31623, eight original tracks, NOT *Cocciante* (1991), and not the 1978 *Riccardo Cocciante* LP. The slug `cocciante-cocciante` can only serve 1982: for the later 1991 same-name record Claude should use a disambiguating suffix (`cocciante-cocciante1991`) once that phase is reached. Sony/Qobuz original-album metadata confirms year and phonograms (some tracks ℗1981), Mogol author of all lyrics, Paul Buckmaster arranger and Norman David Shapiro producer. **Shel Shapiro = Norman David Shapiro: the same person, counted once**. Secondary original personnel list: Paolo Donnarumma bass; Chris Whitten drums/percussion; Carlo Pennisi guitar; Guido Podestà accordion; Alessandro Centofanti keyboards; Paul Buckmaster cello/synth; Shel Shapiro twelve-string guitar; Maurizio Guarini synth; Adriano Giordanella percussion; Mauro Chiari harmonica; Giovanni Culasso trumpet; Baldo Maestri clarinet; backing vocals by named singers. The vinyl sleeve has not been independently inspected for every item. Its list is secondary and therefore priority for verification.

**Genres** (five): `pop` (album-level conventional pop in original metadata and overview), `songwriter` (authorial work Cocciante/Mogol), `poprock` (RCA/Sony Qobuz broad Pop/Rock shelf genre, not bare rock), `ballad` (musician Alberto Moneti explicitly calls both *Celeste nostalgia* and *Un buco nel cuore* ballads). The orchestrated texture is discussed but not promoted to the unsourced `orchestralpop` genre. On *Celeste nostalgia*, the acoustic guitar and lightly jazzed rhythm are directly documented. University scholar Ernesto Virgulti says it sounds more like **Lucio Battisti** specifically on that song. Put `Lucio Battisti [1/8]` under *sounds_like* only. Mogol is the 1982 songwriter, **not** evidence of Battisti as an influence. Musical influence field left empty.

**Eight scales:** `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2`: largely restrained, guitar-led, with *In bicicletta* brighter, and moderate drums/percussion. `electronic=2`: Buckmaster/Guarini synth and Centofanti electric keyboards are credited but acoustic guitars, piano/strings/accordion remain significant. `mood=2`: nostalgia, separation, friendship, quiet melancholy, a few lighter performances. `theatrical=3`: dramatic lead singer, but 1982 arrangements favor modest pop over large older crescendos (Virgulti). `experimental=2`: some synthesizer, cello and compositional variation, otherwise familiar structures. `polish=4`: Paul Buckmaster multi-instrument arrangements and detailed RCA studio finish. **Voice blank**: source discusses Cocciante’s expressive delivery generally; insufficient to assert a consistent album-specific voice label. **Landmark=1**: Rai, Rockol and 1982 year-end charts establish enduring record with three signature songs and No. 2 year-end placement; not merely the hit lead single.

## 2. `cocciante-sincerita` — *Sincerità* (1983; 10 tracks)

**Original edition:** Virgin V 2294, ten Italian-language tracks in original order. **Do not duplicate** French *Sincérité*, English *Sincerity* or CD reissues (AllMusic page dated January 1999 is the reissue, not first publication). Stylistic and recording evidence includes AllMusic, original sleeve transcriptions from Sessiondays/La Bible de la Westcoast Music and Sony track-by-track credits. Original sleeve transcription gives **James Newton Howard and Elliot Scheiner** as the only full producers, even though one Wikipedia secondary compilation adds **Richie Zito**. He is a credited **guitarist**, with no independent producer credit in original sleeve/Sony metadata, so exclude from producers pending direct proof. Cocciante wrote music and Étienne Roda-Gil words.

**Instrumentation:** Mike Baird drums (Sony's different retrospective compilations also list Jeff Porcaro on some tracks; the original sleeve transcription lists Mike Baird), Mike Porcaro and David Hungate bass, Steve Lukather / Dean Parks / Richie Zito guitars, James Newton Howard keyboards, Paulinho Da Costa percussion; Marty Paich and Howard track-specific strings, Jerry Hey horn arrangement. The **Synclavier II programmed by Steve DeFuria** is directly listed on original sleeve transcription, hence `synclavier` goes in *production* rather than a nonexistent instrument token. No unsupported drum machine.

**Genres** six: `pop` (AllMusic Italian Pop), `westcoastpop` (album-wide precise category explicitly named by West Coast music specialist; NEW TOKEN), `aor` (album-oriented rock designation plus LA studio sound; advisory compound genre, Claude may keep only if evidence supports), `poprock` (AllMusic Pop/Rock), `songwriter` (Cocciante author, Roda-Gil lyrics), `ballad` (romantic and lush moods per AllMusic, track-level ballad material). `westcoastpop` is not a claim that members of Toto influenced songwriting: they were session musicians.

**Eight scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: upbeat Di notte (Rockol explicitly calls unusually danceable 80s rhythm) and tight L.A. drums/percussion balance slower ballads. `electronic=3`: keyboard-led pop, a specifically programmed Synclavier II, with full live drums and guitars. `mood=3`: romantic/passionate uplift alternates bittersweet introspection. `theatrical=3`: powerful emotional lead against polished L.A. band, less orchestral melodrama than 1970s. `experimental=2`: pop formats familiar but new L.A. sonic setup and digital workstation. `polish=5`: production in Bill Schnee, Village Recorder, Ocean Way, mixed by Scheiner and mastered by Ted Jensen, layered orchestra and contemporary synth. `voice=powerful`: song *Di notte* Rockol describes forceful vocal against fast rhythm; AllMusic marks album Dramatic/Passionate; review in original album context. **Landmark=0**: stylistically notable US experiment, sources do not independently make album career-defining compared to more widely recognized LPs. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` blank, as no sourced named sonic comparisons or testimony; **Toto, Howard, Scheiner, Lukather are collaborators, not influences**.

## 3. `cocciante-ilmaredeipapaveri` — *Il mare dei papaveri* (1985; 10 tracks)

**Original edition:** Virgin V 2336, ten songs, not October 2026 Sony reissue or French/Spanish/English versions. **Original Virgin sleeve via Recordsale lists Paul Buckmaster as arranger/producer** and Carlos Bonell as classical guitarist/arranger; **Sony/Qobuz original 1985 track credits and Apple Music separately identify BOTH Riccardo Cocciante and Paul Buckmaster as producers**. Since the scope is all credited producers (including artist), `producers=Riccardo Cocciante|Paul Buckmaster`. Mark original-sleeve-versus-digital *album/track granularity difference*, rather than assuming one credit is incorrect. Sony metadata names guitarist Carlos Bonell, violinist Gavyn Wright, electric guitarist Vic Linton, alto saxophonist Ray Warleigh. The spelling in some online summaries is Vic Lylton, so only instrument token, not performer name, belongs in the descriptor. Arrangements are written orchestral/string and folk/classical guitar passages; do not invent bass/drums, drum machine, or synthesizers absent from the original record’s retrieved credited instrument list. **Mina appears on Questione di feeling** but is a duet partner/guest, not co-billed across the album; never add her as a producer or influence.

**Genres** `pop` (Sony's official sound notes), `songwriter` (Mogol/Cocciante original compositions, Rockol), `chanson` and `ballad` (both literally listed as original pressing styles). Sony describes orchestral arrangements, but not explicitly the `orchestralpop` subgenre. **Eight scales:** `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=1`: melodic ballads dominate; lead duet provides a rhythmic bright spot. `electronic=1`: Sony calls orchestrations modern, but no specific synth model credited, so low not zero. `mood=3`: yearning, tenderness and nostalgia, a relatively warm duet. `theatrical=4`: lush string figures and crescendos, expressive voice. `experimental=1`: critical writing describes conventional pop songwriting, despite sophisticated production. `polish=4`: Buckmaster production, written orchestral arrangements and multi-engineer studio finish. **Voice blank** for no specific album-wide category. **Landmark=0**: a major *song* (Questione di feeling) does not automatically confer album-level landmark. `sounds_like`/`influenced_by` blank.

## 4. `cocciante-lagrandeavventura` — *La grande avventura* (1987; 10 tracks)

**Chronology dispute resolved by original pressing:** Virgin VRC 88 dates ℗1987 and runout **19-12-87**, Apple Music original release **19 December 1987**, OndaRock monograph 1987. Some discographies label it *1988*, likely marketing/publication and later promotion; Fandome uses first original issue **1987**. Studio album, after 1986 live *Quando si vuole bene*. The original LP credits **Geoff Westley** as **sole producer, arranger, piano, keyboards, programmer** and Andy Pask (bass), Stuart Elliott (drums), Phil Palmer (guitars), Frank Ricotti (percussion), Jamie Talbot (sax), Malcolm Griffiths (trombone); Rockol and contemporary documentation also give Guy Barker (trumpet). **Mogol** main lyricist; **Lucio Dalla** wrote *Cuori di Gesù*, **Enrico Ruggeri** *Indocina* and *Il funambolo*. None is the album producer or an album-wide co-credited recording artist. Not producer: studio engineer Geoff Calver; production coordinator Stefano Micocci; album cover artist Milo Manara.

**Genres** `songwriter|sophistipop|synthpop|poprock|ballad|technosoul`: OndaRock's **actual album masthead** says 'canzone d'autore, sophisti-pop, synth-pop'; its close reading details long experimental/near-cinematic structures without explicitly labelling `artpop`, and pop-rock guitar episodes, the *Il vero amore* cyber-ballad, and **techno soul** only in the concluding title song (1/10). The latter is a **new token** not yet registered in Fandome; add it separately only if accepted by Claude, not simply convert it to whole-album `soul`. All genres named as part of extended passages are tail-ranked; do not tag generic `rock` as album genre.

**Eight scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=3` because slow atmospheric introductions are followed by strongly driving climaxes. `electronic=4`: synth/electric piano and **Fairlight** explicitly documented in the OndaRock close reading; Westley credited for programming, plus a live band. `mood=2`: shaded mystery, existential doubt, memory of Indochina and spiritual longing with intermittent resolution. `theatrical=4`: powerful vocal trajectories and long dramatic crescendos; OndaRock describes very expressive phrasing. `experimental=4`: interlinked long forms, unusual futuristic/organic textures, techno-soul ending. `polish=4`: Westley tailored studio sound with clear layered instruments at Parsifal in London; studio engineered by Geoff Calver. `groove=2`: rhythm-driven climaxes in several songs; most album introspective, not dance pop. `voice=powerful` on OndaRock's direct 1987 album-specific assessment. **Landmark=1**: OndaRock named it a *pietra miliare italiana* and defended its significance at album scope, even though several more commercially famous Cocciante songs are elsewhere.

**Sounds like / influences:** OndaRock likens the title song's horn/synth sound to **Vangelis** specifically, so `Vangelis [1/10]` is an acceptable *critic sonic resemblance* only. An actual January 1988 Cocciante interview explains he chose **producer Geoff Westley** after admiring arrangements Westley had created for **Lucio Battisti's *Una donna per amico*** (1978). This IS direct evidence of a recording/arrangement reference, but the attraction is Westley's work, not direct musical influence by Battisti; to preserve the strict user rule do **not** turn that into `influenced_by=Lucio Battisti`. The artist's collaborators are not influences either.
