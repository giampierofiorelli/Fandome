# Raffaella Carrà — Phase 03 evidence notes (English)

Scope: four original studio albums, 1978–1981, **only new rows**. All genres mentioned by an album source or grounded in a specific recording are retained, even when represented by a single track. `genre_evidence.csv` gives a clear `whole album` / `some tracks` / `texture` distinction. Do not turn genre prevalence into a requirement. Scales are cautious *editorial aggregate assessments* of these specific records, not laboratory measures. When source access does not substantiate electronic instrumentation album-wide, `electronic` stays blank. English-language sources are preferred for music analysis where available.

## Critical ID collision: 1978 and 1981

The catalog **already** has `carra-raffaella` (1971) and `carra-raffaellacarra` (1971). The correct 1978 and 1981 titles must stay unmodified as *Raffaella* and *Raffaella Carrà*. New unambiguous IDs are `carra-raffaella1978` and `carra-raffaellacarra1981`. The Fandome validator currently accepts a newly proposed album only under `artist-id + slug(title)`, which clashes with 1971 rows. **Claude must add the 1978/1981 records to `catalog.ts` and `seed.ts` under these exact year-disambiguated IDs BEFORE running the validator.** Once `albumIds` includes the correct IDs, validator uses its existing-album branch. Otherwise expect a falsely flagged slug mismatch. Never replace the older row, rename original album titles or merge editions. `carra-applauso` and `carra-mispendotutto` are ordinary slug IDs.

## `Raffaella` (1978) — `carra-raffaella1978`

**Object and sources:** Original CBS 82654, 21 February 1978 per official discography. Ten tracks, including *Black Cat, Sono nera, Dancin' in the Sun, Tango, Amoa, Tanti auguri, Luca, California, Ci vediamo domani, A Million Dollars*. CBS original sleeve credits **Gianni Boncompagni as album producer**; Paolo Ormi as **arranger**; Gaetano Vituzzi as chief engineer. Qobuz digital *Black Cat* track metadata separately credits **both Boncompagni and Ormi as producers on that one track**, so the row lists both but the Ormi producer credit is TRACK-SPECIFIC and not a blanket original-sleeve album credit. Do not extrapolate production credit from songwriter/arranger credits elsewhere.

**All seven genre entries, not merely hits:** `eurodisco` is official album classification and `disco` a dance-pop historical descriptor. Specialist music criticism explicitly identifies **funk** in *Sono nera*, a genuine **tango** in *Tango* and **schlager** echoes in that same recording. It characterises *Tanti auguri* as a pop–Eurodisco–Schlager–**showtunes** fusion. It does not follow that *every* cut is a tango or funk number. *Luca* is a relationship story with a gay character, and *Tanti auguri* has sexual-freedom themes; avoid homogenizing the entire lyric book as uncomplicated party fare. One specialist says 'Sono nera' is from 1979; both the original 1978 sleeve and official track list establish it on the 1978 LP.

**Scales and voices:** energy=4, tempo=4, groove=4: official Eurodisco classification plus the evident dance-oriented *Tanti auguri*, *Luca* and *Black Cat* context. Mood=4 rather than 5 because *Sono nera* and *Luca* introduce more uneasy storylines. theatrical=4: two TV show themes and revue hooks, without implying every track has arena-scale staging. Experimental=1, polish=4: accessible CBS studio construction under documented studio/engineer setup. `electronic` blank: Eurodisco is NOT proof of a synthesizer percentage. `clear` lead voice is a modest inferred tag, not a sourced timbral quote. Lyrics `sensuality|love|party|storytelling|heartbreak|identity` aggregate several distinct tracks and need second-review check. Italian and English confirmed for original Italian LP; the *Hay que venir al sur* Spanish re-recordings are **not** this original edition (`es` is therefore excluded). No documented model-artist influence; `influenced_by` blank. No defensible album-specific artist-comparison data; `sounds_like` blank. Landmark=1 for defining songs.

## `Applauso` (1979) — `carra-applauso`

**Object:** CBS 83687, original Italian LP released April 1979, 10 tracks. The original sleeve, available transcribed with liner notes, explicitly says `PRODUCED BY: GIANNI BONCOMPAGNI`; Danilo Vaona, Adelmo Musso and Paolo Ormi were **arrangers/conductors**; Gaetano Vituzzi and Carlo Assalini engineers/remixers. Do not insert arrangers, remixers or Claudio Simonetti's emergency remakes as co-producers. Rai's 2021 retrospective attributes arrangements to Franco Bracardi instead. Original LP sleeve gives three different arranger names; document the discrepancy, privileging original printed credits.

**Genres:** *Applauso* is not uniformly a dance LP. The **official site explicitly identifies ballads** *Torna da me* and *Riproviamoci*, supporting the `ballad` value alongside mainstream `pop`. *E salutala per me* is described in Carrà's own biography as a story of confronting partner infidelity. In a specialised 2024 history, *Joggin'* is called a largely **instrumental electro/Italo** track, with vocal breath rather than ordinary sung lead. Preserve the minority `electro` and `italodisco` even though the album's overall tone is more intimate. The latter genre is retrospective and not necessarily equivalent to the synthesizer-driven sound of later 1980s Italo disco. *A parole* is a rearrangement/rewording of *Ma ne sai più di me* (1977), not a separately new composition.

**Scales:** energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3 recognise slower ballads alongside dance/pulse tracks; mood=3 mixes heartbreak and light entertainment; theatrical=3 reflects the relatively intimate delivery. Experimental=2 conservatively credits *Joggin'* as an unusual exception; polish=4 reflects multiple professionally documented studios, remix/mastering workflow and CBS releases. Electronic blank: one electro track cannot determine album-wide electronic-source share. `clear|soft|instrumental`: clearer lead on songs, soft ballad delivery and an instrumental/performance-exception in *Joggin'*. Voice `instrumental` is track-minority; the LP itself is overwhelmingly vocal. `it` is correct for the **original Italian version**; `Canta en Español` contains altered Spanish-language recordings and substitutions, and must not be conflated. Landmark=0 because not normally Carrà's defining record, though *E salutala per me* is notable. Influence and sonic-comparison fields blank unless a strong named link is found.

## `Mi spendo tutto` (1980) — `carra-mispendotutto`

**DATE CONFLICT, DO NOT HIDE:** Rockol and Sony Music officially identify the album with **1980**; national library identifies Sony reissues with original ℗1980 and an Estatica LP listing says CBS 84325 (1980). **However the Carrà official discography entry for original CBS 84325 explicitly dates its *Italian physical edition* 19 January 1981 and describes publication in 1981.** The selected `year=1980` follows original-copyright year, the year *Pedro* was released, and Sony original-album chronology; 1981 on some discography editions may reflect the Italian release. Importers must preserve this caveat and not silently change to 1981.

**Producers:** Italy's Biblioteca Nazionale Braidense identifies **Gianni Boncompagni** as album record producer and **Paolo Ormi** as arranger. Rockol's substantial 2024 interview with recording engineer **Gaetano Vituzzi** describes the production trio of Boncompagni, Ormi and Franco Bracardi, but their collaborative songwriter/arranger involvement is *not* sufficient to list all three as credited producers. It describes Ormi's handclaps at the beginning of *Pedro* as probably intended to evoke Spain (Vituzzi's recollection is qualified).

**Genres / unusual pieces:** A 2024 specialist calls *Pedro* **Latin funk**; include `latin` and `funk` even though limited to this well-documented track. Retrospective discography calls *Pedro* **Italo disco**; `italodisco` and parent `disco` retained with low grade because exact historical taxonomy may reflect later reissues/remixes. Multiple other arrangements cannot be assigned specific genres merely by their titles (*Life is only rock'n'roll*, *Latino*, *Maria Marì*): the track title alone does not prove a rock-and-roll performance. *Ratataplan* is officially a **sung rendition of a film theme**, a different source context, but not enough to give the full LP `soundtrack` genre or album type. *Mi spendo tutto* and *Io non vivo senza te* are the opening and closing TV themes of *Millemilioni*, respectively. Original LP has 11 tracks. Spanish reworking *Latino* and South American *¡Bárbara!* are alternative editions/market objects, not separate original Italian studio entries in this phase.

**Scales:** energy=4, tempo=4, groove=4, mood=4, theatrical=4 reflect strong novelty-pop/Latin-dance drive plus TV theatrics; not all 11 tracks share the same energy. Experimental=1 and polish=4 are cautious estimates of broadly accessible CBS studio pop with meticulous sound work corroborated by engineer interview. Electronic blank pending documented original instrumental breakdown. Vocals `clear` broad delivery. Italian and English appear on the original LP. No direct artist-specific recorded influences (`influenced_by` blank). The 2024 viral Jaxomy/Agatino Romero remix must **never** be used to score electronic texture of the 1980 album. Landmark=1 due to lasting *Pedro* association.

## `Raffaella Carrà` (1981) — `carra-raffaellacarra1981`

**Reference edition:** original Hispavox S 90.402 Spanish-market LP of 1981, **11 tracks**: *Mamá dame 100 pesetas, Amore amore, Quiero cantar una canción, Oh María, Dame la libertad, Caliente caliente, Adiós amigo, Super rumbas (medley), México eres tú, Noches tropicales, Por tu amor*. Official discography confirms all titles. Specialist *Il Salotto di Raffaella* documents **Danilo Vaona produced side A**, **Paolo Ormi produced side B**, independently confirming split (Warner/Shazam credits Vaona for *Amore amore*). Do not confuse Hispavox 1981 with Sony compilation of the same title nor with the Hispavox *Raffaella Carrà '82*. Original Italian-language recordings were issued in some countries in 1981; a digital Italian-language version and its first official Italian physical release appeared only in 2025. Original Spanish version should have `language=es`, not Spanish+Italian+English merely because regional editions exist. Argentina's *Yo soy tana* is a regional extra and should not drive the 11-track base aggregate.

**Genres:** Specialist describes side A as international `pop`, side B as `latinpop` dance. The official tracklist *Super rumbas* explicitly demonstrates a `rumba` medley; this is a musical FORM in one track and must remain visible, with the caveat that the title is not a full rhythm transcription. Qualified specialist describes **rock and new wave** touches on part of the LP; those are kept as minority genres alongside Latin and pop, rather than claiming complete genre migration. Secondary album metadata tags `italodisco` but the specialist stresses *partial abandonment* of preceding Eurodisco: keep retrospective descriptor low-strength, warn against over-weighting it. The two claims can coexist because they describe different songs or classification eras. `electronic` blank because style descriptions do not identify how many tracks used electronic versus acoustic instrumentation.

**Scales:** energy=4, tempo=4, groove=4 (specialist says upbeat, rhythm-led); mood=4 despite moments of longing; theatrical=4 due to extrovert Hispanic-pop delivery; experimental=2 because of partial new-wave/rock stylistic turns inside accessible melodies; polish=4 because Hispavox multi-producer professional studio context. `clear` lead provisional; themes `love|sensuality|party|nostalgia` refer to the specialist's examples and do not assign every song a theme. landmark=0 (1981 notable international chapter, but its landmark status relative to *Fiesta*/*Raffaella* 1978 and 1982 *Ballo ballo* requires caution). `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` blank: production collaboration does not establish influence.
