# Nena — Fandome phase 01: evidence notes

**Scope:** The four earliest chronologically issued original studio album releases under the Nena name, including the independently issued 1985 English adaptation. The original German final-band LP *Eisbrecher* (1986) is saved for phase 02. No live albums or compilations. This phase contains no records from Nena's pre-Nena group The Stripes. The singer's solo career (from 1989) will be treated in subsequent phases under the same proposed Fandome artist room unless Claude finds a project-specific rule requiring separation.

**Metadata:** Proposed artist ID `nena` is absent from `catalog.ts` and `seed.ts` on `descriptors` when checked 28 Oct 2026; there is no `docs/descriptors/nena.csv`. All rows are `draft`, not second-person-reviewed. Whole-number 0–5 scales and landmark flags are transparent editorial ratings, not review-site scores. `influenced_by` remains blank in every row: Nena's 2016 interview documents formative musical listening, but never says which of these four *specific albums* those acts shaped.

## Nena (1983) — `nena-nena`

- **Release / personnel / recording:** [official Nena album page](https://www.nena.de/alben-ansicht/nena) lists Reinhold Heil and Manne Praeker as *both* producers, Uwe Fahrenkrog-Petersen keyboards, Carlo Karges guitar, Jürgen Dehmel bass and Rolf Brendel drums; the album was recorded at Spliff Studio Berlin, mixed separately by Udo Arndt. Its page says **17 January 1983**, versus **14 January** in the [German charts discography](https://germancharts.de/showinterpret.asp?interpret=Nena&order=year). CSV records the undisputed **1983**, no disputed precise date.
- **Genres / arrangement:** [AllMusic reviewer Alan Severa](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nena-mw0000545839) associates it with Neue Deutsche Welle but stresses power-pop-styled rock band playing driven by prominent synthesizers, not necessarily the stripped-back avant-garde of earlier NDW; dance-pop and new-wave are explicit taxonomy labels. A [discography overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nena_%28album%29) labels the album NDW, pop rock and new wave. Synth-pop is conservatively **last**, a secondary layer in an otherwise guitar/drum/synth-band album, not a replacement for new wave.
- **Voice / meaning / ratings:** Nena's forceful yet clear performance contrasts playful pop, romance and 99 Luftballons's catastrophic military escalation satire, supporting political/social with other subjects. Severa hears similarity to **Connie Francis** in vocal timbre: `sounds_like`, not influence. Numbers emphasize melodic intensity and danceability without pretending electronic instruments replace bass/guitar/drums. **Landmark=1** for breakout and 99 Luftballons. No production-machine token is asserted, because official credits name keyboards but no synthesizer model and do not document a particular mixing effect.

## ? (Fragezeichen) (1984) — `nena-fragezeichen`

- **Credit and instruments:** [official original LP](https://www.nena.de/alben-ansicht/fragezeichen) dates 27 January 1984 and names Heil + Praeker as co-producers. The same five players supply keyboard/guitar/bass/drums/voice. **David Sanborn's saxophone appears specifically on the title song and Lass mich dein Pirat sein**. It belongs on the album's instrument list but must **not** be taken as evidence for sax on every track. No named 1984 drum machine or synth model is documented.
- **Sound:** [Alan Severa's AllMusic review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/-fragezeichen-mw0000460370) finds the second album significantly softer, more romantic, with yearning vocal tone and *70s Baroque rock* melodic qualities. `baroquerock` is accordingly a **new precise source-named token**, marked as a *textural* (not dominant) link; **do not silently substitute `baroquepop`**. Dance-pop and new-wave are AllMusic style terms; `neuedeutschewelle` is a secondary period/scene label. Less aggressive energy, darker mood and gentler delivery than the 1983 debut support separate scores.
- **Language:** Most songs are German but **Sois Bienvenu** has a French title, but the sung language was not independently verified; the row stays `de`, and Claude should check the original 1984 track before adding `fr`.
- **Landmark=1** as a significant German no.1 follow-up and critically singled-out softer album. No named `sounds_like` artist is forced from Severa's historical baroque-rock style comparison.

## Feuer und Flamme (1985) — `nena-feuerundflamme`

- **Producer and studio technology:** [official 1985 original track-by-track credits](https://www.nena.de/alben-ansicht/feuer-und-flamme) name **Reinhold Heil as sole credited album producer**, not Manne Praeker. Sources name *digital mixing* by Stuart Bruce and Heil and **digital editing** by Siegbert Ernst, which justifies `digitalediting`, **not** `digitalrecording` or `digitaltape`. No brand/model of sampler or drum machine is asserted.
- **Rich original instrumentation:** official credits include live band, synth bass on Ein Brief and Es wird schon weitergehn, *acoustic* guitars, guest percussion Frank Ricotti, strings from the Deutsche Oper Berlin, and David Sanborn sax. The documentation explicitly credits horn arrangements on Utopia and string arrangements on several songs; `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` denotes real documented track techniques, not an orchestral album throughout. Nena / Lisa Dalbello / several other credited singers provide backing; no undocumented voice processing or named harmony device is claimed. Dehmel's `The Stick` appearance is retained in notes, not normalized to a certain Chapman Stick instrument without further liner verification.
- **Critical interpretation:** [AllMusic's Alan Severa](https://www.allmusic.com/album/feuer-und-flamme-mw0000740599) hears a more calculated and mechanical sound than on the first two LPs, although the original record remains a guitar/keyboard pop-rock group album. The English counterpart made from the same sessions is described by [AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/its-all-in-the-game-mw0000784732) as including arena-rock, hard-rock tracks and a saxophone-led power ballad: these appear in **long-tail genre** positions and are not claimed as whole-album identities.
- **Release conflict:** Nena's official site says **28 June 1985**, [chart discography](https://germancharts.de/showinterpret.asp?interpret=Nena&order=year) says **24 June**. Album CSV retains only 1985. **Landmark=1** primarily for Irgendwie Irgendwo Irgendwann and its importance in the group's career.

## It's All in the Game (1985) — `nena-itsallinthegame`

- **Why included:** [Nena's official album listing](https://www.nena.de/alben-ansicht/its-all-in-the-game) treats this as a distinct 10 November 1985 album of English-language reinterpretations of *Feuer und Flamme*. Lisa Dalbello supplied English lyric adaptations. [MusicBrainz track-level relationships](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9a3b3941-804e-4cb0-8591-470529774edb) link the English recordings to the German compositions and repeat the credited instrumentation. It is **not** the 1984 *99 Luftballons* international compilation. The 2008 bonus tracks (including 99 Red Balloons mix) are *not* assigned to original 1985 material.
- **Timbre and separate score:** Most arrangements derive from the same recording sessions as the German LP, so the electronic/energy/mood/arrangement scores remain identical, as do the core instruments and production. English language dominates; `Auf Wiedersehen` is a German phrase used as a title but is not evidence of a German sung track, so the row conservatively uses `en` only. Claude may add `de` only if original sung lyrics are verified.
- **Genres / comparisons:** [Erlewine's professional review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/its-all-in-the-game-mw0000784732) explicitly describes synth-heavy arena-rock moments, hard-rock songs and a sax-led power ballad; its labels also include dance-pop/new-wave. He compares *Woman on Fire* to Pat Benatar and Stevie Nicks: those go under **`sounds_like`**, never influenced_by. Lisa Dalbello is a translator/backing singer, **not album producer or influence**. The [official credits](https://www.nena.de/alben-ansicht/its-all-in-the-game) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9a3b3941-804e-4cb0-8591-470529774edb) confirm **Reinhold Heil** as only album producer.
- **Commercial reception / meaning:** A contemporary international targeting approach, but success of the earlier German single should not be imputed to the new LP; **landmark=0** (comparison of career-defining *Nena* and German album). The global scores reflect sound, not chart position. The site gives **10 November 1985** while chart aggregators sometimes list an undifferentiated June release for both versions.
