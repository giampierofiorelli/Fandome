# Ennio Morricone — phase 01: evidence notes

**Evidence policy:** album-year means earliest demonstrable album-format issue. We score the 1960s album programming, not modern expanded score releases. Scales are transparent editorial estimates mapped onto the descriptor-guide anchors. If no trustworthy album-specific account exists, keep fields blank. The genre source matrix differentiates whole-record style from individual tracks or textural analogies. Sources are resolved to working article / record-pages in the companion CSV.

## 1. `morricone-perunpugnodidollari` — Per un pugno di dollari (album 1966; film 1964)

- **Release history and identity:** Beat Records explicitly states soundtrack first published in a subsequent year and enumerates its seven original cues; 1964 film premiere and 1964 RCA single are not automatically an LP. The first securely identified 12-inch album is **RCA PML 10414 (Italy, 1966)**, combined with *Per qualche dollaro in più*. We isolate the seven Fistful tracks so no Few data leak into this row. Beat's 1965 description is a material competing date: see CLAUDE_IMPORT.md.
- **Genres:** `spaghettiwestern` stylistic idiom is named by the score label and contextualized by BFI; `rock` in the twanging electric guitar title material described by contemporary interviews; `folk` relates to a tune repurposed from Morricone's earlier arrangement of Woody Guthrie as performed by Peter Tevis, not an attribution of Guthrie as Morricone's album-level influence. `avantgarde` refers to experimental whistle / bell / whip / group-voice combination, a textural judgment rather than a uniform album-wide style. `soundtrack` is supplementary format information.
- **Instruments / voice:** electric guitar, acoustic guitar, trumpet, whistling and chorus are confirmed by BFI, Morricone's Independent interview and Beat notes. Percussion, flute and bells are also documented by Rolling Stone and MusicWeb International for the actual Fistful cues. `whistle` is treated as a dedicated instrument token because Alessandroni's performance is central. We have excluded piano, timpani and strings where independent album-specific support was insufficient.
- **Production:** empty; arranging a soundtrack's sound effects is not proof that any specific recording machine, tape technique or production treatment appears on original LP. No named recording producer confirmed.
- **Scales:** energy=3 and tempo=3 reflect galloping but mixed slow cues; theatrical=4 dramatic leitmotifs; experimental=4 unusual timbral additions; electronic=1 electric guitar within acoustic/orchestral instrumentation (not synthesis); mood=2 tense dry-western drama; polish=3 normal studio recording; groove=2 motoric rhythms without a dance-oriented beat. These are not measurements. Lyrics and language blank: chanting has no established semantic words on these seven cues.
- **Comparisons / influence:** BFI likens trumpet finale to Dimitri Tiomkin's *Rio Bravo* music => `sounds_like=Dimitri Tiomkin` only. Woody Guthrie source music and Peter Tevis earlier performance are **not** declared artist influences.

## 2. `morricone-perqualchedollaroinpiu` — Per qualche dollaro in più (album 1966; film 1965)

- **Release and tracks:** Original 1966 RCA Italian LP **PML 10414 side A** comprises eight cuts, as verified by SoundtrackCollector, Beat Records and its explicit A-side original-track listing. Modern 60th anniversary release adds archival alternate cues: original LP only is described here.
- **Instruments:** Beat's reissue gives performer credits for Alessandro Alessandroni whistling, Nino Culasso trumpet, Bruno Battisti D'Amario acoustic and electric guitars, Franco Tamponi violin, Salvatore Schillirò Jew’s harp, Bruno Nicolai organ, Cantori Moderni choir and Edda Dell'Orso vocalise. BFI independently identifies timpani, jaw harp, recorder and player-piano timbre, as well as guitars/whistle. The carillon is a *musicbox* / mechanistic tuned-box motif on the original eight cues, not proof of any electronic sequencer or drum machine. `piano` denotes the honky-tonk/keyboard part, no specific keyboard model claimed. Voice is instrumental-plus-choral wordless performance.
- **Genres / values:** western score with rock-guitar impact and a mechanically repeated carillon. No `folk` genre is assigned solely from traditional acoustic instruments. Darker recurring duel/revenge themes motivate mood=2, theatrical=4; moderate pacing and percussion motivate energy/tempo=3. Non-dance score groove=2; electronic=1 from documented electric guitar (not studio electronics); polish=3 from full orchestrated session scores; experimental=3 from diegetic music box paired with whistles and jaw harp.
- **Production/lyrics/producer/influences:** abstain. Morricone the composer and Bruno Nicolai the conductor are not independently verified as the phonogram's producer. Bach references are musical *allusions*, not demonstrable album-level `influenced_by` artist entries.

## 3. `morricone-labattagliadialgeri` — La battaglia di Algeri (1966)

- **Original release / joint credit:** 1966-matrix **RCA SP 8019** vinyl LP, documented by the specialist Library Music Themes RCA discography; the sleeve says 'Edizione fuori commercio' (promotional/not for commercial sale). A dated, generally distributed retail LP is not independently established. The 1966 Biennale Venezia primary credit lists music by **Ennio Morricone and Gillo Pontecorvo**, corroborated by Criterion's note on the director's contribution of themes. Credit the collaboration explicitly in import instructions; do not classify film producers as record music producers.
- **Instrument evidence:** the French military theme relies on militaristic **snare**, rolling **piano**, **brass/trumpet** and **woodwinds** (Criterion, BFI, Movie Music UK); Ali's lament centers on **pan flute**; North African **percussion** and **organ** are also documented (Criterion, BAMPFA). `snaredrum` and `panflute` are distinct newly declared tokens, not inferred generically from a film's setting. Berkeley mentions choral passages, so voice includes `choral` after `instrumental`. Orchestra specified by Criterion. `percussion` includes Algerian hand drums; neither model nor particular hand drum was named.
- **Genres:** `march` is confined mainly to the French troops motif; `musiqueconcrete` explicitly used by MoMA for the *film's full soundworld* including war effects and machinery, so keep marked **texture** and conditional on a user's desire to recommend by film sound design vs the original pressed LP. `soundtrack` simply describes record use. Do not invent 'Arabic/Algerian folk' genre because regional percussion alone does not establish a named genre.
- **Scales:** mood=0 sombre/harrowing; energy=3 / theatrical=4 militaristic urgency with elegiac contrast; experimental=4 stark found-sound-like design (score/film distinction caveat); tempo=3 mixed march and lament; groove=1 insistent snare, not a dancefloor pulse. electronic=0 predominantly acoustic score instrumentation as described, **not** a claim the film mix has no manipulated sound effects; polish left blank lacking original-LP production data.
- **Production:** blank. Director's manipulated effects for film soundtrack not automatically `sonicmanipulation` or `fieldrecordings` on the 1966 LP. Lyrics/languages blank; soundtrack does not inherit spoken Arabic/French dialogue or political plot as sung lyrics.

## 4. `morricone-ilbuonoilbruttoilcattivo` — Il buono, il brutto, il cattivo (1966)

- **Identity:** existing catalog entry is exactly `morricone-ilbuonoilbruttoilcattivo`. Italian **Parade EPL 2890(S)** original 11-track album from December 1966. Do not count reissue bonus 2004/2020 recordings.
- **Genres:** `spaghettiwestern` music idiom whole work; `contemporaryclassical` is explicitly named by reviewer Nate Patrin at The SHFL (broader `classical` included alongside it); album theme has a specifically described `rockandroll` surge (Apple Music), contrasting the explicitly reviewed folk-opera soldier song and martial passages. Original LP contains *Marcetta* and *Marcetta senza speranza*, hence track-only `march`. No claim all tracks are danceable or rock.
- **Voice / instruments / production:** BFI and AllMusic detail ocarina, whistles, coyote imitative singers, trumpet, solo soprano Edda Dell'Orso, orchestral strings/horns, guitars, harmonica, piano, percussion and castanets. Direct first-person Morricone account in OUP describes **two separately recorded male voices** to build the title's coyote cry, one incorporating **falsetto**, with **light** reverb, so `layeredvocals|falsetto` is firmly documented. He **wrote distorted electric guitar for Ecstasy of Gold** and added synthesized sounds, hence `distortion`, `electricguitar`, `synth`. No machine or analog/digital model identified. Do not add `heavyreverb` because light reverberation does not meet its meaning. `falsetto` is production, not an allowed voice enum.
- **Critics' disagreement:** BFI identifies an *ocarina* for the title's coyote cry; other historical score analysis gives a specially built `arghilofono` for **Angel Eyes's** motif. These are **different roles** and can coexist; Treccani separately describes the coyote imitation as clarinet-led, a further conflicting description. Exact `arghilofono` performer/instrument status was insufficiently secure for an album-level token. `ocarina` is independently attested. Likewise a reviewer refers to 'prepared piano' as timbre but there is no permitted specific token for it and it is not silently remapped into a model-specific production device.
- **Lyrics / language:** Unlike other scores here, the original **11-track** LP explicitly includes *La storia di un soldato* with **English** words by **Tommie Connor**, performed by Cantori Moderni, verified by MusicBrainz plus lyric archival evidence. Thus `lyrics=storytelling` and `language=en` describe **that one song**; majority is wordless.
- **Scales:** energy=4 / theatrical=5 orchestral crescendos; experimental=4 coyote imitations/electronics; electronic=1 electric and synthesized elements within largely acoustic writing; polish=4 multi-layered orchestra and voices; mood=2 dark/tragic with cathartic intervals; tempo=3 fluctuates among march, adagio and sprint; groove=2 martial/galloping accents but not club structure.
- **Producer / influence policy:** no contemporaneous original-Parade LP *record producer* attribution substantiated; leave empty even if later digital reissues list Morricone as producer. Edda Dell'Orso, Alessandro Alessandroni and Nicolai are collaborators, not documented musical influences. Artist-wide inspirations live in CLAUDE_IMPORT.md only.

## Additional reviewer flags

- Every descriptor value is provisional `draft`. Album-specific evidence is stronger for *For a Few Dollars More* and *Good, Bad, Ugly*, and thinner for the Fistful 1966 split-LP framing and film-vs-LP *Battle of Algiers* genre.
- Missing named musician ≠ absence of an instrument. Missing production tags ≠ no production effects. Empty `producers` ≠ a claim no one produced the record.
- Review genre inclusion for Fistful `folk` / `avantgarde` against the user's stringent **source-named genre** policy; if unpersuasive, remove them, rather than overclaim. Where genre provenance is judged insufficient the genre evidence file explicitly labels track/texture-level scope.
