# Phase 05 evidence notes — Ennio Morricone (original-LP programme only)

**Method.** One descriptor per *released LP*, not per film or later deluxe reissue. This phase uses the user-specified eight 0–5 anchors; they are *editorial readings of critics' descriptions*, not measured audio features. Genre tokens reflect source language, with `soundtrack` last as a format token. Instrument and machine entries have album-specific evidence. Empty `producers` does not mean Morricone was not involved: composing/orchestrating/conducting are different credits from an album-production credit. All albums are primarily instrumental; no thematic `lyrics` or `language` values have been invented from film dialogue.

## La classe operaia va in paradiso — 1971
- **Edition:** limited promotional 1971 Italian RCA LP SP 8038 (eight cues, November 1971 per SoundtrackCollector). Later French RCA 1982 LP, 2009 GDM expansion, Quartet 2025 remaster are distinct editions; no new tracks used here. <https://www.prestomusic.com/classical/products/9782697--la-classe-operaia-va-in-paradi>
- **Style, genre:** label reissue identifies avant-garde, musique concrète and a specifically **slo-funk** treatment on *Metamorfosi*, as well as classical orchestration; composers call the main piece a *march*. `slofunk` is new token; `musiqueconcrete` is a source's *style categorization*, not an assertion of actual tape splicing or environmental recordings. <https://www.soundohm.com/product/la-classe-operaia-va-in-p> <https://pdfcoffee.com/composing-for-the-cinema-4-pdf-free.html>
- **Instruments and production:** Quartet lists guitar, solo violin and piano; Morricone and musicologist Sergio Miceli explicitly discuss a **trombone** opposed to a violin cadenza, and Morricone says he generated the metallic noises of welding and machinery using the **Sinket synthesizer**, designed by Paolo Ketoff (*Composing for the Cinema*, 2013, p. 197). `sinket` is thus a *documented new machine token*, and `synth` a documented instrument. Fuzz distortion and wah-wah guitar are directly stated by Goodfellas/Soundohm, supporting `distortion|wahwah`. **Do not mark `fieldrecordings` or `sampling`: what resembled machine recordings was synthesized.** Source book's bibliographic confirmation: <https://www.bloomsbury.com/uk/composing-for-the-cinema-9780810892415/>; searchable excerpt: <https://pdfcoffee.com/composing-for-the-cinema-4-pdf-free.html>.
- **Scores:** energy 4 and tempo 4: forceful industrial march/pounding parts; electronic 2: acoustic ensemble plus *specific* synthetic noises; mood 1: ominous and alienating; theatrical 4: exaggerated/comic abrasive gestures; experimental 4: tonal/orchestral material against industrial synthesis and avant-garde funk; polish 3: documented studio scoring but no claim of state-of-the-art mixing; groove 2: localized funky passages, not a dance album. These are whole-record estimates, not the single *Metamorfosi*.
- **Voice/lyrics/language:** instrumental original cues; no verified lead singing or narrative lyrics. `sounds_like` = Gruppo d'Improvvisazione Nuova Consonanza only as **reviewer sonic comparison**, not an artist influence; Morricone's membership is not evidence of an album-specific external influence. `influenced_by` blank. **Landmark 1** for defining experimental/social-cinema score; John Zorn's later interpretation is downstream reception, not an influence on Morricone.
- **Credits:** Bruno Nicolai or other conductor not promoted to producer; no documentary producer credit for *this LP*, so `producers` blank.

## Questa specie d’amore — 1972
- **Edition:** original Italian **General Music ZSLGE 55077, nine-track LP March 1972**, separately from the two-track 45rpm and later ScreenTrax/Verita Note or 2016 expanded LP. Earlier phase's provisional *1973* first-LP claim is **corrected** by the original 1972 pressing and original discographical listing. <https://ssl.soundtrackcollector.com/title/10352/Questa%2BSpecie%2BD%27Amore> <https://www.dessineeshop.com/view/item/000000028959>
- **Genres:** `classical` as a cautious broad *orchestral score* family grounded in critics' string-led acoustic score description and classical/baroque structures; `soundtrack` is format. The baroque quality of the specific *Roma baldracca* cue is **not** enough to call the entire score `baroquepop`. Do not use a style attributed generically to Morricone or to unrelated pieces. <https://www.movie-wave.net/questa-specie-damore/>
- **Instruments:** string orchestra, piano and specifically solo **oboe** described in main theme by James Southall. *Film Music Journal* also describes a **bassoon** solo on *Federico e la sua solitudine*. `bassoon` is supported by the latter, but the unavailable large PDF warrants confidence medium; an “English horn” mentioned there is omitted pending direct credits. <https://filmmusicjournal.ch/storage/2022/02/FMJ-26-27c-wz.pdf>
- **Scores:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 0 from largely measured/unhurried lyrical themes; electronic 0 because the album-specific descriptions name acoustic instruments, not electronics; mood 2 because regret and melancholy are recurring despite a brighter dance-like cue; theatrical 4 from large swelling melodies; experimental 1 from conventionally tonal/romantic structures; polish 4 from heavily shaped orchestral timbres. Numbers are editorial, not source-published measurements.
- **Voice/lyrics/language:** no lead voice or sung language verified on this original album; `instrumental`; lyric/language blank. No independently verified production techniques/machines or **LP producers**; those fields empty. `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` blank. Critics' comparison of a Morricone theme to another Morricone score is not another-artist attribution. `landmark` 0 is a relative editorial selection and does not mean poor quality; Southall rated it 5 stars.
- **Credits caution:** conductor Bruno Nicolai (45rpm) does not imply he produced the LP. Do not copy producer credit from other Morricone records.

## Il diavolo nel cervello — 1972
- **Edition:** General Music **ZSLGE 55076**, **April 1972**, *ten* original stereo-master tracks chosen by Morricone. Expanded versions are later and longer. <https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/8375/Diavolo%2BNel%2BCervello%2C%2BIl> <https://www.beatrecords.it/it/catalogo/3440/il-diavolo-nel-cervello>
- **Genres:** AMS calls the foundation almost entirely **symphonic**; `classical` is the broad catalogue family. Beat explicitly describes **pop** treatment of the main theme and the travel motif. AMS identifies brushed **jazz** drums as a rhythmic detail; `jazz` appears after pop because its scope is **texture**, not the dominant score. No claim that a fictional giallo film equals a music genre `giallo` by itself.
- **Instruments:** label liner descriptions specifically identify **strings, piano, harpsichord, flute, brass, brushed jazz drums**; symphonic ensemble included. Edda Dell’Orso supplies wordless soprano/choral vocalism and Bruno Nicolai **conducts**; neither is an LP producer by these roles. Voice `instrumental|choral`; lyrics and language empty. <https://ams-music.it/wp/il-diavolo-nel-cervello/>
- **Non-obvious harmonic treatment:** Beat describes dissonant strings on *Viaggio primo*, and a Beethoven *Für Elise* quotation in a suspense theme. Philip Brophy describes the source-motif transformation as proto-spectralism. This is **formal composition**, not proof of sampling, tape editing or synthesizers. Thus `production` intentionally empty. A quotation of Beethoven is **not** a verified influential artist and `influenced_by` remains blank. <https://www.philipbrophy.com/projects/tracesofsoundtracks/02.html>
- **Scores:** energy 2 / tempo 2 for controlled pulse, intermittent brisker passages; electronic 0 for mostly confirmed acoustic palette; mood 1 for suspicion and melancholy; theatrical 4 for heightened string dissonance and psychological drama; experimental 3 for Beethoven/harmonic recombination; polish 4 from articulated studio orchestration; groove 1 for brushed drums in isolated passages.
- **Credits, comparison:** composer and orchestrator Morricone; conductor Nicolai and singer Dell’Orso not credited production. Unknown LP producers and album-specific external artist influences remain blank; `landmark=0` despite critical regard, reserved for a small artist-wide canonical subset.

## La cosa buffa — 1972
- **Edition:** original Italian **Cinevox MDF 33/60**, **December 1972**, *nine* tracks. Label matrix notation ℗ 11/72 is consistent but does **not** establish November retail release; December is the referenced discographic listing. The 2004 Cinevox expanded CD has *25 tracks*; the 2024 yellow LP adds Ballabile variants. Treat original 9-track work as canonical. <https://ssl.soundtrackcollector.com/title/5675/Cosa%2BBuffa%2C%2BLa> <https://musicbase.net/la-cosa-buffa>
- **Genres:** James Southall describes the main theme as gentle **pop** with strings; “Indecisione con Maria” is clearly a **jazzier variation** on the original 9 tracks. `jazz` follows `pop`; soundtrack = format. **Do not include `beat` on this original LP** merely because the *Ballabile N.1/N.2* additions to later editions are called ‘60s beat by the 2024 reissue label. Similarly a second critic's ‘two rock cues’ may concern the expanded release; keep `rock` out pending original track evidence. <https://www.movie-wave.net/la-cosa-buffa/> <https://ams-music.it/wp/ennio-morricone-la-cosa-buffa-ost-vinile-giallo/?lang=en>
- **Instruments:** AMS confirms strings/harpsichord; Southall's explicit album-cue analysis names **solo piano, bass guitar, drums** on *Pensando a Maria*, **accordion** on *Ritorno a casa*, and Edda Dell’Orso's wordless singing, with I Cantori Moderni on the long *Catalogo incompleto* finale. The label's *Ballabile* instrument/treatment details are not imported. `instrumental|soft|choral` reflects instrumental underscore plus tender wordless soprano and identifiable choir. `lyrics` / `language` intentionally blank.
- **Scores:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 1: mainly balladic/relaxed; electronic 0: documented palette acoustic, no album-specific electronics; mood 3: bittersweet romance with happy and ominous variants; theatrical 3: broad strings balanced by intimate variations; experimental 1: tuneful/repetitive approach; polish 4: styled orchestral-pop production. These are own scale judgments.
- **Critical conflict:** James Southall values the melodic theme and its nuanced changes, while Joep de Bruijn regards its many reprises as tiresome. The latter incorrectly dates the 1972 film as 1971; ignore that bibliographic claim. The 25-track expanded edition provokes different judgments than the 9-track original. <https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/la-cosa-buffa/>
- **Production/credits:** no verified album-specific tape/machine/producer credits; `production`/`producers` blank. Nicola Samale appears on original matrix/labels as **orchestra conductor**, not producer. No album-specific noncollaborator musical influences documented. Landmark 0 reflects relative canon selection, not artistic dismissal.

## Whole-phase limitations
- Some discography pages served only through web search archives because the live site was intermittently unavailable; cite the original URL but import curator should re-check when accessible.
- The Fine/High provenance of source statements is **field-specific** even when `confidence` is a single coarse row value. A blank means unverified, not a negative observation.
- New tokens `slofunk`, `sinket`, `wahwah` must be added to genreFamilies, allowed genre list/validator and production list before running validator. No new **instrument** token is required.
- Neither the 2004/2024 editions nor the 2025 remasters are to be classified as historical 1971–72 production techniques (`remastering` intentionally omitted).
