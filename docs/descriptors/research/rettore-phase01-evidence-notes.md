# Donatella Rettore — Phase 01: evidence notes (1974–1980)

Research cutoff: 25 October 2026. Sources were checked against artist discography, licensed remaster credits, specialist album research and artist interviews. All numbers are editorial interpretations against Fandome guide, not measurement. No artist-wide genres assigned blindly.

## Method and strength rubric

- **high**: primary musician/label credits or qualified independent critics explicitly establish the album/track property.
- **medium**: multiple sources agree but track scope is broad or interpretive.
- **low**: a plausible editorial mapping from partial arrangement evidence, not the literal genre wording. Explicitly labelled in genre evidence.
- Album `genres` includes evidence-backed minority styles; `genre_evidence` distinguishes whole album, some tracks and texture. Unverified genre candidates are discussed but not silently asserted.
- Track register below covers every original LP song, but **does not claim each track has a verified sonic analysis**. Distinguishes track existence/lyrical evidence from timbral and genre verification.
- `sources.csv` records what each linked document supports; musicological descriptions are cited directly rather than guessing styles from songwriting, titles or the general reputation of Rettore.

## Ogni giorno si cantano canzoni d'amore (1974) — `rettore-ognigiornosicantanocanzonidamore`

**Scales.** energy=2 / theatrical=2 / experimental=1 reflect early Italian cantautrice and Sanremo repertoire rather than the later disco era; conservatively editorial. `electronic`, `mood`, `polish`, `tempo`, `groove` are blank because no album-wide arrangement measurement or detailed contemporary track review was located. `voice=clear` is a tentative editorial listening descriptor; do not read it as technical singing testimony.
**Year dispute:** official artist site and AllMusic date the first album **1974**, while Rockol and HitParadeItalia list **1975**. A secondary discographical account reports Dec 1974 physical pressing and Feb 1975 wider issue. Because guide says original date, draft uses 1974; importer can amend if pressing/retail evidence establishes another original-publication convention. Original single dates do not determine LP publication.
**Production:** "Sviluppo Discografico del Mezzogiorno" is shown as production company; F. Valgrande and Mario Pagano are credited for arrangements or composition, not named individual record producers. Leave producer blank.
**Influences and resemblance:** artist site explicitly likens debut to Luigi Tenco and Gino Paoli, so include them under `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`. Gino Paoli's writing credit on one song is neither an album production credit nor proof of direct influence.
**Potential tango:** a song called `Il tango della cantante` exists; naming a dance does not prove its recorded groove is a tango. The token was intentionally not added; inspect original audio or a specialist analysis before promoting.

**Genre evidence summary:**

- `songwriter` — whole album; high; Artist bio and critic explicitly describe early singer-songwriter school; socially committed compositions (tracks: Maria Sole|17 gennaio 74 sera).
- `poprock` — whole album; medium; Album entry categorizes pop-rock; no assumption of uniformly rock arrangements.
- `pop` — whole album; medium; Pop is included in album-specific genre metadata.
- `musicaleggera` — some tracks; low; Editorial context: Sanremo and conventional Italian song; no source uses this exact token for LP (tracks: Capelli sciolti).

## Donatella Rettore (1977) — `rettore-donatellarettore`

**Scales.** The stronger rhythmic emphasis and Percussion by Tullio De Piscopo justify energy 3 and a cautious groove 2. Natale Massara orchestration supports ordinary studio polish 3 and stage presentation 3. Tempo and electronic remain blank: no verified album-level speed or synthesis. The 1977 sound is *not* assumed to contain disco because disco defines her later fame.
**Social lyrics:** political narrative of `Carmela` (Spanish revolution), patriarchal critique in `Il patriarca`, abuse/sexual harassment in `Caro preside` documented in the OndaRock interviewer’s question; official biography supports socially committed writing. Source descriptions vary between singer-songwriter, pop, light rock and almost-folk passages. Preserve these as separate tags and scope rather than forcing one homogeneous genre.
**Personnel:** Artist site's 2022 reissue text and HitParadeItalia explicitly identify Roberto Danè as producer, Natale Massara arranger/orchestra director and Tullio De Piscopo on drums and percussion. No producer credit for Claudio Rego simply because he composed.
**Single caveat:** `Lailolà` was released as a single but **not** on this album; `La Berta` is. Do not fold the huge international Lailolà success into this LP's composition or score.

**Genre evidence summary:**

- `songwriter` — whole album; high; Rettore/Rego author-led singer-songwriter record addressing social power relations (tracks: Carmela|Il patriarca|Caro preside).
- `poprock` — whole album; medium; Contemporary-pop/rock hybrid in album genre descriptions and rhythm-oriented arrangement.
- `rock` — whole album; medium; Album-specific classification Rock; not confused with later heavier albums.
- `pop` — whole album; medium; Album-specific classification Pop.
- `folk` — texture; low; Source cautiously says at times almost folk; uncertain which specific passages.
- `orchestralpop` — texture; low; Orchestra directed and arranged by Natale Massara; editorial texture classification not named formal genre.

## Brivido divino (1979) — `rettore-brividodivino`

**Scales.** Energy 4 and groove 4 follow the pronounced pop-rock/disco crossover reported by OndaRock and HitParadeItalia. The ARP/synth credits plus prominent guitars, piano, Fender Rhodes, saxophone, live drums and percussion support electronic only 2. Theatrical 4 and polish 4 are provisional judgements of the Pirazzoli-era studio approach and performance; experimental 2 due to lyrics/concepts more provocative than the catchy structure. Mixed lyrical tone supports mood 3.
**Song-vs-album:** `Splendido splendente` is a disco-influenced pop highlight with gender and aesthetic identity themes; `Eroe` is older rock-era material; `Il mimo` preserves storytelling. Rest of the record is neither proven all disco nor synthesizer-driven. `electronic` is included only as **texture** grounded in credited ARP and synth, not proof of a full electronic album. `dancepop` is an editorial mapping of a subset (low evidence) and should not outrank pop/rock or disco.
**Personnel:** original-era credits and Qobuz's 2023 remaster both explicitly designate Roberto Danè producer and Pinuccio Pirazzoli arranger; HitParadeItalia credits De Piscopo percussion. Qobuz credits Pirazzoli, **not** Rego, as composer of `La mia più bella canzone d'amore`. Site generalizes Rettore/Rego writing; record a song-level exception rather than turning a writing credit into production.
**Edition caveat:** German LP `Eroe` (1979) is an export edition of the same nine-track program, not an additional studio album. Qobuz has per-track ℗ years including 1982 due to licensing: not original composition or album release years.

**Genre evidence summary:**

- `poprock` — whole album; high; Specialist archive describes pop-rock crossover for this album (tracks: Eroe|Splendido splendente).
- `rock` — some tracks; medium; New period described as rock sound and credited electric guitars (tracks: Eroe).
- `pop` — whole album; high; OndaRock album analysis calls its songs pop; licensed metadata concurs.
- `disco` — some tracks; high; OndaRock explicitly says echoes of disco music and archive identifies disco influence (tracks: Splendido splendente).
- `dancepop` — some tracks; low; Editorial intersection of pop hooks and disco dance rhythm; token itself not directly named (tracks: Splendido splendente).
- `electronic` — texture; low; ARP and synthesizers present in lineup; do not imply whole album is electronic genre.

## Magnifico delirio (1980) — `rettore-magnificodelirio`

**Scales.** Energy 4 reflects the self-described harder rock sound; electronic 2 because OndaRock describes added electronic layers but also fully evident orchestrations. Theatrical 5 and experimental 3 derive specifically from `Delirio` effects, quasi-operatic choirs, riotous strings, irony and surreal arrangements. Groove 3, tempo 3 and mood 3 are cautious aggregate editorial assessments rather than proven trackwise measurements.
**Critic disagreement/scope:** OndaRock considers the deeper cuts (`Delirio`, `Gaio`) more interesting than the single, while commercial discographies foreground `Kobra`. Thus album is not represented by dance music alone. `Delirio` has layered treated voices and pseudo-opera choir, but that does NOT establish opera as a genre, nor a classical crossover album. `electronic` and `orchestralpop` are texture-based labels. `disco` and `dancepop` are conservative low-evidence mappings for some dance material, not an album-wide source statement.
**Credits:** Danè is producer per Qobuz full track credits; Pirazzoli is arranger, not co-producer. HitParadeItalia documents Gianfranco Bertagna as a tenor voice on `Delirio`, giving independent footing for the choral/operatic vocal contrast; other choral timbres need not all be the lead singer. Qobuz names **Franco Califano and Maurizio Piccoli** on `Il granchio`, which conflicts with the official website's generalized claim that all material is Rettore/Rego. Preserve track-level digital information and verify original 1980 sleeve before settling split credits.
**Influenced_by:** no direct influence convincingly documented for these 1980 compositions; Rettore's explicit post-punk, new wave and synth-pop listening recollections on the official site refer to her **1982** Kamikaze project, not to 1980, and are deliberately not imported here.

**Genre evidence summary:**

- `poprock` — whole album; medium; Artist history and music journalism place record in rock-oriented melodic pop (tracks: Le mani|Kobra).
- `rock` — some tracks; high; Official bio describes intense rock sounds and press singles out Le mani (tracks: Le mani).
- `pop` — whole album; high; OndaRock considers album pop/rock and distributor calls its genre Pop.
- `disco` — some tracks; low; Dance-oriented singles and Rettore disco-era language; critic does not independently call all of 1980 LP disco (tracks: Kobra).
- `electronic` — texture; high; Critic explicitly identifies electronic layers within some tracks, not full electronic production (tracks: Delirio).
- `orchestralpop` — texture; medium; OndaRock describes daring orchestral arrangements and string writing in Delirio (tracks: Delirio).
- `dancepop` — some tracks; low; Editorial pop/dance intersection, primarily Kobra; not a label applied to every track (tracks: Kobra).
