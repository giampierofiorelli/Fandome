# Kraftwerk — Phase 01 evidence notes

Research date: 2026-10-25. The Fandome descriptor guide, `catalog.ts`, `Genre` list and validator were inspected on the GitHub `descriptors` branch. This package contains only 1970, 1972, 1973 and 1974. Numerical 0–5 descriptors are Fandome editorial judgments based on album-specific sourced audio descriptions and personnel, not source-claimed measurements. `sounds_like` is editorial, not a proven influence. Missing facts stay empty. Genre metadata is explicitly qualified by whole-album / passages / precursor scopes to prevent inflated algorithmic certainty.

## Kraftwerk (1970) — `kraftwerk-kraftwerk`
Original LP has four long instrumental compositions, not synth-pop singles [K70-ORIG, K70-AM]. Ralf Hütter plays Tubon/organ, Florian Schneider flute/violin/electronic percussion; session drummers Andreas Hohmann and Klaus Dinger are on different tracks [K70-ORIG, K70-PROG]. Conny Plank **shares a producer credit** with Kraftwerk on the debut, as distinguished from later albums [K70-ORIG].

**Scores**: energy 3 for bursts of driven drumming within quieter sections; electronic 2 because much source material is acoustic or electroacoustic and tape-altered, *not* predominantly synthesizer-generated [K70-AM, K70-DJ]. Mood 2 for abstract, at times ominous noise, balanced by brighter motorik passages [K70-PROG]. Experimental 4 for extensive improvisation, tape edits and musique concrète. Polish 3: purposeful studio production by Plank, not demonstrably rough or large-budget. Groove 2: rhythmic stretches exist but many passages are non-dance improvisation. Tempo and theatricality unscored due to variation and weak sources.

**Genre dispute**: AllMusic's broad *Club/Dance* filing is reproduced as `dance` **last and low-confidence**, not as an album-wide dance-floor claim; its *art rock* and psychedelic roots are critic-level, distinct from later post-1974 Kraftwerk. The drone and musique concrète tags are retrospective. Experimental electronic is a source genre, not a claim of a fully electronic sound. `voice=instrumental`; leave `language`, `lyrics` and `influenced_by` empty. Comparisons are editorial only.

## Kraftwerk 2 (1972) — `kraftwerk-kraftwerk2`
1972 is the release year; the original six-piece album was recorded in September–October 1971 [K72-ORIG, K72-AM]. Hütter, Schneider and Plank all hold original **producer** credits, despite Plank also acting as engineer [K72-ORIG]. A peer-reviewed music article cites Hütter's 1981 testimony that the second record used a cheap preset rhythm machine transformed by tape echo and filtering [K72-HU]. The album is instrumental, with flute, violin and guitar still important; assigning electronic 5 would project the later band onto it.

**Scores**: energy 2 for sustained low-to-medium experimental movement; electronic 3 for manipulated primitive rhythms and organ/electric instruments but substantial acoustic playing; mood 2 for abstract strange soundscapes interspersed with more playful figures; experimental 4 for severe tape effects and nonstandard structures; polish 3 for professional recorded/mixed work; groove 2 for rhythmic passages including Klingklang but not a club-focused LP. Tempo and theatricality left empty rather than inferring from a single track.

**Genre caution**: AllMusic's Avant-Garde and Pop/Rock are database umbrella labels; encode `avantgarde`, while rock is subordinate, not an acoustic rock LP. `musiqueconcrete` and `ambient` are retrospective and limited to sonic strands [K72-AM, K72-AOTY]. No direct documented album-level influence, so `influenced_by` blank; `sounds_like` references are editorial.

## Ralf und Florian (1973) — `kraftwerk-ralfundflorian`
The German issue is often styled **Ralf & Florian**, while collector discographies also use **Ralf und Florian**. Choose the requested German title for the new catalog tuple and treat variants as the same six-track studio LP; the normalized new ID is `kraftwerk-ralfundflorian` [K73-ORIG, K73-ORIG-ALT]. Recorded May–July 1973, officially produced by Hütter and Schneider; **Plank is credited engineer**, despite some review prose casually describing him as a producer [K73-ORIG, K73-AM].

**Scores**: energy 2 for restrained pulses and long, reflective pieces; electronic 3 because synthesis is prominent but flute, guitar, winds and other played instrumentation are documented; mood 3 because light melodic and eerie experiment coexist; experimental 4 because abstract timbres/extended forms persist; polish 3 given studio craft but not later maximal glossy finish; tempo 2 and groove 2 reflect mostly gentle pieces with isolated more animated sections [K73-ISC, K73-NME]. Theatrical left blank. `voice=instrumental` represents dominant presentation; processed wordless/vocoder utterances are not enough to label lead `spoken` or to assert `language=de` solely from German track names.

**Genre dispute**: `berlinschool` is a stylistic retrospective database tag rather than geographical membership. `synthpop` and `electro` designate **proto**-characteristics on selected tracks, not a completed all-synth pop album [K73-ISC, K73-NME]. **No evidenced influence** on this record sufficient to fill `influenced_by`.

## Autobahn (1974) — `kraftwerk-autobahn`
Use **the five-track 1974 German LP** and its 22-minute title composition, not the ~3-minute 1975 radio single. The second side includes both Kometenmelodie sections, Mitternacht and Morgenspaziergang, which are quieter and more ambient; their existence affects every aggregate score [K74-ORIG, K74-MB, K74-HFN]. The original record credits **Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider** as producers, **Conny Plank as engineer**; no songwriter or performer should be added to producers just for participating [K74-ORIG, K74-PROD]. Wolfgang Flür percussion and Klaus Röder guitar/violin remain relevant live elements.

**Scores**: energy 3 for hypnotic moderate-drive title and less driving B-side; electronic 4 for clearly synthesis-led orchestration with Minimoog and ARP but processed flute, live guitar/violin, and percussion; mood 4 for bright road imagery and melodic ascent with contrasting night piece; theatrical 2 for restrained, detached voices; experimental 3 for sound effects and long-form sequencing balanced by accessible melodic motifs; polish 4 for crafted multi-layered studio work; tempo 3 and groove 3 because motorik pulse occupies much but not all of the LP [K74-AM, K74-HFN, K74-PROD]. Main lead vocal on title is clear/simple sung or chanted German (`clear`); rest is mainly instrumental. `lyrics=storytelling` covers the evocative narrated motor journey, not a romantic or political agenda. `landmark=1` reflects documented major breakthrough [K74-AM, K74-CLASSIC].

**Influence restriction**: critics explicitly hear Beach Boys soundalikes and Wolfgang Flür speaks of a desired positive German analogue; however, Hütter explicitly rejected the claim that the refrain was a direct Beach Boys quotation in both 2009 and 2026 [K74-CLASSIC, K74-HUTTER, K74-HUTTER26]. Thus **The Beach Boys are in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`**. Likewise do not equate a genre-root statement such as 'roots of electro-funk' with the entire album being 1980s electro-funk: that token is included with track/precursor scope, as requested [K74-AM]. `berlinschool` and `postminimalism` are retrospective metadata classifications with low scope confidence [K74-AOTY].
