# Rondò Veneziano — Phase 03: evidence and exception notes

Scope: four **new** original studio albums only (1988–1991). Review is grounded in artist official tracklists, 1988–91 pressings credited in current repositories, original label-derived credits and a small number of album-specific retrospective/period critics. Media databases reuse one another; Wikipedia is used as a secondary route to original sleeve credit, **not** independent primary verification. For these years there are fewer robust signed reviews than for pop/rock records. Blank cells are deliberate abstentions.

## Global source/scale discipline

- `classicalcrossover`, `baroquepop`, `easylistening` and `pop` are kept distinct if **specifically applied to the album**; generic artist classification alone does NOT license a missing genre in an individual row. Therefore *Concerto* contains only the two genres that its album metadata actually names and *Barocco* also includes historic Baroque/Neo-Classical/Classical metadata, ordered after its core baroque-pop.
- `instrumental` applies to the originals; none has reliable album-wide vocal/lyrical evidence. Hence `lyrics` and `language` empty. `influenced_by` also empty: historical composer allusions and 1991 Jarre similarity are not named causal musical influences on individual albums.
- `sounds_like` is a **curatorial timbral/arrangement comparison**, never a historical causal claim. Genre tags describe broad taxonomy; **instrument and production tags NEVER derive from those genres alone**.
- `landmark=1` describes the original 1988–91 run as defining original albums with significant contemporary reach and continuing export incarnations; chart reach does not prove a qualitative score. `confidence=medium` reflects artist/album/edition/producer sources but limited independent listening criticism and the release-reissue entanglement.

## Concerto (1988) — rondoveneziano-concerto

**Original identity.** 1988 Baby Records *Concerto*: 12 tracks on artist official website, including *Concerto*, *Seduzione*, *Sonetto*, *Splendore di Venezia*, *Miniatura*, *Controdanze* and *Chimere*. The export *Poesia di Venezia* is 1988 too, with reordered songs and new *Rêverie* / *Gondole sulla laguna*, but is not a unique original creative album. Official list https://rondoveneziano.com/album/concerto/; specialist pressing survey https://www.monsieurvinyl.com/rondo-veneziano/; independent chronology https://musicbrainz.org/artist/940a5867-a2e5-4af5-ae05-91cf63bd0b52.

**Genres.** Italian album entry explicitly lists `baroquepop` and `pop`, not a narrow genre inferred from the cover. The official history/artist MusicBrainz supports an overarching classical crossover, but the genre evidence file appropriately avoids pretending artist tags are album-specific, so `classicalcrossover` and `easylistening` were **withheld** in this row pending proper album-level evidence. The reviewer describes sophisticated melodic construction rather than a new autonomous electronic genre. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerto_%28Rond%C3%B2_Veneziano%29.

**Instruments.** Album personnel entry names Reverberi on keyboards and synthesizers, the orchestral group and Jesús Eduardo Álvarez Herrera playing classical guitar on *Concerto*, *Sonetto*, *Miniature*, *Carrousel* and *Chimere*. Thus `orchestra|keyboards|synth|acousticguitar`: guitar is materially represented but not throughout, so last. Source Italian album entry, weaker than original sleeve reproduction; retain medium confidence. Do not use a specific synth model; none was validated.

**Stereo composition (not holophonics).** *Controdanze* uses separately audible left- and right-channel dances which form a further compound dance in stereo. The 2023 specialist discussion describes this; an Italian secondary discography gives left minuet/right polka/combined gavotte. It is **not** evidence of the proprietary or technical Holophonics/binaural recording process in Fandome's `holophonics` token. `production` empty rather than invent a `stereopanning` tool name or `holophonics` claim. Source https://www.monsieurvinyl.com/rondo-veneziano/.

**Scales.** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=2` are moderate provisional whole-album estimates supported by critic calling *Seduzione*, *Splendore di Venezia* animated and describing multiple dance meters in *Controdanze*, with mellower songs *Sonetto*, *Nuvole*; they are not BPM analyses. `electronic=2`: credited synthesis exists within a prominently credited orchestra and guitar. `theatrical=3` and `experimental=2`: written orchestral presentation and experimental channel-composite track (only a part of record); if a strict reviewer requires numerous tracks showing experiment, **clear these two scores**, do not increase them. `mood`/`polish` left blank. Reverberi is producer (Qobuz original 1988 track label credits).

## Masquerade (1989) — rondoveneziano-masquerade

**Original vs export.** Ten tracks per official album site: *Costellazioni*, *Venti d'Oriente*, *Luci e colori di Venezia*, *Ultimo incontro*, *Accademia*, *Visioni di Venezia*, *Oltremare*, *Le muse*, *Note veneziane*, *Floralis*. German *Visioni di Venezia* has *Segreto* and *Velieri lontani* and a shorter *Accademia*; *Armonie* includes *Miniatura* from earlier *Concerto*. Different international sequences are not separate creative studio albums. Official https://rondoveneziano.com/en/albums/masquerade/ and pressing commentary https://www.monsieurvinyl.com/rondo-veneziano/.

**Genres.** The English album discography explicitly names `classicalcrossover|easylistening`; Italian discography also names `baroquepop|pop`. Their relative weights are editorial; no genre was inferred from merely calling an album *Masquerade*. Named contemporary critic Pierguido Cavallina, *Radiocorriere TV* no. 46, 1989, is cited in the English album article: he describes its baroque character and accessible listening quality. The original period page was not independently consulted, so the reported review counts as secondary-transcribed, **not an independently inspected primary review**.

**Instruments and production.** Ensemble/keyboard/synth credits from album-specific Italian personnel; Jesús Eduardo Álvarez Herrera is named classical guitarist in album liner-credit transcription. The later Qobuz *Masterpieces* credits name the guitarist on original 1989 recordings *Venti d'Oriente* and *Visioni di Venezia* and the two engineers. Use `orchestra|keyboards|synth|acousticguitar`. Reverberi credited producer/conductor. Harry Thumann and Klaus Strazicky engineers only. No effects, gear model, or recording process independently attested: `production` empty. Source https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/masterpieces-rondo-veneziano/0886970802727.

**Scales.** Only `electronic=2`: both orchestra and synthesizers are explicitly album credited; do not extrapolate an electronic dominance or a separate synthesizer make/model. Other scales are empty since one reviewer saying a record is pleasant/baroque cannot establish album-wide energy, danceability, experimentalism or polish. `landmark=1` stems from this original period's widely circulated German and Swiss albums; refer to export-specific chart rankings as *export performance*, not proof that original Italian program contains those extra tracks. `influenced_by` blank, Pavesi/Fochesato are composers and not producers.

## Barocco (1990) — rondoveneziano-barocco

**Date and source conflict.** Original album title *Barocco* was issued November **1990** (Baby 590 125-1 LP / 590 125-2 CD), per label pressing, official artist list, and specialist collector. One English article's opening paragraph mistakenly says November 1989 while its own infobox says 1990. We prioritize 1990 original dated release; Italian secondary says 5 November 1990, but the exact day is not required for Fandome. Sources https://musicbase.net/barocco and https://rondoveneziano.com/en/albums/.

**Tracks and editions.** Original CD 12 tracks and 8th is *Divertissement* (per dated pressing). Current official site has a 12-track list but its eighth is named *Estate*; label-supported `Divertissement` wins unless original booklet proves a same-recording alternate title. Germany *Musica... fantasia* removes the *Barocco* title piece and adds prior material and *Venezia notturna*, France *Mystère*, Belgium's 2001 *Capriccio veneziano*: these are overseas releases/reissues rather than four new 1990 albums.

**Genre evidence.** Italian album classification provides `baroquepop|pop`. Original 1990 CD release database tags *Classical*, *Baroque*, *Neo-Classical*. These qualify for `classical`, `baroque` and `neoclassical` late in the genre chain, **with metadata source strength only**; `baroque` is a literal token unlike `baroquepop`, and Phase 02 already proposed adding it. *Barocco* English infobox actually says baroque pop, not full album classical crossover; therefore `classicalcrossover` and `easylistening` were deliberately withheld here despite generic group styles.

**Instruments.** Direct original release confirms Reverberi as arranger/conductor and ensemble Rondò Veneziano, but fails to identify individual instruments/keyboard models for the **original Barocco 1990** sessions. Only `orchestra` is secure at current evidence granularity; do NOT copy the previous year's synth and guitar evidence. Credits name engineer and mixer Klaus Strazicky and corporate production locations. All are kept out of `producers` except Reverberi, who is explicitly marked producer.

**Production.** The scanned/transcribed original 1990 CD states **SPARS Code: DDD**, implying digitally recorded, digitally mixed and digitally mastered. Therefore one new, provisional production token `digitalrecording` is applied; it does not imply digital **tape** (a distinct existing token `digitaltape`), DAT, ADAT or a named synth. If SPARS on that edition is not accepted as sufficiently explicit per the project evidence threshold, blank the production cell and omit this new token rather than infer another device. Source https://musicbase.net/barocco. All eight scale values blank for lack of specific whole-album texture/dynamic evidence; mere album title or DDD format does not assign them.

## Prestige (1991) — rondoveneziano-prestige

**Original and period.** Original Italian 12-track *Prestige* Baby 590 132-2 / 590 132-1: *Orion* … *Larmes de pluie*. The artist official page and 1991 Italian CD listing agree. English entry also prints 12 songs. Italian encyclopedic infobox wrongly says 13, but its own Italian tracklist contains twelve. The German *Magica melodia* variant contains 13 **different-selection** tracks including *Magica melodia*, *Incontro* and previous album's *Barocco*; the Belgian *Interlude* uses the core original sequence. Do not describe export tracks as appearing on original *Prestige*. Official https://rondoveneziano.com/en/albums/prestige/; original Italian CD https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/rondo-veneziano-prestige-cd-830135/.

**Genres.** Explicit `classicalcrossover` and `easylistening` in English album discography, `baroquepop` and `pop` in Italian album discography. The German collector calls the original 1991 CD Pop-Klassik; this corroborates crossover style, but is not converted to a novel token. `electronic` **not added as album-wide genre** merely because *Prestige* title song reminds a specialist of Jean-Michel Jarre. That comparison, based on a named recording, justifies *Jean-Michel Jarre* only as track-specific `sounds_like`.

**Instruments/producer.** Italian album's original-formation record names Gian Piero Reverberi piano/keyboards + ensemble orchestra. Those support `orchestra|piano|keyboards`; no credited 1991 synth model, specific synthesizer or drum machine. Original liner note reports Reverberi producing, arranging, mixing and Klaus Strazicky engineering/mixing. `producers=Gian Piero Reverberi` alone. Composer Ivano Pavesi is not an album producer. Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestige_%28Rond%C3%B2_Veneziano_album%29.

**Scales and landmark.** All 8 scales blank. A later specialist hears 1990s electronic resemblance in the title song, but that alone cannot be extrapolated to the whole LP's degree of electronics, danceability or experimentalism. Composer's own favourable quoted description in Italian encyclopedia is reproduced without independent original interview and cannot establish quantitative values. `landmark=1` for last original Baby Records studio record, charted in multiple countries, with significant release variations. No documented individual musical inspiration: `influenced_by` empty.

## Unresolved discographic completeness

The official albums page inserts *The Genius of Vivaldi Mozart Beethoven* (1990) between Barocco and Prestige. Its three-part set reworks music of earlier composers, so the group appears on record under a different authorship model; the 1990 multi-disc issue deserves a **separate original-issue audit** before deciding if it is a distinctive, landmark studio/adaptation album. Same for *Not Quite Jerusalem* film soundtrack (1985), flagged in Phase 02. These titles are not falsely claimed to have been researched or imported in this phase. Next standard-original studio phase starts with *Le stagioni di Venezia* (1992).
