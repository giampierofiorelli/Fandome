# Fugees — Fandome Phase 01 evidence notes

Research date **29 October 2026**. Repository: `giampierofiorelli/Fandome`, branch `descriptors`, READ ONLY. The original **two studio albums** are examined; remix editions and repackaged collections are excluded. Source keys in `fugees_phase01_sources.csv` and the genre-by-genre citations in `fugees_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Scores are independent editorial 0–5 assessments grounded in album-specific sources, not measured technical specifications.

## `fugees-bluntedonreality` — *Blunted on Reality* (1994)

**Identity and context.** Original Ruffhouse/Columbia album, sometimes billed **Fugees (Tranzlator Crew)**; that addition is part of the historical credit, not a separate artist. AllMusic dates its original record in 1994. Some retailer release pages display **2026 reissues**, which do not change its original year; exact day varies among modern databases. No Fugees artist/album is in `catalog.ts` or `seed.ts`; add this ID before validation. Sources: `allmusic_debut`, `qobuz_debut`, `musicbrainz_debut`.

**Genre-by-genre.** The dominant base is hip-hop/rap. AllMusic uses **Alternative Rap** and **East Coast Rap**, while Pitchfork identifies **alternative hip-hop**, with **boom-bap**, **jazz rap**, **reggae fusion** and **ragga rap** especially in rhythmic and vocal substyles. `eastcoastrap`, `reggaefusion` and `raggarap` are faithful tokens to add to Fandome (the project does not yet have them). `reggae` has track-level support from Pitchfork's reggae-inflected passages; it should not outrank the primary rap genre. These specific genre terms are not interchangeable with `eastcoasthiphop`, `reggae` or `ragga`. Source: `pitchfork_debut`, `allmusic_debut`.

**Scales (album-wide):** `energy=4` due to aggressive, rapid-fire rap; `electronic=3` samples and programming balanced by substantial credited live players; `mood=3` exuberance mixed with frustration and social commentary; `theatrical=3` call-outs, interludes and emphatic exchanges; `experimental=2` eclectic elements but conventional contemporary rap framing; `polish=2` critics describe a raw, rough-edged and crowded mix; `tempo=4` generally urgent delivery (not a calculated BPM); `groove=3` generally rhythmic but not a dance-focused album. Critic reservations about not yet expressing the trio's individuality are included, not erased. Sources: `pitchfork_debut`, `allmusic_debut`, `pitchfork_score_retrospective`.

**Vocals and lyrics.** Lauryn Hill, Wyclef Jean and Pras lead with forceful rap; spoken interludes are present. Pitchfork emphasizes social injustices, xenophobia and neighbourhood violence in the background, though the social content is less explicit/fully developed than on *The Score*. `social|political|identity|storytelling` are thematic tags, not claims that every song narrates those themes. `rap|powerful|spoken` records the dominant performance; there are melodic moments but not enough evidence to describe the whole album with `soft` singing. Sources: `pitchfork_debut`, `allmusic_debut`.

**Instruments, verified not guessed.** Original Sony credits document Wyclef guitar and an **acoustic baritone guitar** (represented as `baritoneguitar` plus broadly `acousticguitar` from AllMusic), Wyclef and Rashad Muhammad bass, Derrick Darling drums, Khalis Bayyan saxophone/keyboards, and DJ Boy Wonder record scratches (`turntables`). `sampler` is NOT asserted as a machine token merely because samples were used. Primary source: `qobuz_debut`, corroborated `musicbrainz_debut`, `allmusic_debut`.

**Production and credits.** `sampling` directly supported by track sample relationships, `programming` by Rashad Muhammad's musician/technical credits; `drumprogramming` is omitted because these personnel listings do not establish a specific drum-programming role for the original tracks. The producers/co-producers across the original album: Pras Michel, Wyclef Jean, Khalis Bayyan (formerly Ronald Bell), Rashad Muhammad, Brand X and Stephen Walker. **Salaam Remi is listed in several album-level databases as a producer, but his firsthand 2016 interview distinguishes his production of the 'Nappy Heads' and 'Vocab' remixes and later 'Fu-Gee-La'; he is NOT assigned as producer for an original album master without original-track confirmation.** If Claude later chooses an edition with the Remi remix attached, document that version separately and do not silently alter the original album producers. Sources: `qobuz_debut`, `musicbrainz_debut`, `original_pressing`, `producer_remi`.

**Similarity vs influence.** Pitchfork compares the early record to De La Soul, A Tribe Called Quest, Main Source and Digable Planets. These go only in `sounds_like`, since it is a critic's retrospective analogy; `influenced_by` intentionally blank. Likewise Kool & the Gang, Earth Wind & Fire and Aretha Franklin are *sample sources*, not automatically formative influences. Source: `pitchfork_debut`, `musicbrainz_debut`.

## `fugees-thescore` — *The Score* (1996)

**Identity.** Original 1996 Columbia/Ruffhouse studio LP, not the later *Bootleg Versions* remix album. AllMusic and AOTY date the release **13 February 1996**; Pitchfork's oral history refers to a separate global-market date of **20 February**, so Fandome uses only the uncontested `1996` year. The album drew substantially on work recorded at Booga Basement and other New York facilities, and its original songs should not be conflated with remix/bonus tracks. Sources: `allmusic_score`, `aoty_score`, `pitchfork_oral_history`, `pitchfork_score`.

**Genre method.** Hip-hop and alternative hip-hop dominate; AOTY lists East Coast hip-hop, conscious hip-hop, boom-bap, reggae and neo-soul; AllMusic lists alternative rap and contemporary R&B. Strong sung soul and R&B sections are supported by Pitchfork and the 1996 LA Times review. These genres represent different layers: `neosoul` applies especially to Lauryn-led singing, `reggae` to Wyclef's material and selected songs, `boombap` to beats, and `alternativerap` is a critical label **that Pras explicitly contested in a 1996 interview**. Keep the sourced descriptive genre but record the artist's disagreement. `jazzrap` is excluded because Pitchfork's jazz influence references are not a whole-album genre designation. Sources: `aoty_score`, `allmusic_score`, `pitchfork_score`, `la_times_review`, `la_times_interview`.

**Scales.** `energy=3`: a mix of hard rap tracks and gentler soul/reggae; `electronic=3`: extensive documented sampling and programmed drums with some guitar and live texture; `mood=3`: alternating social anxiety and warmth/optimism; `theatrical=3`: cinematic framing/skits and dramatic hooks balanced by low-key passages; `experimental=3`: jazz, soul and reggae crossovers extend the rap format without being avant-garde; `polish=4`: more deliberate, controlled production than the debut; `tempo=3`: largely mid-tempo beats with varied vocal pacing; `groove=4`: pronounced danceable boom-bap and reggae-informed rhythm even on introspective songs. These are *overall* assessments; don't infer acoustic/electronic scale solely from sampled songs. Sources: `pitchfork_score`, `allmusic_score`, `la_times_review`, `qobuz_score`.

**Voice, lyrics.** Rap verses alternate with Lauryn's powerful but also quiet, clear singing, Wyclef's singing/rap and spoken skits. Social consciousness, refugee experience, policing, displacement, group identity, threat/violence and intimate song material coexist. The theme tags are `social|political|identity|storytelling|love|heartbreak`; no claim that every song has the same subjects. Sources: `pitchfork_score`, `allmusic_score`, `la_times_review`.

**Instruments and production strictly sourced.** `guitar` is credited to Wyclef on original tracks (*Family Business*), and `turntables` from credited Backspin/DJ Scribble scratches. Original-song program and `Fu-Gee-La` support `sampling`; John Forté is specifically credited as drum programmer on *No Woman, No Cry*, establishing `drumprogramming`. Other bass, drums and keyboards in album-level metadata belong **particularly to the Sly & Robbie version of Fu-Gee-La**, an alternate mix on some CDs. Do not assign those instruments as confirmed across the original core 13 tracks, though the original mixes can contain sampled versions of them. Similarly, no documentary evidence of particular hardware (SP-1200, MPC, Ensoniq) was found; don't invent it. Sources: `qobuz_score`, `allmusic_score_creds`, `musicbrainz_score`, `pitchfork_oral_history`.

**Producers (only actual producers/co-producers).** Wyclef Jean, Lauryn Hill and Jerry Duplessis worked across many original songs; Pras Michel appears as co-producer on many. Salaam Remi produced *Fu-Gee-La* (firsthand 2016 confirmation); Shawn King on *How Many Mics*; Diamond D on the title track; John Forté on *Family Business*, *Cowboys* and other tracks. **Do not add Warren Riker merely for engineering or mixing**; don't add Sly & Robbie / Handel Tucker for their alternate remix unless separately describing that remix release. Sources: `qobuz_score`, `musicbrainz_score`, `pitchfork_oral_history`.

**Documented album-level musical influences.** In a first-person interview Wyclef **explicitly** identifies the Beatles' *Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band* and Pink Floyd's *The Wall* as his **blueprints while working on The Score**; they belong in `influenced_by` with specific albums named. This is not mere critic comparison or a sampling/cover relation. In 2026 he also recalls hearing Bob Marley's *Exodus*, early Bob Dylan and The Police's *Synchronicity* while growing up, but does NOT establish those three as precise production models for this LP: record them only under ARTIST INFLUENCES. Enya and Roberta Flack are sample/cover sources on *Ready or Not* and *Killing Me Softly*, **not** `influenced_by` on that basis. John Forté, Diamond D and other guests are collaborators, not influences. Sources: `wyclef_blueprint`, `wyclef_2026_ap`, `qobuz_score`.

**Sounds_like and critical dissent.** Contemporary LA Times compares the trio's technique to A Tribe Called Quest and an interview describes a lineage from De La Soul, sufficient for sonic analogy but not proof of musical influence. LA Times gives a fairly modest 2.5/4 contemporary review, while Pitchfork and AllMusic evaluate the album much more warmly in hindsight. `confidence=high` reflects consistency of core genres/credits; it does NOT mean universal agreement on critical merit. Sources: `la_times_review`, `la_times_interview`, `pitchfork_score`, `allmusic_score`.
