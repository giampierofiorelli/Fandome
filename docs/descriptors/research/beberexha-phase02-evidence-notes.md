## Better Mistakes (2021) — `beberexha-bettermistakes`

- **Release and edition.** May 7, 2021, **13 tracks** (NME release notice, Qobuz, MusicBrainz). AllMusic displays **April 23, 2021** in its page header, in conflict with the contemporary announcement and distributor release: use **2021** in CSV and May 7 as release date in import notes.
- **Genre order and disagreements.** AllMusic frames an album-wide dance-pop / club-dance body with electronics, hip-hop, and some pop-punk energy. LA Times' critic describes *Sacrifice* as retro-disco, *Die for a Man* as emo-rap and *Baby, I'm Jealous* as electro-funk. The three specific styles are placed **after** core styles because they refer to tracks rather than the whole record. `emorap` is a **new** token, not mapped onto plain hiphop.
- **Scales**: energy **4** and groove **4** reflect driving bass-heavy pop with gentler interruptions; electronic **4** because programmed beats/synths dominate yet Travis Barker's live drumming and credited guitars are substantive; mood **2** because jealousy, self-sabotage and loneliness pervade the high-energy material. Theatrical **4** for outsize vocal intensity; experimental **2** for contrasts within conventional song structures; polish **5** for strongly processed mainstream studio pop; tempo **3** reflects mixed-paced mid/up-tempo sequencing rather than treating all tracks as Sacrifice.
- **Voice**: `powerful|raspy|clear` is an interpretive comparison of lead delivery with the GRAMMY profile description of explosive vocals; guest rappers **do not** turn Rexha's lead voice into `rap`.
- **Instrumentation**: Qobuz explicitly credits Travis Barker drums; Richard Boardman and Peter Rycroft synth, bass, drums, keys; David Strääf bass, guitar, violin, percussion; other credited acoustic and electric guitar. `drummachine` is intentionally left out: drum programming credits do not independently prove a dedicated drum-machine device.
- **Production**: `programming|drumprogramming|synthprogramming` attested explicitly by Qobuz credits. No particular DAW, synth model, drum-machine model or autotune asserted. Heterogeneous producer credits from complete track listing, including Travis Barker (explicit Producer credit), rather than making executive Justin Tranter a producer.
- **Lyrics**: first-person GRAMMY interview confirms jealousy, body image/mental health and self-examination; introspective theme outweighs apparent dance-party surface. Sia in `sounds_like` follows AllMusic's sonic analogy to *Sabotage*, **not** album influence.
- **Influenced_by blank**: the artist's general rock listening list in previous phase's ARTIST INFLUENCES does not prove direct musical influence on the whole album. Collaborators Doja Cat / Barker are guests, not influences.

## Bebe (2023) — `beberexha-bebe`

- **Release and edition**: April 28, 2023; original **12 tracks** (Spotify/Qobuz). *I'm Good (Blue)* was already released with David Guetta in 2022 but is part of the original album, so include its sound in the album-weighted descriptor. Featuring Snoop Dogg and Dolly Parton does **not** make them co-album artists under Fandome.
- **Genre order**: AllMusic specifically describes disco, funk and groovy rock, while the album is officially dance-pop; the original *I'm Good (Blue)* is identified as an EDM anthem. Do not mislabel this as a purely 1970s album: modern electro-dance acts are central to its sound.
- **Scales**: energy **4**, groove **4**, tempo **4** because dance numbers predominate but *Seasons* is acoustic. Electronic **4** for electronic club tracks combined with live guitars/keys/strings; mood **4** for celebratory palette tempered by reflection; theatrical **3** for confident standard pop lead; experimental **2** for coherent retro/new mix; polish **5** for high-budget mainstream production. These are comparative ratings against guide anchors.
- **Instrumentation**: Qobuz track credits specifically mention synth, bass, guitar, drums (*Visions*), organ/piano/strings/keys and bass and drum programming (*Born Again*). AllMusic identifies elastic bass and ABBA-like strings in *Satellite*. Drum machine models not named; production includes only documented `programming|drumprogramming`.
- **Producers**: Qobuz names track-level producers Rykeyz, Ido Zmishlany, Jussifer, Ray Goren, Joe Janiak, Mitch Allan, BURNS, David Guetta, Timofey Reznikov, TMS, Nick Monson, Alex Bilo, Tim Pagnotta, Brian Phillips. **Bebe Rexha only Executive Producer**; she does not go in `producers`. Similarly exclude Daniel Cullen when Vocal Producer only, and Chris Bishop/AHH when only Additional Production.
- **Influences**: Associated Press artist interview explicitly links *Seasons* to Fleetwood Mac's *Landslide* and artist interview describes Stevie Nicks/Fleetwood Mac musical direction for record; **both** qualify under documented musical inspiration. This is independent of Stevie Nicks being merely a guitar sound-alike. `sounds_like` is editorial similarity and Dolly Parton there represents the acoustic duet/sonic comparison, not causation or an influence.

## DIRTY BLONDE (2026) — `beberexha-dirtyblonde`

- **Release and edition**: worldwide digital master **June 12, 2026**, *13 tracks, 33 min* (Apple Music and MusicBrainz). Physical CD/vinyl editions have **14** including *I Don't Need Anything*; visual album includes video components. All are editions of one studio album, not additional descriptors. Qobuz capitalization `DIRTY BLONDE` preserves official title in the CSV.
- **Genres**: Apple classifies album **Dance**. AllMusic calls festival EDM, global house and hip-hop-dance. Official Bandcamp description specifies drum and bass for *Tokyo*, pop-rock for *i like you better than me*, Jersey bounce and country for *Drink and a Little Love* and tag `europop`. Genre metadata at Album of the Year independently includes `electropop`, `dancepop`, `electrohouse`, `altpop`, `melodichouse`, `festivalprogressivehouse`. **Those last specific tags come from a lower-confidence metadata database**: Claude can drop them on second-person audit rather than present them as unanimous critics' descriptions. `jerseybounce` reports wording exactly; no forced equivalence to `jerseyclub`. AllMusic’s artist biography additionally calls *Hysteria* a **festival trance** anthem (`trance`, limited to that track).
- **Genre counterweight**: this is predominantly dance music, NOT a rock or country album. Stylistic departures in a small number of tracks go after club music in `genres` order.
- **Scales**: energy **5**, electronic **5**, tempo **4**, groove **5** because club-ready 130-BPM-oriented songs with dense electronic drums/synths dominate (Apple/AllMusic/official artist). Mood **3** because upbeat dancefloor release overlays regret, insecurity, heartbreak and recovery. Theatrical **4** for maximalist anthems; experimental **2** for a few stylistic shifts built around familiar club structures; polish **5** for high-sheen release. Electronic 5 is a *dominance* judgment, NOT a claim that no acoustic guitar appears.
- **Voice/lyrics/language**: emotional vocals, club defiance, relationship regret and dance music as spiritual refuge supported by Apple Music and NPR. Lead voice powerful/raspy/clear is an editorial vocal description, not inferred solely from earlier albums. `en|sq`: Albanian passages are verifiably sung in *Çike Çike* (first-person NPR, Albanian news KOHA); the album is **mainly English**. An Albanian artist identity alone would not justify `sq`.
- **Instruments**: MusicBrainz track credits specify synthesized sounds, bass, drums, keyboards and guitar on *Hysteria*, *Tokyo*, *New Religion*, *Çike Çike* and others; a piano and resonator guitar on *Drink and a Little Love*. `resonatorguitar` is a **new, literal token** because MusicBrainz names a resonator guitar on *Drink and a Little Love*: it is not assumed to be a branded Dobro. `synthbass` is intentionally omitted because an official 808-bassline description does not clearly distinguish a separate synth-bass instrument from a production technique. `drummachine` is supported by MusicBrainz explicit drum-machine programming on *Drink and a Little Love*; this does not prove Roland TR-808 hardware. `sampling` is stated for *Hysteria* (Da Hool) and *New Religion* (Faithless) in Apple editorial, no covered/interpolated track automatically qualifies as influenced_by.
- **Producer audit**: Qobuz thirteen-track listing is the primary role-specific source. MusicBrainz has more granular or conflicting producer credit versions (e.g. DJ Replay, Edwin Serrano, Daniel Cullen, Mitch Allan); the CSV uses Qobuz **main Producer** names while reserving Qobuz's `ComposerLyricist` names out of producers. Qobuz shows some aliases inconsistently: Ryan Buendia/DJ Replay, `NITTI`/Nitti Gritti, `Punctual`/members John Morgan and Will Lansley. Treat each project as one producer credit and do not append personnel solely for writer, additional production, mix or engineering. Qobuz credits for *Sad Girls* span nine production contributors, all kept.
- **Influenced_by blank**: NPR first-person Marilyn Monroe/Madonna reference concerns visual image and persona, not independently identified musical arrangement/production influence. Da Hool and Faithless are **sample sources**, not automatic influences. David Guetta, DJ Snake and Faithless are guests/collaborators, not album influences.
- **Genre/credit disagreement**: AllMusic's review is also shown on alternate CD edition with extra song; review may address physical version. Current album descriptor still covers the **core 13 songs**. `melodichouse` and `festivalprogressivehouse` are only community-level subgenres, not endorsed by reviewers or producer credits. AllMusic is broadly favorable to the dancefloor songs, whereas Jeffrey Davies at PopMatters (quoted on Album of the Year) considers the sequencing / cohesion weak; they disagree on artistic success rather than the foundational genre facts.
