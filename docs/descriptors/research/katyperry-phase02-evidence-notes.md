# Katy Perry — Phase 02 evidence notes (2017–2024)

## Scope and method

Exactly **three** rows: original **standard** studio albums *Witness*, *Smile* and *143*. No 2001–2013 rows repeated. Overall scales express evidence-based editorial judgement for the **whole album**, not measured BPM/loudness or a vote on quality. Genre evidence is itemised per source with whole-record versus track versus texture scope. The row's `influenced_by` remains blank when influences are not expressly documented. Personnel credited only as songwriters, arrangers, mixers, executive producers or featured artists do not automatically enter `producers`. All quoted source titles and URLs are in sources.csv.

## Witness (2017) — `katyperry-witness`

**Edition.** Capitol's original release was **June 9, 2017** (AllMusic). Universal Music Italia's visible 2017 vinyl page describes a **September 29** local vinyl issue, not the first album date. The standard album has **15 tracks**; bonus cuts *Dance with the Devil* and *Act My Age* on the deluxe version are excluded from album-value evidence. The original Capitol artist credit is Katy Perry.

**Genres.** Leonie Cooper's NME review (9 June 2017) explicitly hears high-energy electropop. Stephen Thomas Erlewine's AllMusic review hears studio-slick EDM-pop, primarily electronic dance. Annie Zaleski (The A.V. Club) identifies '90s house, 1980s new wave, mainstream EDM and electro hip-hop, with track-level synthpop/soul/funk on *Pendulum*, vaporwave-like *Tsunami*. Sal Cinquemani (Slant) adds *Tsunami*'s R&B, electro-R&B on *Power* and gospel-lite on *Pendulum*. Hannah J Davies (Guardian) hears trap-pop on *Bon Appétit*, dark dream-pop on *Hey Hey Hey* and funk on *Power*. Mikael Wood (Los Angeles Times) hears disco on *Chained to the Rhythm*. `vaporwave` and `edmpop` are new terms named in qualified criticism. Genre aggregation at Album of the Year independently lists `altpop`, `contemporaryrnb`, and `futurehouse` (last a new token); treat that less-specific taxonomy with medium certainty, and **not** silently replaced with a nearby existing token. Track-specific tags deliberately rank below whole-album electropop/dancepop.

**Scales.** `energy=3`: radio pop with substantial midtempo and ballad passages (Pitchfork). `electronic=4`: frequent synths and programming, with significant credited guitar, bass, piano and choir; electronic arrangement dominates but is not fully electronic (AllMusic, Universal). `mood=2`: anxious and frustrated themes frequently counterbalance *Chained to the Rhythm*'s brightness (Pitchfork; Slant). `theatrical=3`: less outsized camp than the preceding albums; assertive lead vocals remain. `experimental=2`: attempts new textures via Purity Ring/house/vaporwave but still uses familiar song frameworks (A.V. Club). `polish=5`: very dense, pristine multi-producer production (AllMusic). `tempo=3`: many midtempo numbers, occasional fast club tracks (Pitchfork). `groove=4`: persistent house/EDM-derived pulse, offset by several slow piano-driven numbers. These integers are editorial syntheses, not producer-provided numeric metrics.

**Voice.** Perry's assertive lead is `powerful|clear`; Nicki Minaj/Migos are actual rap guests (`rap`), gospel choir on *Pendulum* supports low-priority `choral`. **Instruments**: synth, piano, keyboards, bass and drums, percussion, acoustic/electric guitar and handclaps are specified by Universal's *Witness*, *Roulette*, *Miss You More* and *Chained to the Rhythm* credits. A.V. Club hears saxophone on *Power*; independent official personnel for that saxophonist were not located, so this is critic-audition evidence rather than a named session player. `production`: Jeff Bhasker's and Illangelo's drum-programming credits on *Pendulum*; explicit Roland Clark/Fatboy Slim sampling on *Swish Swish*; critics report audible Auto-Tune use and distorted vocal effects. The filter/Auto-Tune effects are album examples rather than documented plug-in brand/model chains.

**Producers.** Universal's original 15 tracks identify Max Martin, Ali Payami, Shellback, Duke Dumont, Hayden James, Jack Garratt, Corin Roddick, Mike WiLL Made-It, Scooly, Noah Passovoy, Oscar Holter, Elof Loelv, Jeff Bhasker and Dustin O'Halloran. Additional-production-only personnel (e.g. Illangelo, Rachael Findlen, certain tracks' Peter Karlsson) are **excluded** by producer/co-producer rule. Alexis Taylor and Joe Goddard are writers on *Into Me You See* rather than automatically credited record producers. Katy Perry and Max Martin executive credits do not add new producer names.

**Lyrical interpretation.** `social|political` comes especially from *Chained to the Rhythm*, *Bigger than Me*, and *Power*, while desire, heartbreak, self-doubt and interpersonal tensions dominate numerous others. Some reviewers argue 'purposeful pop' is unpersuasive (Slant, Pitchfork), whereas NME credits its attempt to combine politics and dance pop; the `political` tag signals subject matter, not critical agreement. `sounds_like` is explicitly *editorial sonic proximity*; Purity Ring and Hot Chip were involved in writing and sound and are not automatically counted as influences. No securely documented album-wide role-model artist was found: `influenced_by` left empty. `landmark=0`: a notable career pivot but not marked as one of her signature landmark studio albums.

## Smile (2020) — `katyperry-smile`

**Edition/date.** August 28, 2020 standard 12-track Capitol studio album. Universal's page mixes the original twelve songs with bonus cuts (*Small Talk*, *Never Worn White*, acoustic and remix tracks): **exclude tracks 13 onward** when defining the original album. Contemporary July press initially announced an earlier planned release; use the actual August release.

**Genres.** AllMusic calls the whole record deliberately streamlined **dance-pop**. Album of the Year indexes electropop and contemporary R&B; reviewer Samuel Draper separately identifies *Never Really Over* as electropop. *Cry About It Later* is synthpop in David Smyth's Evening Standard review. Emily Mackay (Guardian) hears *Teary Eyes*' house, *Not the End of the World*'s trap-pop, *Smile*'s West Coast R&B, gospel-coloured *Only Love* and countryish *What Makes a Woman*. Pitchfork names disco and dubstep elements. NME identifies *Champagne Problems*' funk bass; KXSC classifies that single as nu-disco. These isolated styles are demoted at the tail of the genre list. The final `country` and `gospel` labels denote individual track textures, not a country/gospel LP.

**Scales.** `energy=3`: several uptempo singles but AllMusic hears a milder, subdued album compared to *Teenage Dream*. `electronic=4`: electronic synth-pop and programmed beats predominate with identifiable guitars, real brass and strings. `mood=4`: resilient optimism dominates but the singer repeatedly describes grief/heartbreak. `theatrical=4`: anthemic, sometimes maximalist performance. `experimental=1`: critics consistently note conventional and safe form. `polish=5`: pronounced major-label sheen. `tempo=3`: mixed pop/dance midtempo, ballad on closer. `groove=3`: substantial danceable songs, but as AllMusic notes, intentionally less club-oriented than pure dance LPs.

**Instruments and techniques.** Universal credits Oscar Holter (bass, drums, keyboards), Johan Carlsson (multiple tracks' keyboards/synth/programming), multiple string players on *Resilient*, horns/trumpets/saxophones and horn arrangers on *Tucked*, guitarists on *Daisies* and *What Makes a Woman*, Rhodes/electric piano on *Harleys in Hawaii* and the closer, and organ and handclaps on later songs of standard edition. Apple Music corroborates trumpet, saxophone and a **Naughty by Nature sample** on *Smile*. NME identifies an interpolation/sample of Steam's *Na Na Hey Hey Kiss Him Goodbye* on *Not the End of the World*. `production=drumprogramming|synthprogramming|sampling|hornarrangements|stringarrangements|belting` is rooted in credited drum/synth programming and arrangers, the title-track sample and Pitchfork's description of dramatic vocal belting. No unsupported hardware/software model names used.

**Producers.** Twelve standard tracks together credit Zedd, Dreamlab, Oscar Holter, Andrew Goldstein, Oscar Görres (also Oscar Gorres), Monsters & Strangerz, Stargate, Tim Blacksmith, Danny D, Josh Abraham, Oligee, G Koop, Johan Carlsson, Charlie Puth, John DeBold, Elvira Anderfjärd, and John Ryan. **Credit caveat:** Apple Music explicitly calls G Koop **co-producer** on *Smile* while some Universal displays say additional producer; Apple Music calls John Ryan producer on *Tucked*, whereas Universal's summary names Johan Carlsson only. We include both due to affirmative explicit co/producer credits; Claude should retain this discrepancy when importing. Peter Karlsson, Gian Stone and other personnel listed only as *vocal producer* are excluded, as are mixers.

**Other fields.** `voice` clear and powerful with multiple choruses/strong belting; guests on standard twelve tracks are not rappers. Lyrics primarily cover recovery, self-image, relationships, desire and dance-through-sadness. Album does not document a named formative artist: `influenced_by` empty. `sounds_like` comparisons are editorial sonic resemblance, not claims Perry studied those acts. Reviewer disagreement: Pitchfork finds loud, clichéd reassurance; AllMusic considers it calmer, melodic and streamlined. `landmark=0` because it is not one of her best-established defining records.

## 143 (2024) — `katyperry-143`

**Edition/date.** September 20, 2024 Capitol **11-track standard album**. *1432*, deluxe bonus tracks and later promotional reissues are **not** independent studio albums; do not count them or their changed credits in the core descriptor. Collaborations with 21 Savage, Kim Petras, Doechii and JID are track guests, not a separately co-billed album.

**Genres.** Apple Music's primary editorial interview calls it full-on dance music and specifically says *Lifetimes* explores piano-house, while *I'm His, He's Mine* samples Crystal Waters. Rich Juzwiak (Pitchfork) names EDM and pop-trap. Alexis Petridis (Guardian) describes a retro *Europop* effort referencing Italo house (piano on *Lifetimes*), Euro-house/O-Zone (*Crush*), French touch filtering, synth-pop (*Woman's World*) and trap-influenced pop. Tom Williams (Slant) hears house rhythms. NME identifies French Affair's Eurodance hit sampled on *Crush*. AllMusic notes disco-style production. Album of the Year's secondary tag includes pop-rap. New exact non-listed genre tokens: **`italohouse`** and **`poptrap`**. The Italo-house reference is explicitly an **allusion/texture** on one track, not the entire album.

**Scales.** `energy=4`: beat-heavy pop with some less intense midtempo songs, compared to *Witness*. `electronic=5`: synth-led, sequenced dance/EDM production with documented limited guitar. `mood=4`: lyrically romantic, sexually confident, celebratory and deliberately upbeat; some cold production per reviewers. `theatrical=4`: big synthetic anthems, guest-star performances and occasional belting. `experimental=1`: multiple reviewers describe formulaic, retrospective dance music, a low score for novelty. `polish=5`: immaculately compressed major-label pop production. `tempo=4`: numerous upbeat house/disco passages. `groove=4`: marketed and structured as dance music; several slower pop-rap features limit a 5.

**Instruments and production.** Apple Music and MusicBrainz explicitly credit Cirkut, Stargate, Cato Sundberg and Thomas Andersson Drabløs with **synthesizer/programming** on *Wonder*, and Knut-Ingolf Brenna with *guitar* (source does not classify it as electric or acoustic, so the precise allowed `guitar` token from `src/data/instruments.ts` is used, rather than guessing). Piano is specifically described on *Lifetimes* in Pitchfork/Apple Music. No synth brand or computer DAW is evidenced. `sampling` confirmed by the original *Gypsy Woman* sample on *I'm His, He's Mine* and the French Affair sample on *Crush*; `synthprogramming` via explicit personnel role; `belting` via Pitchfork. No generic drum-machine, MPC, Ableton, sidechain or Auto-Tune claim made without album-specific evidence. French-touch compression is a musical-style analysis, not evidence of a specific sidechain compressor.

**Producers.** Original release Qobuz/MusicBrainz roles document **Dr. Luke, Rocco Did It Again!, Vaughn Oliver, Aaron Joseph, Malibu Babie, KBeaZy, Stargate, Cirkut**. Malibu Babie co-produced *Gorgeous* and can be absent from some summary lists, but original-track credits explicitly identify the producer. Ryan Ogren and Kalani Thompson are identified as *vocal producers*, not overall credited record producers, and are excluded to respect user rule. **Max Martin is a credited songwriter on Gorgeous, not producer on 143**. Katy Perry is not listed as producer without an explicit producer credit. *Wonder* is the only standard-edition track produced by Stargate/Cirkut, and is not evidence of their producing the other ten.

**Other fields and differences among critics.** Perry's Apple Music interview frames the songs around feeling whole, romantic permanence and dancing. Feminist slogans in *Woman's World* and simulated/artificial love themes justify low-ranked `social`/`identity`; this does **not** endorse the politics or lyrical depth. *I’m His, He’s Mine* quotes an existing Crystal Waters song, but **sampling does not automatically justify placing Crystal Waters in `influenced_by`**: nothing confirms a specifically declared direct artist-level creative influence. `sounds_like` is an editorial similarity based on the Guardian's and NME's stylistic comparisons; source alone is not proof of Perry's stated influences. Critics disagree on whether the record is tolerable (Guardian) or virtually devoid of ambition (Pitchfork/Slant). `landmark=0` despite public debate because the rule covers defining artistic albums, not controversies.

## Source and import integrity

- The sources sheet distinguishes official/rights-holder **track credits** from reviewers' **auditory judgments**. Values marked whole album refer to recurring characteristics, not extrapolated isolated details.
- `guitar` is **already** a permitted token in the actual `src/data/instruments.ts` and guide, despite being omitted from the shortened list embedded in the request. We keep the literal documented guitar without guessing electric/acoustic; no new instrument token needed.
- New genre tokens, if not yet available at import time: `vaporwave`, `edmpop`, `futurehouse`, `italohouse`, `poptrap`. These do not match existing `house`/`edm`/`trappop` tokens exactly. There are no new production tokens.
- The genre evidence sheet may reference a review that specifically mentions style terminology while assessing only a single track. Never use the entire genre list as an indiscriminate album-level description.
- **No next studio phase** is planned: the listed catalogue ends with 143. Subsequent EP, remix, soundtrack or collaborative projects should be assessed separately for landmark status and release availability before adding. Check catalogue updates after the phase is imported.
