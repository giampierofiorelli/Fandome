# Charli xcx — Phase 04 (2026 post-BRAT releases, INCREMENTAL ONLY)

## Scope and omission corrected
The previous Charli phase incorrectly called BRAT (2024) her latest studio album. Two **new 2026 projects** should be added: **Wuthering Heights** (13 February 2026, originally conceived as a film soundtrack/companion record, `type=soundtrack`) and **Music, Fashion, Film** (24 July 2026, `type=studio`). AllMusic's discography timeline lists both in this order; Pitchfork and Metacritic corroborate release dates. A separate 2024 **BRAT remix project** also exists, but is deliberately OUT OF SCOPE for this strictly incremental two-record correction. Do not claim every format/edition is covered.

Guide checked on GitHub: `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md` revision `15105fe1fddc541b6a12237901a800ca4a6d6e4b`; exactly 25 columns; all lists unlimited, but `notes` has a 15-word maximum. `confidence` is evidence quality, `status=draft`; the 0–5 metrics are our *calibrated editorial judgments*, NOT figures supplied by sources.

### 1) Wuthering Heights (2026) — `charlixcx-wutheringheights`

- **Release/type:** Atlantic, 13 February 2026; reviews position it as original soundtrack written for Emerald Fennell's film, but also an autonomous pop album. Classification explicitly `soundtrack` rather than blindly calling it her eighth studio LP. Sources: Metacritic, AP, Pitchfork, Apple Music.
- **Sound:** Pitchfork's Harry Tafoya and The Guardian's Alexis Petridis both describe an intensely dramatic meeting of jagged orchestral strings, synthesizers, vocal processing and pop songwriting. Slant's Michael Savio emphasizes drones and screeching strings. AP stresses contrast with BRAT's rave texture.
- **Genres:** `pop|soundtrack|artpop|synthpop|electronic|rock|industrial|gothpop`. Scope caveat: Guardian uses *industrial* for drums on **House** and **Funny Mouth**; the field should not imply every song is industrial. `gothpop` normalizes reviews' *gothic pop* phrase. `artpop` is a lower-certainty stylistic tag, so the overall row remains medium confidence. Detailed qualifiers in genre-evidence CSV.
- **Scores (our reasoned estimates):** energy **4** (dramatic distortion and harsh dynamic peaks balanced by quieter moments); electronic **3** (synth/drum machines share weight with strings); mood **1** (somber and menacing); theatrical **5** (soundtrack-scale musical drama); experimental **4** (drones/atonality/processed vocal extremes); polish **4** (full orchestration and controlled electronic studio production); tempo **2** (slow/mid-tempo emphasis, not a fast club record); groove **2** (some pulse and rave inflections but typically not dance-floor first).
- **Voice:** clear, powerful, soft, and spoken: Charli's singing and John Cale's spoken-word part in House (guest only; explain before importing if lead-only interpretation differs). Love, heartbreak, sensuality and narration described by critics.
- **Producers:** Finn Keane primary on most tracks; Justin Raisen and Nathan Klein documented on individual tracks via MusicBrainz. **Not** treating guest singer John Cale, instrumentalists or co-writers as producers.
- **Influence:** Guardian explicitly calls Nine Inch Nails an influence *on the track House* and describes Velvet Underground echoes; the former is placed in influenced_by, with limited track scope. Others appear in sounds_like only. Reconsider leaving influenced_by blank if Claude applies an album-wide direct-influence threshold.
- **What is not proven:** the album's exact track-averaged tempo, a formal technical measurement of mood/electronics, how many songs use industrial rhythms. These are editorial scores requiring independent checking.

### 2) Music, Fashion, Film (2026) — `charlixcx-musicfashionfilm`

- **Release/type:** 24 July 2026 via Atlantic, stand-alone studio album and **chronologically latest** found as of 24 October 2026. Pitchfork's 1 June announcement, 24 July review, Metacritic date, MusicBrainz release and AllMusic discography agree. Official Charts text is inconsistent (**26 July** in one line, seventh/eighth album in two others); prefer multiple agreement on **24 July**, and NEVER infer chronology from ordinal numbers.
- **Production:** A. G. Cook and Finn Keane identified as co-producers across album by Resident Advisor Benny Sun and Pitchfork Anna Gaca. MusicBrainz lists producer/exec-producer separately. Executive production role of Charli xcx is not automatically a track producer role.
- **Sound:** rock-oriented, fuzz/crunch guitar hooks alongside prominent drum programming and sparkling synth pop. Pitchfork compares selected sounds to Garbage, the Strokes, Rakes and Pixies; Resident Advisor compares ambience to Air and style to Thy Slaughter. These are **sounds_like** comparisons, *not* historical influences.
- **Genres:** `pop|rock|indierock|slackerrock|indietronica|postpunkrevival|alt|poppunk|electronic|synthpop|alternativedance|grunge`. `slackerrock` explicitly described by Pitchfork, other novel subgenre names come from Album of the Year's genre classification and are less independently supported; `poppunk` is specific to Yeah and `grunge` is a texture/comparison, not an album-wide consensus. If Claude enforces only explicitly qualified expert genre identifications, remove weak genre tags after review. **New list tokens** `indierock`,`slackerrock`,`indietronica`,`postpunkrevival`,`alternativedance` need vetting and potentially registering.
- **Scores (editorial estimates):** energy **3** (driving guitars and brisk highlights but lighter songs); electronic **3** (balanced electric guitars and synths/drum machines); mood **3** (fidgety humor and affection paired with doubt); theatrical **2** (more rough, intimate than high-concept stage show); experimental **3** (conspicuous production quirks, short fragments); polish **3** (deliberately less glossy than BRAT but still carefully engineered); tempo **3** (mostly brisk short pop-rock cuts); groove **2** (song-based rhythms rather than relentless club music).
- **Lyrics:** reflective selfhood, fame and art, irony and identity. Voice `clear|spoken|soft` captures melodic lines and occasional chant/spoken-style phrasing (editorial listening proxy from Pitchfork).
- **Producer versus influence:** do **not** label A. G. Cook or Finn Keane as influences merely because they produced it; their documented role is in producers. The critic-mentioned artists in sounds_like are sonic analogies only; influenced_by deliberately left empty.
