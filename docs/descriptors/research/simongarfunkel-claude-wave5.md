- **energy 5 / tempo 4**: [ND] "Richard took everything up a notch – the tempos, the innuendo and above all, the volume and energy of his vocals"; [CG] "lewd, feral, prophetically fast". Tempo is 4, not 5, because the album has slower cuts ("Baby", "Can't Believe You Wanna Leave").

- **scene**: [ND] "Recorded primarily at New Orleans' legendary J&M studios".
- **sounds_like / influenced_by**: empty. No critic compares the album's sound to anyone. The artist-level gospel influences are in artists.md.
- **landmark 1**: RS 500 in both lists; [RS20] says it ignited rock & roll.
- **confidence medium**: AllMusic and Time could not be opened.

## raycharles-modernsoundsincountryandwesternmusic: Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music (1962)

Sources:
- [RS20] RS 500 (2020, #127), https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-albums-of-all-time-32520/ray-charles-modern-sounds-in-country-and-western-music-2-32894/

- [WOC] Wide Open Country (2018), https://www.wideopencountry.com/ray-charles-modern-sounds-in-country-and-western-music/
- [RS19] RS (Stephen L. Betts), 2019, https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-country/ray-charles-modern-sounds-in-country-and-western-music-reissue-766914/
- [AP] AP via PBS NewsHour, May 14 2026, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/beyonce-taylor-swift-chaka-khan-among-25-artists-added-to-national-recording-registry

Evidence per cell:
- **genres**: [RS20] "Country and soul were deeply entangled Southern traditions... Ray Charles applying his gospel grit and luscious soul-pop strings to standards by Hank Williams"; [CG] "redefine American pop... broke down the walls between classic Tin Pan Alley and declasse Nashville". [AP] calls it a "groundbreaking country crossover" album. That gives country | soul | pop | soulpop | gospel; gospel goes last because it describes his vocal grit. I did not map "Tin Pan Alley" to a token.
- **energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 2**: ballad-heavy country standards ("I Can't Stop Loving You", "You Don't Know Me"), mixed with a few big-band swingers. [WOC] "volume one mixes up the pace throughout".
- **mood 3**: "lover's lament", "Born to Lose", alongside "Hey, Good Lookin'" ([RS20]).
- **theatrical 3 / polish 4**: [CG] "Sonically bolder (and schlockier)... its massed strings, horns, and choruses"; [RS20] "luscious soul-pop strings"; [CG] "overstated shows of emotion".
- **experimental 2**: [RS20] "the audacious boundary smasher its title promised". The boldness lies in the concept; the sound stays classic.
- **voice** raspy: [RS20] "gospel grit"; [CG] "cotton-field grit".
- **instruments / production**: piano (MusicBrainz, Ray Charles); tenor sax (MusicBrainz: David "Fathead" Newman solo, Don Wilkerson); strings, horns and choruses ([CG]) → strings | brass + stringarrangements | hornarrangements | choirvocals. MusicBrainz credits arrangers Marty Paich, Gerald Wilson and Gil Fuller, and backing vocals by The Raelettes.
- **lyrics**: heartbreak and love ([RS20] "lover's lament", "I Can't Stop Loving You"); social ([RS20]: "You Don't Know Me" "recast as a parable about race relations in light of the civil rights struggle").
- **producers**: Sid Feller ([WOC] "with producer Sid Feller"; MusicBrainz). MusicBrainz also lists Marty Paich as a producer, but every other source makes him the arranger and conductor, so I left him out. **Please check the LP credits.**
- **scene**: [WOC] "recorded at Hollywood's United Recording Studios and New York City's Capitol Studios".
- **influenced_by**: empty. The RS 2019 quote "I'm not a country singer. I'm a singer who sings country songs" is not an influence.
- **landmark 1**: [RS20] "the most popular album of Charles' career"; [CG] "defined modern country & western music".

- **influenced_by**: empty. - **lyrics** spiritual: liner notes, "This album is a humble offering to Him" ([RS20], [DB]).
- **producers**: Bob Thiele (MusicBrainz; engineer Rudy Van Gelder).
- **influenced_by / sounds_like**: empty. [BBC] mentions Miles Davis's Kind of Blue as the record that introduced modes to jazz, which is history, not a comparison of this album's sound. Coltrane's artist-level influences are in artists.md.
- **landmark 1**: [CG] "without question Coltrane's most beloved album"; [BBC] "A landmark recording".

Evidence per cell:
- **genres**: folkrock: [Parade] "the folk rock duo released Bookends" (said of the duo); [RSF] "the biggest, most creatively ambitious act to come out of the folk revival since Bob Dylan"; [RS68] "a kind of folk song feeling" on "Old Friends". pop: [Parade] "changed the game for what a pop album could be". rockandroll comes from the lead hit only: [RS68] calls "Mrs. Robinson" "even a rock and roll song", and [SGnews] says it was "the first song of the rock and roll genre to receive the GRAMMY". [RS68] also says the album as a whole is "not rock and roll". **Genre evidence is thin: medium confidence.**
- **mood 1**: [RS03] "a mostly dark, beautifully written voyage"; [SGbio] "the songs of Bookends seem even more unsettling".
- **polish 4**: [RS68] "it exudes a sense of process, and it is slick"; [Parade] more than 50 hours of studio work on "Punky's Dilemma", vocals "re-recorded... note by note".
- **experimental 3**: [RSF] "unprecedented album"; [RS68] "The strangest thing of all is a cut called 'Voices of Old People'"; [SGbio] "a conceptual song cycle".
- **electronic 1**: the Moog appears on one song (MusicBrainz: "Moog" credited to John Simon).
- **energy 2 / theatrical 2 / tempo 2 / groove 1**: [RS68] "nothing too much happens"; [RS03] "brilliant restraint". The side-two singles ("Mrs. Robinson", "Fakin' It") lift energy a little.
- **instruments**: guitar (MusicBrainz, Paul Simon); strings and horns ([SGbio] "a lovely string and horn arrangement"; [RS68] "strings are used with wild abandon", "a fine horn arrangement" on "America"); viola ([Parade] "They brought in viola and brass players"); Moog (MusicBrainz) → synth; drums (MusicBrainz, Hal Blaine).
- **production**: fieldrecordings ([Parade] "Garfunkel visited retirement homes with a tape recorder and captured elderly residents"); stringarrangements and hornarrangements ([SGbio]); moog (MusicBrainz); overdubbing ([Parade] "re-recorded vocal parts note by note").
- **voice**: empty. No source I opened describes their vocal timbre.
- **lyrics**: [SGalb] "themes of loss, the American Dream, youthful innocence and old age" → nostalgia; social ([RSF] "the passing of this nation's ideals in 'America'"); storytelling ([SGbio] "the vivid narrative 'America'"); introspection ([SGbio] "a meditation on the passage of life").
- **producers**: Paul Simon | Art Garfunkel | Roy Halee (MusicBrainz; [RS03] "The duo produced the record themselves"; [Parade] "co-producer Roy Halee"). John Simon and Bob Johnston are credited as "assistant" producers and are dropped, like "additional" credits.
- **sounds_like**: empty. [SGbio] compares the "Old Friends" climax to the Beatles' "A Day in the Life", but the official site speaks for the artist and is not a critic, so the comparison is left out. **The owner may want it as "The Beatles (Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band) [1/12]".**
- **landmark 1**: Paul Simon in [RS03] calls it "the quintessential Simon and Garfunkel album"; [RSF] "this classic". (Simon is the artist; the RS editors' "classic" is the supporting value judgment.)

## Reception notes
- Bookends: importance 4 and acclaim 4. The 1968 RS review was mixed; it is in the RS 500 2003/2012 editions (rank seen only in secondary listings, 233 or 234, so no number is given) but not in 2020, where Bridge Over Troubled Water is #172.
- A Love Supreme mainstream 0: [CG] says it "never cracked the Billboard 200" and that it was a through-composed devotional suite.
- Modern Sounds mainstream 3: an artist-led record ([WOC] "a bold move by both Charles and his bosses"), though Christgau calls the orchestrations "schlockier". Not packaged product.

## Legacy
- Otis Blue and Bookends: no professional source I could open says the album influenced later music, so they get no row. Craft's "influenced many of the world's biggest rock, pop and R&B acts" (on Here's Little Richard) is the label's value judgment, so it is not used.

## New tokens
- `spiritualjazz` (genre): Grammy.com 2024, quoted above. Spiritual jazz is a distinct movement (Coltrane, Pharoah Sanders, Alice Coltrane); `avantgardejazz` and `modaljazz` don't cover it.
