# Kylie Minogue — PHASE 01 evidence and judgement notes

Method: all eight 0–5 scales are independent editor judgements anchored to the Fandome guide; they are **not** numerical values quoted by reviewers. Marked sources are specific to the album. Blank values are abstentions. Source URLs: see companion `sources.csv` and `genre_evidence.csv`.

## Kylie (1988) — `kylie-kylie`

- **Original recording/date:** 4 July 1988, original ten-track album (Apple Music, MusicBrainz; not the US AllMusic reissue shown as December 1988). The original Australian non-SAW recording of *Locomotion* is **not** the canonical album SAW remake; Cherry Red notes the 1987 recording separately as a reissue extra.
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly uses pop, dance-pop and Euro-Dance, describes the album as Stock Aitken Waterman bubblegum; Classic Pop describes the breakthrough songs as hi-NRG; AOTY database provides teen pop, euro-disco and synthpop; Apple notes Europop in a midtempo track. `bubblegum` is literal new token. The non-dominant specific tags are deliberately ordered later. Do not infer `electropop` from `synthpop`.
- **Scales:** energy **4** from driving singles versus romantic midtempos; electronic **4** from dense credited keyboards, Linn 9000 and Apple keyboard textures; mood **4** from predominantly upbeat music with some longing; theatrical **2** from straightforward close pop rather than grand performance; experimental **0** from AllMusic's description of formulaic, accessible SAW songs; polish **4** from meticulously produced PWL studio pop; tempo **4** from broadly brisk arrangements; groove **4** from dance-oriented singles and a minority of less active midtempo songs. These are album judgements, not a tempo calculation.
- **Voice:** `clear` is editorial interpretation of Apple Music's direct account of Kylie's vocal delivery. No undocumented operatic/choral/rap or belting descriptors.
- **Instruments:** `keyboards` (Matt Aitken, Mike Stock, George De Angelis and Neil Palmer), `guitar` (Matt Aitken), `drummachine` (MusicBrainz resolves the fictional **A. Linn** drum credit as **Linn 9000** on *Je ne sais pas pourquoi*). Apple's 'brassy keyboard blasts' does **not** establish a real brass section; no brass token. Avoid `synth` without a credit distinguishing electronic synth from the blanket keyboard role.
- **Production:** `linn9000`, original-album track-specific direct model identification; do **not** mistake this for the separately tokenized `linndrum`. No reliable album-specific signal for analogtape, reverb hardware, autotune, Fairlight or other PWL gear, even though this was a technological studio.
- **Producers:** Matt Aitken, Mike Stock and Pete Waterman, all directly credited (MusicBrainz/AllMusic). Not guest musicians or secondary engineers.
- **Lyrics, language, scene:** predominantly romantic young-love/longing and some upbeat celebration, English with a French title/phrase rather than a separate full French-language repertoire. UK PWL mainstream pop scene despite Kylie's Australian origin.
- **Similarity/impact:** Rick Astley is explicitly compared in AllMusic, Sonia/Jason Donovan are editorial near-neighbour PWL candidates, not inspiration assertions. **Landmark 1** because it defines her first chart breakthrough, supported by Classic Pop and Official Charts. `influenced_by` blank: SAW collaboration is not influence.
- **Contradictions:** source metadata for launch date, drum-credit pseudonym and microgenre tags have differing certainty. Confidence medium, pending audit of studio liner notes for `linn9000` coverage across multiple tracks.

## Enjoy Yourself (1989) — `kylie-enjoyyourself`

- **Original scope:** 9 October 1989 original ten-track UK sequence per Apple Music; later/territorial releases include the Jason Donovan duet *Especially for You*, which was an earlier standalone hit. No duet credit in album artist field or independent album row. The separate *The Delinquents* tie-in does not make this record a soundtrack.
- **Genres:** AllMusic directly confirms album-level dance-pop and Euro-Dance, both retained as separate tokens. Classic Pop identifies bubblegum, R&B, synth-pop, dance and disco across the album, while Apple isolates the jazzy *Tell Tale Signs* and string-backed oldies *Tears on My Pillow*. AOTY lists electro-disco, euro-disco, teen pop and yé-yé. The yé-yé entry is an uncorroborated low-strength tag, preserved last; it must not outweigh the pop core.
- **Scales:** energy **3**, electronic **3** and groove **3** because several retro/string ballads interrupt high-energy dance numbers; mood **4** (upbeat dominant but not uniformly joyful); theatrical **2** (light romantic delivery, an occasional show tune); experimental **1** (stylistically varied yet conventional); polish **4** (professional PWL sheen); tempo **3** (mix of brisk club tracks and ballads). AllMusic underplays the variety relative to Classic Pop and Apple.
- **Voice:** `clear|soft` covers predominantly transparent pop lead and subdued treatment on the jazzy/romantic tracks, based on Apple Music's named songs; not a claim of a new vocal technique.
- **Instruments:** credited keyboards and guitar (Aitken, Stock, Ian Curnow), drum unit as attributed to `A. Linn — Drums` on AllMusic, and strings directly described by Apple Music and Classic Pop for the ballads. Real recorded strings are only evidenced on a subset. No `saxophone` or `piano` without a direct original-album player/instrument credit.
- **Production:** empty deliberately. Album-level `A. Linn` drum credit does not reliably reveal which precise model powered **original** 10 tracks. MusicBrainz identifies Linn 9000 on the original 1988 duet, but that item is a bonus track on later editions; transposition would be unsupported. No automatic `drumprogramming` from genre. This is more conservative than the 1988 row, for which there is original-track model evidence.
- **Producers:** all three SAW members credited as producers. `Tears on My Pillow` is a cover, not evidence that Little Anthony & The Imperials influenced Kylie's *production*.
- **Lyrics, similarity:** love, breakup pleading and celebration dominate. Compare Sonia, Jason Donovan and Rick Astley as sonic peers only. Career milestone in commercial terms, but **landmark 0** editorially: a close sequel rather than a central new artistic turn.
- **Critics disagree:** AllMusic calls it close to the debut; Classic Pop and Apple highlight jazz and oldies arrangements. Secondary `yeye` tag lacks strong direct review support. Confidence medium. 

## Rhythm of Love (1990) — `kylie-rhythmoflove`

- **Original album:** released 12 November 1990, 11 songs. Ignore later *Shocked* DNA/rapper remixes when classifying the original 1990 recording. First clear shift toward London's club language (Classic Pop, Apple Music).
- **Genres:** AllMusic calls it dance-pop and eurodance, Apple explicitly identifies house-influenced *Better the Devil You Know*, disco-pop *Step Back in Time*, electro *Count the Days* and adult-contemporary ballad *The World Still Turns*. AOTY adds house, diva house, freestyle, new jack swing, euro-house and hi-NRG at database-tag level. Specialist Classic Pop calls several album cuts 'swingbeat' and describes Italo-house-style piano riffs on *What Do I Have to Do*. New `swingbeat` is literal. Track-scope variants are downweighted by order and companion scope.
- **Scales:** energy **4** (strong club backbone), electronic **4** (keyboards and documented programmed percussion), mood **4** (upbeat/sensual album with reflective ballad), theatrical **3** (more assertive delivery), experimental **2** (genre expansion but pop structures intact), polish **4** (PWL and LA studio expertise), tempo **4** (dance tracks dominate), groove **4** (prominent club dynamics yet not a continuous dance-only album). 
- **Voice:** `clear` from Kylie's lead vocal, AllMusic's observation of more assured performance. No reason to tag `rap` from an **alternate remix** of Shocked.
- **Instruments:** credited `keyboards` (Stock/Aitken/Bray/Gaudette), `guitar` (Aitken/John DeFaria), `saxophone` (Jim Oppenheim). The original credits include A. Linn drums on the lead single (Qobuz), supporting generic `drummachine` only; do not specify LinnDrum or Linn 9000 for the entire album absent track-level model corroboration. String synthesizers cannot be inferred from the 70s-disco atmosphere.
- **Production:** `drumprogramming` explicitly credited to Michael Jay and Mark Leggett on AllMusic and Qobuz. No `sampling`: the D-Mob/Deee-Lite references below concern composition/style cues, not confirmed audio samples.
- **Producers:** ORIGINAL PWL sleeve summarized by 45cat gives SAW for most songs and exceptions Michael Jay (*The World Still Turns*), Keith Cohen (*One Boy Girl*), Bray/Cohen (*Count the Days*, *Rhythm of Love*). All six proper personal names included; exclude engineers, assistant producer Kimm James and keyboardist Ian Curnow. Streaming-platform per-song generic metadata overlaps; primary sleeve wins.
- **Influences:** *Classic Pop* explicitly links *Better the Devil You Know* to **D-Mob's** *C'mon and Get My Love*, and says **Deee-Lite** inspired *Step Back in Time*. These are **actual source-documented musical cues for this album**, not merely cover/sample relationships; they justify `D-Mob|Deee-Lite`. Madonna is **not** documented as an influence just because Stephen Bray had worked with her (but remains an editorial sonic neighbour in `sounds_like`).
- **Lyrics / landmark:** familiar relationships gain overt sensuality and late-night club attitude; remains predominantly English. **Landmark 1**: recognised critical coming-of-age and album more consistently praised than the prior two (AllMusic/Apple/Classic Pop). Confidence medium due high-granularity AOTY genres and mismatched streaming metadata.

## Let's Get to It (1991) — `kylie-letsgettoit`

- **Original album:** 14 October 1991, ten songs, original PWL album. Matt Aitken had departed SAW as a producing partner; Apple and Classic Pop both document increased Kylie writing involvement with Mike Stock and Pete Waterman.
- **Genres:** Classic Pop explicitly names *hip-hop, new jack swing, house, dance-pop* at album level. AllMusic adds Euro-Dance. AOTY applies Contemporary R&B, UK Street Soul and Euro House. Apple pinpoints house on *Right Here, Right Now* and adult-contemporary R&B ballad *If You Were with Me Now*. The genres are ordered to weight the recurrent dance-pop first and place substyles after; `rave` is **not** included solely because Kylie liked rave culture.
- **Scales:** energy **3** (peak-hour club tracks but significant ballads), electronic **4** (dense sampled/electronic club constructions and keyboards), mood **3** (ambiguous sensual and romantic tone), theatrical **3** (overt performance/persona shift), experimental **2** (genre cross-pollination within accessible pop), polish **4** (professionally engineered, glossy), tempo **3** (fast/slow contrast), groove **4** (multiple dance-floor tracks but not non-stop club music). Critic Chris True calls it inconsistent; Classic Pop praises stylistic breadth. These are compatible observations, not numerical contradictions.
- **Voice:** `clear|soft`, versatile clear pop lead plus soft R&B ballad alongside Keith Washington. No `rap` label just because tracks draw on hip-hop; no unsupported autotune.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz original-release track credits identify **Julian Gingell/Mike Stock keyboards**, **Gary Barnacle saxophone**, **John Thirkell trumpet** and **Peter Thoms trombone** on *Word Is Out*, and **Paul Riser strings arrangement** on *If You Were with Me Now*. `strings` is documented on a specific song through an arranger credit, but the string performers themselves are not independently listed; that makes this token more tentative than the others. Instruments are album-some-tracks, not necessarily all ten.
- **Production:** `sampling` Apple Music explicitly identifies use of a 2 Unlimited *Get Ready for This* sample on *I Guess I Like It Like That*. `stringarrangements` explicitly credited to Paul Riser by MusicBrainz. The featured 2 Unlimited record is **not** automatically listed in `influenced_by`; sample source ≠ declared inspiration. Further drum-machine model attribution is withheld.
- **Producers:** Mike Stock and Pete Waterman alone, per original MusicBrainz track production, AllMusic and label references. Matt Aitken's name is a songwriter credit on some songs and must **not** be entered as producer; Keith Washington is a guest vocalist/writer, not producer.
- **Lyrics, scene, impact:** flirtation, sensuality, romance, breakup and dance-floor invitation. Kylie contributes to more song credits and aligns herself with UK club music. **Landmark 0** as album quality and lasting canonical rank are mixed despite historically important autonomy; later pop reinventions become stronger defining albums.
- **Disagreement / edition issue:** AllMusic sees weak consistency, Classic Pop hears adventurous variation; both are logged. Source metadata occasionally labels Waterman as Paul versus Pete (his full name is Peter Waterman; **one producer only**). Album remains solo billed despite the Keith Washington duet. Confidence medium; no machine-model overclaims.
