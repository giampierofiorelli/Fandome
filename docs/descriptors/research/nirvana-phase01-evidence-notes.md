# Nirvana — phase 01 evidence notes

**Research cutoff:** 2026-10-27. **Status:** draft. All scales are editorial whole-album judgments, not machine measurements. Instrument and technique tokens require release-specific primary credits, performer/engineer recollections, or direct critique. Genre tokens reflect the linked genre evidence table.

## 1. Bleach (1989) — `nirvana-bleach`

- **Genre:** AllMusic classifies the album as grunge, alternative/indie rock and alternative pop/rock. The published *Louder Than Hell* book excerpt describes this specific record as a **punk-metal hybrid**. `punkmetal` is not yet an allowed token; add it to Fandome before validation. This description is not a general claim that the band was a metal act.
- **Scales:** `energy 4` because reviews describe aggressive, dense riffing and shouted singing while *About a Girl* reduces intensity. `electronic 0`: a documented guitar/bass/drum studio ensemble with no evidenced electronic sources. `mood 1`: menacing minor-key sound, isolation and friction; this is editorial tone, not a clinical description. `theatrical 2`: harsh expressive vocals without ornate staging. `experimental 2`: garage-rooted riffs with melodic deviations but recognisable song forms. `polish 1`: producer Endino's eight-track, quickly captured band sound versus the much glossier next album. `tempo 3`: contrasting stomping mid-tempos with quicker attacks. `groove 1`: riffs and head-nod pulse rather than dance-oriented arrangements. Anchors per repository guide.
- **Voice:** `raspy|powerful`: Cobain's rough scream is described in the contemporary interview and reviews, but there are contrasting quieter melodic tracks.
- **Instruments:** `electricguitar|bass|drums` from the Northwest Music Archives, track-level MusicBrainz and Jack Endino's studio accounts. Chad Channing is on most original material; Dale Crover drums on earlier recordings including *Floyd the Barber* and *Paper Cuts*. Jason Everman received a sleeve credit, but did **not** perform on the recorded album, as confirmed by the oral history and interview archive. Do not insert a second guitar based on that sleeve credit.
- **Production:** `distortion` from period electric guitar descriptions and Guitar World gear feature. **Do not** specify a named machine or analogue tape format simply because an eight-track recorder was used. Endino states it was a short, economical eight-track session, but the exact record format is not a required token without clear independent source.
- **Lyrics:** `social|introspection|irony`: Cobain's retrospective remark says the lyrics largely concern life in Aberdeen; social frustration, alienation, and sardonic caricature appear across the repertoire. Not every song fits every label.
- **Producer:** Jack Endino, supported by MusicBrainz and recording personnel histories. A recording engineer may be a producer if explicitly dual-credited; Endino is.
- **Influenced by:** `Wipers` directly substantiated by Cobain's 1990 statement to a journalist in Rolling Stone's retrospective; `Melvins` grounded in Cobain's account of watching their rehearsals and the parallel guitar/drum style discussed by early collaborators. Exclude Shocking Blue despite covering *Love Buzz* (cover does not equal documented artistic influence). `sounds_like` are transparent editorial sonic comparisons, not claimed direct influence.
- **Caution:** Release day differs from first limited availability at a June 1989 show, but calendar year is fixed. `Bleach` is an original studio album even when modern reissues include live bonus tracks. No instruments or software from those bonus tracks enter the row.

Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/bleach-mw0000202932), [Northwest Music Archives](https://nwmusicarchives.com/record/bleach/), [Endino interview](https://www.prosoundweb.com/in-the-studio-an-interview-with-grunge-pioneer-jack-endino/), [Wipers / Cobain](https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/wipers-greg-sage-interview-is-this-real-16328/).

## 2. Nevermind (1991) — `nirvana-nevermind`

- **Genre:** AllMusic names grunge, alternative/indie rock and alternative pop/rock. `punk` is a secondary sonic layer grounded in Vig's description of hook-driven pop songs with punk attitude; `punk` follows the broader main identities.
- **Scales:** `energy 4`: dominant high-volume attack, with *Polly* and *Something in the Way* limiting an unqualified maximum. `electronic 0`: rock instrumentation; outboard studio processing does not transform instrument-source classification. `mood 2`: anxious and sour lyrical register with exuberant or anthemic passages. `theatrical 3`: explosive dynamics and urgent vocals without an overtly dramatic concept. `experimental 2`: striking loud/soft contrasts inside mostly conventional songcraft. `polish 4`: intentionally doubled vocals and guitars, strong mix separation and a comparatively glossy finish. `tempo 3`: mix of midtempo choruses, fast punk attacks and ballad. `groove 2`: rhythmic hooks, not an album made for dance floor.
- **Instruments:** `electricguitar|bass|drums|acousticguitar|cello` documented in release credits and Butch Vig's recollections. Acoustic guitar appears on *Polly* and *Something in the Way*; Kirk Canning added cello to the latter. Dave Grohl, not Chad Channing, plays drums on the commercially released Sound City material; the *Polly* demo's recording provenance is different. These are track-specific accents, ordered after the core trio.
- **Production:** `layeredvocals|overdubbing|analogtape|distortion|liverecording`. Vig explicitly describes doubling the lead voice and instruments, then overdubbing onto live-taken band performances. *Sound on Sound* specifies a **Studer 24-track analogue** machine and live room. Electric-guitar distortion is directly identified in these accounts. `liverecording` means tracking performed together **in the studio**, not a concert. Not `sampling`: Vig/Wallace reverb and sample discussion varies by source and use is not required for coherent album-level description. Do not add a specific model of reverb hardware.
- **Voice / lyrics:** `raspy|powerful|soft` captures shouting against hushed songs; `introspection|identity|social|storytelling` from critical discussions of alienation, fractured identity and narrative in *Polly*. Themes are not imposed on every song.
- **Producer:** MusicBrainz release credits say `Nirvana|Butch Vig` as producer; main externally credited producer is Vig, so he appears first. Andy Wallace is **mixer**, not producer, and is excluded.
- **Influenced by:** Kurt Cobain explicitly called *Smells Like Teen Spirit* an attempt to emulate the **Pixies'** loud/soft dynamics. The named song is central, and `Pixies` is the only influence attached to this album without extending the claim to every track. Beatles/John Lennon were cited by Vig as precedent for accepting double-tracking; that is a **production persuasion anecdote**, not documentation that the Beatles inspired the whole album's writing.
- **Disagreement:** Cobain later criticized the slickness of the final mix, while Vig defended its straightforward band feel. `polish 4` describes what can be heard and what production details demonstrate, without assigning motive to all band members.

Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nevermind-mw0000185616), [Vig at Waves](https://www.waves.com/behind-the-music-with-butch-vig), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/butcvig-nevermind-garbage), [1994 Cobain interview](https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/features/kurt-cobain-the-rolling-stone-interview-anniversary-death-38446/), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/eccae410-7577-4daa-b602-92d305828331).

## 3. In Utero (1993) — `nirvana-inutero`

- **Genres:** AllMusic labels grunge and alternative pop/rock / alternative indie rock. SHFL explicitly calls it noise rock and alternative rock. Album of the Year explicitly classifies it post-hardcore as a secondary genre. `punk` is documented by SHFL's tag and critics' description of the album's punk attack, placed last as a broad/texture value. Not every ballad is noise rock.
- **Scales:** `energy 4`: relentless distorted material plus tender passages (*Dumb*, *All Apologies*). `electronic 0`: recorded guitar/bass/drum ensemble, guest cello; noise does not imply electronics. `mood 0`: repeatedly bleak, confrontational, painful imagery described by critics, while tenderness remains. `theatrical 3`: unguarded screams and contrasts but not overt stage drama. `experimental 4`: noisy arrangements, room-focused recording and abrasive feedback extending mainstream-rock conventions. `polish 2`: purposeful raw spatial naturalism compared with *Nevermind*. `tempo 3`: fast outbursts, lurching midtempo tracks and subdued ballads. `groove 1`: rhythm attacks rather than dance patterns.
- **Instruments:** `electricguitar|bass|drums|cello`. MusicBrainz release-specific track credits identify Cobain, Novoselic, Grohl and cellist Kera Schaley. Do not substitute Kirk Canning (who played on *Nevermind*) or Lori Goldston (the later Unplugged guest).
- **Production:** `roomambience|liverecording|distortion`. Cobain in his MTV interview, Dave Grohl's 1993 interview and the scholarly analysis describe Steve Albini's natural room-mic method, live-playing band, minimal manipulation and high-volume distorted guitars. Avoid `layeredvocals`: Pitchfork reports the lead singing here was recorded without overdubs. **Scott Litt remixed two tracks**, but that does not give him a producer credit.
- **Producer credit nuance:** Steve Albini is entered as `producer` because MusicBrainz's original-release metadata explicitly credits him that way, and contemporary trade and qualified reviews call him producer. However the **original sleeve says “Recorded by Steve Albini”**, and Albini himself rejected the producer title. The receiving editor should honour the original sleeve if the database is strictly keyed to sleeve roles, in which case clear the cell and preserve the documented recordist in the note. This is not evidence of Scott Litt being a producer.
- **Influenced by:** Cobain's Sept. 1993 interview specifically associates the album's target sound with Albini-recorded **Pixies** and **Breeders** records. Grohl also names Albini's earlier **Jesus Lizard** work in connection with the band's chosen drum tone. These are recording/aesthetic reference points, not evidence of lifted songwriting. `sounds_like` includes The Jesus Lizard, Pixies, Shellac and Big Black as **editorial similarity**, not direct artist statements; Shellac postdates the recording and is explicitly *not* listed as an influence.
- **Lyrics / voice:** `introspection|identity|social|irony|love` justified by critics' references to body and privacy, fame and sardonic songs; `raspy|powerful|soft` reflects abrasive delivery and vulnerable ballads.
- **Disagreement:** The album's sound is consistently described as less polished than *Nevermind*, but critics differ on how much its melodies undermine the stated retreat from pop. Keep the broad-genre weight and experimental score independent from this disagreement.

Sources: [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/6ddad044-c72c-3eb9-8ae7-45f783a92e0f), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/18517-nirvana-in-utero-20th-anniversary-edition/), [Dave Grohl 1993 interview](https://www.livenirvana.com/interviews/9311ts/index.php), [Cobain Sept. 1993 interview](https://www.livenirvana.com/interviews/9309jg/index.php), [Albini technical study](https://www.artofrecordproduction.com/aorpjoom/arp-conferences/arp-archive-conference-papers/19-arp-2007/126-ohare-2007).

## 4. MTV Unplugged in New York (1994) — `nirvana-mtvunpluggedinnewyork`

- **Format and status:** A Sony Music Studios concert for MTV on **18 Nov. 1993**, first issued as an album in **1994**. This is a **landmark live exception**, not a studio record. Its exceptional role is demonstrated by Pitchfork's retrospective and the official Grammy win for Best Alternative Music Performance in 1996. It is already in Fandome catalog.
- **Genres:** Album of the Year lists `acousticrock`, `alternativerock`, `folkrock` and `grunge`. AllMusic additionally supplies `alternativeindierock` and `alternativepoprock`. Grunge reflects re-arranged repertoire and performance identity more than instrument density; `folkrock` particularly suits the covers. Explicitly **do not** treat an acoustic live release as electronic just because stage microphones amplified the instruments.
- **Scales:** `energy 2`: mostly contained performance with peaks at final vocal crescendos. `electronic 0`: acoustic guitar, bass, drums, cello and accordion; no synthesizers on original concert. `mood 1`: subdued, mournful and often stark despite some playful remarks. `theatrical 2`: restrained arrangement, atmospheric set, heightened emotional vocal endings. `experimental 2`: bold cover choices and radical recontextualisation but straightforward acoustic forms. `polish 3`: professionally mixed and balanced documented concert recording without studio-scale multitrack layering claim. `tempo 2`: mostly languid to moderate; `groove 1`: not dance-oriented.
- **Instruments:** `acousticguitar|bass|cello|drums|accordion` are directly confirmed by album/track credits. Cobain and Pat Smear play guitars; Lori Goldston plays cello on multiple tracks; Novoselic plays accordion on *Jesus Doesn't Want Me for a Sunbeam*; Dave Grohl sometimes switches instruments. Do not claim wholly unamplified instruments or that **all** guitar sounds are a single acoustic-guitar type. No invented keyboard models.
- **Production:** `concertrecording` only. The performance was recorded in front of a studio audience for MTV; no sourced instrument processing/machinery is entered. The music is live in the performance sense, not the different `liverecording` studio-tracking category.
- **Producers:** Album and MusicBrainz credits name **Nirvana and Scott Litt**. Alex Coletti receives an MTV program producer credit in some records; do not combine show-level and recording-level album credits. MTV executive producer is also excluded.
- **Influenced by:** left empty deliberately. Covers by David Bowie, Vaselines, Meat Puppets and Lead Belly do **not** automatically prove those artists inspired the recorded album's musical choices. (The Vaselines are directly attested as a broad Nirvana influence, and appear in ARTIST INFLUENCES in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`.) The actual covered artists may appear under editorial `sounds_like` where relevant, without masquerading as sources of composition.
- **Lyrics:** Album lyrics include covers by other writers. `introspection|heartbreak|identity|storytelling` reflects content across the set, not an invented Cobain-written concept. Scope varies by track.

Sources: [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/nirvana-mtv-unplugged-in-new-york/), [Amoeba original credits](https://www.amoeba.com/ajax/album_credits.php?id=784344), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9e54862b-f0ad-4f08-9dbc-cfbe1a0b24ab/disc/1), [Grammy official](https://www.grammy.com/awards/categories/best-alternative-music-performance/1996/).
