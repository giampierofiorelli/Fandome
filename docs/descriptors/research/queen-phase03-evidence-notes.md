# Queen Phase 03 — album evidence notes

**Scope:** Original 1980/82/84/86 records, not bonus tracks or later reissues. Source IDs correspond to `queen_phase03_sources.csv`. Scale numbers are editorial Fandome judgements based on the album descriptions, recording history and cited instrumentation; the genre matrix maps every selected genre to specific sources and track/album scope.

## Flash Gordon (1980) — `queen-flashgordon`

**Album identity / production.** [F1] explicitly calls *Flash Gordon* the band's one full feature-film soundtrack; the film contains Howard Blake's additional orchestral cues. The recorded album, however, contains **some**, not all, of those orchestrated passages. [F5] original sleeve states Brian May and Mack as producers, Queen as the writers/arrangers/performers. Because [F1] also says the material was produced collectively by Queen, this is a **credit-level ambiguity**: the narrower printed May/Mack producer credit is preferred; the wider Queen creative role goes in these notes. Howard Blake was an arranger/conductor and must not be inserted in `producers`. This is a film **score** and should not be classified simply as a rock album [F1,F2].

**Genres and source disagreements.** [F2] calls it a primarily instrumental album of short passages with film dialogue, identifying *Battle Theme* as a heavy-metal moment and *The Hero* as a proper rocker. [F3,F4] explicitly pair **rock** with **progressive electronica** and the original album with overlaid cinematic voices. [F8] separately uses **progressive electronic** and ambient as genre terms. The former is **not silently mapped** onto *progressive electronica*; both terms appear because they are lexically different classifications. The original `soundtrack` token remains accompanied by actual sonic genres. Generic AllMusic rock taxonomy sits behind its two most distinctive film/electronic elements. Strictly avoid adding `opera` purely because Queen called the movie high-camp or because the film's score has orchestral components [F2,F3].

**Instruments.** Four musicians’ synthesized sounds, Brian's guitar, John's bass and Roger's kit from original album musician credits [F5]. Brian specifically played piano on *Flash's Theme* / *The Hero* and organ on *The Wedding March* [F5]. `orchestra` only for cues involving Blake's arrangements that survived onto the record [F1]. **No individual synth model is verified for the whole original 1980 album**; a speculative Oberheim on a single cue [F7 and Queen Songs' Football Fight reconstruction] does not warrant a model token. No Fairlight/Emulator is invented.

**Production and voice.** `sampling` is meant in the broader sense of interweaving authentic film dialogue and effects into the recorded music, documented by band testimony [F1,F3]; it is NOT a claim that Queen used a digital hardware sampler for those excerpts. `overdubbing` is supported by layering musical cues and film speech described in [F1,F3]. No specific synth-programming technique is documented, so `synthprogramming` is left out of the row. `voice=instrumental|powerful|spoken|choral` puts mostly instrumental first; `spoken` covers cast dialogue that recurs without becoming sung narrative. `lyrics=storytelling` is a **limited two-song** and thematic tag; identity/romance relate primarily to film songs. `language=en` refers to the English-language songs and dialogue, not just narration.

**Scales.** `energy=3` covers quiet cue transitions and surges such as *Battle Theme*; `electronic=4` follows the high synthesizer share beyond *The Game* [F1]. `mood=3` balances ominous Ming cues and triumphal heroic passages [F2]. `theatrical=5` reflects score-plus-dialogue function [F1,F3]. `experimental=4` for radical-for-Queen track format and dialogue montage, not atonal composition [F2]. `polish=4` for studio film production and arranged ensemble [F1]. `tempo=3` variable from atmospheric intervals to fast rock action; `groove=1` fragmented dramatic cue flow rather than dance structures [F2]. `sounds_like` list is editorial only; no specific musical influence is firmly documented for this album.

## Hot Space (1982) — `queen-hotspace`

**Genre argument.** Queen's own official account [H1] identifies the explicit disco/funk direction following *Another One Bites the Dust*; [H2] describes the first side as new-wave dance-pop dominated by machines and the second as a partial return to rock. Aggregator [H7] gives exact `synthfunk`, `electrodisco`, `newwave`, `funkrock`, `dancepop` genre labels; they are source-attributed with the mix of album-wide and narrow scope. [H5] hears soul and R&B influence in individual tracks. Contemporary and later reviews sharply disagree whether the dance experiment worked [H1,H2,H5]. **The whole album is not solely disco**: *Under Pressure*, *Put Out the Fire* and *Life Is Real* have clear rock/ballad identities.

**Credits / co-billing.** Producer Queen and Mack on album [H1], Queen and Bowie on *Under Pressure* [H1,H4], so `producers=Queen|Mack|David Bowie` includes a **track-specific** co-producer. Arif Mardin's horns and `additional producer` label on *Staying Power* do **not** satisfy the rule for regular producer; exclude despite some metadata sites listing him [H3,H4]. David Richards worked on sessions but was assistant engineer, not full album producer [H1,H3]. David Bowie was **not** a guest throughout; his early vocals from *Cool Cat* were removed before retail release [H5]. The album is credited **Queen**, not Queen & David Bowie, even though the closing song is a duet.

**Instrument / machine specificity.** The specialist recording dossier [H3] documents the synthesizer, **synth bass**, numerous **drum computers**, piano, electric/acoustic guitars, acoustic drums, a clavinet on *Cool Cat*, electric organ on *Under Pressure*, and saxophones on *Action This Day* / *Under Pressure*; brass players on *Staying Power*. Include `electricpiano` for the track-specific electric piano on *Cool Cat* per detailed MusicBrainz credits [H4]. [H3] expressly reports **Roland Jupiter-8** and the **Linn LM-1** drum computer. The latter does **not** equal LinnDrum LM-2, which is why this package proposes new `linnlm1`. If the newer genre/instrument validator does not accept it yet, Claude must add it before import. [H3] mentions possible other guitar/key models but unverified ones are excluded. Production `analogtape` and `overdubbing` based on multitrack/synchronized overdubs; `programming` and `drumprogramming` supported by band/technical credits [H3]. 

**Vocals / words / inspiration.** Freddie's prominent high/falsetto *Cool Cat* vocal is documented by [H5], hence `production=falsetto`, **not** `voice=falsetto` (not in the guide). Scat-like play and rhythmic backing voices appear elsewhere [H4]. Lyrics relate to attraction (*Body Language*), social/nuclear themes (*Put Out the Fire*), and Lennon tribute (*Life Is Real*) [H2]. `language=en|es` because *Las Palabras de Amor* includes a recurring short Spanish phrase; album is overwhelmingly English. A 1982 interview with Brian May specifically cites **the Isley Brothers** and black American music as stylistic influences in describing this LP [H6]; this is album-level musical influence, not guest performance or a mere resemblance. No proof that Bowie is an artistic model here simply because he was a co-writer/guest. 

**Scales.** `energy=3`: medium funk/dance and some fierce guitar songs; `electronic=4`: first album dominated by machines and synth bass [H2,H3]; `mood=3`: playful/sexual material with a moving closing duet and cautionary songs; `theatrical=3`: more direct pop than 1975's grand operatic displays; `experimental=3`: sharp stylistic reset while using familiar dance structures; `polish=4`: clean controlled studio 24-track sound [H3]; `tempo=3`: mid-tempo disco/funk with faster and slower cuts; `groove=4`: rhythm-based side one with substantial counterexamples [H1,H2].

## The Works (1984) — `queen-theworks`

**Album-level genre.** Queen explicitly presents this record as a return to rock [W1] following the disco-directed predecessor. [W2,W6] cover both guitar songs and machine pop. [W6] explicitly labels *Radio Ga Ga* `synthpop`, **not** generically electropop; [W5] calls *Machines* early electronica. `electronica` therefore sits among the late, limited genres rather than defining the whole LP. `newwave` is similarly centered on radio-pop arrangement [W7]. Retro 1950s `rockandroll` belongs only to *Man on the Prowl* [W2]. AllMusic generic `albumrock`, `arenarock`, `contemporarypoprock` and `artrock` are documentary publisher categories, not inferred directly from all songs.

**Producers/instruments.** Queen and Mack (not Richards or Mandel) are credited [W1,W3]. Fred Mandel supplied the piano finish of *Man on the Prowl*, synthesizers and programming to *Radio Ga Ga* / *I Want to Break Free*; [W3] explicitly credits Mack with the Fairlight programming on *Machines*. Roger Taylor provided the drums and vocoder; [W4] identifies synth/drum computer/sampler contribution on *Radio Ga Ga*. Acoustic guitar and piano evidence also from *Is This the World We Created?* and studio analyses [W2,W9]. Human handclaps in *Radio Ga Ga* are explicitly part of its arrangement [W7]. 

**Model discrimination.** Roland instrument manufacturer's research [W7] names **Jupiter-8**, LinnDrum and **Roland VP-330** for *Radio Ga Ga* (note that the article begins with a demo, then covers the released track). Universal's [W6] also names **Oberheim OB-Xa** and **Linn LM2** used on *The Works*. The Linn LM2 is the LinnDrum token already in Fandome; the Oberheim OB-Xa is **neither** the OB-X (1980) nor OB-8 (a different model). **New** token `oberheimobxa`; do not replace by the generic `oberheim` and lose the distinct model. `fairlight`, `sampling`, `vocoder`, `synthprogramming`, `drumprogramming` are all recording-credit or studio account supported; no digital effect is claimed simply from the modern video remix of *Machines* [W3,W4,W5,W7]. `overdubbing` and `layeredvocals` are attested via the multi-part studio contributions and choruses [W8]. 

**Lyrics / influences.** `nostalgia` and `social` on *Radio Ga Ga* reflect lament for disappearing radio culture; `political` on *Hammer to Fall* is the Cold War/nuclear threat, [W2]. `love` and `heartbreak` cover *It's a Hard Life*, `identity` *I Want to Break Free*, `introspection` *Is This the World We Created?* [W2,W6]. A 2025 Roland analysis hears similarities to Roxy Music on *Radio Ga Ga*'s guitar, but cautiously says it **seems** influenced: that is **insufficient** to assert actual influence [W7], so `influenced_by` is empty. Comparisons in `sounds_like` are consciously subjective. 

**Scales.** `energy=4`: hard-rock songs *Tear It Up*, *Hammer to Fall* with synthetic anthems; `electronic=3`: mixes synth/drummachine/fairlight with recognisable live quartet [W4,W6]; `mood=3`: uplift and nostalgia counterbalance nuclear anxiety / heartbroken material; `theatrical=4`: stadium choruses and marked vocal delivery; `experimental=3`: track *Machines* and electronic/human sonic duality [W5]; `polish=4`: controlled 1980s production with synthetic layers, reviewer [W2] critiques somewhat reduced sonic punch; `tempo=3`: rock speeds and ballad/slow tracks; `groove=3`: rhythmic opener/dance songs but also rock and acoustic finale.

## A Kind of Magic (1986) — `queen-akindofmagic`

**Original identity.** This is a **studio** LP including several *Highlander*-inspired songs revised for the record and several unrelated tracks; Queen's actual full film score was the prior *Flash Gordon* [A1,A2]. The title track is a notably funkier pop adaptation of a darker film version [A8,A10]. Some reviewers call the album moving anthem rock; some criticize it as inconsistent because it mixes different songwriting mandates [A2,A6]. *One Vision* arose after Live Aid, **not** originally from *Highlander* [A1]. Therefore `lyrics=storytelling|identity|love|heartbreak|introspection` includes film mortality and relationships but does not mean each lyric is film dialogue.

**Credited producers.** Original album credits list Queen plus Mack **or** David Richards across tracks [A3,A4]. `Queen|Mack|David Richards` exactly encompasses credited full producers. **Michael Kamen was the score composer and conductor/arranger for *Who Wants to Live Forever***, not producer of this Queen LP, and not a singer; separate official Kamen catalogue [A5]. Joan Armatrading guest incidental vocal on *Don't Lose Your Head* and saxophonist Steve Gregory on *One Year of Love* are musicians, not producer credits [A1,A3,A4]. Lynton Naiff contributes strings arrangement to the latter [A3]. 

**Instruments.** Core band guitars/bass/drums/synth/piano, track keyboards from Spike Edney, drum machines and sampled keyboard as specifically credited on individual tracks [A3,A4]. `orchestra|strings` from the National Philharmonic Orchestra on *Who Wants to Live Forever* per the arranger's site [A5]. `saxophone` from Steve Gregory on *One Year of Love* [A3]. Acoustic guitar is excluded because no sufficiently precise original-LP credit was verified. Instruments are ordered by prevalence and significance rather than alphabetical; none derived merely because they are common in 1980s synth pop.

**Sampled/altered vocals.** In an original February 1986 producer interview [A7], Mack describes using the **Kurzweil 250** to sample lines sung by Mercury and play them lower in pitch, creating “One Vision”'s opening and closing effects. This directly supports `kurzweil250|sampling|pitchedvocals`; not confused with later Kurzweil K2000. In that same account he explains multilayer backing vocals and recorded stereo mix, supporting `layeredvocals` and `overdubbing`. Some reverse sounds are reported by bandmember Roger Taylor [A7 and A6]; `backmasking|tapeeffects` concern this **one track**. A 1986 mixing-to-digital-stereo statement is not enough to classify all multitrack recording as DDD or `digitalrecording` [A7], so that token is omitted. `synthprogramming|drumprogramming` have verified track-specific support [A4,A7]. The detailed model for Spike Edney's keyboards on CD-only *Forever* is disputed (DX-7 vs Emulator II): do not infer either model on the original album [Queen Songs, original non-LP track excluded].

**Genres and scale assignments.** [A2] explicitly gives AllMusic hard rock, dance-rock, art rock, arena rock and contemporary-pop rock; synth textures and 1986 dance grooves are supported by [A6,A8]. `synthpop|funk` concern predominantly the recut title track, not the epic rocker *Princes of the Universe*. `heavymetal` comes specifically from reviewer [A12] describing *Gimme the Prize*; it is weaker than broad hard-rock tagging. `energy=4` anthems and strong rock balanced by orchestral ballads; `electronic=3` drum machines/programmed pop alongside guitars, drums and orchestra; `mood=3` mortality/bitterness alongside triumphant hit singles; `theatrical=5` sword-and-sorcery dramatic delivery + Kamen orchestration; `experimental=3` pitch-sampled One Vision opening but otherwise standard pop song form; `polish=5` extended multitrack work and synthesizers/orchestral production; `tempo=3` dance mid-tempo + ballad and heavy rock spread; `groove=3` title and other rhythm tracks beside ballads/cinematic numbers.

## Import safeguards

- Genre rows have one line **per token**, with evidence ID, track-to-album scope and evidence strength. New `progressiveelectronica` is not an alias for `progressiveelectronic`.
- `sounds_like` entries have no source because they are **explicitly editorial** proposed sonic comparisons, not asserted historical influence.
- No archival live/compilation tracks or 12-inch remixes are used to characterise the original albums. *A Kind of Magic* is **not** the independently released Michael Kamen *Highlander* score.
- The sources vary in authority; where an exact equipment model derives from a specialist historical site or later manufacturer article it is explicitly indicated. Don't claim model certainty from a studio photograph or stage gear alone. The Arif Mardin producer role on *Staying Power* and Brian May versus Queen production interpretation on *Flash Gordon* are documented, not silently erased.
