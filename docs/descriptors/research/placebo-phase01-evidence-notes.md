# Placebo Phase 01 — evidence notes (editorial, not lyrics/review reproduction)

## `placebo-placebo` — *Placebo* (1996)

- **Genres and contested scene:** AllMusic places the 1996 debut in alternative/indie rock, neo-glam and Britpop and hears post-punk and art rock. Molko's retrospective self-description is unequivocally *indie rock*, a fast, punk-oriented trio. **Britpop is contested:** Molko repudiates the grouping. The token is deliberately at the end of the genre list with contextual, disputed scope. `punkrock` reflects Molko's first-person punky alternative description, not a claim of full orthodox punk instrumentation. Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/placebo-mw0000648920), [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/).
- **Eight scales (own anchored judgments):** energy **4** driven by hard, brisk trio songs; electronic **1** because guitars and acoustic drums dominate despite Moog/keytar; mood **1** from tense, outsider themes rather than brightly euphoric arrangements; theatrical **3** for vivid, androgynous frontman delivery; experimental **3** for guitar noise, extended track structure and unconventional textures; polish **1** because Molko calls the record under-produced; tempo **4** driven by brisk songs; groove **2** driving rock beats but not a dance record. Moods are album tendencies, not every song.
- **Vocals and themes:** `powerful|soft` approximates emphatic high-register Molko delivery and quieter passages; `identity`/`sensuality` especially relate to *Nancy Boy*, while self-questioning and irreverence recur. Molko discusses identity in [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/androgyny-in-the-uk-392400). Avoid the false equation of high voice with classical operatic singing.
- **Instrument credits:** MusicBrainz originals identify both acoustic and electric guitar, bass, drum kit, percussion, Stefan Olsdal's Moog, baby grand and Rhodes piano, Molko's keytar, Robert Schultzberg's didgeridoo. `piano` is baby grand, `electricpiano` is Rhodes, `synth` is Moog. `didgeridoo` and `keytar` are **new tokens**; both named explicitly but their exact prominence track by track is uncertain. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/09e6b825-e770-4cdf-b1fe-68b8f9104396).
- **Production:** Molko recalls analog tape editing with a razor blade and a largely live power-trio recording, supporting `analogtape|liverecording`. Credits support the generic `moog` machine tag without giving an exact model number. **No Pro Tools:** Molko expressly says they were not using it. Brad Wood is main producer; Phil Vinall credited *Nancy Boy*. [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/09e6b825-e770-4cdf-b1fe-68b8f9104396).
- **Influences vs similarity:** Sonic Youth, Pixies and PJ Harvey have first-person band-level influence evidence, documented in [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/androgyny-in-the-uk-392400); their weight specifically in this album cannot be isolated. Smashing Pumpkins and Rush in [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/placebo-mw0000648920) are the critic's **sonic comparisons** and belong to `sounds_like` only. Suede similarity is editorial and not claimed as a documented influence.
- **Landmark:** defining debut containing *Nancy Boy* and *Teenage Angst*, and significant initial UK success, supported by [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/placebo-mw0000648920) and [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/).

## `placebo-withoutyouimnothing` — *Without You I’m Nothing* (1998)

- **Genres:** Source agreement on alternative/indie guitar rock with explicit glam rock, neo-glam, punk, post-punk. `electrorock` is **track-local** to *Brick Shithouse* according to [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/without-you-im-nothing-r380605). Britpop remains a low-priority and disputed AllMusic style tag, not a blanket genre. [JAM! Music interview](https://www.placeborussia.ru/press/press98-99/jammusicnov98) and [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-nov-28-ca-48385-story.html).
- **Scales:** energy **3** (rock singles contrasted with slow fragile pieces); electronic **2** (keyboards and programmed *Pure Morning* but still a guitar band); mood **0** (Molko specifically frames the album as heartbreak and melancholy); theatrical **3** (stylised glam stance with vulnerable vocals); experimental **3** (noise, electronics and hidden experimental end track); polish **3** (more deliberate layering than debut); tempo **3** (moderate across slower songs and uptempo cuts); groove **2** (some strong rhythmic cuts, but not dance-forward). Sources: [Molko interview](https://www.placeborussia.ru/press/press98-99/jammusicnov98), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-nov-28-ca-48385-story.html), [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/).
- **Instruments:** guitar, bass and drums confirmed in [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/without-you-im-nothing-r380605); keyboards are explicitly recalled by Molko as part of the 1998 expansion in [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/); piano/other percussion appear in original personnel summaries, track use variable. Do **not** infer specific synthesizer models from a keyboard credit. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/febbd996-fc2b-4811-a389-ef339c015664) distinguishes track producers and programming.
- **Production:** `programming` is Phelan Kane's specific *Pure Morning* credit. `sampling` is supported by MusicBrainz's credit identifying an Aphex Twin sample on the hidden *Evil Dildo*; this is a **small-track-scope** attribute. Neither Aphex Twin nor the later Bowie vocal duet is an album-specific influence / producer. Steve Osborne produced tracks 2–12; Phil Vinall produced track 1. Other engineers and mixers are excluded from `producers`.
- **Vocals / lyrics:** `soft|powerful` reflects alternating intimate and urgent lead delivery; lyrical tags reflect Molko's explicit accounts of broken romance, introspection and identity. [JAM!](https://www.placeborussia.ru/press/press98-99/jammusicnov98), [Los Angeles Times interview](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1999-jan-24-ca-1022-story.html).
- **Documented influences:** Molko expressly identifies Sonic Youth, PJ Harvey, Nick Cave and the Cure when discussing **this record** in JAM!. Joy Division/Bowie discussed and acknowledged as shaping the early Placebo sound in [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/all-made-up-with-somewhere-to-go-1173047.html); unlike the Cure comparisons alone, these have artist testimony. `sounds_like` contains noncausal press comparisons to the Jesus and Mary Chain, Dinosaur Jr., Velvet Underground etc. [LA Times review](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-nov-28-ca-48385-story.html).
- **Landmark:** signature second record including *Pure Morning* and *Every You Every Me*, widely discussed in contemporary criticism. Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/without-you-im-nothing-r380605), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-nov-28-ca-48385-story.html).

## `placebo-blackmarketmusic` — *Black Market Music* (2000)

- **Genres and scope:** original contemporary reviews classify alternative/indie, neo-glam and pop punk; Pitchfork calls Placebo power-pop. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/6327-black-market-music/) specifically calls *Taste in Men* industrial funk; [Drowned in Sound](https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-album-review-placebo-black-market-music-13/) notes electronic rock overlays and a guest rap on *Spite & Malice*. Accordingly `industrialfunk` and `hiphop` are **single-track descriptors at the end**, not the album's core genres. `electrorock` is track-limited stylistic synthesis based on explicitly described electro/rock elements; Claude may downgrade if requiring literal genre-name wording rather than composite judgement. `punkrock` follows NME's punk-pop stylistic reporting and is secondary.
- **Scales:** energy **4** (hard-driving aggressive rock tracks); electronic **2** (drum loop, keyboard textures and programmed strings amid conventional rock band); mood **0** (Molko himself retrospectively calls it very somber); theatrical **3** (signature vocal theatricality); experimental **2** (a rap experiment and electronic textures within conventional song forms); polish **3** (thicker, controlled studio sonics); tempo **3** (fast songs interspersed with slower darker pieces); groove **3** (especially bass/loop rhythm on *Taste in Men*). [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/), [Drowned](https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-album-review-placebo-black-market-music-13/).
- **Instrument evidence:** guitars, bass and drums of the named three-piece; keyboard and piano heard/described in contemporaneous NME and Drowned in Sound reporting. Bass specifically credited for guest Bill Lloyd in *Peeping Tom*. No unsupported live orchestra. Source: [Liner credit transcription](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/placebo/black-market-music-album_frfcnv.html), [Drowned in Sound](https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-album-review-placebo-black-market-music-13/).
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` belongs to Rob Ellis' arranger credit and `programming` to credited Dimitri Tikovoï string programming, neither musician is a producer. `sampling` = *Slave to the Wage*'s credited sample of *Texas Never Whispers* by Pavement, explicitly acknowledged as looped/speeded up by Molko; `drumloop` = the contemporaneous review of *Taste in Men*. No `tb303` or `heavyreverb`: there is not a sufficiently precise studio source to claim the former, and piano reverb alone does not establish *heavy* reverb. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/80a3fcd3-e777-4778-8087-8a7327517c60), [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/), [Drowned](https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-album-review-placebo-black-market-music-13/).
- **Producer caveat:** On the original core album, Paul Corkett and **Placebo** share producer credits; Dare Mason has an actual *producer* credit on *Taste in Men* only. Distinguish credited producer from engineer (Lorraine Francis), string arranger (Rob Ellis), programmer (Dimitri Tikovoï) and guests (Severe Loren, Justin Warfield). [Original-release credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/80a3fcd3-e777-4778-8087-8a7327517c60).
- **Vocals / themes:** `powerful|soft|rap`, where `rap` is isolated to guest Justin Warfield on *Spite & Malice*. Lyrics include drugs/sex, melancholy, capitalist work, racial discourse and protest/riot reference; categories cover identity, sensuality, introspection, social and some political lyrical material without suggesting political concepts dominate all tracks. [Drowned](https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-album-review-placebo-black-market-music-13/), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/6327-black-market-music/), [Molko interview](https://www.placeborussia.ru/press/press2000-2001/nyrockapr01).
- **Influences vs similarity:** NME says *Slave to the Wage* owes a musical debt to **Six by Seven**, whom Molko had been praising. This is an attributable qualified critic's album-specific influence, albeit not explicitly stated by Molko as a causal influence: assign with caveat. **Pavement** is excluded from influenced_by *despite Molko admitting he was a fan*, because the concrete evidence concerns a directly sampled recording. Electronic resemblance to Depeche Mode and guitars resembling Smashing Pumpkins go in `sounds_like`. [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/placebo-43-1396767), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/6327-black-market-music/), [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/).
- **Critical disagreements:** Drowned in Sound's contemporary review is enthusiastic while Pitchfork's is emphatically negative. VICE's Molko interview is unhappy with the record's blanket sound. Those reactions contextualise `landmark=1` as a well-known career/UK-chart milestone, **not** a unanimous positive review. No source claims stylistic experimentation was extreme.
