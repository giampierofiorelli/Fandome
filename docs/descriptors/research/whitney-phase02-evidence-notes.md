# Whitney Houston: Phase 02 — album-level evidence and critical reconciliation

Research target: **original releases** (1995–2002), not deluxe editions or club remix packages. Draft proposals, not independently audited listening tests. Column values are Fandome editorial judgments grounded in the cited album-specific sources and personnel. "Soundtrack" is a type; we do not insert it as a sound genre.

## whitney-waitingtoexhaleoriginalsoundtrackalbum — *Waiting to Exhale: Original Soundtrack Album* (1995)

**Scope and personnel.** Original sixteen tracks and almost entirely female vocal roster. Whitney Houston sings **three** tracks (*Exhale (Shoop Shoop)*, *Why Does It Hurt So Bad*, *Count On Me* with CeCe Winans), while Toni Braxton, Aretha Franklin, Brandy, Mary J. Blige, TLC, Patti LaBelle, Chaka Khan and others are principal voices on the remaining selections. Source: [estate discography](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/waiting-to-exhale-soundtrack/) and [full original programme](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/23446-various-artists-waiting-to-exhale.php). This is correctly an ensemble / various-artists film soundtrack assigned to Whitney's collection for its landmark role, **not** a Whitney-only solo studio album.

**Producers.** Official album printed notes unequivocally say *Produced by Babyface*. The website elsewhere describes Whitney and Clive Davis as soundtrack producers, but album- and executive-level association is distinct from musical production; exclude them from the musical producers cell. Forest Whitaker is **executive producer of the soundtrack**, not a musical track producer. See the [official original production credits](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/waiting-to-exhale-soundtrack/) and [Himes contemporary review](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/11/29/a-soundtracks-suite-success/65995562-c117-435b-aa2a-3643b81f65a8/).

**Instruments.** Track-by-track reconstructed credits at [Sessiondays](https://sessiondays.com/2021/03/17/1995-soundtrack-waiting-to-exhale/) identify Babyface's **keyboards, synthesizers and guitars** on multiple songs, **Nathan East bass**, **Greg Phillinganes and Alex Alessandroni piano**, **Luis Conte and Larry Bunker percussion**, **Brandon Fields saxophone** on *Count On Me*, and **violin/viola/cello string sessions** (*Exhale*, others). Listed only documented instrumental families: `keyboards|synth|guitar|bass|piano|strings|percussion|saxophone`. No named sequencer, drum-machine brand, vocal effects hardware or sampler has been reliably matched to a track in original credits. The blank production field is intentional. Engineers and MIDI programmer Randy Walker are not producers.

**Genres.** Contemporary R&B leads; Himes calls the record adult contemporary and pop/soul rather than a patchwork of unrelated contemporary hit artists. We omit `quietstorm` and `jazz`, despite isolated quiet ballads and Chaka Khan's standard *My Funny Valentine*, because neither album-level label was independently verified as a documented genre for the recording. See [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/waiting-to-exhale-mw0000645165), [Himes](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/11/29/a-soundtracks-suite-success/65995562-c117-435b-aa2a-3643b81f65a8/) and [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/23446-various-artists-waiting-to-exhale.php).

**Scales.** `energy=2, tempo=2, groove=2`: many reflective mid- and slow-tempo ballads, even with some sharper rhythms (Brandy/TLC). `electronic=2`: synthesizers share arrangement duties with piano, guitars and session strings. `mood=2`: most narratives explore romantic disappointment, grief and self-respect; supportive closing duet contributes uplift. `theatrical=3`: combination of restrained and forceful singers. `experimental=0`: conventional pop/R&B structures throughout, although conceptual unity is unusual. `polish=5`: meticulous high-budget studio production noted by reviewers. **Voice** varies because the record has a cast of distinct performers; `choral` includes multi-part vocal ensemble, not just Whitney.

**Lyrics and comparisons.** Love, heartbreak, female autonomy and self-regard follow the film's women protagonists. *Toni Braxton / Babyface / Anita Baker / Boyz II Men* represent editorial sonic neighbors, **not** documented influences. No influence has been asserted. Contemporary Himes praised the unified sympathetic song-cycle; album-centered critics can also find the uniformly silky sonics conventional. `landmark=1` reflects its success and unusual cultural/music-program unity, not that Whitney sang all sixteen songs.

## whitney-thepreacherswifeoriginalsoundtrackalbum — *The Preacher’s Wife: Original Soundtrack Album* (1996)

**Scope.** Original US **15 tracks** with both film and single versions of *I Believe in You and Me*, a remix of *Step By Step*, six Georgia Mass Choir settings, guest gospel musicians, and **Cissy Houston fronting *The Lord Is My Shepherd***. Some localized official web tracklist widgets omit numbers or individual tracks; [the German-language estate list](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/de/music/preachers-wife-soundtrack/) and [Qobuz original tracklist](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/the-preachers-wife-whitney-houston/0078221895125) make the full original 15-track program clear. Do not count the separate orchestral film score by Hans Zimmer as if included on this vocal album.

**Producers.** [Qobuz label track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/the-preachers-wife-whitney-houston/0078221895125) name **Whitney Houston and Mervyn Warren** as joint musical producers of choir/gospel tracks; **Stephen Lipson** on *Step by Step* (album cut), **Babyface** on *You Were Loved* and *My Heart Is Calling*, **Rickey Minor** alongside Whitney on *Somebody Bigger Than You and I*, and **David Foster** on the separate record version of *I Believe in You and Me*. The Teddy Riley track is explicitly a **remix**, so remix credit is not silently turned into base-track production. The estate summary mentions Clive Davis as producer, while the detailed sleeve calls him executive; he is omitted. Whitney is correctly **included** because she has explicit track-level production credits independent of executive role. [Warren's first-person discography](https://mervynwarren.com/discography/whitney-houston-the-preachers-wife-soundtrack) establishes his producer/arranger role, though its broad summary need not replace individual variant credits.

**Instruments and production.** Geoffrey Himes [explicitly reports synthesizers and drum machines](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1997/02/05/the-gospel-according-to-houston/81d9a0ee-3c31-46bd-b892-61f1fd003fb6/) on the Whitney–Warren pop-gospel treatments. Mervyn Warren's [own producer/musician account](https://mervynwarren.com/discography/whitney-houston-the-preachers-wife-soundtrack) records piano/keyboards. Hence `synth|drummachine|keyboards|piano`, none deduced merely from gospel. His film-production role also includes percussion programming, but that does **not** identify a particular named drum unit; no TR-808/909, sampling hardware or workstation guessed. The `belting` tag reflects the strong, sustained vocal gospel climaxes described by Himes and the [LA Times review](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-11-24-ca-2290-story.html). Choir singing is carried in **voice=choral**, not misrepresented as a studio vocal-effect processor. Lush ballad accompaniment is discussed in reviews, but no specific violin/strings instrument inserted without resolved sleeve credit.

**Genres and scales.** Dominantly `gospel|contemporarygospel` with R&B/pop crossover; **contemporarygospel** is **new** in Fandome taxonomy (AllMusic names it exactly). *Somebody Bigger Than You and I* is explicitly a slice of **gospel-funk** (tag `funk`, limited to one track). `christmas` is localized to *Joy to the World*, not the whole record. `energy=3, tempo=3` reflects driving choir numbers balanced by measured hymns and ballads. `electronic=2` follows Himes's hybrid arrangements. `mood=4` thanks to faith, celebration, consolation. `theatrical=5` for massed choir and heroic vocal projection; `experimental=0, polish=5, groove=2`. `social` lyrics cover community/collective support in addition to devotion and relationships.

**Disagreement.** [Elysa Gardner, LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-11-24-ca-2290-story.html) considered Whitney's precision more compelling in crossover pop than in traditional gospel beside Shirley Caesar, whereas [Geoffrey Himes, Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1997/02/05/the-gospel-according-to-houston/81d9a0ee-3c31-46bd-b892-61f1fd003fb6/) highlighted the Whitney–Warren modernization of gospel, with space for choral climaxes. Do not translate disagreements about gospel authenticity into the absent `influenced_by` field. `landmark=1` reflects a defining gospel repertoire and record, not universal critical approval.

## whitney-myloveisyourlove — *My Love Is Your Love* (1998)

**Scope.** Original **13-track studio album**, first non-soundtrack studio album since 1990, completed on a tight studio schedule. [Estate official record](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/en-ca/music/my-love-your-love/) and [release-history text](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/news/whitney-houstons-my-love-is-your-love-album-was-released-in-november-1998/). No later dance remixes or duet substitutions are used to skew the original.

**Musical producers.** Track-/musician-level metadata [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/my-love-is-your-love-whitney-houston/0078221903721) and [MusicBrainz release documentation](https://musicbrainz.org/release/6937b4a7-6cf0-4d3e-91be-d659c9eb5100) support **Rodney Jerkins; Babyface; Wyclef Jean; Jerry Duplessis; Missy Elliott; Kelvin Bradshaw; Lloyd Turner; David Foster; Soulshock; Karlin; Lauryn Hill**. Babyface produced several ballad sessions; Wyclef and Duplessis wrote/produced the reggae-inflected title cut; Elliott/Bradshaw/Turner worked on *In My Business* / *Oh Yes*; Lauryn Hill produced the Stevie Wonder cover. Names in `producers` are musically credited persons/teams, not merely guest vocalists (Faith Evans, Kelly Price, Mariah Carey), engineers or label executives. Some data aggregators give Whitney/Clive a generic "producer" field; original person-specific credits identify them as executive producers on some tracks, so exclude until a nonexecutive Whitney production credit for this release is documented.

**Instruments, from album-specific published credits.** [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/my-love-is-your-love-whitney-houston/0078221903721) names **keyboard performances/programming**, **drum programming and drum kit**, **bass**, **acoustic bass (not automatically double bass)**, **Greg Phillinganes piano**, **Michael Thompson electric guitar and guitar**, **live orchestra/strings**, **Sheila E. percussion**, **V. Jeffrey Smith flute**, **Rob Paparozzi harmonica**, **Jeff Mironov sitar**, **Loris Holland celesta**, and **Ali Boudris acoustic baritone guitar**. `baritoneguitar` maps the explicitly named specific instrument without inventing a new token. Textbook technology caveat: generic programmed drums establish programmed percussion, but not an identifiable LinnDrum, Roland 808 or sampler model. We describe the programmed rhythmic instrumentation as `drummachine`, aligned with [MusicBrainz drums programming credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/6937b4a7-6cf0-4d3e-91be-d659c9eb5100), while machine model stays unknown. `belting` is source-backed as part of the deliberately retained traditional ballads, **not** a claim that every performance is belted. No software brand/token is verifiable. Some critics describe unusually restrained verses, so `soft` precedes `raspy` only in localized ballad/cover passages.

**Genre precision.** Album-wide center is contemporary R&B/hip-hop soul/pop soul; [AOTY's specific categories](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/20687-whitney-houston-my-love-is-your-love.php) also include smooth soul and adult contemporary. **Washington Post** [explicitly characterizes the title song](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1998/11/22/whitney-houston-all-over-the-map/c949c1d2-4348-4e57-9ab6-61b82267ed9e/) with **dub/reggae and gospel/soul**; `reggae|dub` are placed near the end as **track-limited**, not main album classifications. Also notes Lauryn Hill's hip-hop/soul rendition of the older Stevie Wonder song. The label [retrospective citing Billboard](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/news/whitney-houstons-my-love-is-your-love-album-was-released-in-november-1998/) specifically names pop/R&B/gospel/dance, though no source asserted a distinct disco/house/dancepop subgenre for the **original album**, so such tags are excluded. Main `hiphop` is restricted to certain Jerkins/Missy/Hill production textures.

**Scales and disagreements.** `energy=3, groove=3, tempo=3` because midtempo urban material and uptempo grooves are balanced with large slow ballads. `electronic=3` because programmable rhythms/keys coexist with session playing, `polish=5`, `theatrical=3`, `experimental=1`, `mood=3` (confrontational relationship songs mixed with love and collective faith). [Erlewine at AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/my-love-is-your-love-mw0000176301) praised stylistic ambition and restrained singing while finding some older large productions more stiff. The [1998 Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1998/11/22/whitney-houston-all-over-the-map/c949c1d2-4348-4e57-9ab6-61b82267ed9e/) agreed about the successful title song but found some club-focused productions less emotionally compelling than classic ballads. Do not mistake aesthetic debate for factual instrument/producers disagreement. `sounds_like` are editorial neighbors; no unsupported "influenced by Lauryn Hill" claim (she is a producer). `landmark=1`.

## whitney-justwhitney — *Just Whitney* (2002)

**Scope and chronological boundary.** The original **10-track** US edition is the reference, released 10 December 2002. Later dance remixes, maxi-singles and bonus tracks (including P. Diddy guest remixes) are **excluded**. The original official estate album [lists ten songs](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/just-whitney/); do not import a 2003 remixed single as an album record. The `landmark=0` choice reflects the weaker critical standing relative to the 1985–98 landmarks, not a judgment on Whitney's personal life.

**Musical producers.** The estate's [original track pages](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/one-of-those-days/) name **Kevin “She’kspere” Briggs** (*One of Those Days*), [Babyface](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/tell-me-no/) (*Tell Me No*, among others), [Teddy Bishop and Gordon Chambers](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/my-love/) (*My Love*), [Babyface and Rob Fusari](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/love-that-man/) (*Love That Man*), and [Babyface](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/try-it-on-my-own/) (*Try It on My Own*). Album-level [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/just-whitney-whitney-houston/0078221474726) and [US CD pressing credits](https://readdork.com/album/whitney-houston-just-whitney/pressing/1489730) support **Missy Elliott / CKB, Troy Taylor, Rickey Minor, The Formula, Bobby Brown, Whitney Houston, Muhammad 2G** in original-musical-producer or co-producer roles, while track numbering disagrees between pressings / secondary transcriptions. The original estate calls Antonio L.A. Reid a producer in an album blurb, but elsewhere explicitly describes his role as executive; he is **not** included without a track-level musical production credit. **Diddy** is a single-remix guest, not a base-album producer.

**Instruments and production.** [*Tell Me No* original credits](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/tell-me-no/) include **Ricky Lawson drums, Nathan East bass, Michael Thompson guitar, Greg Phillinganes piano**. [*My Love* original credits](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/my-love/) list Teddy Bishop keyboards/**drum programming** and Greg Charley guitar. [*Try It on My Own* original credits](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/try-it-on-my-own/) add **Babyface nylon-string guitar, programmed drums/keys; Ricky Lawson drums; Reggie Hamilton bass; Michael Thompson guitar; Greg Phillinganes piano; Wayne Linsey Rhodes; strings arranged by Bill Meyers**. Thus `piano|keyboards|bass|drums|guitar|electricpiano|strings|drummachine|acousticguitar` (nylon-string guitar mapped to acousticguitar); no unsupported specific hardware. The *One of Those Days* sample of [The Isley Brothers' *Between the Sheets* is officially confirmed](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/news/whitney-houstons-one-of-those-days-released-20-years-ago-today/), making `sampling` unequivocally documented. Isley Brothers are listed in `influenced_by` **as a direct documented musical source through sampling**, not an inferred overall career influence. Vocal layering and Auto-Tune are **not** guessed from the mere presence of background vocalists.

**Genres and scales.** [AOTY genre index](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/18152-whitney-houston-just-whitney.php) describes contemporary R&B, hip-hop soul, neo-soul, dance-pop, pop soul, pop (mapping its **Neo-Soul** descriptor to existing `nusoul`). [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/just-whitney-mw0000230336) also assigns adult contemporary. [Alexis Petridis](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2002/nov/22/popandrock.artsfeatures1) identifies conservative old-school pop/soul, one sparse downtempo exception (*Things You Say*), prominent rock-guitar solo on *Tell Me No*, big conventional ballads; `powerballad` belongs to part of album. Later club remixes do **not** turn the 2002 source studio album into a dance/house record. `energy=3, tempo=2, groove=2`, `electronic=2` (live rhythm section and programmed cuts), `mood=2` (defensiveness, confrontation and introspection balanced with a few bright romantic songs), `theatrical=4`, `experimental=0`, `polish=4`. Lyrics include response to public attention, autonomy, self-care, love.

**Critical divergence and edition nuance.** [Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/just-whitney-mw0000230336) found several Babyface productions successful but the album more risk-averse than 1998; [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2002/nov/22/popandrock.artsfeatures1) was sharply negative about the musical retreat; [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2002-dec-08-ca-rack8-story.html) praised retained voice while criticizing tentative arrangements. [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/just-whitney/whitney-houston/critic-reviews) shows mixed reception, not unanimous dismissal. Several liner transcriptions attach mismatched track numbers or bonus-remix entries, so list the full reconciled producer roster but rate the row **medium** rather than falsely high. No speculative use of named synths/software.

## Cross-album controls

- **Production-role guard:** no engineer/arranger/label executive automatically becomes `producers`. See per-record caveats.
- **Credit/sound partition:** producer collaborations do not enter `influenced_by`; sampled artist Isley Brothers is the single expressly documented exception for original 2002 recording.
- **Genre hierarchy:** specific stylistic track tags last; `soundtrack` remains **type only**.
- **Technique guard:** `drummachine` references actual documented drum-machine/programmed drum use, not default R&B genre inference. No machine model identified in Phase 02. Blank production values are meaningful unknowns.
- **Album-level generalizations:** eight 0–5 figures reflect musical balance across original tracks and are reasoned judgments, **not** mathematically measured acoustic analyses.
- **One new genre:** `contemporarygospel`; no new instrument or production tokens. The original guide supports `guitar`, `baritoneguitar` and `nusoul` even if abbreviated lists omit some; no additions needed for these.
