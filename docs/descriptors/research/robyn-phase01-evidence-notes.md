# Robyn — Fandome Phase 01: researched evidence and audit

Scope: the first **three** original Robyn studio releases. These rows are **new only**; no 2005/2010/2018/2026 rows and no previous artists are included. Scores are defensible editorial estimates rather than audio feature extraction.

## Discography and original-edition policy

- Authoritative Norwegian expert encyclopaedia (DISC_SNL) and Robyn's official site (DISC_OFFICIAL) confirm the 1995, 1999, 2002 sequence. Fandome currently lists only `Robyn` (2005), `Body Talk` (2010), `Honey` (2018). **All three titles in this batch must be added to the album identity catalog before validation.**
- `Robyn Is Here` is **1995 in original Sweden**. AllMusic and Qobuz prominently use the later **1997 North American** version, with modified tracks, mixes and additional variants. Album `year` stays **1995**, not 1997, and critical observations are treated as broad sonic cues, **not** as assertions about the exact 1995 tracks [R95_AM, R95_Q, DISC_SNL].
- `My Truth` was released in **1999**. Many available streaming listings feature a **13-track** edition while some historical catalogues mention 14 tracks; regional and digital issue dates differ (March, May and December). We score the recognisable full-length album, not bonus-track additions. Avoid interpreting the December digital entry as original Swedish release [R99_Q, R99_A, DISC_SNL].
- `Don't Stop the Music` originally came out **30 October 2002** in Sweden. AllMusic gives a later **2003** entry, while the 2019 digital issue carries its original 2002 recordings. Album `year` is 2002 [R02_MB, R02_AM, R02_APPLE].
- As of 2026-10-24, Robyn also released *Sexistential* on **2026-03-27** [DISC_SEX]. This is a **future batch**, not present in this ZIP. Treat `Body Talk Pt. 1` and `Pt. 2` as separately released 2010 projects rather than inferring that they are identical to compilation/full-length `Body Talk`; choose catalog modelling in a later phase.

## Robyn Is Here (1995) — robyn-robynishere

**Expert profile**: Stephen Thomas Erlewine [R95_AM] describes it as well-executed European dance-pop, catchy teen-pop songs and R&B inflections. The record is much more mainstream than later self-produced Robyn. It combines glossy, predominantly programmed rhythms with a little instrumentation from the original credits [R95_Q]. Secondary AOTY classifications (new jack swing, hip-hop soul, bubblegum pop, trip hop) should not be represented as equally central as dance-pop [R95_AOTY]. All style attributions are enumerated with scope in genre-evidence CSV.

**Numeric judgement (0–5) and source anchors**
- energy=3: lively hits, but not maximal club intensity [R95_AM].
- electronic=2: programmed rhythmic pop alongside live guitars, horns and other players in credits [R95_Q].
- mood=4: largely bright melodic delivery; romantic disappointments appear but do not dominate [R95_AM, R95_Q].
- theatrical=2: studio-forward teen-pop rather than large dramatic staging [R95_AM].
- experimental=1: accessible conventional songwriting, minimal genre destabilization [R95_AM].
- polish=4: contemporary mass-market studio production consistently praised by reviewer [R95_AM].
- tempo=3: up-tempo hits and moderate swing/R&B songs; not uniformly fast [R95_AM].
- groove=4: dance-pop rhythm central to most defining cuts, with some un-danceable passages [R95_AM].

**Voice and lyrics**: `clear`; critic's description of a light, relatively thin lead does not prove the literal `soft` tag [R95_AM]. Love and relational autonomy/loss recur through named recorded songs [R95_Q]; `identity` is a lower-confidence inference from the repeated assertion of respect, not an album concept attested by a critic. No uncorroborated named `sounds_like` or `influenced_by` entered. `landmark=1` is an editorial career-impact designation for the international breakthrough rather than a measured creative high-water mark [DISC_SNL, BIO_AM].

**Producers**: Use named producer credits, not executive producer Peter Swartling or mere songwriters. Qobuz explicitly credits Lindström/Ekhé, Max Martin/Denniz Pop and Christian Falk [R95_Q]; secondary album listings provide Anders Bagge and Harry Sommerdahl [R95_AOTY, R95_SONG]. **Ghost** is a production alias representing the Lindström–Ekhé pairing: do not count it as a third person. US remix producers such as QD III are excluded as not defining the original 1995 album [R95_Q]. `confidence=medium` due to edition and secondary niche genres.

## My Truth (1999) — robyn-mytruth

**Expert profile**: John Lucas [R99_AM] observes a more organic, mature, intimate and lyrically focused album, with the distinctly minimal 'Electric' and funk-inflected 'Main Thing', compared with the immediate bubblegum hooks of the debut. Qobuz documents acoustic guitar, piano, horns, strings and some electronics [R99_Q]. Dance and house are secondary elements, not a nonstop club record. Album-year metadata issues addressed above.

**Numeric judgement (0–5) and source anchors**
- energy=2: reflective tone predominates over club propulsion [R99_AM].
- electronic=2: mixed acoustic players and digitally created beats, not primarily all-synth [R99_AM, R99_Q].
- mood=2: weighty personal themes balanced by a few playful tracks [R99_AM].
- theatrical=1: relatively direct intimate vocal presentation, introspective narrative [R99_AM].
- experimental=2: the relatively minimal Electric and sophisticated shifts within conventional pop [R99_AM].
- polish=3: carefully recorded, more organic arrangements, not maximal studio sparkle [R99_AM, R99_Q].
- tempo=2: slow and moderate songs, with exceptions such as Play/Main Thing [R99_AM].
- groove=2: several notably rhythmic songs, but the bulk is narrative R&B-pop [R99_AM].

**Voice and lyrics**: `clear|soft` is a broad interpretive label drawn from the more intimate, subdued approach [R99_AM]. `introspection|identity|love|heartbreak` derives from album's self-reflective writing and critic's discussion of emotionally specific songs, rather than inferred genre templates. Source-supported material about pregnancy and loss is handled as commentary, not as a forced `political` or `social` tag. `landmark=0` is editorial relative-to-canon judgement, not a claim that it lacked historical importance.

**Producers**: Qobuz song-level credits [R99_Q]: Ulf Lindström and Johan Ekhé (numerous songs); Billy Mann and Kenneth Fambro; Cherno Jah on title track; Kenny Gonzalez and Louie Vega (jointly **Masters at Work**) for `Main Thing`; Christian Falk on two latter cuts; co-producer Tom Hofwander on `88 Days`. Neither engineers/remixers nor executive producers are imported solely for engineering or executive titles. `sounds_like`, `influenced_by` remain empty, because the reviewer mentions Lisa Loeb only as a contrast, not a documented influence [R99_AM].

## Don't Stop the Music (2002) — robyn-dontstopthemusic

**Expert profile**: John Lucas [R02_AM] emphasises strong hooks, dance/R&B synthesis, upbeat momentum and ballads; the 2002 Danish GAFFA critic [R02_GAFFA] hears a mixed-style pop sound moving beyond earlier R&B. AllMusic explicitly gives dance-pop, Euro-Dance, Euro-Pop and R&B/Urban style categories; secondary album tagging lists electropop and trip hop [R02_AOTY]. **The critical disagreement is useful**: GAFFA emphasises movement away from R&B, while AllMusic emphasises the retained R&B streak; label R&B as significant but non-dominant, not exclude or overstate it.

**Numeric judgement (0–5) and source anchors**
- energy=3: driving Keep This Fire Burning and brisk title track coexist with softer ballads [R02_AM].
- electronic=3: programmed beats/keyboards and polished studio tracks, but not fully electronic production [R02_AM, R02_Q].
- mood=3: dynamic bright songs and remorseful emotional writing [R02_AM].
- theatrical=2: melody-driven, emotionally direct recording rather than theatrical performance [R02_AM].
- experimental=2: more sophisticated genre mixtures yet clear mainstream pop song architecture [R02_GAFFA, R02_AM].
- polish=4: strongly produced radio-oriented studio pop [R02_AM].
- tempo=3: up-tempo material offset by gentle mid/slower tracks [R02_AM].
- groove=3: significant dance-oriented set of songs, not uninterrupted club tracks [R02_AM].

**Voice and lyrics**: `clear|powerful` for upfront singing and more driving emphases, with no unjustified rap tag; lyrical love/heartbreak/identity supported by reviewer description of more emotionally direct and defiant performances [R02_AM]. The reviewer calls `Should Have Known` bitter, so `mood` is not euphoric despite the hooks.

**Producers**: Ghost, Guy Sigsworth, Cherno Jah, Max Martin, Alexander Kronlund are named as production contributors [R02_AM]. Cutfather appears as **vocal producer**, not automatically as full track producer; an alternate discographic album-credit aggregation additionally says Cutfather & Joe. **Claude should reconcile before adding them**; nothing was silently inferred from songwriting or mixing credits. As on debut, `Max Martin` appears as a producer on some cuts, but on the title song specifically he is reported as a mixer [R02_AM, R02_MB]. `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` are deliberately blank.

## Taxonomy and evidence limits

Tokens not present in 2026-10-24 GitHub guide: **contemporaryrnb, hiphopsoul, bubblegumpop, europop**. Genre tokens remain normalized ASCII lowercase without punctuation, `|` separated; no cap on number of supported values. Specific album tags and general tags coexist intentionally; however, note lower-quality secondary style labels before admission. In particular, `hiphopsoul`, `bubblegumpop`, `triphop` for 1995 and `triphop` for 2002 appear only in secondary taxonomy and should be reviewed for admissibility by Claude.

All three rows `draft`, whole-row `confidence=medium`, explicit missing comparisons/influences. They are artist-specific **original English editorial descriptions**, not copied reviews. `notes` are 15 words or fewer per current guide. The import script appends only missing album IDs, rejects any conflicting existing rows and is idempotent. It has no ability or permission to write to GitHub itself.
