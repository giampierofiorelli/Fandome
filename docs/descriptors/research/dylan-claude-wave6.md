- `talkingblues` (genre): Far Out (Jack Whatley, 2020) and Albumism (Jesse Ducker, 2023) both name the "talkin' blues" form for Freewheelin'. Talking blues is a spoken-rhythm folk and blues form, not covered by `blues` or `countryblues`.

## jeffersonairplane-surrealisticpillow (Surrealistic Pillow, 1967)

Sources:
- Austin Chronicle, Jay Trachtenberg, 2003, 4 stars. https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/reissues-11717614/
- Spectrum Culture, Jacob Adams, 2012. https://spectrumculture.com/2012/09/24/revisit-jefferson-airplane-surrealistic-pillow/
- Harvard Crimson, Alasdair P. MacKenzie, 2019 (Kaukonen interview). https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2019/7/29/jorma-kaukonen-surrealistic-pillow-jefferson-airplanes/
- Ultimate Classic Rock, Gary Graff, 2022. https://ultimateclassicrock.com/jefferson-airplane-jerry-garcia-surrealistic-pillow/
- Alt Rock Chick, 2013 (blog). https://altrockchick.com/2013/04/18/classic-music-review-surrealistic-pillow-by-jefferson-airplane

Cells:
- **genres:** psychedelia, folk and pop from Spectrum: "a distinctive brew of heartland folksiness, Haight-Ashbury psychedelia and pop-oriented song craft". The Crimson intro lists "harmonious folk-revival music", "overdriven guitar blues", "driving early rock and roll", "melodic pop-rock" and "a spooky and intense vision of psychedelia", giving poprock, blues and rockandroll. I did not add folkrock, acidrock or psychedelicrock: Austin's "folk rock" refers to the debut, and the other labels appear only on Wikipedia and in Progarchives user reviews.
- **energy 3:** Austin praises "soaring, intertwined vocals, thunderous bass, and searing guitar licks", next to acoustic pieces.
- **mood 2:** Spectrum calls it "ultimately about uncertainty and doubt". The Crimson calls its psychedelia "spooky and intense".
- **theatrical 3:** Slick is "in total command" with "high-powered intensity" (Alt Rock Chick).
- **voice powerful|soft:** powerful for Slick (as above). soft for "Marty Balin's tender voice" (Spectrum).
- **instruments:**
  - acousticguitar: "light acoustic guitars" (Alt Rock Chick).
  - recorder: "the organic sounds of a recorder (played by Grace Slick)" (Alt Rock Chick).
  - bass and electricguitar: Austin's "thunderous bass" and "searing guitar licks".
  - guitar: Garcia's "high-pitched guitar lead" (Spectrum).
  - drums: drummer Spencer Dryden joined the band for this album (search snippet).
- **production roomambience:** Kaukonen says the echo on "Embryonic Journey" is "real room echo" (Crimson). I left out the Rolling Stone remark on Jarrard's "Phil Spector-like echo" because the review was behind a paywall.
- **producers:** Rick Jarrard: "I produced the album from start to finish" (UCR). Jerry Garcia is credited only as "Musical and Spiritual Adviser", and Jarrard says Garcia "was never present", so Garcia is left out.
- **sounds_like:** from the Crimson writer's own comparison: "Peter, Paul and Mary's harmonious folk-revival music, Howlin' Wolf's overdriven guitar blues, Bo Diddley and Chuck Berry's driving early rock and roll, and melodic pop-rock à la 'Revolver'-era John Lennon". The Lennon comparison is written as The Beatles (Revolver).
  - Not added: Ravel's Boléro, compared with "White Rabbit" by Alt Rock Chick and Progarchives users (blog and user reviews, and a composition, not an album).
- **influenced_by:** empty. Kaukonen says he "just played like me".
- **landmark 1:** Austin calls it "one of the defining albums of 1967's Summer of Love" and says it "catapulted the Airplane to stardom".
- **lyrics introspection:** "uncertainty and doubt" (Spectrum).
- **scene:** "San Francisco sound" (Austin). RCA: Jarrard was "RCA's house producer" (UCR).
- **confidence medium:** several sources agree, but the main reviews (RS, Louder) could not be opened.

## smallfaces-ogdensnutgoneflake (Ogdens’ Nut Gone Flake, 1968)

Sources:
- Ultimate Classic Rock, Dave Swanson, 2013. https://ultimateclassicrock.com/small-faces-ogdens-nut-gone-flake/
- The Line of Best Fit, Tyler Boehm, 2012. https://thelineofbestfit.com/reviews/albums/the-small-faces-small-faces-from-the-beginning-small-faces-ogdens-nut-gone-flake-reissues-90292
- Clash, Robin Murray, 2018. https://www.clashmusic.com/?p=59177
- Psychedelic Baby Mag (review). https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/?p=2264
- Strawberry Bricks (credits and review). https://strawberrybricks.com/guide/releases/ogdens-nut-gone-flake

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - psychedelia: TLOBF says their "flirtation with psychedelia" came to fruition here. Clash: "the psychedelic era's most creative".
  - rockopera: Clash calls it one of that era's "rock opera's".
  - musichall: UCR calls "Lazy Sunday" "music hall-inspired". TLOBF finds music hall on "Rene" and "Lazy Sunday".
  - hardrock: TLOBF on "Afterglow" and "Song of a Baker".
  - rnb: TLOBF calls "The Journey" a "doped-up take on R'n'B".
  - blueeyedsoul: Clash calls Marriott's vocal "the definitive white soul document". "White soul" is mapped to `blueeyedsoul`.
  - psychedelicpop: Strawberry Bricks says "The Hungry Intruder" "opts for popsike".
  - powerballad: Strawberry Bricks calls "Afterglow" "the prototypical power ballad".
  - The last three are on single tracks, so they come last.
- **mood 4:** "tongue-in-cheek cockney humor" (Psychedelic Baby); "campy" (UCR).
- **theatrical 4:** a concept side with Stanley Unwin's narration; Marriott sings in "an exaggerated cockney accent" (UCR).
- **polish 3:** Strawberry Bricks hears a "lo-fi sound", while Psychedelic Baby describes "spatial effects" and phasing. The two balance out at 3.
- **voice powerful:** Marriott's "white soul" vocal (Clash). Unwin is the narrator, not the lead voice, so `spoken` is not used.
- **instruments:**
  - Psychedelic Baby: Marriott guitars, Lane bass, McLagan keyboards, Jones drums and percussion, plus piano, acoustic guitar and a "harp intro".
  - organ: "McLagan's phased organ arc-ing from speaker to speaker".
- **production:**
  - phasing: "phasing and fuzzy guitars galore" (Strawberry Bricks) and the "phased organ" (Psychedelic Baby).
  - flanging: Clash describes flange effects on the drums of the title track.
- **lyrics storytelling|irony:** the side-two fairy tale about Happiness Stan (TLOBF) and the cockney humour.
- **producers Steve Marriott|Ronnie Lane:** Strawberry Bricks lists Marriott and Lane as producers and Glyn Johns as engineer ("Knobs"). UCR calls Johns the producer, but the credits call him engineer, so he is left out.
- **sounds_like:** TLOBF: "The album plays like a response to Sgt. Pepper's revolutionary sound."
- **influenced_by:** empty. Glyn Johns's line ("Every band at that point were trying to keep up...") is a general remark by the engineer.
- **landmark 1:** the band's "masterpiece" (UCR); "many consider the band's masterpiece" (TLOBF).
- **scene:** Immediate label and Olympic Studios (Clash). Mod roots: "a mod group to the core" (Strawberry Bricks).

## nickdrake-fiveleavesleft (Five Leaves Left, 1969)

Sources:
- Far Out, Aimee Ferrier, 2022. https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/nick-drake-debut-album-five-leaves-left/
- uDiscover, Paul Sexton, 2025. https://www.udiscovermusic.com/?p=9969
- The Absolute Sound, Greg Cahill, 2025. https://www.theabsolutesound.com/articles/nick-drake-the-making-of-five-leaves-left/
- String Section, 2013. https://stringsection.co.uk/?p=205

Cells:
- **genres folk|songwriter:**
  - Far Out: "classic English folk music with lush string arrangements".
  - uDiscover: "the quintessential sensitive singer-songwriter"; Absolute Sound: "gentle folk singer".
  - Not added: BBC (Chris Jones) "English folk and jazz", which I only saw through Wikipedia.
- **mood 1:** "moody", "haunting" (Absolute Sound), plus the album's melancholy.
- **theatrical 1:** intimate solo voice and guitar.
- **polish 3:** lush strings, cut live with the string section.
- **voice soft:** "warm voice" (Far Out), "gentle folk singer" (Absolute Sound).
- **instruments:**
  - Far Out: "pastoral flutes, vibraphone (mallets), oboe, guitar and piano", English horns on "Fruit Tree", and fingerpicked open-tuned acoustic guitar.
  - uDiscover: Richard Thompson on guitar, Danny Thompson on bass.
  - String Section: Kirby's strings, solo cello on "Cello Song".
- **production:** John Wood says Drake "would track live, singing and playing along with the string section", with no overdubs (Far Out), giving liverecording. stringarrangements: Kirby and Harry Robinson (String Section).
- **producers:** Joe Boyd (Far Out, uDiscover).
- **sounds_like:** NME's Nick Kent (quoted by uDiscover) puts it with "albums that seem tied to exhorting and then playing on a particular mood in the listener", alongside Astral Weeks and Forever Changes. That is a mood/atmosphere comparison, so it is borderline. Drop it if the owner reads it as non-sonic.
  - Also from uDiscover: International Times (Mark Williams) said Drake's voice "would be compared with Donovan's", then dismissed the comparison. Not used.
- **influenced_by:** empty. The Romantic poets (Far Out) are literary, not musical.
- **landmark 1:** the album is in the all-time lists uDiscover reports (RS 500 #280, NME #74) and is #61 in Pitchfork's 1960s list. The label-affiliated value judgement ("classic") is not counted.
- **lyrics introspection:** melancholy; Drake was "greatly inspired by the Romantic poets" (Far Out).
- **scene:** Island label (uDiscover), Sound Techniques studio (Absolute Sound).

## francoisehardy-touslesgarconsetlesfilles (Tous les garçons et les filles, 1962)

Sources:
- The Line of Best Fit, Hayley Scott, 2016. https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/articles/francoise-hardy-reissues
- Exclaim, Cosette Schulz, 2015. https://exclaim.ca/music/article/francoise_hardy-tous_les_garcons_et_les_filles_le_premier_bonheur_du_jour_mon_amie_la_rose_lamitie_la_maison_ou_jai_grandi
- Keith Christiansen, Substack, 2024. https://keithchristiansen.substack.com/p/french-music-francoise-hardy-tous
- NPR via WFAE, Eleanor Beardsley, 2018. https://www.wfae.org/2018-09-12/francoise-hardy-remains-frances-national-treasure

Cells:
- **genres yeye|pop|rockabilly:**
  - TLOBF: "rockabilly-tinged pop" and a "slight nod to yé-yé traditions".
  - Christiansen: yé-yé is "a light francophone version of American-styled rock'n'roll".
  - NPR: she belonged to the yé-yé movement.
  - Pitchfork's line ("between rockabilly shimmy and Gallic introspection", Marc Hogan) was seen only second-hand and is not cited.
- **polish 2:** "simply produced arrangements" (Exclaim).
- **mood 2:** the songs are upbeat and sweet, but TLOBF calls it "honest, romantic music for lovelorn wallflowers", delivered with "wistful conviction".
- **theatrical 1:** "her simplest, most affecting" (TLOBF); her conversational delivery.
- **voice soft:** "seductive but coy, deeper in pitch yet delicate" (Exclaim).
- **instruments:** "bright guitars and muted bass" (Exclaim); "subtle jazz percussion" (TLOBF).
- **lyrics love|heartbreak|introspection:** "learning and yearning in love" (Exclaim); "lovelorn" (TLOBF).
- **producers:** empty. Jacques Wolfsohn appears only on Wikipedia, and the MusicBrainz release has no credits.
- **landmark 1:** her breakthrough. The title single sold millions (NPR), the title track is "archetypal of Hardy's aesthetic" (TLOBF), and the album is #90 in Pitchfork's 1960s list.
- **influenced_by:** empty at album level. Elvis Presley is noted at artist level in artists.md.

## dylan-thefreewheelinbobdylan (The Freewheelin’ Bob Dylan, 1963)

Sources:
- Library of Congress National Recording Registry essay, Cary O'Dell (PDF read in full). https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/FreewheelinBobDylan.pdf
- Albumism, Jesse Ducker, 2023. https://albumism.com/features/bob-dylan-the-freewheelin-bob-dylan-album-anniversary
- Far Out, Jack Whatley, 2020. https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/bob-dylan-the-freewheelin-bob-dylan-album-ranked/
- uDiscover, Jim Allen, 2026 (Tom Wilson). https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/tom-wilson-feature

Cells:
- **genres folk|talkingblues|blues:**
  - LOC: "a primer for '60s rock and folk".
  - Albumism: "changed folk music"; "talking blues" and blues numbers.
  - Far Out: folk, "12-bar blues", "talkin' blues".
- **electronic 0 and polish 1:** "instrumentation is stunningly minimal—mostly just Dylan, his guitar and harmonica" (LOC).
- **theatrical 1:** "the album's economics causes the lyrics and vocals to sound all the more intimate and immediate" (LOC).
- **voice:** empty. LOC only says his voice was "an acquired taste", and a 1963 newsletter called it "affected".
- **instruments acousticguitar|harmonica:** Albumism and LOC. Only "Corrina, Corrina" has a few backing musicians.
- **lyrics political|social|heartbreak|love:**
  - political: "Masters of War", "perhaps Dylan's most overt anti-war song" (LOC).
  - social: "Blowin' in the Wind", adopted by civil rights and other movements (LOC).
  - heartbreak: "Don't Think Twice" is a "falling-out-of-love song" (LOC).
  - love: "Girl from the North Country" (LOC).
- **producers John Hammond|Tom Wilson:**
  - Hammond: Albumism.
  - Wilson: "Freewheelin' Bob Dylan was two-thirds done, but Wilson aided in its completion" (uDiscover).
  - Wilson's credit is disputed in some listings.
- **sounds_like Woody Guthrie:** Albumism: Dylan "in the mold of Woody Guthrie".
- **influenced_by:** empty.
  - Far Out also names Guthrie, but as the writer's own claim, not Dylan's statement.
  - Dylan's liner-note remark about "real blues singers" names no artist.
  - "Masters of War" uses the melody of "Nottamun Town", which counts as a quotation, not an influence.
- **landmark 1:** LOC says his second album "would change that", the album where he became a songwriter. It is in the Registry's first class.
- **confidence high.**

## byrds-fifthdimension (Fifth Dimension, 1966)

Sources:
- Ultimate Classic Rock, Frank Mastropolo, 2016. https://ultimateclassicrock.com/the-byrds-fifth-dimension/
- Uproxx, Steven Hyden, 2022 (McGuinn reviewing the albums). https://uproxx.com/indie/roger-mcguinn-reviews-albums-by-the-byrds-the-byrds-1964-67
- Something Else!, 2014 (McGuinn talk). https://somethingelsereviews.com/?p=107865
- Classic Rock Review, 2021. https://classicrockreview.com/?p=19841
- Psychedelic Sight, 2012. https://psychedelicsight.com/fifth-dimension-byrds/
- Head Heritage, user review, 2004. https://www.headheritage.co.uk/unsung/reviews/the-byrds-fifth-dimension

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - psychedelicrock and acidrock: UCR names psychedelic rock and acid rock, plus a move "from folk-rock to a more experimental style".
  - ragarock: Psychedelic Sight says the press "dubbed it 'raga rock'". Head Heritage: "full-on psychedelic raga-rock".
  - folkrock and psychedelia: Classic Rock Review describes a folk-rock group moving toward psychedelia.
  - surfrock: Classic Rock Review hears surf-rock influence on single tracks, so it comes last.
- **experimental 3:** "cluttered, borderline dissonant instrumental sections" (Psychedelic Sight), next to plain folk covers.
- **voice choral:** "gorgeous 4-part harmony vocals" (Head Heritage); "harmonized vocals" (Classic Rock Review).
- **instruments:**
  - electricguitar: "a distorted 12-string Rickenbacker" (Psychedelic Sight).
  - guitar: Crosby's "driving rhythm guitar".
  - organ: Van Dyke Parks on "5D" (Classic Rock Review).
  - strings: a string section on "John Riley" (Head Heritage).
  - bass and drums: Head Heritage.
- **production soundeffects:** jet-engine and cockpit-radio sounds on "2-4-2 Fox Trot" (UCR, Psychedelic Sight).
- **producers Allen Stanton:** Classic Rock Review.
- **influenced_by John Coltrane [2/11]|Ravi Shankar [1/11]:**
  - McGuinn (Uproxx 2022): "We were influenced by Coltrane and Ravi Shankar"; "I'd recorded Ravi Shankar on one side of a tape and John Coltrane on the other."
  - On "I See You" (UCR): "I'd been playing around with that jazz-, Coltrane-type of influence."
  - Something Else!: "When we came up with 'Eight Miles High,' with that riff, we were trying to emulate Coltrane's saxophone playing."
  - Coltrane is documented on "Eight Miles High" and "I See You" (2 of 11 tracks), Shankar on "Eight Miles High" (1 of 11).
- **sounds_like The Beatles [1/11]:** Psychedelic Sight says "I See You" "brings to mind mid-period Beatles".
- **landmark 1:** "seminal psychedelic album" (Psychedelic Sight); "helped introduce fans to psychedelic rock" (UCR).
- **lyrics:** empty, because the sources I opened don't describe the themes.
- **confidence medium:** the main critical sources (AllMusic 4.5 stars, seen in a snippet) could not be opened.

## can-monstermovie (Monster Movie, 1969)

Sources:
- Holger Czukay to Richie Unterberger, 1997, published by KQED 2017. https://www.kqed.org/arts/13807595/a-good-way-to-master-the-future-an-interview-with-holger-czukay-1997
- Head Heritage, "Serotonin", 2001 (review of Monster Movie and Delay 1968). https://www.headheritage.co.uk/?p=6298
- The New Perfect Collection, blog, 2015. https://thenewperfectcollection.com/2015/06/02/monster-movie-can-1969/
- Sputnikmusic user review (ValiumMan), 2006. https://www.sputnikmusic.com/review/9361/Can-Monster-Movie

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - krautrock: Sputnik user; TNPC tag.
  - garagerock: Head Heritage hears "roots in VU-trance and garage rock".
  - psychedelia and experimental: TNPC tags.
  - Not added: "noise rock" (a hypothetical label in the Sputnik review), "proto-punk" (Progarchives user, seen only in a snippet) and "garage punk" (a TNPC tag with no matching token).
- **energy 4:** "wind-tunnel barrage" (TNPC); "high-energy" (snippet); "manic preaching" (Sputnik).
- **experimental 4:** "atonal"; the 20-minute improvisation of "Yoo Doo Right" (Sputnik).
- **polish 1:** "primitive recording equipment"; "Yoo Doo Right" edited down from about 1.5 hours "rather messily" (Head Heritage).
- **groove 3:** "extremely rhythm-oriented" (Sputnik); "heart-thumping tribalism" (Head Heritage).
- **voice powerful:** Mooney's "manic preaching" and near-screaming (Sputnik).
- **instruments:**
  - drums, bass, electricguitar: Liebezeit, Czukay, Karoli's "superfuzz" guitar.
  - organ: "throbbing organ" (Head Heritage).
  - harmonica: on "Outside My Door".
- **production tapesplicing:** the tape edit of "Yoo Doo Right" (Head Heritage).
- **producers:** empty. No source I opened names a producer.
- **sounds_like The Velvet Underground:** Head Heritage: "VU-trance". TNPC compares "Mary, Mary So Contrary" to "Venus in Furs".
- **influenced_by The Velvet Underground|The Beatles:**
  - Czukay (1997): "The influence, for me, of Beatles and Velvet Underground was most important." He speaks of the first albums, when "Can was most innocent", and says "That's the reason I really love" Monster Movie.
  - TNPC adds that Can made no secret of an "early debt to the Velvets".
- **landmark 0:** TNPC says Tago Mago and Ege Bamyasi are more admired, and no source calls this the defining Can album.
- **lyrics:** empty. "figuring out 'lyrics' isn't the point" (Head Heritage).
- **confidence medium:** the sources are mostly blogs and user reviews, but they agree. I could not open the BBC (Mike Barnes) or Pitchfork texts.
- **Legacy:** no row. TNPC (a blog, not a professional outlet) links "Father Cannot Yell" to Joy Division, PiL and The Fall.
