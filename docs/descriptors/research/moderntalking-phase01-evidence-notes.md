# Modern Talking — Fandome phase 01: album-level evidence notes

Evidence checked 27 October 2026 against branch `descriptors`, AllMusic, MusicBrainz, Bertelsmann's physical record archive, Hansa chronology and the four album personnel pages. This batch is a researched **draft**, not an independently reviewed audio analysis. Numeric descriptors are reasoned comparisons with Fandome's published 0–5 anchors, not measured acoustic features. Every album has at least six distinct sourced genres. AllMusic genre labels are **retrospective**; in particular `eurodance` must not imply the specific early-1990s house-oriented Eurodance sound. We use `eurodisco` first as the more historically precise album genre.

## The 1st Album (1985) — `moderntalking-the1stalbum`

- **Original LP**: 1 April 1985 (Hansa), nine tracks, including *You're My Heart, You're My Soul*, *You Can Win If You Want*, and *There's Too Much Blue in Missing You*. The latter has disputed individual lead-vocal attributions (sources identify Bohlen and/or Birger Corleis); the vocal descriptor describes the **album**, and no unsupported exclusive assignment is made. A 1984 single date does **not** make it a 1984 album.
- **Genres**: the album is explicitly `Eurodisco` in the dedicated album entry; AllMusic credits Dance-Pop, Synth Pop, Euro-Pop, Euro-Dance, Disco, Club/Dance and a broad R&B catalogue shelf. `pop` is a broad record-catalogue category and `rnb` is placed **last** (shelving metadata only), not asserted as prominent soulful instrumentation. AllMusic's composite *Punk/New Wave* is not forcibly converted to a distinct `newwave` token.
- **Instruments**: contemporary album personnel credits Bohlen guitar and Ralf Stemmann piano + keyboards. `keyboards|piano|guitar` is restricted to documented categories. SRF's history describes synthesizer-driven sound but does not name a documented instrument model for the *entire* album. No DX7, LinnDrum, Jupiter-8 or exact machine codes are entered: unsupported/blog gear lists for a single are not album-wide primary evidence.
- **Production and voices**: Wikipedia lists the falsetto chorus musicians Rolf Köhler, Michael Scholz, Detlef Wiedeke and Birger Corleis; independent interview with Wiedeke explains the original Bee Gees-like head-voice concept. `production=falsetto`; voice `clear|choral|soft` corresponds to Anders's clear male lead and high harmonised group chorus. No unverified `layeredvocals` machine process is assumed solely because multiple singers were present.
- **Producers**: Dieter Bohlen producer and Luis Rodríguez co-producer (album-level source); neither Stemmann's arrangements nor session choir are entered as album producers.
- **Scales**: energy3/tempo3 reflect radio-oriented mid-tempo dance tracks versus melancholy slow songs; electronic4 from synthesiser-based Hamburg production; mood4 driven by romantic and optimistic melodic hooks yet offset by *There's Too Much Blue in Missing You*; theatrical3 from prominent anthemic choruses; experimental1 from conventional short-form structures; polish4 from organised commercial studio productions; groove4 from sustained disco/synth-pop rhythm. Anchored **editorial judgements**, not BPM measurements.
- **Influenced by**: `Bee Gees` **album-specific** because primary participant Detlef Wiedeke describes the original *You're My Heart, You're My Soul* falsetto approach as modelled after them. This should not be stretched automatically across later albums without further named sourcing. All `sounds_like` artists are comparisons only.

## Let's Talk About Love (1985) — `moderntalking-letstalkaboutlove`

- **Original LP**: 14 October 1985, ten songs. Single *Cheri, Cheri Lady*; title track is also on the album. The album has the same core credited production partnership. Arrangement personnel list Bohlen guitar and Ralf Stemmann piano/keys, plus original-session falsetto group.
- **Genres**: dedicated album source `eurodisco`; AllMusic lists `dancepop|synthpop|europop|eurodance|clubdance`; Bertelsmann original edition is broad Pop/Rock, hence `pop` last. The AllMusic composite Punk/New Wave is not presented as evidence for a standalone `newwave` tag.
- **Production**: `falsetto` from credited backing-choir performance; no exact synthesizer or drum-machine models confidently substantiated for this album from checked original credits. Apple Music title-track metadata backs guitar, keyboards, Bohlen producer and Rodriguez co-producer.
- **Scores**: 3 energy / 3 tempo / 4 groove: buoyant disco passages mixed with softer love songs; 4 electronic based on synth-pop credited keyboard arrangements; 4 mood romantic brightness; 4 polish studio polish; 3 theatrical dramatic falsetto choruses; 1 experimental conventional radio forms. These are editorial, not factual measurement.
- **Themes**: `love|heartbreak` supported by original titles *Cheri, Cheri Lady*, *You're the Lady of My Heart*, *Love Don't Live Here Anymore*. A title alone does not prove nuanced subtopics, hence no further tags.

## Ready for Romance (1986) — `moderntalking-readyforromance`

- **Original LP**: 26 May 1986; German Hansa ten-track running order (*Brother Louie*, *Just We Two*, *Lady Lai*, *Doctor for My Heart*, *Save Me – Don't Break Me*, *Atlantis Is Calling*, *Keep Love Alive*, *Hey You*, *Angie's Heart*, *Only Love Can Break My Heart*). **UK edition adds older hits** and is not the reference for album-wide descriptors. Studio 33, Hamburg, recorded late 1985 through early 1986 according to album source.
- **Genres and feel**: `eurodisco` from album source; AllMusic explicitly assigns Dance-Pop, Synth Pop, Euro-Pop, Euro-Dance, Club/Dance and light/joyful/romantic/energetic moods. `pop` supported by original pressing archive. The high-chorus arrangement remains central; energy4/tempo4 distinguish the more driving momentum of *Brother Louie* and *Atlantis Is Calling* from a standard mid-tempo 3, while individual slow album cuts prevent a 5.
- **Instrumentation and production**: original personnel lists Bohlen guitar, Ralf Stemmann piano and keyboards, high falsetto choir; no direct verified DX7/Jupiter/Linn attribution. Falsetto is entered as vocal-production feature, while exact brand machines remain blank. Labels credit Bohlen production and Rodríguez co-production on the record and multiple tracks.
- **Other scales**: electronic4, mood4, theatrical3, experimental1, polish4 and groove4 from source-backed genre/mood context and the original mixture of songs. No evidence that the record is nearly fully electronic (`5`) or experimental.

## In the Middle of Nowhere (1986) — `moderntalking-inthemiddleofnowhere`

- **Original LP**: 10 November 1986, original ten tracks. AllMusic and Qobuz list original titles; `Give Me Peace on Earth` accompanies *Geronimo's Cadillac* and *Lonely Tears in Chinatown*.
- **Genres**: the album source calls it `eurodisco`; AllMusic explicitly labels `dancepop|eurodance|europop|clubdance`, but **does not list Synth Pop for this specific album**, so `synthpop` is intentionally absent. `pop` is supported by Apple Music album genre; no `newwave` or `rnb` mapped from assumptions.
- **Instrumentation and producer**: album personnel document Anders' piano/keyboards, Bohlen guitar and backing vocals plus the falsetto chorus; MusicBrainz, AllMusic and Qobuz credit Bohlen with production and Rodriguez as co-producer. `falsetto` is the only equipment-independent verifiable technique here. No brand model or explicit drum-machine assignment has been verified.
- **Scores**: emotional mix is more subdued (mood3 instead of 4) given the peace-oriented ballad and lonely-separation titles; energy3/tempo3 from dance-pop versus softer songs, theatrical3, experimental1, electronic4, polish4, groove4 are anchored editorially. `social` is limited to *Give Me Peace on Earth* and should be lower weight than love/heartbreak.

## Cross-album restrictions and disputed credits

- Original-era **uncredited high vocalists** are discussed by first-hand singer Wiedeke and are present in album-credit discographies. They must not be confused with Anders's lead vocal or depicted solely as Bohlen falsetto. Fan blogs differ about who sang specific passages, so no person-by-person voice tagging is attempted.
- **Influences** are not extrapolated from similarity. Bee Gees are album-specific only for the original 1984/1985 high choir per participant testimony. Anders naming Barry Manilow as a childhood influence in a 2022 interview qualifies as artist-wide influence, **not** a named cause of any specific 1985–1986 track. Neither Ralf Stemmann nor Bohlen solo project Blue System is an `influenced_by` artist because they are collaborators/self-related entities.
- Do not attribute Yamaha DX7, Roland Jupiter-8, Oberheim DMX, TR-808 or LinnDrum to any album **only** because a recent fan article names them on hit songs; verified primary 1980s studio reports were not secured. These models are intentionally withheld.
- AllMusic's catalogue genre Euro-Dance is a **retrospective taxonomy**, not proof of 1990s Eurodance arrangements; notes preserve this temporal ambiguity.
