# Lisa Stansfield — Fandome phase 01, evidence and descriptor decisions

Research cutoff **28 October 2026**. Four *original* studio albums only. The numerical ratings are Fandome editorial judgements based on sourced reviews and the 0–5 anchors, not measured acoustic properties. `sources.csv` gives the direct URLs; `genre_evidence.csv` lists every assigned genre. Inferences are flagged below.

## 1. Affection (1989) — `lisastansfield-affection`

**Genres (priority):** `soul|rnb|contemporaryrnb|dancepop|house|newjackswing|adultcontemporary|phillysoul|disco|downtempo`.

- AllMusic's Alex Henderson (https://www.allmusic.com/album/affection-mw0000654618) describes the central combination of warm 1970s R&B and newer dance production, with house/hip-hop traces. AllMusic indexes Contemporary R&B, Adult Contemporary and Dance-Pop. `newjackswing` is a lower-confidence album genre in secondary album metadata; not extrapolated from a breakbeat. `phillysoul` reflects the contemporary Rolling Stone assessment reproduced in the album's original review bibliography. The original 1989 UK LP lists Electronic and Funk/Soul with Downtempo and Soul styles (https://rectangletriangle.com/products/affection-lisa-stansfield); keep `downtempo` last. AllMusic explicitly isolates the disco quality in “This Is the Right Time”; this is a track-level tail.
- **Instrument credits:** original UK album sleeve records *all instruments and programming* by Ian Devaney / Andy Morris and live trumpet by Stephen Gibson; Andy Morris trumpet solo. Since the sleeve does not enumerate the keyboard models or a drum machine, we include `keyboards` only because a contemporary review describes driving keyboard patterns. `strings` denotes arrangements heard/reviewed, **not verified live string players**. No model tokens, `drummachine` or `sampling` added.
- **Production:** `programming` explicitly credited; don't infer `tr808`, `synthprogramming` or `digitalrecording`. `producers=Ian Devaney|Andy Morris|Coldcut`, Coldcut limited to “This Is the Right Time”. The 1990 US edition remixes and track resequencing are not used to score the original UK album.
- **Voice:** `soft|clear` reflects critics' smooth, restrained yet soulful voice, not a blanket operatic or belting delivery. **Themes:** heartbreak and looking for loved ones (“All Around the World”), love/companionship (“Live Together”), romantic sensuality and social togetherness. **Scores:** energy 3 and groove 3 for the alternating dance groove and ballads; electronic 3 for extensive programmed backing in otherwise soul-centred music; mood 3 for bright/dark changes; theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3 for conventional polished late-1980s crossover production.
- **Sounds_like:** Barry White is explicitly heard in the soul arrangements in AllMusic. A contemporary Rolling Stone critic (reproduced as review citations) likens individual vocal moments to Chaka Khan and Deniece Williams; this is *critic resemblance*, not an album influence. The 1990 Los Angeles Times interview names those singers among Stansfield's **general** influences, but doesn't say they shaped this exact album. `influenced_by` empty.
- **Landmark 1:** debut and breakout with “All Around the World”, most widely awarded and commercially defining record. **Confidence high** for core classification; individual low-weight genre tags are lower-confidence in `genre_evidence.csv`.

## 2. Real Love (1991) — `lisastansfield-reallove`

**Genres:** `rnb|soul|contemporaryrnb|dancepop|adultcontemporary|pop|disco`.

- AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/real-love-mw0000270157) documents Adult Contemporary, Contemporary R&B, Dance-Pop, and ballads including “All Woman”; critic highlights stronger tracks “Soul Deep” and “Set Your Loving Free”. Contemporary review by Mike Joyce (Washington Post, 21 December 1991) hears a blend of soul/R&B and 1970s dance tradition (https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1991/12/22/pop-recordings/50e88af8-2db5-4b59-b1a0-f64303790326/). `pop` and `disco` are the lower-priority crossover/track textures, not a claim this is a disco album. Record vintage pop language is not a licence to add unsupported genre tokens.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic credits and original sleeve transcription name keys, guitar, live drums, bass, percussion, strings, trumpet, flugelhorn, trombone, saxophone, flute and harmonica. Andy Morris, Ian Devaney, Jerry Hey, Snowboy and Simon Vance have documented arranger roles; only **Devaney and Morris** are credited record producers. `programming|hornarrangements|stringarrangements` reflects original credits. No specific machine or synthesizer model is supported.
- **Voice/themes:** slow soulful, clear singing; explicit longing and heartbreak in “All Woman”, love and desire on uptempo singles; no unsubstantiated political themes. **Scores:** energy 3, electronic 2 (live horn/strings/drums plus sequencing), mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3; overall groove lower than an uninterrupted club record.
- **Sounds_like:** Washington Post reviewer links this record to Anita Baker and Mariah Carey, which is a sound comparison. `influenced_by` empty. **Landmark 1:** important second solo album and “Change”/“All Woman” era, while AllMusic is less enthusiastic than about the debut. **Confidence high** on personnel/production; rating is editorial.

## 3. So Natural (1993) — `lisastansfield-sonatural`

**Genres:** `contemporaryrnb|rnb|soul|adultcontemporary|dancepop|downtempo|swingbeat`.

- William Cooper's AllMusic review (https://www.allmusic.com/album/so-natural-mw0000459115) says the album moved **away** from dance-pop toward many ballads. Its categorisation nevertheless includes Dance-Pop and Adult Contemporary. The 1993 CD's style metadata (https://musicbase.net/so-natural) adds Downtempo and Swingbeat, which are retained with medium confidence after the dominant soul/R&B and adult contemporary. This is an explicit source attribution, **not** a claim every song is swingbeat. “Too Much Love Makin'” and “Marvellous & Mine” are comparatively brisk.
- **Instruments:** original CD credits, label-distributed Qobuz music credits and AllMusic name guitar/bass/keyboards (Ian Devaney), drums (Andy Gangadeen), percussion (Snowboy), trumpet/keys (Andy Morris), flute/saxophone (Snake Davis), strings (multiple players). No licence to invent sampled orchestra or drum-machine types. `programming` has general credited backing in studio personnel data, but exact technical units remain unknown.
- **Producer uncertainty:** contemporaneous album summaries list four individuals (Ian Devaney, Lisa Stansfield, Bobby Boughton, Andy Morris). Original-track label credits and music database entries explicitly label Devaney, Lisa and Bobby as producers on original songs. **Andy Morris appears as performer/composer on the track data**, so was not added to CSV producer list without album-wide credit confirmation. Label credits sometimes list Bobby as an *associate* producer; this conflicts with other producer tags. See https://musicbrainz.org/release/6b3e67da-a11f-48e6-8d34-77c10ed2cfd4 vs https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/so-natural-lisa-stansfield/0888880780027 ; Claude should recheck original packaging, but do not silently treat the mixer as an executive producer.
- **Voice/lyrics:** intimate, soft and clear romantic delivery; love, heartbreak, sensuality and personal longing. **Scores:** energy 2, electronic 2, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 2, groove 2, distinguishing it from the debut.
- **Sounds_like:** Barry White is referenced by an album reviewer in regard to the lush soul arrangement style; that is *sonic resemblance*, not evidence Barry White created the record or influenced this particular album. **Landmark 0:** narrower reception and fewer defining hits than *Affection* or *Real Love*; AllMusic gives a relatively mixed evaluation. **Confidence high** on core sound and instruments, medium on obscure genre tail and production labels.

## 4. Lisa Stansfield (1997) — `lisastansfield-lisastansfield`

**Genres:** `rnb|soul|contemporaryrnb|dancepop|disco|funk|pop|adultcontemporary`.

- Stephen Thomas Erlewine (https://www.allmusic.com/album/lisa-stansfield-mw0000071133) specifically describes polished **disco-inflected dance-pop** with old-school soul and romantic ballads. Original album sources list R&B, soul, dance, pop, funk. A contemporary critic in South China Morning Post (Stephen McCarty, 4 April 1997; https://www.scmp.com/article/190929/lisa-stansfield) calls out the funk of “The Line” but is much more critical of its slick ballads. **This is a real disagreement**; note it rather than treating every critic as enthusiastic.
- **Instruments:** Sessiondays' original personnel transcription (https://sessiondays.com/2025/10/01/1997-lisa-stansfield-lisa-stansfield/) documents orchestral strings, horn section, guitars, bass, percussion, and synth/keyboard programming. Specifically Jerry Hey, Gary Grant, Bill Reichenbach and Dan Higgins contribute horns, with session guitar Wah Wah Watson, percussion Snowboy/Luis Conte, bass Neil Stubenhaus on named tracks. The CSV deliberately does **not** claim every instrument plays on every song. `programming|hornarrangements|stringarrangements` is documented by credits; exact machine models not established.
- **Producers:** Ian Devaney main, Peter Mokran co-producer. The Sony/Qobuz label track list shows a contribution to “Don't Cry for Me” from the Mark Morales / Cory Rooney team, so they appear after the primary producers. The broad wiki infobox additionally lists Dan Bewick and Matt Frost, but original-edition songs vs 1998 single remixes/deluxe tracks are unclear: don't add those two without identifying an original 1997 track-production credit. https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/lisa-stansfield-lisa-stansfield/0888880755124 . Barry White's hit is a COVER, not an album influence record.
- **Voice/lyrics/scores:** glossy soft/clear soul vocals; relationships, heartbreak and sensuality. energy 3; electronic 3 (programming balanced by many instrumentalists); mood 4 (bright disco material balances softer tracks); theatrical 2; experimental 1; polish 4; tempo 3; groove 4. **Landmark 1:** commercially prominent fourth studio record and a recognisable return to the dance floor. **Confidence high** for basic sound and credits, lower for completeness of producer list.

## Scope restrictions and QA caveats

- Fandome describes whole albums, **not singles' extended remixes**; bonus tracks on the 2014/2016 expanded editions do not change an original album's style or producer field.
- The `influenced_by` field is intentionally empty in all four rows. Original direct statements are in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` as ARTIST INFLUENCES, with dated sources. Covering a singer, collaborating with them, or being compared to them does not prove a particular album was influenced by them.
- Discography has eight original studio albums through 2018 (MusicBrainz). *Swing* (1999) must be explicitly checked for soundtrack inclusion. Exclude *In Session*, *Biography*, *The Collection*, *The Remix Album* for this batch.
- Some review/metadata pages have later reissue dates, including So Natural under 1997 or Real Love under 1996; **original years are 1989, 1991, 1993 and 1997**. Qobuz's 2014 digital deluxe credit presentation is used only for personnel/producers known to be on original tracks.
- Numeric ratings and landmark flags are **editorial**; source agreement does not imply scientific measurement. Draft means waiting for second reviewer/validator.
