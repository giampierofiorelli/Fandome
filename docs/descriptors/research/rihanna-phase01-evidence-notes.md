## Music of the Sun (2005) — `rihanna-musicofthesun`

**Scale rationale (energy/electronic/mood/theatrical/experimental/polish/tempo/groove = 3/3/4/2/1/4/3/4):** Dancehall beats and dance-pop hooks coexist with slow jams and string ballads. Keyboard programming documented, so electronics 3; acoustic strings make 5 inappropriate. Bright opening and slower middle rule out uniform 5 mood/groove.

**Artist resemblance versus influence:** Beyoncé is a reviewer-stated connection specifically for the dancehall-pop lead single, not evidence that the entire debut imitates the artist.

**Track-specific verification and limitations** (original album running order):

1. **Pon de Replay:** Dancehall-inflected dance-pop; reviewer names reggae cadence; programmed beats, keyboard credits.

2. **Here I Go Again:** Caribbean pop/R&B with keyboard programming and J-Status; reviewer cites bright opening.

3. **If It’s Lovin’ That You Want:** Club/pop R&B, Poke & Tone production with Spanador co-production.

4. **You Don’t Love Me (No, No, No):** Dawn Penn song adapted with Vybz Kartel; keyboard/guitar credits; reggae roots versus current production.

5. **That La, La, La:** Full Force-written/produced; critic notes freestyle team, production style not independently confirmed.

6. **The Last Time:** Slower string-led arrangement; cello, viola, violin, guitar and keyboard credits.

7. **Willing to Wait:** Slow R&B; critic compares to Janet Jackson Let’s Wait Awhile.

8. **Music of the Sun:** Guitar and keyboards credited; Caribbean-pop title track.

9. **Let Me:** Stargate production; program/keyboard credits; club-pop material.

10. **Rush:** Kardinal Offishall rap guest; keyboard programming and Caribbean/hip-hop link.

11. **There’s a Thug in My Life:** J-Status feature; live drums credited (Rudy Maya), keyboard program.

12. **Now I Know:** Piano and actual violin/viola/cello ensemble; ballad, NOT dancehall.

13. **Pon de Replay (Remix):** Elephant Man featured; album closing remix, not used to define original track production.


**Critic disagreement / methodological caution:** Slant stresses uneven track sequencing while AllMusic emphasizes the effective Caribbean crossover. The genuine late-album strings and piano are retained instead of declaring a homogeneous reggae album. Freestyle is only a texture-level inference from Full Force association.

## Key open questions / abstentions
Instrument families derive from credited players and named review textures, not presumed synths on every track. No invented proprietary drum-machine models. No samples in `influenced_by` without evidence they were deliberate artistic inspiration. The first two albums are new catalogue entries; the 2007 and 2009 ids are already in the catalog. The original 2007 album is not the 2008 Reloaded version.
