# The Verve — Phase 01: Evidence notes (research date 2026-10-26)

These are draft, album-specific judgements. `sound_like` is a similarity hypothesis rather than a sourced causal influence. Scores follow `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`; descriptions are paraphrases, not copied criticism. Genre scope labels distinguish album-wide classification, occasional tracks and mere rhythmic/timbral texture.

## `verve-verve` — *Verve* (1992, EP)

- **Identity / date**: MusicBrainz catalogues the release group as **Verve**, with the US issue named **Verve EP** (1992-12-01). AllMusic calls it **The Verve EP** and says November 1992; some discographies say 7 December. Use year 1992 and canonical historical release-group title `Verve`, ID `verve-verve`. Verify canonical title with Claude before import. Original artist billing was **Verve**, before the later name **The Verve**; the Fandome `artist` uses the current catalog display name.
- **Why included**: Landmark standalone debut EP, not a compilation or live set. Its five studio recordings predate *A Storm in Heaven*. The alternate extended/live singles and the compilation *No Come Down* are not separate albums here. Sources E1–E4.
- **Genres**: AllMusic documents shoegaze, space rock, indie rock and alternative/indie rock. Album of the Year tags dream pop, neo-psychedelia, post-rock and **Space Rock Revival**; AOTY is a genre-tag aggregator, so those more marginal tags have medium evidence. MusicBrainz also tags ambient (texture only). `spacerockrevival` is a new source-exact token, not a substitute for `spacerock`. E1–E3.
- **Instruments**: Guitar, bass, drums explicitly listed in EP credits E4. Original `Gravity Grave` single has guest flute Yvette Lacy and harmonica Rob Ford (E5); included only because the edited studio track is on the EP; their audible prominence is track-specific, not throughout the EP.
- **Production**: Barry Clempson produced four tracks; Paul Schroeder produced *A Man Called Sun*. Tony Harris mixing alone must not appear as a producer. No effects model is claimed: the Copicat/Space Echo retrospective E6 associates them with **early Verve recordings generally**, not unequivocally with these five EP mixes. Column deliberately blank.
- **Scales**: Energy 3 (swelling, sustained rock passages), electronic 1 (largely electric ensemble with effects, not synthesis-led), mood 2 (trance-like, tense), theatrical 2, experimental 4 (lengthy exploratory structures), polish 2 (early EP), tempo 3, groove 2. Source: E1, E3; editorial judgements about this EP, not band-wide defaults.
- **Voice and lyrics**: Album-specific vocal-type descriptions are insufficiently precise, so `voice` is **empty**. Album-wide lyrical themes are likewise insufficiently documented, so `lyrics` is **empty**. No documented album-specific artist influence; `influenced_by` **empty**. The comparisons in `sounds_like` are editorial, not genealogical.

## `verve-astorminheaven` — *A Storm in Heaven* (1993)

- **Production**: John Leckie credited sole producer/mixer, John Cornfield for engineering and **programming**. Kick Horns credited with their horn arrangements. E6 states that McCabe used a **Roland GS-6** by the time of *A Storm in Heaven*, so `rolandgs6` is a new production token. MusicRadar A5 explicitly calls the album heavily reverberant and describes effects-laden guitars; `heavyreverb` is documented, not assumed from shoegaze. A2–A5/E6.
- **Instruments**: Core electric guitars/bass/drums, plus McCabe piano/keyboards/accordion, Ashcroft acoustic guitar and percussion, and Kick Horns trumpets/saxophone plus Simon Clarke and Yvette Lacey flutes. Distinguish occasional guest horn/flute colours from the dominant guitars. A2–A3.
- **Genre**: AllMusic's full album style list includes space rock, shoegaze, dream pop, indie rock, neo-psychedelia, noise pop, indie pop, alternative/indie rock, alternative pop/rock and Britpop. Britpop is primarily a contextual tag, not the dominant sound. A1.
- **Scores**: Energy 3 and tempo 3 because long floating/quiet sections alternate with squalls; electronic 1 (electric guitar effects are not synthesizer-led), mood 3 (light/dark contrasts), theatrical 3, experimental 4 (extended passages and layered sonic space), polish 3 (Leckie studio recording), groove 2. Compare John Leckie's direct comments on extremes of volume (A4) and critic description (A1).
- **Voice/lyrics/influence**: John Leckie describes the band's force and singer's energy, supporting `powerful` without assuming other vocal types. Abstract lyric content is not established systematically, hence `lyrics` blank. Although McCabe has named a number of general musical influences (see import document), **none is claimed specifically for this album**: `influenced_by` empty.
- **Reception**: A6 notes a mixed reception at release, followed by stronger retrospective appreciation. `landmark=1` refers to its role in establishing the original sonic identity, not uniform initial critical acclaim.

## `verve-anorthernsoul` — *A Northern Soul* (1995)

- **Production / equipment**: Album credits name **Owen Morris & The Verve** as joint producers; recorded/mixed at Loco Studios, with orchestral overdubs for “History” at Abbey Road. Morris described capturing full-band room sound and often using **first playback** and the run-through vocals; tag `liverecording` means *ensemble performance captured live in a studio*, **not** a live concert album. `roomambience` directly supported by N3. `stringarrangements` refers specifically to “History”, credits to Wil Malone/Morris. A Moog is explicitly credited to Nick McCabe on “Stormy Clouds”; tag `moog` is model-family-level as source gives no exact model. N2–N3.
- **Instruments**: Electric and acoustic guitars, piano, Hammond organ (`organ`), electric piano, keyboards, Moog synthesizer (`synth`), bass/drums/percussion, string ensemble and Liam Gallagher handclaps (“History”). Guest Liam Gallagher has **no producer credit** and is **not a musical influence** by virtue of performing. N2/N7.
- **Genre / critical disagreement**: AllMusic tags shoegaze yet directly says the album moved away from the overt shoegazing of its predecessor; `shoegaze` ordered low and scoped **some tracks**. MusicBrainz also tags neo-psychedelia; N6 explicitly names psychedelic rock and alternative rock. N1/N5/N6.
- **Influence**: McCabe directly discussed **Richard Thompson** and **Funkadelic / Eddie Hazel** in relation to the guitar approach during *A Northern Soul*. Enter `Funkadelic|Richard Thompson`; Eddie Hazel is the band member behind that documented Funkadelic reference, and could also be a separate person-level link if Claude's artist catalogue supports it. N4.
- **Scores**: Energy 4, electronic 1, mood 1, theatrical 4, experimental 3, polish 2, tempo 3, groove 2. AllMusic documents bleak emotional force; Morris describes a relatively minimally overdubbed, live-in-the-room sound. “History” strings do not mean the entire record is orchestral. N1/N3.
- **Lyrics**: Painful isolation, disillusionment and intimate self-scrutiny documented by AllMusic; `introspection|heartbreak|identity` is a controlled-vocabulary interpretation. No fabricated political or spiritual themes.

## `verve-urbanhymns` — *Urban Hymns* (1997)

- **Producers**: Contemporary liner notes U3 credit **Youth / The Verve** on most songs and **Potter / The Verve** on “Catching the Butterfly”, “Neon Wilderness”, “Space and Time”, “Weeping Willow”, and “Come On”. Therefore `Youth|The Verve|Chris Potter`. Chris Potter's **additional production** role on other songs is **not** the basis for his inclusion. Do **not** list **Andrew Loog Oldham** as an album producer: he produced the historical **sample source** by the Andrew Oldham Orchestra, not The Verve's 1997 tracks. U3/U5/U8.
- **Instruments**: Orchestral strings and looped orchestral sample particularly distinguish “Bitter Sweet Symphony”; Wil Malone credited arranging/conducting strings, and the original recording features core drums, bass, guitars, keyboard playing by Simon Tong and credited programming by Mel Wesson / Paul Taylor. U3. `orchestra` and `strings` both explicit, but **not** dominant on every track.
- **Production**: `sampling` confirmed both by the original liner and Youth's interview; `stringarrangements` Wil Malone; `programming` credited to Mel Wesson and Paul Taylor. **Do not** tag the specific sample as The Rolling Stones' own recording: it came from **the Andrew Oldham Orchestra's instrumental interpretation** of “The Last Time”. Nor does sampling establish a musical `influenced_by` relation to the Stones. U3/U5/U8.
- **Genres**: Britpop lead; AllMusic lists indie rock, dream pop and alternative pop/rock. MusicBrainz lists neo-psychedelia, alternative rock and post-Britpop. Pitchfork highlights cosmic funk and hip-hop-informed beats (both as **textures**, not overall primary genres) and soft-rock ballads on part of the album. Genres are ranked accordingly. U1/U2/U4.
- **Influences**: In a reprinted **1997 interview**, Richard Ashcroft states that Black soul music, **hip-hop and Funkadelic** have significantly informed The Verve's work and points explicitly to those elements on *Urban Hymns*, also naming Curtis Mayfield, Marvin Gaye, and Sly and the Family Stone in the same passage. These qualify as direct album-linked musical influences, not merely comparisons. U7. `hiphop` remains a genre/texture token, not a performer name in `influenced_by`.
- **Scales**: Energy 3, electronic 2, mood 3, theatrical 4, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. Large string arrangements and programmed loops coexist with a live rock band; lush pop ballads alternate with rhythm-driven pieces. Main evidence U1/U2/U3.
- **Lyrics**: Love/devotion, loss, pressure of money/work, mortality, self-definition and optimism appear across the album. `introspection|love|social|heartbreak|identity` controls are album-level synthesis from U2, U6, U9; no religious or spiritual theme is asserted merely from the album title.
- **Reception**: Critical retrospective U2 treats *Urban Hymns* as the band’s commercial high-water mark and a defining record. The three preceding records deserve separate recommendation matching: do not project “Bitter Sweet Symphony” onto them.

## Data quality / caveats

- **Fandome IDs** for existing studio albums follow the normal slug rule (`verve-astorminheaven`, `verve-anorthernsoul`, `verve-urbanhymns`, `verve-forth`), but **Claude must check IDs produced by seed.ts on import**. No descriptor file for `verve` was available via repository paths checked at the beginning of research.
- **New tokens** used: `spacerockrevival` genre (AOTY specifically distinguishes Space Rock Revival), `rolandgs6` production (Total Guitar’s A Storm in Heaven era documentation). Update genreFamilies.ts and the validator's own genre allowlist to register `spacerockrevival`; add `rolandgs6` in production.ts.
- **Sources can disagree**: original EP naming and original 1992 release month, *A Storm in Heaven* initial versus retrospective acclaim, and whether *A Northern Soul* should still be considered shoegaze. They are documented here rather than suppressed.
- No remixes, deluxe-edition bonus live sets, singles, cover recordings or compilations treated as new albums.
