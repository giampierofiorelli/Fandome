# Billie Eilish | Fandome Phase 01 | Evidence notes

Date cut-off: 26 October 2026. This package includes **only the three phase-01 descriptor rows**. The source and genre-evidence files describe this batch, not future albums. Reference album editions are the 2017 eight-track original EP, the 2019 standard 14-track debut, and the 2021 standard 16-track second album. All scale scores and `sounds_like` are editorial judgments grounded in contemporary descriptions, not measurements. `status=draft` throughout.

## billie-dontsmileatme — *dont smile at me* (2017, EP)

- **Identity / provenance.** The defining eight-track release is a genuine original EP (not the subsequent expanded editions); MusicBrainz track entries and AllMusic confirm original release and FINNEAS as credited producer. The later “&burn” featuring Vince Staples appears in expanded sequences, **not** a co-billed album by Billie Eilish and Vince Staples. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/dont-smile-at-me-mw0003095797); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9c2db1c7-08bc-41b2-9b21-840241690d7d).
- **Genres and balance.** AllMusic describes electronic pop and indie electronic; Album of the Year’s genre taxonomy additionally says alt-pop, electropop, alternative R&B, trap and bedroom pop. Billie herself described the material she was then making in terms of hip-hop, R&B and trap production. Thus the latter styles, especially trap, refer to selected tracks and production textures; the package does not claim that every song is trap. `bedroompop` is absent from the current scenes.ts Genre type and must be registered. **Genre metadata is weaker evidence than named critics or artist statements**. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/dont-smile-at-me-mw0003095797); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/86566-billie-eilish-dont-smile-at-me.php); [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/pop-newcomer-billie-eilish-wants-to-make-sure-you-never-forget-her).
- **Instruments.** Piano and synthetic keyboard colors feature in “COPYCAT”, “idontwannabeyouanymore” and “ocean eyes”; Billie’s ukulele is documented on “party favor”; mixer Kinelski directly recalls organic drums and acoustic elements on “bellyache”. FINNEAS states in his studio interview that “Ocean Eyes” used modified stock Logic instrument sounds. The recording-specific proof for ukulele comes from the original EP liner-note credit as reproduced in published discographic reporting; neither a bass guitar nor a physical TR-808 is asserted simply because the music has heavy low end. [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/pop-newcomer-billie-eilish-wants-to-make-sure-you-never-forget-her); [FINNEAS video](https://pitchfork.com/video/watch/finneas-critical-breakthroughs); [Waves interview](https://www.waves.com/breaking-the-mix-rules-with-billie-eilish-rob-kinelski); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/billie-eilish).
- **Production.** FINNEAS demonstrates lit matches recorded at home and used to form the snare sound in “watch”, and multiple vocal doubles / harmonies assembled for “ocean eyes”; these justify `sampling`, `drumprogramming`, `layeredvocals`. `logic` rests on his explicit description of stock Logic instruments in “ocean eyes”. An “808” bass/kick *sound* was mentioned by Kinelski, **not a documented Roland TR-808 device**, so `tr808` is deliberately excluded. [FINNEAS video](https://pitchfork.com/video/watch/finneas-critical-breakthroughs); [Waves](https://www.waves.com/breaking-the-mix-rules-with-billie-eilish-rob-kinelski); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/billie-eilish).
- **Voice, words and scoring.** Close, soft melodies alternate with clearer lead phrasing. Fictional-persona storytelling (“bellyache”), intimate romantic songs (“ocean eyes”) and identity/self-image (“idontwannabeyouanymore”) justify the lyrical tags. The EP is noticeably brighter and less maximalist than the horror-oriented 2019 album: energy 3 / electronic 3 / mood 2 / theatrical 2 / experimental 2 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 3. [Coup de Main](https://www.coupdemainmagazine.com/billie-eilish/13081); [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/pop-newcomer-billie-eilish-wants-to-make-sure-you-never-forget-her); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/dont-smile-at-me-mw0003095797).
- **Documented album-specific influences.** Billie says Tyler, the Creator’s “Garbage” explicitly inspired “bellyache”. FINNEAS says “ocean eyes” was developed with sonic references including AURORA and Låpsley, and tentatively Lana Del Rey. Those names therefore enter `influenced_by`, with their **song-limited evidentiary scope** recorded here. The artist’s general fandom of other musicians is not automatically used to populate this album field. [Teen Vogue](https://www.teenvogue.com/video/watch/billie-eilish-creates-the-soundtrack-to-her-life); [Pitchfork/FINNEAS](https://pitchfork.com/video/watch/finneas-critical-breakthroughs).
- **Potential ambiguity.** “Bedroom pop” in aggregator tags is a genre classification; this is not a claim that all of the EP was literally recorded in a bedroom. Instruments are based on named tracks in the specific EP rather than inferred from tags. Source-dependent scores remain `confidence=medium`.

## billie-whenweallfallasleepwheredowego — *When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go?* (2019)

- **Identity.** 2019 studio debut, **not** the 2017 EP; existing Fandome catalog ID is preserved. Standard 14-track album only: 2019–20 deluxe bonus tracks (including later stand-alone singles) must not be allowed to contaminate the album’s aggregate features. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/e9602130-b5a0-48da-9a95-27e3c01bef0a); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/when-we-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-mw0003250262).
- **Genres.** AllMusic identifies pop, indie electronic, and hip-hop; the AOTY genre listing adds alt-pop, electropop, alternative R&B, art pop and ambient pop. A contemporary Pitchfork review explicitly notes its pop-to-trap-and-EDM approach and calls the single “you should see me in a crown” a dubstep bass-drop example. Rolling Stone's contemporary description, preserved in Metacritic, names *avant-pop*. The Guardian describes show-tune passages. These latter terms are **track-level or occasional textures**, placed last in the genre ordering. Crucially, `synthpop` has **not** been substituted for documented `electropop`. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/when-we-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-mw0003250262); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go/); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/137995-billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go.php); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go/billie-eilish/critic-reviews); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/mar/31/billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-review).
- **Instruments.** Credits identify bass and synthesizer on “you should see me in a crown”, and bass, percussion and piano on “when the party’s over”; sound engineer/producer accounts cover live acoustic and electric guitar, bass, layered virtual piano, sub-bass and drums. Critics and production analysts document the ukulele on “8”. The main synth/sub-bass focus should not erase ballads, though the instruments list includes occasional album-specific instruments. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/e9602130-b5a0-48da-9a95-27e3c01bef0a); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/billie-eilish); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/how-to-sound-like-billie-eilish-bad-guy); [FINNEAS demo](https://pitchfork.com/video/watch/finneas-critical-breakthroughs).
- **Production and machine caution.** FINNEAS names Apple Logic Pro X, Logic Vocal Transformer and deliberately altered voice timbres; he demonstrates severe vocal distortion (“bury a friend”), extensive vocal stacks (“when the party’s over”), and pitch-shifted airplane button tones (“i love you”). MusicRadar reports pitch-shifted vocals on “8”. Sound On Sound reports purposeful Auto-Tune effects on selected tracks, **not general corrective Auto-Tune**, and stresses generally dry vocals with only selective reverb. Foley and captured pedestrian-crossing and dental sounds justify `fieldrecordings`/`sampling`; sounds from *The Office* are also explicitly reused in “my strange addiction”. `vocoder` is excluded: a critic's “vocoder-style” description does not prove a vocoder was used. No model-specific sampler or drum machine is asserted. [Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/billie-eilish); [FINNEAS demo](https://pitchfork.com/video/watch/finneas-critical-breakthroughs); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/how-to-sound-like-billie-eilish-bad-guy); [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/billie-eilish-album-review-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-2467653).
- **Voice, words, scales.** Soft near-whispered and occasionally spoken deliveries, gothic dreams, alienation and relational unease are well described in Pitchfork and The Guardian. The record balances an unusually sparse arrangement with hard low-end attacks: energy 3 / electronic 4 / mood 1 / theatrical 3 / experimental 4 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 3. Do **not** elevate all album tempos to “fast” based on “bad guy” or single dance highlights. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go/); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/mar/29/billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-review); [Waves](https://www.waves.com/breaking-the-mix-rules-with-billie-eilish-rob-kinelski).
- **Sounds-like vs influence.** Stylistic neighbors Lorde and Lana Del Rey are documented as critic comparisons, **not proven album-specific inspirations**, hence `sounds_like` rather than `influenced_by`. Grimes and Tyler the Creator are editorial sonic neighbors; leave `influenced_by` blank for this album rather than propagate general artist influences without track-level production attribution. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-when-we-all-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go/); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/when-we-fall-asleep-where-do-we-go-mw0003250262).

## billie-happierthanever — *Happier Than Ever* (2021)

- **Identity and credits.** Second studio LP, 16-track standard edition, existing album ID. FINNEAS is consistently named producer on the release and individual tracks. MusicBrainz’s transcription explicitly credits piano, synthesizer, bass guitar, synth bass, acoustic / classical / electric guitar, Mellotron, Wurlitzer electric piano, percussion, drum machine programming and general programming. The track “Happier Than Ever” includes guitar, percussion, synth and programmed drums. These justify the actual **instrument list**; no specific drum machine model appears in reliable credits. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/7aac47e0-4f8f-4b3f-a168-52fd4a2ac211); [Billboard credits](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/16/the-players-behind-billie-eilishs-happier-than-ever-see-the-full-credits/).
- **Genres and counterexamples.** Genre reference services list alt-pop, songwriter, alternative R&B, electropop, pop and indie electronic. Pitchfork describes a substantial jazz/torch-song shift. The Guardian singles out bossa nova on “Billie Bossa Nova” and techno on “Oxytocin”; the LA Times identifies the climactic pop-punk rupture of the title song; TIME describes a dance-pop element and a critic observes trip-hop on “my future” and “Lost Cause”. NME identifies a funk texture on “I Didn’t Change My Number”. These last track-specific genres are placed late: the album is **not** predominantly pop-punk or techno. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/360793-billie-eilish-happier-than-ever.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0003516209); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2021/jul/30/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-review-inside-pop-stardoms-heart-of-darkness); [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2021-08-02/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-album); [TIME](https://time.com/6086022/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/); [Sound Words Central](https://soundwordscentral.com/2021/07/31/album-review-billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/); [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-review-3006594).
- **Production.** “Goldwing” uses layered voices and repeated vocal source material, and the title track shifts into loud digitally distorted guitars with multiple vocal layers. The Guardian explicitly describes the beginning as an intentionally muted lo-fi acoustic ballad (thus `lofi` applies to part of the record, without implying the entire album has rough production). Production credits repeatedly identify `drumprogramming` and `programming`. The record’s instrumentation is more acoustic and jazz-influenced than the 2019 debut but retains strong electronics on a handful of songs; energy 2 / electronic 3 / mood 2 / theatrical 2 / experimental 3 / polish 4 / tempo 2 / groove 2. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/7aac47e0-4f8f-4b3f-a168-52fd4a2ac211); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2021/jul/30/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-review-inside-pop-stardoms-heart-of-darkness); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/).
- **Voice, subjects.** Hushed and clear singing is still predominant. The full-throated vocal climax of the title track justifies `powerful` as a secondary category, and “Not My Responsibility” supports `spoken`. Themes include unwanted attention, body scrutiny, agency, exploitation and heartbreak. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/); [ELLE](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a37598890/billie-eilish-is-calling-the-shots/); [ABC triple j](https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/news/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-feature-album-review-interview/13482004).
- **Documented album-specific inspiration.** Billie explicitly named Julie London as the atmosphere she wanted the album to capture in her Los Angeles Times interview. Other contemporaneous interviews acknowledge Peggy Lee and Frank Sinatra as reference points among the older torch/jazz repertoire. No claim is made that Eilish copied specific arrangements. [LA Times interview](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2021-07-23/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-album); [ELLE](https://www.elle.com/culture/celebrities/a37598890/billie-eilish-is-calling-the-shots/); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/).
- **Critical disagreement.** Some reviewers find the intimate restraint compelling, while others call the long sequence of softer tracks monotonous; the loud, harsh second half of “Happier Than Ever” is a dramatic **track** outlier. The Sound Words Central trip-hop designation is a weaker source and appropriately restricted to a couple of tracks. The album’s dominant scoring is not changed to match its loudest moment. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/); [ABC triple j](https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/news/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-feature-album-review-interview/13482004); [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/billie-eilish-happier-than-ever-review-3006594); [Sound Words Central](https://soundwordscentral.com/2021/07/31/album-review-billie-eilish-happier-than-ever/).

## Evidence limitations / guardrails

- `sounds_like` is an editorial, critic-informed similarity map; it is not the same as a claim of conscious influence. Numeric scales are cross-album editorial rankings, not observations about hardware or measured waveforms.
- Sound On Sound is directly findable in search indexing but sometimes serves a 403 to automated readers; claims included from its indexed first-person producer excerpts are attributed to that publication, cross-checked with Mix, Pitchfork and Waves where possible.
- Genre labels from Album of the Year are metadata/aggregation; named reviews and producer evidence get priority. `bedroompop` is a new genre token because it is not in the type as of this audit.
- `tr808` is **never** inferred from a mixer talking about “808s.” `vocoder` is not assumed from a reviewer saying “vocoder-style.” A sampled sound is not an `influenced_by` artist. No live or hits/compilation albums are eligible here.
- The EP's original tracklist differs from its regional later issues; 2019 album reissues or bonus tracks and 2021 edition variants must not leak into album-level tags.
