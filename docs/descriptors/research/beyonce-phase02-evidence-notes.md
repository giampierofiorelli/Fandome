# Beyoncé — Phase 02: evidence notes (three new rows only)

Read alongside `beyonce_phase02_sources.csv` and the per-genre scope table. These are **research-led editorial scores**, not measured BPM, streaming popularity, or claims of direct audio analysis. All producer assignments are based on **credited producer or co-producer** roles and deliberately omit additional-only credits. Some digital and discographic releases disagree; conflicts are detailed below.

## 2013 — Beyoncé (`beyonce-beyonce`)

- **Type/date/id:** Studio album and original 13 December 2013 surprise release. Source: [Sony original announcement](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2013-12-13-BEYONC-Available-Worldwide-Now); Fandome catalogue already includes it.
- **Genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/beyonc%C3%A9-mw0002607521) supplies `contemporaryrnb`, `rnb`, `pop`, `adultcontemporaryrnb`. [Album of the Year profile](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/11907-beyonce-beyonce.php) explicitly names `alternativernb`, `poprap`, `electropop`, `artpop`, `altpop`. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/18821-beyonce-beyonce/) documents strongly hip-hop-informed styles. `trap` is independently documented for *Flawless* in a [Pitchfork track discussion](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/17969-beyonce-formation/) and confined to selected tracks, not a blanket label for the album. Genre evidence assigns different scopes.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (hard bass and rhythmic vocals coexist with slower intimate passages); electronic **3** (programmed electronic synth layers mixed with documented live players); mood **2** (dark and sexually charged, with tender moments); theatrical **3** (controlled but dramatic at points); experimental **3** (multi-part and discontinuous song structures, but accessible); polish **5** (dense high-budget mixing); tempo **2** (many slow/midtempo songs); groove **3** (rhythmic cuts but no continuous dancefloor sequence). Based on [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/18821-beyonce-beyonce/), [Guardian review 1](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/dec/13/beyonce-album-first-review) and [Guardian review 2](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/dec/13/beyonce-beyonce-review).
- **Voice:** Powerful, soft and clear sung passages and unmistakable rhythmic rap/half-rap. Guardian reports falsetto, included as `falsetto` in production rather than as a voice token. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/dec/13/beyonce-beyonce-review).
- **Instruments:** [Qobuz original track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/beyonce-beyonce/0886444905411) list Derek Dixie/Caroline Polachek synthesizer, Caroline as programmer, BOOTS and others on drums/guitars/keyboard/piano, Dwayne Wright bass, Margot violin, and real horns for *Blow*. These are **track-credited instruments** ordered by album prominence; no specific synth brand is inferred.
- **Production:** `programming` (Caroline Polachek on *No Angel*), `drumprogramming` (MusicBrainz lists drum-machine programming on *Mine*), `sampling` (the speech excerpt on *Flawless*; a spoken-text sample does not count as musical influence), `falsetto` ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/dec/13/beyonce-beyonce-review) vocal description). Do not infer Pro Tools from engineer Stuart White's later equipment roster for a different album.
- **Producers:** [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/beyonce-beyonce/0886444905411) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/84eb58b5-c03d-4528-b730-6b01d576259e) support producer/co-producer track roles for the listed people. Competing edition metadata conflict for several **additional production** credits: BOOTS is a principal producer on multiple tracks, so kept; Mike Dean on *Partition*, Majid Jordan/Omen on *Mine*, and HazeBanga on *Jealous* receive differently scoped roles between databases and are excluded to avoid credit inflation. Hit-Boy has a full producer role on *Flawless* and/or *XO*, not merely a writer. Frank Ocean, Drake and Jay-Z are guests, not credited producers by default. Some personnel are producers only on specific tracks, not album-wide creative directors.
- **Influences:** Left **empty**. Guardian hears M.I.A./Rihanna resemblances; Pitchfork speculates about Solange as a possible cue. Neither is sufficient as a demonstrated named *musical influence on this specific album*. Those resemblances appear in editorial `sounds_like` only; sampling and guest credits do not populate `influenced_by`.
- **Scope/edition:** Use the **original 14-track 2013 album**. Later platinum-edition bonus tracks (*7/11*, *Ring Off*, remixes, etc.) cannot change these descriptions. Beyoncé's speeches/guest voices are not separate albums. [Sony](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2013-12-13-BEYONC-Available-Worldwide-Now).

## 2016 — Lemonade (`beyonce-lemonade`)

- **Type and main idiom:** Original 23 April 2016 12-song studio album. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/MW0002940342) anchors R&B/pop. [AudioTechnology's Stuart White interview](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-making-lemonade) names soul, hip-hop, blues, rock, gospel and trap in the project; [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21867-lemonade/) confirms *Daddy Lessons* country and *Don't Hurt Yourself* psychedelic-soul/rock; [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/style/beyonces-lemonade-turns-lifes-lemons-into-furious-pop/2016/04/24/cce58aec-0a4d-11e6-8ab8-9ad050f76d7d_story.html) explicitly describes *Hold Up* as reggae, [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/05/30/beyonces-lemonade) as dancehall, and [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/recording/beyonce-is-a-scientist-of-songs-ive-never-seen-anyone-work-the-way-she-works-why-beyonces-lemonade-remains-an-enduring-21st-century-classic) explicitly calls *Don't Hurt Yourself* blues-rock. [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/54075-beyonce-lemonade.php) gives art-pop and pop-soul/alternative-R&B classifications. Country, blues-rock, psychedelic soul, reggae and dancehall are **narrow/track-specific** and not implied to dominate the record.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (rock fury and punchy rap contrast with slow piano-led interludes), electronic **3** (sample-based production plus extensive live strings/drums), mood **1** (pain, rage and betrayal outweigh late reconciliation), theatrical **4** (high-stakes narrative and expressive singing), experimental **3** (extreme genre shifts within coherent pop structures), polish **5** (large session and complex in-the-box mixing), tempo **3** (balanced slow ballads and driving cuts), groove **3** (several rhythm-driven tracks, many reflective moments). Evidence [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21867-lemonade/), [Stuart White production interview](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-making-lemonade).
- **Voice/lyrics:** The record alternates intimate singing, clipped rap-like delivery, full-throated belting and the distorted vocal performance of *Don't Hurt Yourself*. Themes `heartbreak|love|identity|political|social|introspection|spiritual|sensuality|storytelling` are supported by criticism on betrayal, marriage repair, *Freedom* and *Formation*, not assumed autobiographical facts. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21867-lemonade/); [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-making-lemonade).
- **Instruments:** [Original credited session details](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2016-04-24/14173/here-are-the-over-129-names-responsible-for-beyonces-lemonade-lp) and [digital track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/lemonade-beyonce/qacn1updzfzwc) establish Kevin Garrett/James Blake piano, multiple named violin/viola/cello players (grouped `strings`), Jack White bass/drums/organ parts, guitar and brass on *Daddy Lessons*, and keyboards. `trumpet` is specifically credited on *Formation*. Do not add a specific hardware synthesizer based on another person's studio equipment; James Blake's 'Jupiter Bass' is an instrument description **not** evidence of a Jupiter-6/8 physical machine.
- **Production:** `sampling` is confirmed by Stuart White (drum augmentation on *Don't Hurt Yourself*) and the Alan Lomax source recordings on *Freedom*. `stringarrangements` is directly credited to Jon Brion for *Pray You Catch Me*. `drumprogramming` is an explicit Derek Dixie credit on *Daddy Lessons*. `protools` is directly described by Stuart White as the album recording/editing platform. `analogtape` is **specific to *Don't Hurt Yourself***, whose incoming multitrack was recorded on eight-track analog tape, later integrated digitally. `distortedvocals` comes from his direct Shure SM58/overdriven monitor description of Beyoncé's *Don't Hurt Yourself* take; `belting` from Pitchfork's voice characterization. [Engineer interview](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-making-lemonade); [credits](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2016-04-24/14173/here-are-the-over-129-names-responsible-for-beyonces-lemonade-lp).
- **Producers:** The [FADER release-day tracklist](https://www.thefader.com/2016/04/23/full-album-credits-beyonce-lemonade) is the primary roster; [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/lemonade-beyonce/qacn1updzfzwc) and [REVOLT full credits](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2016-04-24/14173/here-are-the-over-129-names-responsible-for-beyonces-lemonade-lp) add Emile Haynie (credited producer on *Hold Up*), Pluss (co-producer on *Formation*), Hit-Boy (co-producer on *Sorry*) and Derek Dixie (co-producer on *Don't Hurt Yourself*). Some reprints list only the headline producer rather than all co-producers. Stuart White is principally recording/mixing, so excluded; on this edition he should **not** be listed as an album producer solely because he says he occasionally co-produced material unless a track-specific producer/co-producer credit is identified. Kendrick Lamar and The Weeknd are featured musicians only.
- **Influences:** No `influenced_by` value assigned. *Don't Hurt Yourself* evokes Tina Turner/Janis Joplin in [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21867-lemonade/) and uses Led Zeppelin's sampled *When the Levee Breaks*: neither constitutes a directly documented named musical influence at album level. [Grammy oral history](https://www.grammy.com/news/beyonce-oral-history/) confirms Jack White is a collaborator/producer, not necessarily a preceding influence.
- **Disagreements:** The critic's vivid reading of betrayal is **a reading of the artistic narrative**, not external confirmation about real-life private events. The engineer lists stylistic elements that do not all span every song. The 2019 streaming expansion includes *Sorry (Original Demo)*: do not let bonus material change the original 2016 descriptor.

## 2018 — Everything Is Love (`beyonce-everythingislove`)

- **Credit and album identity:** Originally released 16 June 2018 as a new studio collaboration **billed THE CARTERS** (Beyoncé and JAY-Z). The source [MusicBrainz release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/37055326-02e0-4e6d-9b01-0ab860ca7559) uses `THE CARTERS`; [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/the-carters-everything-is-love/) and [FADER release-day report](https://www.thefader.com/2018/06/16/beyonce-and-jay-z-album-credits) independently corroborate. `artist` remains Beyoncé **only** to place the row in `beyonce.csv`; actual printed credit goes in `coBilled.ts` as `{ credit: 'The Carters' }`. This is **not** a Beyoncé solo album and not a compilation.
- **Genres:** [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/the-carters-everything-is-love/) documents predominantly hip-hop/rap, a trap-focused *Apeshit* and a synth-pop *Heard About Us*. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/everything-is-love-mw0003186245) uses contemporary R&B/R&B. The contemporary [HipHopDX review quoted by Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/everything-is-love/the-carters/critic-reviews) explicitly reports reggae, soul and **boom bap** used on the record: the latter is the **new token** `boombap`, not mapped to trap or hiphop. Some styles are only track-scoped.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (explosive *Apeshit* and *Boss* counter quieter *Summer*, *Friends*), electronic **3** (trap and synthetic textures with verified bass, brass, strings), mood **4** (reconciliation/pride/celebration), theatrical **3** (deliberate grandeur but rap-first delivery), experimental **2** (variety within mostly familiar rap/R&B forms), polish **5** (detailed ensemble and glossy rap production), tempo **3** (mid-tempo emphasis with faster flows), groove **3** (repeated pulse but not an uninterrupted dance album). [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/the-carters-everything-is-love/).
- **Voice:** Both principals rap; Beyoncé adds powerful, clear, softer singing. `rap` goes first because this is a genuinely co-billed hip-hop/R&B record, not Beyoncé singing over a guest rap on one track.
- **Instruments:** [MusicBrainz original CD](https://musicbrainz.org/release/37055326-02e0-4e6d-9b01-0ab860ca7559) specifically credits Damien Farmer bass, multiple horn players and a string section, plus Derek Dixie's keyboards on *Summer*, and horns on *Boss*. No live drums or drum-machine model added in absence of album-specific credits.
- **Production:** Only `hornarrangements|stringarrangements`, both directly attributed to Derek Dixie on *Summer* [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/37055326-02e0-4e6d-9b01-0ab860ca7559). The credits do not justify naming a Roland TR-808, trap-only drum machine, Auto-Tune, or particular DAW. Some reviewers recognize quoted famous hooks, but a quotation is not automatically confirmed as sampling.
- **Producers:** [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/37055326-02e0-4e6d-9b01-0ab860ca7559) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/everything-is-love-mw0003186245) distinguish main production and co-production from additional-only roles. Keep primary Beyoncé, JAY-Z, Cool & Dre, El Michels, Pharrell, D’Mile, Boi-1da, Jahaan Sweet, NAV, Sevn Thomas, Vinylz, !llmind and David Andrew Sitek. **Exclude Mike Dean, Derek Dixie, MeLo-X, Stuart White, Fred Ball and 808-Ray when their sole relevant album credit is 'additional production'.** The [release-day FADER list](https://www.thefader.com/2018/06/16/beyonce-and-jay-z-album-credits) uses loose producer headings in *Boss* and *713*, while the granular [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/37055326-02e0-4e6d-9b01-0ab860ca7559) roles explicitly classify them as additional. The original **nine-track** project excludes separately released bonus cut *Salud!*; do not add Beat Butcha from bonus credits.
- **Influences:** Left empty: lyrical homage to *Still D.R.E.* and a Notorious B.I.G. reference, artist guests (Offset/Quavo), and credited producers do not demonstrate artistic influence on the whole album.
- **Classification caveat:** This **should** be a co-billed album, not silently counted as another Beyoncé solo LP; add album in catalogue and add `beyonce-everythingislove` to `coBilled.ts` with printed credit 'The Carters'. If a JAY-Z artist room exists, Claude should decide whether to reference its ID in `with` after independently checking its actual ID.
