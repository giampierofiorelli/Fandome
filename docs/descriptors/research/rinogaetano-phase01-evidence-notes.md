# Rino Gaetano — Phase 01: album-by-album evidence and doubts

Branch: `descriptors`; artist ID: `rinogaetano`. Album ratings are *whole-record editorial judgments* under the eight guide anchors, not objective measurements. Source keys below refer to the included URL-bearing source CSV. Blank values mean insufficient album-specific evidence.

## `rinogaetano-ingressolibero` — *Ingresso libero* (1974; 9 tracks)

- **Genres:** songwriter/folk are explicitly identified in Rolling Stone’s review; AllMusic calls the album Italian pop and comedy rock, while chanson and pop-rock come from other style listings; `folkblues` is supported only for *L’operaio della Fiat*, so it appears last (1/9). No plain `rock`: nobody specifically documents the *whole album* as rock. Do not turn the style of a single track into a whole-record genre.
- **Energy 2, tempo 2, groove 1:** Rolling Stone describes acoustic folk and mostly song-led writing and ballads, not club-oriented rhythms. Tempo/groove reflect that distribution of tracks, not the mere existence of acoustic instruments.
- **Electronic 1:** acoustic guitars alongside a documented Hammond organ; do not infer a drum machine, synthesizer or other electronic models.
- **Mood 3:** Rolling Stone contrasts sunny folk with melancholia and bitter satire; no one mood dominates.
- **Theatrical 3, experimental 2:** Treccani documents theatrical/burlesque songs, paradoxical images and the studio process of trying unusual timbres; still recognizable 1974 songwriter structures, not avant-garde techniques.
- **Polish 2:** critics describe an uneven/rough debut and spontaneous studio arrangements; this is a comparative judgment, not a claim of four-track or lo-fi recording.
- **Instruments:** `acousticguitar|organ|bass|guitar`: Rolling Stone explicitly acoustic guitars and Hammond, Treccani recounts Gaetano’s guitar in these sessions, and SentireAscoltare explicitly credits Piero Ricci bass and Luciano Ciccaglioni guitars. Do not silently assign the report’s doubtful Massimo Buzzi 'chitarra' to the usual drummer; the credits vary. No other instruments guessed.
- **Production and producers blank:** SentireAscoltare explicitly says the original sleeve does *not* identify the person at the console; Vincenzo Micocci was a record executive and champion, not necessarily the credited producer of this particular LP. No model/effect documented here. No remastering token for the 2024/2026 reissue.
- **Voice blank:** Gaetano's general rough timbre is well-known, but the sources read do not explicitly pin a voice type to this particular 1974 LP, so no artist-wide automatic filling.
- **Lyrics:** love/introspection (opening song), southern identity/emigration, workers and protest, sarcastic stories; Rolling Stone, Treccani and Sony Legacy document these directly.
- **Sounds_like / influenced_by blank:** a critic refers to an airy Pink Floyd-like opening, but the search-indexed SentireAscoltare page cannot be opened reliably (403 in researcher browser), so it is deliberately not converted into an album-specific similarity. Contemporary artist names in Gaetano's notebooks are **global influences**, not proof of influence on individual tracks.
- **Landmark 0:** an important debut and later cult release, but independent reviews explicitly place his breakthrough in 1976–78, not with this album. Confidence medium chiefly because of the uncredited producer and sparse original personnel.

## `rinogaetano-miofratelloefigliounico` — *Mio fratello è figlio unico* (1976; 8 tracks)

- **Genres:** OndaRock’s milestone listing explicitly classifies this particular album `folk`, `pop-rock` and singer-songwriter. Sony reissue style metadata identifies `chanson` and `ballad`. All five are documented rather than extrapolated from Rino's usual sound.
- **Energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 2:** Rolling Stone describes eight often dark but tuneful ballads and folk/pop-rock pieces, with some eccentric rhythmic detail. Neither uniformly slow nor a dance LP.
- **Electronic 2:** a real band with Hammond, an Eminent keyboard and credited Moog/synth; the electronic instruments coexist with drums, guitar, bass, mandolin, sitar and banjo.
- **Mood 2:** Rolling Stone specifically calls the record dark/cohesive, with melancholy, protest and moments of humor.
- **Theatrical 3 / experimental 2:** persona and paradox-driven text plus the banjo/sitar/Moog color are unusual, but songwriting structures remain accessible.
- **Polish 3:** the album has a fuller arranged production than its rough debut; not modern big-budget gloss.
- **Voice `raspy`:** original 1976 review quoted in Treccani characterizes Gaetano’s rough/abrasive singing. Additional backing singers do not automatically make his *lead voice* choral.
- **Instruments:** Hit Parade Italia lists original contributors and their instruments. `guitar|bass|drums|piano|organ|harpsichord|synth|banjo|mandolin|saxophone|trumpet|sitar|accordion|percussion` includes instrument families actually named; `synth` corresponds to Stalteri’s Moog/Eminent, rather than inventing an exact Eminent model.
- **Production:** `moog` is explicitly named as a studio keyboard by Rockol and the LP credits; `choirvocals` is justified by Baba Yaga and other backing choir credits. No inferred analog tape/noise gating/overdubs. No need to add a new token for Eminent when model/version is unspecified.
- **Producers:** Vincenzo Micocci is explicitly labeled Producer on all eight original recordings in Sony/Qobuz metadata. Rino’s `Performance Arranger` role does **not** establish a co-producer credit.
- **Lyrics:** OndaRock and Rolling Stone document exclusion, economic injustice, irony, love and private alienation; note this album was originally issued with an edited *Sfiorivano le viole*. The Sony anniversary reissue due **20 November 2026** proposes to reinstate original censored lyrics, but that edition is **not out as of 30 October 2026**; base descriptor on 1976 album.
- **Sounds_like and influenced_by blank:** no album-specific comparison or qualified musician-documented inspiration verified. One may know Gaetano admired Enzo Jannacci generally, but cannot apply it mechanically to this album.
- **Landmark 1:** OndaRock describes this LP as the decisive artist breakthrough and separately lists it as an Italian-music milestone; this is stronger than a favorite/best-of accolade. High confidence.

## `rinogaetano-aida` — *Aida* (1977; 9 tracks)

- **Genres:** `canzonedautore|poprock|folkrock|pianorock|softrock` arise from explicit genre/style metadata for this specific LP (AOTY and Discogs-indexed Groovespin). `rnb` is *only* for *Standard* (1/9) as directly identified by OndaRock, and `latin` reflects the Spanish-inflected *La festa di Maria* and the Latin tendencies identified by Rolling Stone. Do not add opera because the title and Verdi allusion are **not** operatic sound.
- **Energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3:** OndaRock explicitly calls the whole album faster and more rhythmic than earlier work. A measured title song and quieter pieces keep scores midrange.
- **Electronic 1:** credited keyboards are present, but no specific synthesizer/drum machine or electronic sequencing is verified for this album. An electric keyboard alone is limited electronics.
- **Mood 3:** political satire coexists with mournful history and love/solidarity; no uniformly happy or bleak mood.
- **Theatrical 3:** broader arrangements, narrative drama and vocal performance; not an operatic vocal style.
- **Experimental 2, polish 3:** a wider instrumental palette, choral voices, and designed string arrangements, but still accessible studio singer-songwriter music; OndaRock calls production more refined.
- **Voice `raspy`:** Rolling Stone *specifically for Aida* calls the voice scratched/rough.
- **Instruments:** Hit Parade Italia’s 1977 LP credits guitars, bass, keyboards, percussion, sax/flute, accordion and U.M.R. strings. `drums` deliberately omitted because the 1977 credit calls Buzzi's role percussion, while elsewhere it is sometimes described generically as drums. `piano` not added solely from `pianorock` genre metadata: no verified named 1977 piano player was found.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` is tied to credited orchestral U.M.R. parts arranged by Ciccaglioni/Ricci and conducted by Alessandro Blonksteiner; `choirvocals` is supported by Baba Yaga and Pandemonium on the sleeve. Neither proves analog tape, a specific mixing desk or recorded-on-one-take.
- **Producers blank:** Treccani calls Foffo Bianchi the session leader and Ruggero Cini the arranger, and Sony/Qobuz lists Bianchi as project coordinator/arranger. That is **not** a confirmed original credited producer. Qobuz has a single `Unknown, Producer` on *Fontana chiara*; do not turn it into a named album producer or guess.
- **Sounds_like:** `Bob Dylan [1/9]` for *Spendi spandi effendi* (Rolling Stone's 'Dylan mediterraneo'); `The Beatles [1/9]` for *Rare Tracce* (Indiependent's Lennon/McCartney-styled sound). Those are **critics' sonic comparisons**, not influences. Albums for the cited comparison artists are not identified by sources, so no false bracketed album title.
- **Influenced_by blank:** interviews of Gaetano are not specific enough to link his overall Dylan/Beatles admiration to these two tracks by provenance.
- **Landmark 0:** independent writing calls it artistically mature and forward-looking, but does not unambiguously say it **defined** his whole career or a genre. Medium confidence because of unverified producer and broad pressing-level style classification.

## `rinogaetano-nuntereggaepiu` — *Nuntereggae più* (1978; 9 tracks)

- **Genres:** songwriter from Rolling Stone's album analysis; pop from the Sony/Apple album category; pop-rock from original LP genre/style metadata; reggae **explicitly** the title song, but not proof every song is reggae (it is intentionally fourth). Folk stornelli and Latin elements are described as parts of the mixed record and consequently appear in the tail.
- **Energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** a bouncy reggae title track, popular uptempo *Gianna* and less forceful ballads support the moderate–active rather than full club score.
- **Electronic 1:** keyboards and piano are in the lineup but synthesizers and drum machines are not confirmed for *this* LP; no genre-based inference.
- **Mood 3:** sunny infectious pop and reggae contrast with somber portraits of emigration and institutional corruption.
- **Theatrical 4:** the 1978 *Gianna* performance established the deliberately stagey, clownish delivery of this album's biggest hit; critics describe satirical/popular singalong gestures throughout. This is stronger than usual canzone storytelling.
- **Experimental 2 / polish 3:** satirical constructions/wordplay and the record's larger professional arrangement do not constitute avant-garde studio technique or high-gloss synthpop.
- **Voice `raspy`:** Sony Legacy directly describes the rough voice on this record's title track; no attempt to infer a duet lead voice from De Gregori's single-song backing role.
- **Instruments:** Vinyl Story's printed-album personnel gives `guitar|bass|drums|keyboards|saxophone|flute|accordion|piano`. Rino's own guitar/piano are directly named. More precise sax type cannot be established for every track; generic is appropriate.
- **Production:** `choirvocals` supported by the credited Schola Cantorum and Baba Yaga choruses. `stringarrangements` deliberately **excluded**: Hit Parade Italia repeats a 1977 U.M.R. strings credit for the 1978 LP, but Vinyl Story's independent 1978 credit transcription omits it. Disagreement is not evidence for a named model or sound effect. No digital processing assumed.
- **Producers:** Sony/Qobuz credits Rino Gaetano, Foffo/Rodolfo Bianchi and Ruggero Cini on all tracks (three separately listed Producer roles, not just arrangers).
- **Lyrics:** political satire and real-person names, corruption, speculative construction, migration, women/love, personal sadness. `social|political|irony|storytelling` have explicit critics' evidence; minority themes follow.
- **Sounds_like and influenced_by blank:** the wordplay `Nuntereggae più` and the title track's reggae style do not create an influence link to a reggae performer; a guest De Gregori contribution to *Fabbricando case* is not an influence.
- **Landmark 1:** Treccani independently calls this album the definitive public breakthrough/consolidation of Gaetano’s style; Rolling Stone says it is where his sound and audience decisively coalesce. The label's value judgments are **not** used for landmark.

## Cross-album source disputes and safeguards

1. **No duplicate pre-existing rows:** the path `docs/descriptors/rinogaetano.csv` returned 404 in the branch when checked. Recheck before Claude imports each new row to avoid a race with parallel changes.
2. **No studio albums released under early alias:** the single *I Love You Maryanna / Jaqueline* (1973) belongs to the pre-career pseudonym Kammamuri's, so excluded. *Ma il cielo è sempre più blu* (1975) is a non-album single in a discography with studio LPs, so no standalone single descriptor under current owner rule.
3. **No live/compilations:** the 1981 *Q Concert* is a jointly billed live release, excluded; the 1990 onward anthology/reissue LPs excluded.
4. **Production credits:** avoid equating arranger, mixer, recording engineer, or record executive with Producer. Exact provenance recorded above.
5. **Genres:** track-specific `folkblues` (1974), `rnb` (1977), and `reggae` (1978) appear low; one-hit tracks never drive the primary three unless lead single/biggest hit.
6. **Reissues:** do not use 2024/2026 remaster tools as original 1974–78 techniques; 2026 store listing labels are genre metadata for original album music, not personnel.
