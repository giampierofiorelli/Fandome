# The Notorious B.I.G. — Fandome phase 01 research and value rationale

**Research status:** 29 October 2026. URLs map to the source IDs in `biggie_phase01_sources.csv`. Three 27-column **draft** rows, no existing descriptor duplicated. The record label / artist credits should win over later biographies when sources conflict.

## `biggie-readytodie` — Ready to Die (1994)

- **Discography and album ID:** `biggie` already exists in `catalog.ts`; the 1994 record is listed, with no matching descriptor file. Original album, not the later remastered bonus-track editions. AllMusic, the 1994 MusicBrainz release and XXL confirm date and scope. The often-reissued **Who Shot Ya?** is not in the original 17-track album: do not add its sound or producer to the album-level credit list.
- **Genres:** MusicBrainz album-wide tags name hip hop, East Coast hip hop, gangsta rap, hardcore hip hop and boom bap; AllMusic identifies East Coast, gangsta and hardcore rap. Fandome needs **new** `gangstarap` token; `hardcorehiphop` and `boombap` already exist. `gfunk` is placed *last* for an identified crossover aspect (not a full G-funk album): AOTY lists G-Funk as secondary and Pitchfork hears the West Coast-style synth on **Big Poppa**. If a stricter validator demands whole-album genre proof, preserve as track-level and keep it at the tail.
- **Scales (0–5):** energy 4 (assertive beats, high-intensity story tracks with softer singles); electronic 3 (SP-1200 and samples mixed with organic soul/R&B samples); mood 1 (fatalism, paranoia, grief, suicidal thoughts outweigh the playful hits); theatrical 4 (role-playing on Gimme the Loot, interludes); experimental 2 (classic song structures but complex characters and sample manipulation); polish 3 (split 1993 raw sessions and 1994 Bad Boy commercial sheen); tempo 3 (largely mid-tempo, punctuated by more urgent cuts); groove 4 (beat-driven hooks, Big Poppa, Juicy). Rationale: AllMusic review, Pitchfork retrospective and XXL session interviews; these are album-level judgments, not measured BPM.
- **Voice:** Biggie's commanding lead *rap* and performed/acted spoken dialogue. `powerful` qualifies forceful projection, not a claim of singing timbre. Interludes support spoken.
- **Instruments:** sampler and drum machine specifically supported by Easy Mo Bee's first-hand E-mu SP-1200 account for **Machine Gun Funk** (MusicTech); turntable scratch in **Unbelievable** directly described by DJ Premier in XXL; sampled piano figure on **One More Chance** confirmed by engineer Prince Charles Alexander in XXL; piano/bass feel on **Things Done Changed** described by RapReviews; synthesized lead/bass timbre on **Big Poppa** noted by Pitchfork. These are audible/electronic sound sources, **not** a claim that every named sampled instrument was performed live for the album.
- **Production:** `sampling|drumprogramming|sp1200` supported by XXL, MusicTech. `sp1200` is associated with Easy Mo Bee's part, not every other producer's sessions. Do not infer a universal drum-machine model from style; no alleged analog tape / model without a session document. No `remastering` as the 1994 recording is the source.
- **Producers:** original recordings documented by MusicBrainz + XXL: Easy Mo Bee, Sean "Puffy" Combs, Chucky Thompson, production duo the Bluez Brothers (Norman & Digga), Jean "Poke" Oliver, Darnell Scott, DJ Premier and Lord Finesse. Keep producers/coproductors only, excluding writer-only sampled artists and executive-only titles. Some later bonus-track editions carry other producer credits; these were not added.
- **Lyrics:** stories of borough violence and crime, economic and social distress, criminal identity, aspiration and fame, sex/parties, despair/suicide. Do not encode themes not present in these songs.
- **Similarity:** editorial comparisons to Nas *Illmatic* (New York street narratives, boom-bap) and Wu-Tang Clan *Enter the Wu-Tang* (hard, dark East Coast sound). This is **not** artist testimony or `influenced_by`.
- **Influence:** Producer Easy Mo Bee recounts Biggie explicitly said he studied Big Daddy Kane. But an actual March 1995 Los Angeles Times interview quotes Biggie saying he had no rap influences. Preserve both in artist-level influences with this apparent conflict. Because no original statement directly pins an artist to the compositional approach of **this particular album**, `influenced_by` is blank.

## `biggie-lifeafterdeath` — Life After Death (1997)

- **Discography:** studio-recorded during Biggie's lifetime, commercially released **25 March 1997** (16 days after his death). Unlike 1999 Born Again, this is not reconstructed from a later patchwork of outtakes. Original **24-track double album**, no reissues or later alternate takes.
- **Genres:** hiphop, eastcoasthiphop, gangstarap and poprap have AllMusic/credits support for the release as a whole; hardcorehiphop, mafiosorap and boombap from album-specific reviews, with `mafiosorap` appropriately a secondary storytelling mode, not the whole album. `rnb` is subordinate for several R&B crossover duet tracks; do **not** confuse the samples/guest R&B vocalists with a full R&B album. New tokens: `gangstarap`, `mafiosorap`.
- **Scales:** energy 4 (hardcore tracks, club singles); electronic 3 (heavy production/sampling plus organic keys/arrangements); mood 2 (grim narratives and feuds, but also glamorous and bright singles); theatrical 4 (film-like narratives, interludes, shifting characters); experimental 3 (two discs, wide producer/genre palette); polish 4 (carefully finished Bad Boy hit production); tempo 3 (mostly mid-tempo and swinging); groove 4 (R&B crossover and hip-hop beats). Sources: AllMusic, 1997 RapReviews, 2017 Pitchfork retrospective.
- **Instruments:** sample-based foundation in both discs (MusicBrainz), organ actually played by producer Carlos "July Six" Broady on **My Downfall**, keyboards in credits/arrangements and sampled samples from soul/disco, strings *arranged* by Stevie J on **Somebody's Gotta Die**. An arranger credit is evidence of strings as a production element but NOT on its own proof of a live orchestra session. Bass heard in the sampled/disco arrangements; drum-machine construction is indicated by programmed rap production, but without a specific machine credited. `turntables` deliberately omitted from this LP because DJ Premier's presence alone is not proof of cuts on the record.
- **Production:** sampling, drum programming and overdubbed vocals / background performances from numerous guest rappers and singers. Exact sampler models are not documented album-wide. Documentary recording and producer roles from MusicBrainz. Interludes list Biggie *co-producer*, so he appears in producer field, not just as singer.
- **Producers:** Track credits list Puff, Stevie J, Nashiem Myrick, Carlos Broady, D-Dot, Amen-Ra, DJ Premier, Easy Mo Bee, Buckwild, RZA, Clark Kent, Havoc, Kay Gee, Daron Jones, DJ Enuff, Chucky Thompson and Biggie. **Do not insert 2Pac, Bone Thugs, Jay-Z, Lil' Kim or any sampled artists as producers**. A few less prominent credits, including Jiv Pos/Paragon, need independent original sleeve confirmation before importing as credited album producers; no invention.
- **Lyrics:** stories and braggadocio, vulnerability/grief, money, romantic relationships, social conditions, revenge, party and fame. These are not coded as 'political' without clear textual basis.
- **Similarity:** AllMusic's Jason Birchmeier compares the ambitious double-album form to **2Pac's All Eyez on Me**; Nas's *It Was Written* is an editorial similarity in polished New York crime narratives. A critic's analogy is not documentation of artistic influence; `influenced_by` blank.

## `biggie-thekingi` — The King & I (2017), Faith Evans & The Notorious B.I.G.

- **What this is:** officially **co-billed** album (Rhino, Faith Evans discography, Qobuz). Faith Evans recorded new lead and supporting vocals; Biggie's parts are archival, some previously released and some less heard. The artists did **not** meet in a 2017 studio. **Keep Fandome metadata from presenting this as Biggie's third lifetime studio LP**. `type=studio` in the limited Fandome schema describes the studio-constructed duet album; it is a collaboration, not a compilation or greatest-hits issue.
- **Add:** `biggie-thekingi` to artist's catalogue and `CO_BILLED` entry with `credit: 'Faith Evans & The Notorious B.I.G.'`. Faith Evans has no `faith` artist object in the consulted catalogue; only add an artist link when that room exists. Do not give this row the ID of a Faith Evans solo album.
- **Genre ordering:** R&B / contemporary R&B forefront (Faith vocals); hip-hop, eastcoasthiphop, gangstarap, hardcorehiphop anchored in the archived Biggie verses (AllMusic/Rhino). `boombap` and `newjackswing` are song-level Salaam Remi throwback textures named by Pitchfork and intentionally last, **not full album genres**.
- **Scales:** energy 3 (often mid-tempo R&B with a few forceful rap tracks); electronic 3 (programming, hip-hop sampling and studio instruments); mood 2 (bereavement and memory with playful and sensual passages); theatrical 3 (programmatic narrative / family interludes); experimental 2 (conventional studio-assembled duet structure); polish 4 (2017 glossy studio productions, though 1990s vocal fidelities do not always match); tempo 3; groove 3 (varies by track). Critics disagree about cohesion; preserve that in interpretation rather than invent consensus.
- **Voice:** Faith's `clear|soft` sung lead and Biggie's `rap|powerful`; Voletta Wallace's recorded speaking and various spoken transitions provide `spoken`. This row should not describe Biggie as a 2017 participant in new sessions.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz original industry credit metadata names Salaam Remi on bass, drums and keys, James Poyser keyboards, Zach Brunson piano, Rodney Jones Jr. bass guitar and DJ Premier scratches. Sampler reflects archival rap vocals assembled with new beats (Rhino + critic descriptions). Credit is track-specific: album-wide tokens aggregate substantial documented audible sources.
- **Production:** vocals from separate decades combined via recording/overdubbing and sampling (Rhino/Faith/Pitchfork), modern programming and drum programming (Qobuz), layered lead and backing vocals (Qobuz). Don't claim a 1990s machine was used for 2017 assemblies.
- **Producers:** Faith Evans, Stevie J, Salaam Remi, James Poyser, DJ Premier, Just Blaze, Chucky Thompson, Sean Combs, JD Sheard II, Jeffery J-Dub Walker, Achaia Dixon (co-producer) and DJ Battlecat from Rhino+Qobuz original track records. **Producer field consists of credited producer/co-producer only**, not Faith's featured vocalist credits, guest rap collaborators, mixing engineers or executive-only roles. Some tracks have additional specialist producers; verify against full Qobuz album metadata before claiming exhaustive coverage.
- **Lyrics / comparison:** Faith's love, remembrance and grief with Biggie's remixed old narratives and romance. Sonically resembles 1990s/2010s R&B-rap collaborations; *Faithfully* represents Faith's older solo recordings editorially. No artist-level retrospective interviews can supply new **Biggie** album-level influences in 2017, so `influenced_by` remains blank.
- **Critical disagreement:** The Guardian 18 May sees passionate tribute; the Observer 21 May calls it an artificial archival compilation; Pitchfork describes a technically unconvincing blend of old and new takes. The label calls it a duets album. Our classification **studio, collaborative but posthumous** is a schema compromise; flag for the human importer. If owner decides all archival-constructed projects are excluded, **drop this single third row**, never relabel it as an original Biggie LP.

## Excluded/deferred works

- **Born Again** (1999): Pitchfork documents previously recorded freestyles, outtakes and demos reassembled with new producers and guest voices. Strict exclusion of posthumous compilation-type projects, regardless of AllMusic displaying it as a third album or some retailers shelving it with studio albums.
- **Duets: The Final Chapter** (2005): old vocals coupled to newly recorded guests; Apple Music explicitly says posthumous assemblage. Excluded as an archival remix/compilation rather than a co-recorded landmark.
- **Conspiracy** (1995): a Junior M.A.F.I.A.-billed group album; Biggie is involved, but it is *not* a Biggie-billed studio or co-billed duo album. Catalog as Junior M.A.F.I.A. if its own artist research is commissioned.
- **Historical metadata:** Do not silently date *Life After Death* by its recording sessions. Its 1997 release is posthumous (though completed in life). Do not promote alternate versions/bonus-track additions into the original records.
