# Miley Cyrus — Phase 01: evidence notes and interpretative decisions

**Method.** Coverage begins with *Bangerz*; all 4 rows use original releases and only the `miley` artist. Source file records credits/reviews (links below and in `miley_phase01_sources.csv`). All 8 scales are original comparative album-level judgments anchored to the Fandome guide; evidence for a sound is not a numerical score in a source. Album-level genres ranked by salience, with particular-track types at the end, as itemized separately in genre evidence CSV. Avoid treating *sounds_like* as a claim of directly documented influence.

## Bangerz (2013), `miley-bangerz`

**Sound and genres.** The album's defining combination is aggressive, low-register Southern hip-hop/pop-rap production and mass-market pop melodies, interspersed with polished emotional ballads. Contemporary critics use dance-pop, pop-rap, R&B and Southern rap; the Guardian describes sub-bass and dance/trap patterns, while Slant isolates individual styles: pop-funk in “#GETITRIGHT”, dubstep impact on “FU”, Euro-house/Auto-Tune on “SMS (Bangerz)”, and a country/rap hybrid on “4x4”. `trap` therefore does not mean all 13 songs are trap. Physical-release style metadata also says `synthpop`, lower-ranked because it is not an all-track description. Review disagreement: some critics found the album credible genre-crossing, others regarded it as trend-chasing and tonally inconsistent. See [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/bangerz-mw0002577863), [Guardian (Caroline Sullivan)](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2013/oct/03/miley-cyrus-bangerz-review), [Guardian (Kitty Empire)](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2013/oct/04/miley-cyrus-bangerz-review), [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/miley-cyrus-bangerz/), [Chartsurfer](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/miley-cyrus/bangerz-album_fhpggf.html).

**Eight scales:** energy **4** (hard, insistent rhythm tracks offset by ballads); electronic **4** (programmed bass/drums/synths dominate its club side, but orchestral strings and less programmed material feature); mood **3** (defiant party songs vs acute breakup sorrow); theatrical **4** (oversized hooks and belting); experimental **2** (genre changes/quirks without largely abandoning mainstream pop songcraft); polish **5** (big-budget multiteam studio sound); tempo **3** (dance and mid-tempo cuts with slow power ballads); groove **4** (several driving rap/pop tracks, not nonstop dance).

**Instruments:** `drummachine` represents explicitly reviewed heavy drum machines; `synth` is supported by synth-pop style descriptors and reviews, not *deduced just* from hip-hop. `strings|violin|viola|cello` are real credited session parts on “Wrecking Ball”: [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9776c084-d1ff-4857-8c4f-0e33e80ac4e4). `piano` appears in the original “Wrecking Ball” physical-album personnel transcription; `guitar` is mentioned in pop-funk/guitar review passages. Instrument order reflects recurring audible sounds first, with track-specific acoustic components later. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2013/oct/03/miley-cyrus-bangerz-review), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9776c084-d1ff-4857-8c4f-0e33e80ac4e4).

**Production:** `programming` is credited to Cirkut/Dr. Luke on “Wrecking Ball”; orchestral personnel include David Campbell as strings arranger, supporting `stringarrangements`. “SMS” uses a Salt-N-Pepa sample (`sampling`), discussed in period coverage. `autotune` is explicitly noted for vocal texture by period critics (especially track treatment), and `belting` is noted on vocal performances such as “FU”. No signature drum machine brand/model was confidently supported so no 808/909 guesses. Sources: [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9776c084-d1ff-4857-8c4f-0e33e80ac4e4), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/entertainment/music/miley-cyrus-barely-makes-a-noise-on-bangerz/2013/10/03/0b7723c4-2adb-11e3-8ade-a1f23cda135e_story.html), [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/miley-cyrus-bangerz/).

**Voice/themes/producers:** documented raspy/powerful vocals and occasional rap. Repeated subjects are breakup, romance, partying, sexuality and personal transformation. Named producers and co-producers are transcribed from **original/deluxe track metadata**, but personnel tied **only** to deluxe cuts were not added. ChartSurfer's producer/co-producer designations distinguish `P-Nasty`, `Marz`, `will.i.am`, etc. from guest rappers and vocal-only production; no songwriting credit alone included. [Chartsurfer full track roles](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/miley-cyrus/bangerz-album_fhpggf.html). `sounds_like` is comparison, not evidence that these artists taught Cyrus the material. Album landmark **1** for the reinvention era.

## Miley Cyrus & Her Dead Petz (2015), `miley-mileycyrusherdeadpetz`

**Sound and genres.** Original *23-song* free album: psychedelic-pop/neo-psych and unusual experimental rock, skewed-pop song structures, synth-pop, and sporadic Southern hip-hop. AllMusic lists experimental rock and left-field pop; the Guardian review describes psychedelic pop and synthesizer-oriented passages, while Pitchfork describes wildly varied, sometimes extended textures. `boombap` is isolated to “Dooo It!”; `spacepop` is an **existing Fandome genre token** included at texture scope because AllMusic explicitly uses space pop; lower-ranked `alternativernb` and `dreampop` are softer descriptions of some tracks, weaker support than main psychedelic directions. Views diverge: Pitchfork critically calls it scattered and indulgent, while other reviewers celebrate its unrestrained exploration. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-mw0002877528), [Guardian first listen](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/aug/31/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-first-listen-review), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21023-miley-cyrus-and-her-dead-petz/), [TIME](https://time.com/4016820/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-review/).

**Eight scales:** energy **2** (long, slow/dreamy passages and intermittent harder beats); electronic **3** (many synth/keyboards, alongside guitars/piano/tactile percussion); mood **2** (mourning, confusion, relationships and druggy euphoria; emotionally mixed but introspective); theatrical **2** (casual/raw intimacy dominates, with occasional odd spectacle); experimental **5** (unconventional collage, sound explorations, extended pieces); polish **2** (looser DIY production, recorded and offered outside major-label supervision, though not invariably lo-fi); tempo **2**; groove **2** (only selected club/rap songs).

**Instruments:** contemporary critics explicitly name `synth|keyboards`, fuzzy `guitar`, occasional `acousticguitar`, piano ballads. Secondary artist-personnel reproduction lists Miley on `piano`, `keyboards` and *Tibetan bowls*, represented as new `tibetanbowl` token. This last claim is of **lower evidentiary strength** and must be reviewed rather than silently asserted as verified liner original. Sources [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/aug/31/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-first-listen-review), [TIME](https://time.com/4016820/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-review/), [reproduced artist-site credits](https://newikis.com/en/Miley_Cyrus_%26_Her_Dead_Petz).

**Production:** `programming` explicitly credited to Oren Yoel (listed also as Oren Kleinman on some credit databases); `distortedvocals` explicitly described by period reviewers for select songs. No exact synthesizer make/model is documented: none invented. Source [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-mw0002877528) and [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/aug/31/miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz-first-listen-review). Production personnel from both AllMusic and [Dork](https://readdork.com/album/miley-cyrus-miley-cyrus-her-dead-petz) include main **co-producers**; consolidated name Oren Yoel. AllMusic labels **Dave Fridmann and Sarah Barthel as additional-production only**, despite some reproductions calling them full producers: excluded per contract. `A+`, Dennis Coyne, Pluss and Josh Knapp retained where properly attributed. Since the Flaming Lips **worked on** this album, they are producer and sonic analogue, **not automatically a documented pre-existing album influence**.

**Voice/themes:** soft, raw raspy singing, passages of spoken monologue and occasional rap; melancholy/intimacy and reflective grief interweave with sex/drugs, mysticism and environmental concerns. Landmark **1** is a stylistic milestone, *not* a value judgment on review quality. Confidence **medium** due to variable/partially conflicting track-level personnel and some speculative fringe genre vocabulary; reviewers strongly support the core descriptor.

## Younger Now (2017), `miley-youngernow`

**Sound and genres.** Country-pop and pop-rock singer-songwriter reset away from the heavier 2013/2015 pop/psychedelic production. Explicit reviewers describe Nashville-country influences, guitar-based softer pop and intermittent R&B/disco on songs such as “Thinkin’.” The record remains pop and does **not** sound country on every cut. Dolly Parton sings on “Rainbowland” (feature, not evidence of an influence tag). See [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-younger-now/), [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/oct/01/miley-cyrus-younger-now-review), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/younger-now-mw0003095582).

**Eight scales:** energy **2** (relaxed mid-tempo and ballad prevalence); electronic **2** (band-style and session acoustic instruments, with some programming); mood **4** (warm romantic sunlight and reconnection despite regret on other songs); theatrical **2** (understated country-pop performance); experimental **1** (mostly familiar conventional pop and country forms); polish **4** (carefully assembled studio arrangement); tempo **2**; groove **2** (dance-suggestive “Thinkin’” exception).

**Instruments:** named by [AllMusic official credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/younger-now-mw0003095582): guitar (Jamie Arentzen/Jaco Caraco), pedal steel (Paul Franklin, standardized `steelguitar`), bass (Nicole Row), drum (Stacy Jones), violin (Paul Dateh), cello (Jerry Johnson/Matt Walker), trumpet and trombone. `strings` and `brass` represent the credited ensembles, with detailed parts listed separately. [Apple Music She's Not Him](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1462327863) verifies Oren's programming and cello, violin, horn performing credits. `programming|hornarrangements|stringarrangements` are credited to Oren Yoel in album/track personnel; actual makes/models unknown. [Apple track credits](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1462327863), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/younger-now-mw0003095582).

**Producer conflict:** [Dork metadata](https://readdork.com/album/miley-cyrus-younger-now) adds Stacy Jones as “producer”, but [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/younger-now-mw0003095582) credits him drummer/musical director; [MusicBrainz recording credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/89306cc1-29ac-4a62-9f4b-81dad9b8045b) and [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/younger-now-miley-cyrus/0886446685601) name Miley and Oren as producers. Stacy left out pending direct album-specific producer credit from primary material. Neither playing on nor writing for the album makes Dolly Parton or Stacy an album musical influence. Themes love, aging/change/identity and environmental care on “Inspired”. Landmark **0**; well-documented material and personnel warrant high confidence, not necessarily positive review consensus.

## She Is Coming (2019), `miley-sheiscoming`

**Release scope and genres.** The **original six songs** of 31 May 2019 comprise the EP; planned successor parts *She Is Here/She Is Everything* were announced but never independently released. [Sony Music's original announcement](https://www.sonymusic.ca/press_release/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-out-now). Contemporary [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/may/31/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-ep-review) calls it rock- and trap-inflected pop; [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-review-2504286) details hip-hop-influenced swagger and R&B. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-ep/) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/she-is-coming-mw0003286268) identify tonal range, the gentler closer and party music. `country` limited to “The Most”; electropop and dance-pop concentrated on energetic tracks.

**Eight scales:** energy **4** (dominant brash, heavy party tracks, softer last song); electronic **4** (predominantly synthetic pop/trap textures, with audible real instruments); mood **3** (self-celebration vs anxiety and romantic regret); theatrical **4** (assertive performance and vivid characters); experimental **2** (hip-hop/pop collage with familiar structures); polish **4** (finished radio-grade recording); tempo **3**; groove **4** (majority beat-led).

**Instruments:** original-track licensed credits [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/se-en/album/she-is-coming-miley-cyrus/qazgf6zxuj41a) document guitars/keys (John Cunningham, Ilsey Juber), keyboards (Buddy Ross, Jasper Sheff, Francis Starlite), synthesizer and acoustic guitar (Mark Ronson on “The Most”), violin (Marc Szammer and Christina Liberis), viola (Mario Gotoh), cello (Justin Kantor) and string contribution (Andrew Wyatt) on “Party Up the Street”. `strings` thus appears in a limited track, not across entire EP. `programming` from BJ Burton and others in credits; `sampling` has been **withheld**: “D.R.E.A.M.” reworks/references Wu-Tang “C.R.E.A.M.”, but the reviewed sources do not establish that an audio sample of the master was used rather than an interpolation. A verbal reference is not sufficient to tag sampled production. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-ep/); [Apple editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/she-is-coming/1689672048).

**Credited producers:** Qobuz specifies Andrew Wyatt, John Cunningham, Jasper Sheff, RZA, King Henry, Mike WiLL Made-It, Pluss, Mark Ronson and BJ Burton across six cuts. The metadata for “Slide Away” is **a reissue seventh track**; its credits are **not** included. Where Tay Keith is listed only as programmer and Buddy Ross only as additional production, exclude them as producers even if a secondary summary flattens their job titles. [Qobuz licensed credits](https://www.qobuz.com/se-en/album/she-is-coming-miley-cyrus/qazgf6zxuj41a), [original Sony tracklist](https://www.sonymusic.ca/press_release/miley-cyrus-she-is-coming-out-now). Themes sexual agency, hedonism and personal tension; the subdued “The Most” examines guilt and love. Landmark **0**, EP relevant as the pivot between country reinvention and later rock-oriented albums.
