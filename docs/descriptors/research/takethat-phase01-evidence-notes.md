# Take That — phase 01 evidence notes

These are evidence-based **editorial descriptors** for the original releases (not sampled audio analysis); URLs supporting the discussion occur in `sources.csv`. Broad AllMusic styles are prioritised over narrow user tags; reviewer analogies are not treated as proven artist influences.

## `takethat-takethatparty` — Take That & Party (1992)

- **Genre hierarchy:** AllMusic classifies album `dancepop|europop|teenpop|pop`. Apple's album editorial emphasises dance-pop singles and the piano-led 'A Million Love Songs'; the contemporaneous dance genre applies. Album of the Year's secondary user tags add house, contemporary R&B, sophisti-pop, garage house and Italo house. Those are lower-confidence track-level colours: do not treat them as strongly corroborated album-wide subgenres. The classic disco arrangement underpins the 'Could It Be Magic' cover but not the full album. Critic genre labels are broader than community genre labels; all evidence links supplied separately.
- **Scales:** Energy 4, tempo 4, groove 5 track brisk club cuts and the debut-era dance singles; mood 4 recognises celebratory pop balanced by 'A Million Love Songs'; electronic 4 is a *provisional editorial assessment* of programmed club-pop versus Barlow piano, not proof of any named synth model. Theatrical 3 reflects group harmonies and vocal projection; experimental 1, polish 4 describe conventional glossy pop songwriting and dance production. All are whole-album editorial ratings, not average BPM calculations.
- **Instruments:** Gary Barlow's piano and Snake Davis saxophone for 'A Million Love Songs' are explicitly documented by the group's site. AllMusic album personnel credit Jonathan Wales keyboards and Tommy Musto drums/keyboards. **Do not assume** synthesiser models, drum machines, acoustic guitars, or sampled percussion without session-level evidence.
- **Production:** AllMusic album credits James T. Alfano with programming, so only token `programming` is included; remixer/bonus-track machinery is not attached to the original album. Production roster cross-checked against MusicBrainz original tracks; the Rapino Brothers produced and remixed the *Could It Be Magic* album/single version. Mark Beswick's additional vocal-production and Ian Curnow/Phil Harding's additional/re-mix credits are not included as primary producers. If later liner evidence alters exact version credit, amend rather than invent roles.
- **Vocals and writing:** Gary Barlow handles most romantic leads, Robbie Williams also sings lead on early up-tempo material; varied clear, strong and gentle vocal presentation. Love, desire and heartbreak dominate; 'party' covers the upbeat presentation and title track.
- **Sonic comparisons:** New Kids on the Block (concept/era), early Kylie Minogue and Jason Donovan (bright UK chart-pop); editorial `sounds_like` only, **not** documented influence. No album-specific `influenced_by` proven.
- **Release/version caveat:** 17 August 1992 UK date from chart archive; AllMusic 1993 reflects later configuration. Official site reports 'Could It Be Magic' not appearing on some cassette editions. Treat the 1992 standard album as one entity.

## `takethat-everythingchanges` — Everything Changes (1993)

- **Genre hierarchy:** AllMusic names dance-pop, Euro-pop, teen pop, pop and adult contemporary; 1993 version of 'Relight My Fire' is specifically dance-pop/house and a disco-song cover. These textures are *one component* of the album; don't call the entire album house/disco.
- **Scales:** Energy 3, electronic 3, tempo 3 and groove 3 balance the upbeat title song, 'Pray' and 'Relight My Fire' against 'Babe', 'Love Ain't Here Anymore' and other ballads. Mood 3 spans euphoria and separation, theatrical 3 includes projected multi-voice choruses; experimental 1 because standard mainstream pop arrangements dominate; polish 4 due to radio-ready, multistudio production.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz track personnel explicitly identify Cary Baylis guitar, Gary Barlow keys, Chuck Sabo drums, violins and cello on 'Love Ain't Here Anymore', John Noyce bass on 'Relight My Fire' and Keith Beauvais Rhodes piano on 'If This Is Love'. `electricpiano` maps to credited Fender Rhodes, not inferred piano. No unnamed drum-machine model.
- **Production:** MusicBrainz credits Dave James and Marcus D. Bush specifically with programming on 'If This Is Love', supporting `programming`. No `remixing` or other token inferred from the 2006 expanded bonus disc.
- **Producers:** Original MusicBrainz credits Eliot Kennedy, Mike Ward, Jervier brothers, Jonathan Wales, Livingstone, Joey Negro, Dave James, Rapino Brothers, Nigel Wright. Gary Barlow is listed as **co-producer on 'Whatever You Do to Me'** and included; source explicitly says Cary Baylis 'additional producer' and Mark Beswick 'additional producer (vocal)' so both omitted despite broad Qobuz role export. Robbie, Mark and Howard alternate lead vocals, but co-billing remains Take That (Lulu is featured on one track).
- **Critic disagreements:** Interpretations range from teen pop to adult contemporary; genre weights reflect the many love ballads as well as the era's major dance singles. The five whole-record tags are at the front.

## `takethat-nobodyelse` — Nobody Else (1995)

- **Genre hierarchy:** AllMusic lists pop, dance-pop, Euro-pop, teen pop. The Independent's account of the bid for a US R&B sound is borne out by MusicBrainz credits on 'Every Guy', 'Sunday to Saturday' and 'Sure', including Brothers in Rhythm and bass/guitar/drum players. Funk is a **track-scoped editorial** mapping of those grooves; David Morales programmes drums on 'Hanging Onto Your Love' and supplies a house-oriented track. `adultcontemporary` summarises the Barlow ballad dimension but is **not** an AllMusic-listed style for this particular album.
- **Scales:** Energy 3, electronic 3, tempo 3 and groove 3 span house/R&B cuts versus the slow, strongly melodic 'Back for Good' and title ballad. Mood 3 ranges nostalgic to upbeat; theatrical 4 reflects the choral scale of 'Never Forget'; experimental 2 acknowledges juxtaposition of R&B/dance production and overt anthem production; polish 5 is fully professional pop studio craft.
- **Instruments:** Original track credits specify Steve Anderson bass, drums, keys; guitar Greg Bone, Neil Oldfield, Phil Palmer; percussion Andy Duncan, sax Nigel Hitchcock; choir singers on 'Never Forget'. Richard Niles credited with **brass and strings arranging**. `strings` is included as arranged instrumental layer with lower direct-player detail than guitar/bass; do not infer precise trumpet, violin or cello lineups without part credits.
- **Production:** Tim Weidner additional programming and Barlow/Chris Cameron programming support `programming`; David Morales explicitly drum-programmes 'Hanging Onto Your Love' (`drumprogramming`). Richard Niles brass/string arrangement credits support both arrangement tokens; they're **not** producer credits. Gary Barlow and Chris Porter produce ballads; Gary Barlow and Brothers in Rhythm others; Dave James and Brothers in Rhythm 'Never Forget'; David Morales one dance track. `choral` voice is confined to gospel-like ensemble finale, not every song.
- **Edition conflicts:** UK release 8 May 1995 from artist's official site; the AllMusic 15 August date belongs to the North American release, which has different material. *Never Forget* quotes Verdi's Requiem; neither `classical` as a pervasive album genre nor Giuseppe Verdi as a musical influence is defensible on this basis. Earlier recordings on US tracklist are **not** reassigned to this album.

## `takethat-beautifulworld` — Beautiful World (2006)

- **Genre hierarchy:** The Guardian calls the sound polished **pop-rock**, with AOR comparison, 'folky Wooden Boat', quasi-psychedelia on 'What You Believe In' and glitzy 'Shine'. AllMusic additionally assigns contemporary pop/rock, pop, dance-pop and Euro-pop. Track-specific `folkpop` and `psychedelicpop` are **qualified editorial encodings**, placed after the album-level styles. `aor` describes an arrangement tendency noted by the critic, not a second full-album primary genre. `adultcontemporary` is an editorial adult-ballad reading.
- **Scales:** Electronic 1 follows original credits showing electric/acoustic band instruments, Barlow piano and orchestra, not synthesiser-led dance production. Energy 3, tempo 3, groove 2 represent midtempo ballads and rock-driven choruses. Mood 3 balances 'Patience' and reflective tracks with uplifting 'Shine'; theatrical 4 reflects expanding choruses and orchestra; experimental 2 acknowledges relatively unusual psych/folk songs without disguising accessible song structures; polish 5 follows Shanks/Rothschild high-gloss recording.
- **Instruments:** The original CD MusicBrainz credits John Shanks with bass, guitars, keys; Gary Barlow piano; Jeff Rothschild drums; actual London Session Orchestra on 'Patience', 'Hold On' and others. `strings` and `orchestra` both supported, distinct levels. No named synth or drum machine is established on those credited recordings. `falsetto` derives directly from contemporary Guardian critic description, not genre inference; any processing beyond that remains unspecified.
- **Production:** John Shanks is consistently credited as **sole record producer** for core album; Jeff Rothschild as recording/mix engineer and drummer, Wil Malone as string arranger, not producers. `stringarrangements` is the only technique with formal score/credit support. If a song has an additional contributor with a production role, require explicit release credit before expanding producers.
- **Original-release caveat:** 'Rule the World' was written and issued in 2007 and first appears on a later souvenir edition, **not** on the 2006 first pressing. Do not infer that its film connection makes the 2006 album a soundtrack.
- **Sonic comparisons:** Guardian explicitly suggests the mainstream pop-rock palette of Keane, Coldplay, James Blunt and glitzy Scissor Sisters (on 'Shine'); these are *editorial sonic analogies*, not artist-declared song influences. No album influence filled.
