# U2 — Phase 03: evidence notes (27 October 2026)

Exactly three new entries: *Rattle and Hum* (1988), *Achtung Baby* (1991) and *Zooropa* (1993). **Numeric scores are reasoned Fandome judgments**, not claims that the cited reviews assign these numbers. The source ledger separates original label credits, independently written reviews, retrospective accounts and expert/participant interviews. Scope in the genre ledger matters: many late tokens name the music of **one song** rather than the record as a whole. "New genre" means the token was absent from the current `scenes.ts` and validator list at branch audit, not an automatic recommendation to reclassify every song as that genre. No phase-01/02 row is included.

## `u2-rattleandhum` — *Rattle and Hum* (10 October 1988)

**ID, type, form.** Original U2 catalogue lists 16 tracks and **Jimmy Iovine** as overall record producer. The 1988 companion to Phil Joanou's concert documentary is neither a new wholly-live LP nor merely a conventional studio record: live tour performances, original studio tracks, covers and short pieces by other performers appear together. MusicBrainz marks its release group **Album + Soundtrack**, AllMusic shows **Soundtrack / Studio & Live**. Fandome's single enum choice is therefore `soundtrack`; `live` would misrepresent roughly half the record. The 1988 LP, not the 1989 five-track *Excerpts from Rattle and Hum* or later soundtrack variants, receives one row. The label and MusicBrainz dates agree on **10 October 1988**; AllMusic sometimes dates a later release/film, which is not grounds to change the original year. [U2 original](https://www.u2.com/products/rattle-and-hum) · [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/96927375-9ebc-4592-9507-b47c0274a56c).

**Genre evidence.** Album of the Year explicitly names `poprock`, `bluesrock`, `heartlandrock`, `alternativerock`, `rootsrock`; AllMusic contributes its distinct categories. `rootsrock` is **new** to the audited registry and is not converted to `americana` or `countryrock`. A contemporary [Knight-Ridder review](https://www.u2songs.com/news/u2s_rattle_much_much_more_than_a_soundtrack) calls *Angel of Harlem* Memphis soul, identifies Irish/American folk, and names blues and gospel songs; `memphissoul` is therefore a new **track-specific** genre, while `soul|folk|blues|gospel` are deliberately last in ranking. `heartlandrock` was proposed in phase 02 and may still need adding if it has not been imported. Billboard-era arena rock appears on concert tracks; it does not make the whole LP genre-pure. Original 1988 criticism is divided: Anthony DeCurtis praised its exuberant inclusiveness while Stephen Thomas Erlewine found several live portions pompous and the album inconsistent. The contradiction justifies mixed `mood` and moderate `polish`, not omission.

**Scale values**: energy **4**, electronic **1**, mood **3**, theatrical **4**, experimental **2**, polish **3**, tempo **3**, groove **2**. Stage material such as *Bullet the Blue Sky* is fierce and declarative; *Love Rescue Me*, *Heartland* and the gospel-choir performance are much more restrained. That spread places intensity below a relentless-metal 5 and mood near mixed 3. Very little keyboard/electronic signal is the organizing timbre: electronic 1. Large concert address and gospel finale warrant theatrical 4, whereas formal experiment is mostly in sequencing live/studio/interstitial tapes rather than novel composition (2). Recorded/mixed studio highlights and remote concert audio yield polish 3. The Bo Diddley-influenced *Desire* has a strong shuffle but one danceable track does not turn this into a club record.

**Instruments, strictly source-based.** Core U2 instruments are present in the original live tracks. *Angel of Harlem* credits [the Memphis Horns, Joey Miskulin organ](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/in-the-locust-wind); the identifiable Memphis Horns featured saxophone and trumpet in the recording/film credits, so `brass|saxophone|trumpet` are retained behind the core instruments. The original [music issue credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/u2/rattle-and-hum-album_frevgn.html) separately document Bob Dylan's Hammond organ on *Hawkmoon 269* and Benmont Tench's keyboard on *All I Want Is You*, while that closing song has strings arranged by **Van Dyke Parks**. No other instruments are inferred solely from genre. `harmonica` reflects the actual *Freedom for My People* street-performance feature by Adam Gussow, and `timpani` a *Hawkmoon 269* credit. Gospel singing (New Voices of Freedom) is tagged in **voice** as `choral`, not an instrument. Sound fragments by Jimi Hendrix do not entitle him to `influenced_by`, and Hammond is entered as the approved `organ`, not a fictional `hammond` instrument.

**Production.** `concertrecording` is literal tour recording on multiple dates; `overdubbing` is explicit in the original sleeve. `fieldrecordings` is the found-street performance *Freedom for My People* included as music rather than a studio take; it must **not** be mistaken for a sample made by the U2 band. `stringarrangements` follows Van Dyke Parks' credit. Horn performances alone do **not** prove named horn-arrangement credits, so `hornarrangements` is not inferred. Neither a mixing console model nor a specific reverb unit was established for the issued album. The album-level credited producer is **Iovine**, not its engineers David Tickle, Thom Panunzio, David Hewitt or arranger Parks. Some crowdsourced later reissues distribute per-track producer metadata differently; original label-level album credit controls this row.

**Lyrics, voice, resemblance and influence.** Politics and social conscience remain audible on *Bullet the Blue Sky*; spiritual striving becomes literal gospel singing on *I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For*. The new material also concerns desire, love, memory and ambivalent hero worship. The lead is mostly Bono's clear, forceful rock voice plus the choir on the one documented concert piece. No album-wide musician is placed in `influenced_by`: Bob Dylan, B.B. King and other guest performers are collaborators here; Hendrix and the Beatles are performed or quoted, not proof of independent artistic influence. U2 explicitly pursued American roots traditions, but the text available does not assign a particular *noncollaborating* artist as an influence on this specific album. `sounds_like` remains intentionally empty rather than invent a comparison. `landmark=1` recognises the full-length original album/film project as an important, divisive milestone.

## `u2-achtungbaby` — *Achtung Baby* (18 November 1991)

**Release and credits.** This is the already-catalogued original studio album, not the 2011 anniversary issue. Crucially, the [original label overview](https://www.u2.com/products/achtung-baby) compresses the production heading to **Daniel Lanois with Brian Eno** and identifies Steve Lillywhite as mixer on selected songs. The [1991 song-by-song sleeve](https://albumlinernotes.com/Achtung_Baby.html) **also credits Steve Lillywhite as producer/co-producer on identified tracks**, especially *Who's Gonna Ride Your Wild Horses* and *Even Better Than the Real Thing*. The latter role is documented in [MusicBrainz track credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0f20dea8-bf8a-3d93-bfa2-56d543209641), so `producers=Daniel Lanois|Brian Eno|Steve Lillywhite` is the complete **album-wide distinct-producers list**, not a claim of equal involvement on each of 12 songs. Flood and Robbie Adams are engineers/mixers, not recorded as producers of this original 1991 album. The inclusion of Lillywhite corrects a misleading simplification if one consults only the original overview.

**Genres.** The [AOTY album index](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/4432-u2-achtung-baby.php) gives `alternativerock|poprock|baggy|alternativedance`. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/achtung-baby-mw0000202435) explicitly adds `albumrock|alternativepoprock|alternativeindierock|contemporarypoprock|dancerock|collegerock`. Contemporary and retrospective informed criticism hears **industrial** and **shoegaze** textures, dance grooves, guitar **glamrock**, occasional **funk** and **heavymetal** effects. These narrower styles come after the main album labels as *texture* or *some tracks*. The [Music Waves](https://www.musicwaves.org/frmReview.aspx?ID=3963) 'electro' and 'experimental' tags are kept last and tagged accordingly, rather than misrepresented as dominant whole-record styles. No new genre token is required here.

**Scale values**: energy **4**, electronic **3**, mood **1**, theatrical **3**, experimental **4**, polish **4**, tempo **3**, groove **3**. The engineer's detailed history, critical discussions of distortion and processed sound, and the deliberate turn to club rhythms justify electronic 3 and experimental 4 against conventional U2 1980s guitar rock. Melodic love songs and forceful drumming remain, so it is not a fully electronic 5. Multiple lyrics explicitly explore betrayal, damaged love, vulnerability and self-doubt, placing mood on the dark side (1) even when the basslines move. Huge choruses coexist with fragile arrangements, giving theatrical 3 and energy 4; careful, prolonged multitrack sessions produce polish 4 even though the intentionally ragged sound is *not* sleek. Speed and dancing vary significantly from *One* through *Mysterious Ways*, giving tempo 3, groove 3. Every number describes the **album aggregate**, not Zoo TV's later theatre presentation.

**Instruments.** The original sleeve names The Edge's **guitar and keyboards**, Bono's **guitar**, Adam Clayton's **bass**, Larry Mullen's **drums/percussion**. The original track credit for *So Cruel* adds [Nell Catchpole violin and viola](https://albumlinernotes.com/Achtung_Baby.html) and Eno/Edge's string arrangement; `violin|viola` reflect this narrower track, not an album-wide string orchestra. [Apple Music song personnel](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1443063592) credits piano to Edge on *So Cruel*, supporting `piano`. Do not invent a particular vintage synthesizer, a Fairlight or a drum machine from the genre or Flood's other productions; keyboard playing is established but specific models are not.

**Production with original engineer support.** [Robbie Adams to Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/robbie-adams-recording-u2s-achtung-baby-zooropa) describes an **Otari MTR100 analog 24-track**, Neve and Soundcraft consoles, active overdubbing and deliberate room-bleed. The sleeve credits **digital editing** by Stewart Whitmore. `analogtape|digitalediting|overdubbing|roomambience` are therefore warranted together, with analogue recording **not** contradicting later digital edit stages. Critics and track guitar descriptions corroborate `distortion|feedback|wahwah`; `stringarrangements` is literal, and `sonicmanipulation` describes the unorthodox processed and rearranged sound substantiated by the engineer. Avoid claiming `sampling` as the same as loop-based *Zooropa*: the latter is documented, but no distinct replayed sample gear is established for this LP in cited sources. Some sonic devices (e.g. specific delay pedals) are often associated with The Edge, but none was independently established for the released **1991** master, so no named model token.

**Album-specific influences.** This is the exceptional row where contemporaneous qualified critics explicitly identify inspiration. [The New Yorker, February 1992](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1992/02/17/1992-02-17-075-tny-cards-000145798) names **My Bloody Valentine, Happy Mondays, The KLF and Sonic Youth** as inspirational musical points for this album. [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/music/2024/09/27/u2s-country-mile-and-picture-of-you-x-w-two-fantastic-songs-but-its-hard-not-to-be-haunted-by-what-they-represent/) explicitly connects *Achtung Baby* to **The Stone Roses**. [AllMusic artist biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/u2-mn0000219203) describes the record as shaped by late-1970s **David Bowie** and Madchester. These are cited album-specific critical attributions; they are **not** an inference from Flood having recorded Depeche Mode. The Edge's [first-person Manchester-scene account](https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/u2-edge-interview-achtung-baby-rarities-33721/) concerns *Lady With the Spinning Head*, an outtake not on the standard 12-song album, so the Charlatans are **not** automatically added here. `sounds_like` deliberately remains empty: sources explaining documented inspiration do not necessarily claim one-to-one sonic similarity.

**Lyrical and critical disagreement.** [Robert Hilburn's original LA Times review](https://news.u2.com/news/title/achtung_baby_review__robert_hilburn_in_la_times_1100/) stresses crisis of identity and relationships; intimacy and irony transform the band's earnest public sound. The band themselves and Eno frame deliberate dark, sexual, trashy and industrial aesthetics. Rock critics differed over self-conscious reinvention versus substantial artistic advance. `landmark=1`: the album underpins the new 1990s sonic direction. `confidence=high` refers to credited instruments/producers and cross-source genre agreement, not objective validity of subjective scales.

## `u2-zooropa` — *Zooropa* (5 July 1993)

**Release, type and producer conflict.** This already-catalogued album started as a tour-related EP but was issued as a new **ten-song studio album** on 5 July 1993. [Original U2 discography](https://www.u2.com/products/u2-zooropa) and [original liner transcription](https://u2songs.com/discography/u2_zooropa_album_original_release) name **Flood, Brian Eno and The Edge** as its three album producers. Robbie Adams and Rob Kirwan handled recording/mixing. Some [1993 cassette and later metadata](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/u2/zooropa-album_frcgnn.html) credit **Daniel Lanois as co-producer of 'Numb'**, whereas other original-edition metadata state the track had been *originally recorded with* Lanois and the official LP overview does not name him as producer. As the role conflicts, **do not silently promote him** into the album's producers column: Claude should resolve against physical first-pressing track notes if required. This is an explicit unresolved credit rather than an undocumented guess.

**Genre evidence.** [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/4431-u2-zooropa.php) identifies `alternativerock|artpop|baggy|alternativedance|artrock`. Original original-format [1993 catalogue styles](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/u2/zooropa-album_frcgnn.html) classify the LP `electronic|rock`, and some original cassette editions specifically tag `synthpop|poprock|experimental|abstract`. Thus **`abstract` is a new literal source token**; do not substitute ambient or avantgarde as semantic synonyms. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/zooropa-mw0000098950) provides its own broad post-punk, dance-rock and pop-rock categories; [Stephen Thomas Erlewine's review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r183482) describes *Lemon* specifically as German disco. Contemporary [Newsweek](https://www.u2.com/news/title/newsweek__zooropa_1107) calls *Numb* industrial; [Music & Media, July 1993](https://worldradiohistory.com/UK/Music-and-Media/90s/1993/MM-1993-07-10.pdf) uses electro and techno for the same single. The weakest (`industrialrock|edm`) are openly flagged as single-reviewer, track-scoped evidence, not authoritative primary whole-album genre claims. Genres are kept literal, including `synthpop` rather than assuming `electropop`.

**Scales**: energy **3**, electronic **4**, mood **2**, theatrical **2**, experimental **5**, polish **3**, tempo **3**, groove **3**. Producer [Flood's original account](https://u2conference.com/return-to-zooropa/) describes songs built from disjointed performances, DAT-sourced jams and loop recombination, with abrupt stylistic moves from *Numb* to *Stay* and the Johnny Cash-led *Wanderer*. Hence experimental 5, while synths and edited drum loops make electronic 4 without completely replacing guitars/drums. There are dance-driven tracks but also extremely understated ballads and art-pop detours: typical tempo/groove near 3, energy near 3. Alienation, tech-mediated distance and social anxiety dominate much of the lyrics, yet *Lemon* is gleeful and *Stay* gentle, so mood 2 rather than uniformly bleak 0. Less concert-style declamation means theatrical 2; Flood describes quick, exploratory, sometimes unfinished tracks compared with the meticulously revised *Achtung Baby*, so polish 3.

**Instruments.** The original sleeves list [The Edge guitar, piano and synthesizers](https://u2songs.com/discography/u2_zooropa_album_original_release), Adam's bass, Larry's drums/percussion, Bono's guitar, and Des Broadbery programming. The [issue-level credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/u2/zooropa-album_frcgnn.html) credit Brian Eno synthesizers on multiple tracks and piano/**harmonium** on *The First Time*, hence `synth|keyboards|piano|harmonium`. Sound on Sound specifically says Adams made the band's performed drum-loop material using an **Akai S1100** sampler, supporting `sampler` instrument and machine token `akais1100`. The sampler is not named simply because the genre is electronic. No source in this evidence package establishes a TR-808, 909, LinnDrum, vocoder, generic electronic drum kit or named Eno synth model in this exact album, so none is included.

**Production.** [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/robbie-adams-recording-u2s-achtung-baby-zooropa) explains Otari analogue 24-track, a synchronised Fostex DAT system, **180 two-hour DAT recordings**, an Akai S1100 sampler and Apple Macintosh/Vision sequencing of edited Larry Mullen drum performances. The named `akais1100|analogtape|datrecording|sampling|drumloop|programming|overdubbing|sonicmanipulation` therefore have direct production evidence. The issue-level credits also document digital editing by Stewart Whitmore. Flood details **vocal-take comping** to achieve a deliberately neutral Johnny Cash performance, and Eno manipulating a 1992 New Zealand soundcheck into the second half of *Zooropa*; these are production process decisions, not proof of `autotune`/`vocoder` or any specific pitch-processing machine. Contemporary Newsweek clearly hears Bono **falsetto** on *Lemon*, warranting `falsetto`, and The Edge's near-spoken vocal lead on *Numb* supports `spoken` voice alongside Bono's clearer singing and Cash's guest baritone. We do **not** categorise Cash as album influence merely for performing on it.

**Lyrical fields and caveats.** Mass media, contradictory advertising and information overload fuel `social|political|identity|irony`; *Stay*, *The First Time* and *The Wanderer* involve melancholy, faith and intimacy. All the album's main sung text is English (`en`); bits of slogans do not establish a second full album language. The album's production was self-experimentation driven by Zoo TV and Eno/Flood, not documented inspiration from a named external musician, so `influenced_by` is intentionally blank. `landmark=1` flags the unusually adventurous artistic statement rather than its commercial ranking. Be careful not to create another release row for 2018 and 2023 anniversary editions or the unmade original EP.

## Incremental boundary and handoff

The only 27-column rows delivered in this phase are **u2-rattleandhum**, **u2-achtungbaby**, **u2-zooropa**. No U2 group-wide `ARTIST INFLUENCES` repeat (that material was confined to phase 01). `sounds_like` is empty when a specific reliable audible comparison is unavailable: it is not filled with neighbouring artists simply from genre or shared studios. No shared billing changes occur in phase 03.
