# Tiromancino — phase 02: album-specific evidence notes

Scope: released originals from 2000, 2002, 2004, 2007. `draft` descriptions; all scalar ratings are editorial judgments using the Fandome 0–5 anchors. No extrapolations from later remasters or tours. Source keys below resolve to working URLs in `tiromancino_phase02_sources.csv`; one genre/source/scope row occurs in `tiromancino_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`.

## La descrizione di un attimo (2000) — `tiromancino-ladescrizionediunattimo`

**Genres:** AllMusic independently names Trip-Hop, Italian Pop, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock and Alternative/Indie Rock; this is stronger album-level evidence than inferring from one single. Contemporary Rockol identifies a hip-hop character especially around *Strade*. Radio Italia independently documents contemporary electronics and scratching. Hip-hop is **some tracks**; electronica is texture, not full electro-only instrumentation. (allmusic2000; rockol2000; radioitalia2000; officialbio)

**Scale reasoning:** energy 3/tempo 3/groove 3: mix of mid-paced lyrical songs and rhythmic electronic/hip-hop numbers, rather than one relentless club pulse; electronic 3: meaningful programmed/sampled texture over conventional melodic arrangements. Mood 2: relationship rupture, urban stress and anxiety in official lyrics, alongside warmer love songs. Theatrical 2: intimate conversational delivery, despite orchestrated moments. Experimental 3: scratches, sampling and contrasting orchestration were non-standard for early-2000s Italian pop. Polish 4: unusually deliberate recorded orchestration and studio production. (official2000; rockol2000; rockol2025; qobuz2000)

**Voice & lyrics:** `soft|clear` for the restrained lead songwriting; themes `love|heartbreak|introspection|storytelling|social` supported by *Due destini*, the title song, *Il peggio non è tranquillo* and *Roma di notte*. Primary language Italian. (official2000)

**Instruments:** `keyboards|guitar|bass` supported by AllMusic performer credits; `strings|woodwinds|brass` explicitly recorded for *Strade* and the orchestral arrangements for the title song and *Due destini*. These are prominent but not every track. Distinct woodwind/brass arrangements on *Strade* are conducted by Peppe Vessicchio. No synthesiser brand identified for this release, and no assumption that DJ scratching proves a specific turntable. (official2000; allmusic2000)

**Production & producer:** Riccardo Sinigallia is explicitly sole credited producer on band's detailed original credits (not Federico, Emiliano Di Meo, Francesco Valente or the engineer Gianluca Vaccaro). `sampling` (AllMusic), `programming` (Qobuz Sinigallia credits), `stringarrangements` and `hornarrangements` (original liner credits, horn category includes recorded brass). Frankie hi-nrg and DJ Stile are collaborators; neither is promoted to album producer. (official2000; allmusic2000; qobuz2000; rockol2000)

**Sounds like / influenced_by:** left empty. General Roman indie-circle association, tracks included in films, and producer relationships do **not** establish direct musical influence from a particular artist. Landmark 1 for breakthrough status (official biography and Rockol retrospective). Confidence high for core data; broad scales still editorial.

## In continuo movimento (2002) — `tiromancino-incontinuomovimento`

**Genres:** OndaRock explicitly classifies as pop; AllMusic lists Pop/Rock, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock and Italian Pop. Band biography specifies `electro` rhythms and psychedelic passages. Hip-hop applies to some tracks, notably *Nessuna certezza*. Rockol describes a post-trip-hop sonic quality, but this is a **texture**, not independently documented genre `posttriphop`: no new token introduced on that basis. (ondarock2002; allmusic2002; officialbio; rockol2002themes)

**Scales:** energy 3 and tempo 2: predominantly reflective/slow-to-midtempo with a handful of rhythmically dynamic numbers. Electronic 3: organic piano/guitars/strings sit beside programmed rhythms, sampling and synths. Mood 1: the reviewers explicitly identify a nocturnal, tense, relatively dark and introspective sound. Theatrical 2, experimental 3: measured songwriting and spoken passages combine with unusual arrangements. Polish 4: intricately worked, layered studio detail. Groove 2: hip-hop groove on some songs, but several ballads and instrumental passages temper album-wide danceability. (ondarock2002; rockol2002)

**Instruments:** OndaRock explicitly identifies `piano|keyboards|synth|bass|electricguitar` and electronic beats; AllMusic names Fender Rhodes (`electricpiano`), Moog synth, Prophet 5, piano and acoustic guitar; Qobuz lists Piero Monterisi on drums, strings/Quartetto d'Archi and electric guitar Stefano Palandri on *Sarebbe incredibile*. `strings` are certain on *Per me è importante*, not necessarily ubiquitous. (ondarock2002; allmusic2002; qobuz2002)

**Production & producers:** Qobuz's label-supplied per-track credits assign **Producer** to Federico Zampaglione, Andrea Pesce and Luigi Pulcinelli on multiple tracks. AllMusic omits Pulcinelli as Producer in a condensed credits view, while Qobuz does not: retain all three but note credit granularity. `drumprogramming` is direct Qobuz credit, `sampling` direct review, `moog|prophet5` actual model credits in AllMusic. No fictional DAW or mix software. (qobuz2002; allmusic2002; ondarock2002)

**Lyrics/voice:** Zampaglione's intimate vocals and Roberto Pedicini's narrated passage on *Il progresso da lontano* support `soft|spoken`. Lyrics discuss love, estrangement, introspection, social progress and identity. Italian only. (official2002; ondarock2002)

**sounds_like:** David Sylvian and Franco Battiato are specific arrangement/vocal comparisons made by OndaRock; Rockol contextualises similar acoustic/electronic singer-songwriter crossings by Beth Orton and David Gray (editorial analogies only). **influenced_by empty**: these comparisons are not direct documented inspiration for this album. Landmark 1; high confidence. (ondarock2002; rockol2002)

## Illusioni parallele (2004) — `tiromancino-illusioniparallele`

**Genres:** Rockol describes Italian songwriter-pop modernised with played/resampled electronics; `electronic` belongs to whole-album sound, `italianpop` to the Italian songwriting element. Italian discography metadata identifies `poprock|triphop|alternativerock` with weaker authority; trip-hop is not forced to dominate the ranking. The *Esplode* guest spot alone does not turn the whole album into Afterhours-style rock. (rockol2004; wiki2004; auditorium2004)

**Scales:** energy 3/tempo 3: principally mid-tempo songs with a few more forceful numbers. Electronic 3: electric/acoustic blend, with self-sampled electronic parts, never fully synthetic. Mood 2: reflective dreams, distance and everyday disillusion offset by hope. Theatrical 2: understated, narrative singing; *Esplode* changes intensity but is not the typical vocal register. Experimental 3: self-sampling and vintage synthesis foregrounded but clear melody stays central. Polish 4: finely worked production. Groove 2: electronic figures support songcraft rather than club-first dance arrangement. (rockol2004; tgcom2004; official2004)

**Instruments:** The contemporary Auditorium presentation and 2004 listener review name Moog and Rhodes (`synth|electricpiano`), plus trumpet and strings. `guitar` is part of the acoustic/electric hybrid, supported by the band's description and contemporary review; no unverifiable guitar type assigned. The venue text calls another instrument `Prophet V`, but the model is ambiguous; **do not silently remap it to `prophet5` and do not introduce `prophetv`** until the original album sleeve is inspected. (auditorium2004; debaser2004; rockol2004)

**Production/producers:** Rockol specifies that electronic bases were played by the group, then re-sampled: `sampling` is documented. Moog use is directly named (not a genre deduction): `moog`. The official biography names Federico Zampaglione, Andrea Pesce and Luigi Pulcinelli as actual album producers. Lorenzo Amurri is described as a collaborator, *not* credited a producer. (rockol2004; auditorium2004; officialbio)

**Lyrics/voice:** `soft`, with occasional more forceful duet with Manuel Agnelli in *Esplode*. Themes of love, fantasies/illusions, everyday social judgment and personal identity are supported by official lyrics and artist interview; Italian only. (official2004; tgcom2004)

**sounds_like/influenced_by:** Lucio Dalla is a qualified reviewer's specific writing comparison (`sounds_like`), reinforced by Zampaglione naming Dalla among career influences in phase 01; the cover *Felicità* is *not itself* an influence and **influenced_by remains empty** absent specific album evidence. Manuel Agnelli is a guest collaborator, not an influence. Landmark 1 (artist's recognised defining era). Confidence high core credit/production, genre Trip-Hop less certain.

## L’alba di domani (2007) — `tiromancino-lalbadidomani`

**Classification:** recorded and sold as a named Tiromancino album, also explicitly designated by the band's own biography as the soundtrack to Federico's film *Nero bifamiliare*. It combines stand-alone songs and four instrumentals. **Set `type=soundtrack`** as the most accurate descriptor of original function, despite some discographies calling it studio: Claude can discuss whether site-wide conventions use `studio`. This is not a second, duplicate score album. (officialbio; rockol2007; official2007)

**Genres:** `poprock` for song material; `rock` and `blues` for specified songs/cues (*Nero bifamiliare*, *Stop Making Numbers*, *Linea di confine*, *Empty Can*); `electronic` for named tracks *Angoli di cielo* and *Non per l'eternità*; `soundtrack` marks the film use, but isn't mistaken for a sonic genre. (musicalnews2007; wiki2007; rockol2007)

**Scale values:** energy 3 and tempo 3 cover radio pop through livelier rock/blues; electronic 2 because electronic treatments coexist with extensive played guitar and drums. Mood 2 for reflective, uneasy film-coloured material with a hopeful title song. Theatrical 3 reflects score cues and variety; experimental 2 less exploratory than earlier work, although vintage gear and alternate languages matter. Polish 3 for coherent but more directly self-recorded production; groove 2 because much is song- or cue-oriented rather than dance-first. (musicalnews2007; rockol2007; rockolinterview2007)

**Instruments:** Federico said he played most of the instruments on the record. Contemporary report identifies guitar, rock/blues emphasis and Piero Monterisi's drums, Rosario Iermano percussion; album personnel include keys, bass and drums. Federico's 21 March 2007 interview explicitly mentions an old vocoder and vintage Prophet **without specific model**: use `vocoder|synth`, and `production=vocoder` for the treatment/device; no `prophet5`/`prophetv` guess. (rockolinterview2007; musicalnews2007; wiki2007)

**Producer dispute:** contemporary March 2007 album press says produced/arranged by Federico Zampaglione, and explicitly says Francesco Zampaglione produced *Non per l'eternità*; retain both, with track scope noted. A lower-authority discography lists Rudolph Gentile as album co-producer, while the contemporary report identifies him as a collaborating recording partner and separately a film producer. **Omit Rudolph Gentile from `producers` until an original record sleeve unambiguously identifies his music-production role.** He is not a ghost credit. (musicalnews2007; wiki2007; officialbio)

**Voice, languages, lyrics:** soft Federico lead, four film-score instrumentals and credited guest vocals by Claudia Gerini. Italian plus English on *Kill the Pain* and other English songs, Spanish on *Niña de Luna*. Love, life choices, isolation/social observation and storytelling from film. Avoid classifying an instrumental cue as sung-language. (official2007; rockol2007; debaser2007)

**Critical disagreement:** Rockol's Paolo Panzeri judges the songs plus film score a coherent album; a contemporary DeBaser review considers it a weaker, provisional chapter. DeBaser compares only *Nero bifamiliare*'s instrumental sound to mid-1970s Pink Floyd: `sounds_like=Pink Floyd` with narrow **track** scope, absolutely not an asserted Pink Floyd influence. 2007 `landmark=0` because this album is not comparably defining to the 2000–04 breakthrough. `confidence=medium` reflects producer credit ambiguity and hybrid type. (rockol2007; debaser2007)

**Date discrepancy:** contemporary March reporting says release 23 March 2007; a secondary discography reports 11 May 2007. Fandome CSV records year only (`2007`), so no false day-of-month precision. (musicalnews2007; cinemaitalia2007; wiki2007)
