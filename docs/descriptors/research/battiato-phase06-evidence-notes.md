## Messa arcaica (1994) — `battiato-messaarcaica`

**Recording, date and format.** EMI Classics original 1994 CD, catalogue CDC 7243 5 551582 7, recorded in **October 1993 at Logic Studio, Milan**, per the CiNii university library catalogue of the physical original. Premiere in October 1993 is **not** its album year. The 1996 English-language *Shadow, Light* album includes re-presented mass movements plus other pieces and should not be conflated with or replace this original record. **Type `studio`**: the original CD is documented as a studio recording of a mass previously premiered live; not a concert capture. The album is absent from `catalog.ts`.

**Genres.** `sacredmusic` first: an original full-scale liturgical mass setting the Ordinary. `classical` second: released EMI Classics with named choir, orchestra and soloists. Avoid `opera`, `soundtrack`, `ambient`, or electronic genre labels simply because Battiato also worked in those domains. The work is often described as contemporary but the evidence does not specifically label its recording a distinct modern-classical subgenre.

**Instruments and voices.** `orchestra|organ|strings` confirmed across CiNii original-CD bibliographic record and Apple Classical's instrument/ensemble credits: **Filippo Destrieri** plays Hammond organ and organ, and I Virtuosi Italiani are credited as orchestra/string quartet; these are track-level, not all-track claims. **Athestis Chorus** and soloists Franco Battiato and mezzosoprano **Akemi Sakamoto** justify `choral|clear|powerful`; choir is voice, not `instruments`. `la` matches the bibliographic text-language metadata (liturgical Latin; Kyrie has Greek liturgical formulae, but the library's recording-level language is Latin). **Production** intentionally blank: recording location and engineer do not by themselves evidence a listed signal-processing technique.

**Producer.** **Conflicting reissue metadata:** Qobuz names **Franco Battiato, Producer**, and **Enrico Maghenzani, Executive Producer**. Apple Music Classical, for the original 1994 album, expressly names **both Battiato and Maghenzani as Producers**, and Maghenzani as Executive Producer too. Therefore both are provisionally listed in `producers`, because at least one rights-data source calls Maghenzani an actual producer. Verify 1994 EMI physical booklet before resolving this credit conflict; do not confuse an executive-only credit with producer evidence.

**Influences.** `influenced_by` blank. Bach and Mozart are named by Battiato's site as historic liturgical composers, *not proven album-specific composers he directly modelled on*. Scale reasoning: ceremonial dynamics but much slow measured music, hence `energy=1`, `tempo=1`, `groove=0`, `theatrical=5` for dramatic liturgical choir, `electronic=1` due to Destrieri's credited Hammond organ against predominantly orchestral/choral sources, `experimental=3` for unusual setting in a pop catalogue, `mood=2` contemplative and grave, `polish=4` fully arranged studio work. `landmark=0` means not a core popular breakthrough; does not devalue it as a classical work. **Disagreement**: cultural histories emphasize minimalist spirituality while official materials call this one of Battiato's most traditional scores; no unsupported minimalism label is added.

## L’ombrello e la macchina da cucire (1995) — `battiato-lombrelloelamacchinadacucire`

**Date and identity.** March 1995 first issue EMI, separate from 1994 *Unprotected* live and from *Il cavaliere dell’intelletto*, the stage work with Sgalambro. Nine Sgalambro texts set to music by Battiato. Not in current `catalog.ts`.

**Genres and disagreements.** `experimentalpop` denotes the direct collision of compositional experiments and song-form, supported by contemporary artist interviews (La Sicilia) and specialist critics. `italianpop|songwriter|pop`: contemporary discussions identify the Italian pop/cantautore grammar and streaming label categorises it Pop. `electropop|electronic` apply principally to computer-built arrangements and *Tao*, not a club or synthpop album (no `synthpop`). `neoclassical` and `ambient` are secondary *track or texture* terms drawn from FOND/SOUND's critical analysis rather than a statement that the whole album is ambient. OndaRock dismisses the album as heavy, wearisome and oppressively cerebral; FOND/SOUND argues it is an innovative, undervalued electronic opera. This evaluation disagreement should remain visible; Fandome scales judge traits, not quality.

**Instrumentation and studio process.** Contemporaneous interview *Il Giorno* (22 March 1995) specifies a string quartet, piano, *computer programmati*, and choruses. Digital reissue track credits name **Benedict Fenner** as engineer/programmer, **Carlo Guaitoli** on piano, and Athestis Chorus voices; reviewer explicitly mentions synthesizers. Therefore `piano|strings|synth` and `programming|stringarrangements`; `strings` represents the credited quartet, `choral` is a voice role. Arranging and choral writing supported by 1995 first-hand interview in *Il manifesto*. No machine, sampler brand or DAW is named for this particular record; do not invent one. `voice=clear|choral`, Sgalambro writes words but is **not credited lead singer** here. Scores: `energy=2` mostly restrained, `electronic=3` computer plus string/piano ensemble, `mood=1` grave/uneasy, `theatrical=3` developed choral writing, `experimental=4` text-first and atypical forms, `polish=4`, `tempo=2`, `groove=1`.

**Producer / real musical quotation.** Qobuz 2008 remaster *track-level* credit names **Enrico Maghenzani as Producer**, confirmed by original-discography reference. **Joseph Haydn** in `influenced_by` is exceptional: *Un vecchio cameriere* actually adapts material from **String Quartet op. 64 no. 5** and credits Haydn as *composer* (UK Charts track credit), making a verifiable compositional borrowing rather than mere sonic resemblance. Neither Gesualdo, Ockham, Charlie Parker, Lautréamont nor the surrealists qualify as proven *musical influence* based merely on lyrical reference; Battiato denied being inspired by surrealism at the 1995 press conference.

**Lyrics and language.** Philosophical exploration, spirituality, satire of society, and sexuality inform `introspection|spiritual|social|irony|sensuality`; original album mainly Italian. `en` is justified by English sentences actually sung/spoken in the title track; `de` by German phrase in *Un vecchio cameriere* (track-level), both secondary, not entire translated albums. Album-scale `landmark=0` is an editorial threshold, despite renewed critical appraisal.

## L’imboscata (1996) — `battiato-limboscata`

**Release and artist credit.** First Mercury / PolyGram album issued **23 October 1996**; not the 1997 Spanish **La emboscada** release. First-pressing sleeve transcription and label-supplied Qobuz identify **Franco Battiato as the sole producer**. Do **not** promote executive producer **Maurizio Salvadori** or recordist/mix engineer **Benedict Fenner** into `producers`. Absent in original artist catalogue.

**Genres and dissent.** OndaRock describes a return to the electric guitar-led pop-rock/cantautore approach, with *Strani giorni* specifically rock, *La cura* orchestrated, and many other songs soft ballads. `poprock|songwriter|rock|pop` therefore denote the overall blend; `ballad|orchestralpop` are **some-tracks** labels. This is not primarily experimental electronic music and a change of record label does not imply production similarity. Critics praise standout tracks but OndaRock thinks some others are pedestrian, while the album was a major comeback; neither critical view alters genre/scale values.

**Session credits.** Original sleeve: **David Rhodes** guitar (both types), **Saturnino** bass, **Gavin Harrison** drums/percussion, **Carlo Guaitoli** piano/keyboards, original string quartet (two violins, viola, cello) and Jean-Pierre Vermeeren soprano saxophone. `electricguitar|piano|keyboards|bass|drums|percussion|strings|acousticguitar|saxophone` main-first. **Benedict Fenner** records/mixes in Paris/Capri and plays guitar/keys; engineer is not a producer. `stringarrangements` follows Battiato's credited arrangements and detailed string parts. No proof of a specific drum machine, software, brand or sampling process for this album; do not add these from a 1998 record. The general 'computer' performance credit is insufficient proof to label `programming`. `clear|soft|spoken` for lead and *Di passaggio*'s text/guest speech. Scores `energy=3`, `electronic=2`, `mood=2`, `theatrical=3`, `experimental=2`, `polish=4`, `tempo=3`, `groove=2` compare electric rock passages to the slower love ballads.

**Language.** `it|en|fr|pt|de` supported by OndaRock and the 2026 retrospective expressly identifying all five languages; *Segunda-feira* has verified extended Portuguese lines (not just a title). `en` features in *Strani giorni*, `de` appears in a track's German title and vocals; flag possible **French/ German individual phrase coverage** for second reviewer; not 5 equally prominent languages. **Influences.** No record-specific direct musical influence proved by these contributors: neither the artist's use of David Rhodes nor his work with Peter Gabriel constitutes an attributed inspiration. `sounds_like=Peter Gabriel|David Sylvian` is an **editorial sonic comparison** from layered guitars, vocal delivery and orchestral rock, **not** a reported declared influence. `influenced_by` blank. `landmark=1` thanks to landmark *La cura* and successful return.

## Gommalacca (1998) — `battiato-gommalacca`

**Date and original identity.** First Mercury/PolyGram CD out **24 September 1998**, first pressing catalog 558 907-2; already in `catalog.ts`, **retain ID `battiato-gommalacca`**, descriptor only. Artist official source explicitly credits Battiato with **music, arrangements and production**, corroborated by first-edition sleeve. **Maurizio Salvadori** listed only as executive, **Madaski / Pino Pischetola** recording/mixing, not co-producers.

**Genres.** Official source foregrounds electronic and digital methods with Italian alternative rock collaborators; OndaRock describes *techno hard-rock* as a hybrid of machine feel and abrasive guitars. For explainability `electrorock|electronic|rock` dominate. Separately **`alternativerock`** is explicitly named by the album discography and directly corroborated by the 1998 scene; additional catalogued `poprock|newwave|synthpop` (MusicBase original-CD genre tags) belong lower: style vocabulary does not imply the *entire* record sounds like synthpop. `hardrock|techno` are **some tracks** / **texture** respectively; the techno term is explicitly critic metaphor for arrangements, **not** evidence of dance-club techno throughout. `pop` is base song-format metadata. Rockol's contemporaneous review also emphasises contrasts between aggressive and meditative songs.

**Real instruments / production.** Original CD sleeve credits **Morgan** bass, **Gavin Harrison** drums and percussion, **Ru Catania** distorted electric guitar, **Marco Pancaldi** acoustic/electric guitar, Battiato electric guitar and keys. OndaRock and Fabio Zuffanti's extensive track-specific study additionally identify analogue/digital sounding synthesizers, orchestral/string parts, and sampled choirs. Thus `electricguitar|synth|bass|drums|percussion|keyboards|acousticguitar|strings|piano` (the original credited piano is secondary). *Rolling Stone / Zuffanti* analyses distorted vocal delivery on *Shock in my town*, back-reversed phrase at c. 3:02, sampled choirs and *Shakleton* radio-like voice manipulation and samples. Together with OndaRock's explicit **overdubbing and distortion**, this grounds `distortion|distortedvocals|sampling|overdubbing|vocalfilter|backmasking|programming`; the last is supported by Pino Pischetola programming on *Shakleton* in album credits. `backmasking` is used strictly as **reverse playback**, not to imply a hidden subliminal message. No brand-name sequencer, samplers, DAW or drum-machine model has been established; omit them. Scores `energy=4` hard explosive passages, `electronic=4` heavily processed with real rhythm section, `mood=1` ominous/dystopian, `theatrical=4`, `experimental=4`, `polish=4`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` strong motoric rhythms with considerable slow sections.

**Important edition variation — Casta diva.** Rockol (June 1999) reports a rights-related change to the sample: Paola Romanò's voice was present in copies when the original Maria Callas sound recording could not be cleared; **some later copies use Callas**. The report also describes an earlier circulating demo with Callas; edition history is not a uniform single take. Do not claim 'Maria Callas sample' on **every 1998 CD**; the general `sampling` token is safe because additional samples occur elsewhere on the record.

**Language and influence.** `it|en|de` (English refrains, German spoken/sung poem in *Shakleton*, primary Italian); Rolling Stone Zuffanti documents text and guest-voice context. An explicit *Velvet Underground* lyrical name-drop does not establish that the Velvet Underground influenced the **music**; none is entered in `influenced_by`. `sounds_like=CSI|Bluvertigo` is editorial, based on independently documented abrasive electronic-rock/alternative-scene similarities; shared players alone do not create influenced_by evidence. `landmark=1` is justified by the commercially and stylistically important 1998 pivot, supported official biography, Rockol and OndaRock.

## Method / caution about older packages

- Do not silently normalize unknown scale values to zero. Numeric values above use album-specific review and credit evidence and remain judgements.
- Critics disagree on *L’ombrello*'s merit, *Gommalacca*'s consistency, and how to label the electronic/rock hybrids; scope labels in genre ledger reflect this.
- Song **lyric quotations** of poets, composers, and musical works are not direct record-production influences. All `influenced_by` values must be separately justified.
- No new instrument or production token is required: all proposed values are present in guide `descriptors` branch. No new genre token is proposed for this phase.
- `Qobuz` is used for attributed label metadata, including the 2008 credit reissue. Where original pressing is pivotal, early sleeve copies and CiNii original-CD bibliography outrank remaster format dates.
