# Queen — Fandome phase 01: album-level evidence notes

**Scope:** four original studio albums, 1973–1975, excluding remaster/remix bonus material. Descriptors are album-wide editorial assessments, not track-wise measurements. Where uncertainty exists the genre scope and evidence strength are explicitly preserved. Source IDs resolve to `queen_phase01_sources.csv`.

## queen-queen — *Queen* (1973)

- **Genre weighting:** `hardrock` foregrounded because guitar-heavy hard rock is the record’s prevailing musical surface. `artrock` and `prog` reflect multi-section songs, particularly *Great King Rat* and *My Fairy King* [D2, D5]. `glamrock` is qualified: AllMusic identifies the appearance and ornate style but says the band is too heavy and artful for a simple glam category [D2]. `heavymetal` relates to hard early tracks; `arenarock` and `contemporarypoprock` are comparatively generic AllMusic style classifications [D2].
- **Instruments:** acoustic/electric guitars, bass, drums, piano and percussion are in original/session credits [D3, D4]. Organ is audible/identified on *Liar* [D3]. Tambourine appears in the track-by-track credits [D4]. No synthesizers: the original sleeve and band explicitly say none [D1, D6]. The presence of an organ at Trident is **not** evidence it was played on every song.
- **Production:** recorded to 16-track analogue tape; overdubbed guitars and separately tracked ensemble harmony parts [D1, D3]. `layeredvocals` is about the layered vocal arrangements, **not** a synthesizer sound. Avoid naming exact bass-guitar or microphone models: D3 considers some possibilities unresolved. Avoid assigning tape or software beyond the 1971–72 sessions.
- **Producers:** `Queen`, `Roy Thomas Baker` and `John Anthony` are documented for most tracks. *The Night Comes Down* is an **exception** produced with Louie (Louis) Austin and Queen; hence Louie Austin appears at the end of the album-wide list [D1]. Later mixing/engineering roles do not qualify [D3].
- **Scales (0–5):** energy 4 (riffs and heavy drum passages [D2]); electronic 1 (amplified rock but no synthesizers [D1]); mood 2 (generally dark fantasy offset by exuberant songs [D2]); theatrical 4 (ornate, operatic vocal/guitar writing [D2]); experimental 3 (suite structures [D2]); polish 2 (original constrained sessions, before 2024 reconstructed mix [D1, D6]); tempo 4 (many brisk songs [D2]); groove 2 (beat-driven rock but limited dance orientation [D2]). Values represent judgments anchored to the Fandome guide.
- **Voice:** `powerful`, `clear`, `raspy` describe Mercury’s contrast between projected singing and rough rock delivery [D2, D5]; multi-person backing harmony itself is encoded in `production`, not an extra vocal gender classification.
- **Lyrics:** storytelling/fantasy and spiritual allegories (including *My Fairy King*, *Great King Rat*, *Jesus*), with personal concerns elsewhere. `storytelling|spiritual|introspection` are broader theme encodings, not claims of consistent narrative [D2, D4].
- **Similarity vs influence:** *Black Sabbath* and *David Bowie* are Pitchfork sonic comparisons, not automatically documented influences [D5]. `Led Zeppelin` is tied to critic discussion of May’s early guitar layers [D2]. May explicitly credits Rory Gallagher for the actual guitar/amp sound, predating the record (ARTIST INFLUENCES sources). Do **not** add 2024 mix personnel as 1973 producers.

## queen-queenii — *Queen II* (1974)

- **Genre weighting:** `hardrock|artrock|prog|heavymetal` follows the combination of aggressive rhythm section, elaborate extended compositions and operatic harmonies. AllMusic explicitly questions calling the whole work prog because of its great heaviness [Q3]. Music hall occurs in the *Fairy Feller* passage, not on every track [Q6]. The 2026 release review and official reissue characterise the record as very heavy [Q2, Q7].
- **Instruments:** 2026 published **original album credits** identify Mercury vocals, piano/harpsichord; May guitars, piano, vocals, bells; Deacon bass and acoustic guitar; Taylor percussion and vocals; Roy Thomas Baker castanets [Q2]. The specialist discography documents Hammond organ, marimba and tubular bells at track level [Q5], assigned `organ|mallets|tubularbells` with weaker confidence; `tubularbells` requires a **new token** rather than silently relabelling as generic synthesizers. No synth appears on original record [Q2].
- **Production:** overdubbed vocal and guitar groups are explicitly attested [Q2, Q6], recorded on analogue tape; *Ogre Battle* features reversed musical material [Q6], encoded as `tapeeffects|backmasking`, **not** subliminal backmasked messages. No named electronic synthesizer; no guessed model numbers.
- **Producers disagreement:** QueenOnline **2024** says Robin Geoffrey Cable, Roy Thomas Baker and Queen produced it [Q1], but the original 1974 production wording republished by Queen in **2026** is `Produced by Roy Thomas Baker and Queen; additional production by Robin Geoffrey Cable and Queen` [Q2]. Since Fandome forbids an **additional-production-only** credit in `producers`, the import field uses only `Queen|Roy Thomas Baker`. Claude can re-evaluate against specific original *track* co-producer credits, but must not promote ambiguous additional production silently.
- **Scales:** energy 4 (metallic heft), electronic 1 (no synths), mood 1 (menace and brooding White/Black contrast), theatrical 5 (explicit themed sides and broad operatic choruses), experimental 4 (sound and sequence structure), polish 3 (complex but sometimes dense/murky mix), tempo 3 (many dynamic contrasts), groove 1 (primarily dramatic riff-driven and suite-oriented rather than dance-driven) [Q1–Q4, Q6].
- **Voice:** layered vocal ensembles and strongly projected Mercury lead; choral corresponds to the ensemble effect, **not** an actual choir [Q2, Q3]. `lyrics` encodes fantasy narrative, longing and spiritual undertones based on White/Black songs [Q1, Q2].
- **Similarity/influence:** *Genesis*, *Uriah Heep*, *Led Zeppelin* are editorial sonic reference points; reviewers explicitly debate the resemblance [Q3]. The `influenced_by` cell is intentionally empty: artist-wide listening is not sufficient proof of specific album causation. *The Fairy Feller’s Master-Stroke* draws on Richard Dadd’s **painting**, not a musician, so this is not a musical influence [Q2].

## queen-sheerheartattack — *Sheer Heart Attack* (1974)

- **Genres:** AllMusic labels hard rock/glam/art rock/heavy metal and more general album/arena/contemporary pop-rock and British Metal [S2]. The *Brighton Rock*, *Now I’m Here* and *Stone Cold Crazy* tracks justify heavier tags; *Bring Back That Leroy Brown* specifically invokes music hall / vaudeville [S6, S7]; a specialist review calls *Misfire* reggae-tinged, marked low-prevalence [S8]. `britishmetal` and `vaudeville` are **new tokens** rather than substitutes for existing labels. Pitchfork describes *Stone Cold Crazy* as a **precursor** to speed metal, so `speedmetal` is **not** applied as a firm album genre [S6].
- **Instruments:** original sleeve and track musician credits prove guitars, bass, piano/jangle piano, organ, acoustic guitar, double bass, percussion, timpani, tambourine, and the genuine **George Formby ukulele-banjo** [S3, S5]. The latter is coded `banjoukulele` (new token): not both independent banjo + ukulele, which would invent separate parts. *Killer Queen* has regular and jangle piano playing in unison [S9]. **No synthesizers** per 1974 sleeve [S3, S9].
- **Production:** guitar and choir-like vocal overdubbing [S6, S9], analogue-era studio layering (not digital sampling), plus tape delay on *Brighton Rock* as directly described by Brian May [S4]. `distortion` is instrument overdrive, not processed/overdriven lead vocals [S4]. Specific Echoplex modifications were discussed by May, but the exact model variant is not substantiated; do not add a model token.
- **Producers:** only Queen and Roy Thomas Baker (Mike Stone engineering) [S1, S3]. Avoid reissue producers/engineers listed on modern aggregations.
- **Scales:** energy 4 (very fast *Stone Cold Crazy*, heavy songs), electronic 1 (electric band, expressly no synth), mood 3 (bright wit alternating with dark/abrasive rock), theatrical 4 (cabaret and ensemble vocals), experimental 3 (diverse arrangements), polish 4 (finely assembled coherent studio tracks), tempo 4 (frequent fast sections), groove 3 (bouncy rhythmic pop and music-hall passages, amid riff rock) [S1, S2, S6].
- **Voice/lyrics:** many vocal techniques and lead timbres; `choral` is the human stacked harmonies [S6, S9]. Lyrics cover character stories, social posing, romance and sardonic episodes; chart themes are selective rather than a uniform plot [S1, S6, S7].
- **Album-specific influence:** Brian May unequivocally says *Now I'm Here* was inspired by Led Zeppelin's spirit and Jimmy Page's riff language [S4]. A Queen specialist critic specifically calls Bowie's influence continuing on *Tenement Funster* [S8]. The album's actual guest players are not treated as influences.

## queen-anightattheopera — *A Night at the Opera* (1975)

- **Genre weighting:** hard rock, prog/art rock, pop and British music hall/vaudeville are distinct, source-described components [N3, N9]. `rockopera` is used for the **central quasi-operatic suite** *Bohemian Rhapsody*, not to claim the LP narrates one rock-opera plot [N3]. `skiffle` appears at the tail of the list, from a narrower specialist description [N10]. Neither fake `opera` as a dominant classical genre nor synthpop is assigned.
- **Instruments:** 1975 sleeve credit includes the full amplified band, Freddie’s Bechstein piano, John Deacon’s **electric piano** (*You're My Best Friend*) and double bass (*'39*), Brian May’s **harp** (*Love of My Life*), ukulele (*Good Company*) and **toy koto** (*The Prophet's Song*) [N6]. `koto` reflects a documented toy version, not a full traditional koto ensemble. `percussion` is credited to Roger Taylor. In *Seaside Rendezvous*, brass/woodwind orchestration was **vocal mimicry**; do **not** tag trumpet, saxophone, flute, brass or woodwinds as recorded instruments [N6]. No synthesizers on original sleeve [N6].
- **Production:** original **analogue** multitracks, extensive vocal/guitar overdubbing and tape bounces are documented by Roy Thomas Baker, assistant engineer Gary Langan and Queen's reissue participants [N4, N5]; no 2025/2005 remix technique is imputed to 1975. `tapeeffects` includes *The Prophet's Song* vocal canon and guitar delay [N8]. `vocalfilter` describes the metal-container microphone/earphone routing on *Lazing on a Sunday Afternoon*, a documented effect [N7]. The `layeredvocals` production tag must not imply an outside choir [N4].
- **Producers:** original sleeve lists Queen and Roy Thomas Baker only [N1, N6]. Mike Stone and Gary Lyons are engineers; neither is silently added to `producers` despite broad biographical descriptions. Further note: *God Save the Queen* was developed earlier than much of the album; 1975 release remains one unified original LP.
- **Scales:** energy 4 (many forceful climaxes even with calm passages), electronic 1 (no synth), mood 3 (light, comic and poignant alongside dramatic), theatrical 5 (Fandome’s own scale anchor), experimental 4 (multi-part dramatic construction, unusual recording), polish 5 (detailed large-budget studio production), tempo 3 (range from slow ballad to fast rock), groove 2 (some bouncy pop but not dance-oriented) [N1, N3–N5, N9].
- **Voice/lyrics:** powerful and clear leads, gently sung ballads plus stacked choir-effect and Roger/Brian-led songs [N3, N6]; themes embrace love, betrayal, fantasy, mortality and playful character writing. These theme tags summarize representative tracks, not an inferred overarching narrative.
- **Influence/similarity boundary:** *10cc*, *The Beatles*, *Led Zeppelin* and *ELO* are editorial comparisons only; no claim that all four directly shaped this particular recording. `influenced_by` intentionally empty pending a defensible **album-specific** musical influence. Musical-theatre format and titled reference to the Marx Brothers' film *A Night at the Opera* should not be misfiled as musical-artist influences.

## Cross-album interpretive caveats

1. Music critics disagree over the useful extent of `glamrock`, `prog` and `heavymetal`. Retain genre tokens with genre-evidence **scope**, rather than extrapolating minority tracks across an album.
2. The scores are independently assessed and grounded in the linked album accounts. Scores are not source quotations or physically measured values.
3. Original credits take precedence over metadata from 2011/2024/2025/2026 reissues for `producers` and original record production methods.
4. AllMusic sometimes blocks automatic page opening (HTTP 403). Its search-indexed review text and page metadata were visible during verification. Original official and specialist sources provide a separate evidential path.
5. The absence of synthesisers does not mean the records were acoustically recorded: electric-guitar amplification and tape treatments justify a small electronic score, not a synth instrument.
6. `sounds_like` is an editorial comparator, explicitly **not** proven lineage. The `influenced_by` list contains only corroborated music-specific cases.
