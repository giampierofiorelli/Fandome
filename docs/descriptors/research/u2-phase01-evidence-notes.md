# U2 — Phase 01 evidence notes

Research cutoff: **27 October 2026**. Album scale scores are original Fandome editorial judgements based on the linked evidence. They are not measurements or reviewer ratings. The `sounds_like` matches are editorial sonic comparisons, **not** undocumented historical influences. All rows are `draft`.

## u2-boy — Boy (1980)

**Dates, personnel, production.** Original studio LP, 20 October 1980; Steve Lillywhite is the sole credited producer. Bono (vocals), The Edge (electric guitar; he confirms using a Gibson Explorer), Adam Clayton (bass), Larry Mullen Jr. (drums). The original sleeve offers no support for invented keyboards or synthesisers. [Official U2](https://www.u2.com/products/boy); [original sleeve](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_boy_original_release); [Edge interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-edge-u2-october-achtung-baby).

**Genres.** `postpunk` is explicitly attached to the album by AllMusic and central to its sound. `rock` is supported by the contemporary Hot Press review. The six remaining tags from AllMusic (`albumrock`, `alternativepoprock`, `alternativeindierock`, `collegerock`, `contemporarypoprock`, `poprock`) reproduce that site's album-level taxonomy verbatim as normalized genre tokens; they should be weighted below post-punk. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/boy-mw0000192151); [Hot Press, Declan Lynch](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1980-u2-release-their-debut-album-boy-22830396).

**Production.** `roomambience`: Lillywhite recounts relocating Larry's drums to Windmill Lane's hard-surfaced reception/hallway after being dissatisfied with the earlier single sound. `delay`: AllMusic describes the actual album's echoed, treated guitar. `sonicmanipulation`: Lillywhite recalls bottle-breaking and a bicycle wheel being used to make recordable noises for “I Will Follow”; the source does *not* establish tape-loop machinery, samplers or specific synthesizers. [Lillywhite interview](https://www.hotpress.com/opinion/interview-steve-lillywhite-on-u2-boy-and-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind-22832605); [official production soundbite](https://www.u2.com/products/boy); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/boy-mw0000192151).

**Scales, reasoning.** Energy **4**, driving performances (“I Will Follow”, “Out of Control”) but not continuous extreme intensity. Electronic **1**, electric instruments and processed guitar, no credited electronic instruments. Mood **3**, optimism tempered by anxiety and darker pieces. Theatrical **4**, large vocal projection and wide anthemic arrangements. Experimental **2**, unconventional textures within concise songs. Polish **3**, inventive professionally engineered rock, still less layered than later U2. Tempo **4**, predominantly propulsive. Groove **2**, rhythmic urgency without dance-floor orientation. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/boy-mw0000192151); [1980 Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1980-u2-release-their-debut-album-boy-22830396); [Lillywhite](https://www.hotpress.com/opinion/interview-steve-lillywhite-on-u2-boy-and-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind-22832605).

**Voice, lyrics, language.** `powerful`: Bono's impassioned high-intensity singing. `identity|introspection` from adolescence and maturation; `love` from the maternal loss/familial bond behind “I Will Follow”; `spiritual` as secondary recurring strand noted in period criticism. Main language English. [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1980-u2-release-their-debut-album-boy-22830396); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/boy-mw0000192151); [Hot Press, review of October referring back to Boy](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-40-years-ago-u2-released-october-22875387).

**Influence vs resemblance.** The Edge's retrospective official interview directly says he hears Siouxsie and the Banshees, Buzzcocks and The Skids on *Boy*: these qualify for `influenced_by`. The sonic `sounds_like` options Echo & the Bunnymen and The Sound are editorial parallels in guitar-led post-punk; neither is represented as a documentary influence on *Boy*. [Official U2](https://www.u2.com/products/boy). Landmark **1**, inaugural studio statement with contemporary critical recognition.

**Possible disagreement.** Some later reviews emphasise its darkness, while Declan Lynch heard overt positivity. Mood sits at **3**, rather than forcing all tracks bright or all dark.

## u2-october — October (1981)

**Dates, personnel.** Official U2 release date **12 October 1981**. Other discographic transcriptions say 20 October: prefer first-party release date and preserve the discrepancy. Produced by Steve Lillywhite (sole album production credit). The Edge plays guitars and piano, including slide guitar according to his interview; Vinnie/Vincent Kilduff played **uilleann pipes and bodhrán**, noted in the original 1981 credits and linked by the Edge specifically to “Tomorrow”. [U2](https://www.u2.com/products/october); [liner notes](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_october_remaster); [Edge](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-edge-u2-october-achtung-baby); [October liner transcription](https://www.u2station.com/u2ography/albums/october.php).

**Genres.** `postpunk` and the six additional AllMusic styles are album-level classifications. `rock` underlies the band's playing. Original reviewer Neil McCormick describes *specific parts* of the album as driving `dance` music and says “I Threw a Brick Through a Window” has `funk` elements, while “Tomorrow” combines Irish folk music and rock (`folkrock`). The last three are *track/some-track* tags, deliberately low weighted. His Christian/spiritual observations establish lyrics and context: they do **not** automatically make the whole album `gospel` or `christianrock`. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/october-mw0000650915); [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-40-years-ago-u2-released-october-22875387); [Edge](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-edge-u2-october-achtung-baby).

**Production.** `delay` covers the clearly documented echoed guitar sound of this particular album described by McCormick. Do not claim a specific brand/model of delay pedal or analog recording platform from the generic account of Edge's career. Layering is mentioned by Edge, but not sufficiently specific to insist on `overdubbing` here. [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-40-years-ago-u2-released-october-22875387); [Edge](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-edge-u2-october-achtung-baby).

**Scales, reasoning.** Energy **4** from strident first-side rock contrasted with slower second side. Electronic **1** for guitar effects, despite acoustic/traditional instruments. Mood **3**, celebratory choruses alongside self-doubt and loss. Theatrical **4**, passionate vocal and big-screen arrangements. Experimental **3**, pipes, piano, slide guitar and folk-rock fusion extend the established post-punk palette. Polish **3**, coherent but sometimes ragged production. Tempo **3**, faster first half and reflective second half. Groove **3**, first-side dance propulsion, plus funk-informed bass/drums. [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-40-years-ago-u2-released-october-22875387); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/october-mw0000650915); [Edge](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-edge-u2-october-achtung-baby).

**Voice, lyrics and language.** `powerful|clear`: McCormick describes Bono's strong singing and newly controlled softer/harder delivery. Spiritual faith, conscience and inner struggle (`spiritual|introspection|identity`) dominate. “Gloria” contains a *short Latin refrain*; English remains the principal language, so `language=en`. `influenced_by` remains deliberately empty, because Irish tradition is a musical tradition rather than a named artist, and references to influences on U2 *as a group* alone do not confirm an album-specific artist influence. [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-40-years-ago-u2-released-october-22875387); [Post-Punk.com](https://post-punk.com/u2-october/).

**Critics disagree.** Neil McCormick's 1981 *Hot Press* review praises the album's emotional expansion; Stephen Thomas Erlewine's retrospective *AllMusic* review finds the performances overly grand and the songs uneven. Marked **medium** confidence for its aggregate descriptor / relative weighting, not for the verified credits. Landmark **0** relative to *Boy*, *War* and the albums to follow.

**Credit caveat.** An older archived U2 discography page names Chris Thomas as engineer; the transcription of the original sleeve lists Paul Thomas. Neither is placed in the `producers` field: only Lillywhite is the credited producer. [U2 archive](https://www.u2.com/products/october); [liner notes](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_october_remaster).

## u2-war — War (1983)

**Dates and producers.** Released 28 February 1983 according to the band's official catalogue; commercial listings sometimes describe a March rollout. Steve Lillywhite produced the album *except* “The Refugee”, for which **Bill Whelan** is credited as producer. Whelan is a genuine track-level producer and therefore belongs in the album-level producer list. [U2 official](https://www.u2.com/products/war); [War liner notes](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_war_album_original_release).

**Instrumentation.** Core electric guitar, bass, drums and piano. The album's original sleeve additionally credits The Edge on lap steel guitar (`steelguitar`), Larry on percussion, electric violin by Steve Wickham (notably “Sunday Bloody Sunday” and “Drowning Man”) and trumpet by Kenny Fradley. The contemporary Hot Press critic documents acoustic guitar in “Seconds”. No unverified synth/drum machine names were added. [Liner notes](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_war_album_original_release); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/1be68d56-dbbd-3e4e-9fa8-98a71dc63ff3); [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1983-u2-released-war-22893666).

**Genres.** `postpunk` plus the standardized AllMusic album-level rock categories; `rock` supported independently in critical reporting. Liam Mackey directly notes urban New York `funk` on “Two Hearts Beat as One”, so it appears last as an explicit **some-tracks** attribution. African-influenced rhythms on “The Refugee” are an adjective in the critic's account, not enough to assert an entire afrobeats/afrobeat genre. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/war-mw0000650916); [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1983-u2-released-war-22893666).

**Production.** Kevin Killen, an engineer who worked on the sessions, describes Lillywhite's hard reception room/stairwell drum recording, close/stair microphones, heavy compression and **UA 1176** compressors. Those specifically support `roomambience|compression|ua1176`. A MusicRadar technical retrospective describes Edge's guitar delay on “New Year's Day”, supporting `delay` for that album track. The compressor model is a direct participant's evidence, not a guess based on the period. [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/67/kevin-killen); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/new-years-day-set-the-template-for-a-more-direct-sonic-vision-for-u2-and-marked-the-point-at-which-they-shifted-their-collective-gaze-towards-political-issues-around-the-globe-the-making-of-an-80s-rock-classic).

**Scales, reasoning.** Energy **4**: the most forceful of the first three, with brief subdued passages, and not constant metal-level energy. Electronic **1**, electric guitars with processing but fundamentally acoustic drum/bass performance. Mood **2**, martial anxiety and anger countered by romantic/uplifting pieces. Theatrical **4**, big unison anthems, commanding vocal. Experimental **2**, direct songwriting, expanded instrumental colours, less overt studio experimentation than 1984 onward. Polish **3**, crisp enough but deliberately vivid/raw midrange. Tempo **4**, driving or march-like across much of album. Groove **2**, forceful rhythm with one funk-leaning song. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/war-mw0000650916); [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1983-u2-released-war-22893666); [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/67/kevin-killen).

**Themes.** `political|social` at the centre (“Sunday Bloody Sunday”, “New Year's Day”, “Seconds”); love on “Two Hearts Beat as One”, spirituality on “40”. Voice `powerful`. English principal language. `sounds_like` Simple Minds and The Alarm is an editorial comparison of anthemic, guitar-based early-80s rock, not a claim about influences. `influenced_by` left empty for lack of robust **album-specific named-artist** testimony. The album was U2's first British chart #1: landmark **1**. [U2 official](https://www.u2.com/products/war); [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/on-this-day-in-1983-u2-released-war-22893666).

**Critics' emphases.** AllMusic especially stresses guitar power and march rhythms; Hot Press hears unusual funk and African-derived rhythmic colours in *specific songs*. Both can be true; none warrants making the album predominantly a funk or African-genre record.

## Cross-album and technical cautions

- AllMusic's `albumrock`, `alternativepoprock`, `alternativeindierock`, `collegerock` and `contemporarypoprock` tags are repeated across the three albums. These are **source taxonomic tags**, not five distinct specialist-reviewer claims. Genre evidence scopes and ranking make their lower weight explicit.
- Do not retroactively assign Eno/Lanois production, synthesizers, sampling or digital workstations to Lillywhite's three records without period-specific source evidence.
- `bodhran`, `compression`, `ua1176` are not in the current permitted token dictionaries and **must be added before final validation**. No new genre tokens are proposed.
- Web retrieval of the repository's `docs/descriptors` GitHub directory returned a restricted listing; a direct raw-path check for `u2.csv` returned HTTP 404. Verify it again before import if the branch has changed.
