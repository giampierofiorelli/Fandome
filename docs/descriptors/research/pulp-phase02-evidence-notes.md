# Pulp — Phase 02 | album evidence and editorial reasoning

Four original **studio** albums, 1995–2025; no phase-01 repeats. Evaluations use the Fandome 0–5 anchors, are independent editorial assessments of *entire albums*, and remain `status=draft`. Where a source describes one track, `genre_evidence.evidence_scope` discloses it; the first listed genre has the greatest weight. Sources are available by exact URL in `pulp_phase02_sources.csv`.

## Different Class (1995) — `pulp-differentclass`
- Original release 30 October 1995, Island; not confused with deluxe bonus tracks. Producer **Chris Thomas**; David Nicholas is engineer, Anne Dudley arranged orchestral sessions, neither added as producer. [PulpWiki](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/DifferentClass) / [session log](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/DifferentClassLPSessions) / [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/065ff5e8-6658-4c18-8d33-3168047039f5/disc/1).
- **Genres and scopes:** Britpop/alternative pop-rock plus new-wave/disco inflections; a **show-tune** element on *Mis-Shapes* does **not** make the whole album show tunes. See [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/different-class-mw0000182602), [Simon Reynolds, Pitchfork 2016](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/22054-different-class/).
- **Scales:** energy **4** (surging *Common People* and fast anthems amid ballads); electronic **2** (several documented synthesisers, but a live rock group and strings); mood **3** (uplifting music/caustic and wistful stories); theatrical **5** (Cocker narrative acting and epic climaxes); experimental **2** (expansive yet pop-accessible); polish **4** (48-track overdubs on *Common People*, orchestrated sessions); tempo **3** (mixed tempi); groove **4** (dance and pop propulsion, never a pure disco album).
- **Instruments:** session notes identify Vox guitars, Farfisa, Mellotron, MicroMoog, Rhodes, Juno 6, SH-09, Trident II, Ensoniq sampler, Synare electronic percussion, violin and three orchestrated tracks. `stylophone` is directly named in the [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/22054-different-class/), not blindly inferred from its use by other musicians. `vocoder` reflects a listed VP-330 machine **as hardware used**, not proven vocal effects; **do not infer processed vocoder vocals** without song-level proof. The Roland VP-330 is NOT a Roland SVC-350; the correct `rolandvp330` token is proposed instead. Recording and layering/overdubs are documented; models represent session credits, not necessarily prominent on every song.
- **Themes:** social class, perceived outsiders, identity, drugs/rave culture, sexuality, longing and nostalgia; [Simon Reynolds](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/22054-different-class/). `influenced_by` records a critic-supported Ray Davies lyrical lineage only; the influence is less certain than the instrumental evidence.

## This Is Hardcore (1998) — `pulp-thisishardcore`
- Released **30 March 1998**; produced **Chris Thomas** ([original credits](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/ThisIsHardcoreAlbum), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/166b98b8-29f9-3df1-86c8-793c7456e874)). Programming by Olle Romo, Matthew Vaughan, Magnus Fiennes and Mark Haley; these people are **not** automatically producers.
- **Genres:** art rock, chamber pop, glam and late Britpop appear as album labels in [Ned Raggett's review](https://www.theshfl.com/album/This-Is-Hardcore); lounge/jazz are explicitly described by [contemporary Salon review](https://www.salon.com/1998/04/15/sharps_138/) as elements, **not** primary whole-album genres.
- **Scales:** energy **3** (restrained ballads alongside *Party Hard* and *I'm a Man*); electronic **2** (synth/programming and samples complement live players); mood **0** (despair, addiction and disillusionment, with limited lightness); theatrical **5** (noir/cinematic crescendos, character acting); experimental **3** (extended arrangements, unease); polish **4** (lavish, detailed production); tempo **2** (predominantly slow/mid); groove **2** (select rhythmic peaks).
- **Production:** documented **programming**, track-specific **sampling** of Peter Thomas's *Bolero on the Moon Rocks* in the title song and **string arrangements**. The album-session page lists certain keyboards as *probably* used; the exact devices were therefore deliberately **not** copied as verified production models. [Session log](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/ThisIsHardcoreLPSessions), [sample credit](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/ThisIsHardcore).
- **Lyrics:** fame, ageing, self-disgust, desire, agency and estrangement; supported by [Pitchfork retrospective](https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/10045-the-50-best-britpop-albums/?page=3) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/this-is-hardcore-mw0000035589).

## We Love Life (2001) — `pulp-welovelife`
- Original UK release **22 October 2001**. **Scott Walker = producer; Peter Walsh = co-producer and engineer**. Earlier incomplete sessions with Chris Thomas were abandoned; do **not** credit him as a producer of the *issued album*. [PulpWiki sleeve](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/WeLoveLife), [Pitchfork 2001](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/6507-we-love-life/).
- **Genres:** art rock, chamber pop, pop rock and synthpop listed by the [curated SHFL review](https://www.theshfl.com/album/We-Love-Life). Pitchfork explicitly says cheap electronic sounds are much less prominent; keep `synthpop` as a lower-confidence *catalogue tag*, not evidence of a synth-led album. `triphop` describes the groove of **Weeds II** only. `postbritpop` is from a weaker retrospective tagging site.
- **Scales:** energy **3** (*Sunrise* delivers a genuine climax, much is subdued); electronic **2** (samplers/drum machines exist, but acoustic guitars, live rhythm section, choir and strings lead); mood **2** (loss and mortality tempered by acceptance and renewal); theatrical **4** (Walker’s dramatic orchestration, spoken narratives); experimental **3** (lengthy recitations, odd timbres); polish **4** (arranged multistudio sessions); tempo **2**; groove **2** (Weeds II/Sunrise, rather than steady dance music).
- **Instruments:** evidence includes baritone/acoustic guitars, Rhodes, drums, Akai sampler, steel guitar, 10 **double basses** and choir, **glass harmonica on Wickerman**, **musical saw on The Birds in Your Garden**, ocarinas and flutes, arranged strings ([session record](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/WeLoveLifeLPSessions)). Ocarina was **not** added because the `woodwinds` family and `flute` cover principal winds, but can be added as separate token if editor wants granular timbres. Glass harmonica and musical saw are already in the current instrument list.
- **Influence:** album recorded *with* Scott Walker as producer; his work is an acknowledged musical antecedent in [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/6507-we-love-life/). He is in `influenced_by` for documented stylistic influence as well as producer, with distinct meanings (not just a collaborator association).

## More (2025) — `pulp-more`
- Original release **6 June 2025**; not to be confused with a 2026 live record or new single compilations. **James Ford** alone has album producer credit. The **Elysian Collective** performs **Richard Jones** string arrangements; members of Brian Eno’s family contribute background vocals. **Richard Hawley, Jason Buckle, Eno family and Richard Jones are not co-billed recording artists or album producers** merely because they contributed. [Official label statement](https://pulp.roughtraderecords.com/more?lang=en_US), [Pulp Bandcamp](https://pulpmusic.bandcamp.com/album/more), [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/more-pulp/opdofej4p29oa).
- **Genres:** expert album labels art rock, alternative rock and post-punk ([SHFL](https://www.theshfl.com/album/More-2)); chamber pop, alternative dance, post-Britpop derive from aggregator metadata ([AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/genre/99-art-rock/2025/rolling-stone-uk/)); *Pitchfork* calls out baroque/pop arrangements; *Observer* names disco and **Northern soul** on *Got to Have Love* and **chanson** on *Tina*. The latter are explicitly **track-scoped**, not claims that the whole album is Northern soul or chanson.
- **Scales:** energy **3** (mix of sparkling, forceful singles and elegiac ballads); electronic **2** (synths and rhythmic treatments but live-band/string-driven); mood **3** (love and resilience coexist with loss); theatrical **4** (Jarvis's spoken staging and anthemic strings); experimental **2** (mostly clear songwriting, some adventurous flourishes); polish **4** (Ford's carefully shaped orchestral production); tempo **3** (midtempo, lively dance songs); groove **3** (disco moments, though ballads predominate).
- **Voices and words:** Cocker's more weathered/low singing with spoken interludes; Eno-family and choir backing vocals, [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/pulp-more/). Subjects include love, aging, memory, desire and coming to terms with past choices. **Steve Mackey died in 2023** and album is dedicated to him; no posthumous performer credit invented.
- **Recording:** documented three-week James Ford sessions in Walthamstow with separate string/choir recording; **no unverified equipment model** was inserted. [Sessions](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/MoreSessions), [label](https://pulp.roughtraderecords.com/more?lang=en_US).

## Evidence policy and boundary conditions
- No `sounds_like` item is promoted into `influenced_by` without an articulated documentary/critical basis; these columns have different semantics. Similarities are editorial, not credits.
- All 27 columns retained, no note >15 words, no individual column length cap elsewhere, and only released original works included. Strings/choirs are instruments/arrangements; sample and tape techniques are not guessed from genre.
- Genre scopes in the CSV can be `whole album`, `some tracks`, `texture` or `secondary tag`; retained for editorial reassessment in Fandome. The unresolved impact of very weak tags is explicit, not concealed.
