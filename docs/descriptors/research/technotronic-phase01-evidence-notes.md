# Technotronic — Fandome phase 01 — evidence notes

Research date: 28 October 2026. Draft analyses, not second-person reviewed. All scores are editorial judgments based on album-specific evidence and scale anchors. Sources below are separately itemised in the sources CSV.

## 1. Pump Up the Jam: The Album (1989)

- **Identity:** The original 1989 studio debut, often shortened to *Pump Up the Jam*. AllMusic's more explicit title resolves catalogue ambiguity. The 1989 album has 12-track and territory-dependent editions, not different studio records.
- **Genre and sound:** AllMusic's album-level House, Acid House, Dance-Pop and Club/Dance labels and Alex Henderson's explicit *hip-house* description underpin first five tags. Insomniac's Eurodance/new-beat identification is tied primarily to the title single, so those follow the core tags. Electronic is an album-wide parent genre.
- **Instrumentation:** Synthesizer is explicitly credited (Patrick De Meyer on lead single); Roland's technical editorial identifies Roland TR-909 rhythms in that track, justifying drummachine and the `tr909` production token. MusicBrainz credits DJ Seik with turntable on *Get Up!*. No specific synthesizer model is asserted from a general equipment inventory.
- **Production:** `sampling` reflects the documented source material of the hit; `drumloop` reflects contemporaneous Bogaert discussion of sample/loop workflow, corroborated by the specific rhythm documentation. The 1990 *Musician* PDF snippet lists machines in the producer's setup, but does **not** establish every device on this album, so no Akai/E-mu/Prophet model is encoded. Jo Bogaert is sole primary album producer; Patrick De Meyer is synthesizer/composer on the hit, not automatically co-producer.
- **Vocals and image:** Ya Kid K's recorded rapping and singing, with MC Eric on the album; model Felly appeared in videos and sleeves and **should not be treated as the lead singer of Pump Up the Jam**. The release database contains some Felly vocals on different editions/remix tracks; do not generalise.
- **Numbers:** energy 4 and tempo 4 for propulsive four-on-the-floor singles; electronic 5 for electronics-centered arrangements; groove 5 for the club orientation; mood 4 given celebratory/energetic track descriptions; theatrical 2 for straightforward vocal delivery; polish 3 for original studio sound; experimental 2 for the rap-house intersection. These are judgments, not measured BPM.
- **Lyrics/scene:** Club celebration, party commands and occasional sensuality; English vocals. `landmark=1` for the act's career-defining debut.
- **Similarity, not provenance:** Black Box, S'Express and M/A/R/R/S are editorial sounds-like references. Marshall Jefferson is a **sample source**, not a documented album artistic influence, and does not go in `influenced_by`.

## 2. Trip on This! The Remixes (1990)

- **Identity:** A distinct 1990 remix album, not a second studio album. The Telstar, BCM and US issues differ in track sequence/length. MusicBrainz documents Shep Pettibone, David Morales, The Dust Brothers, Bernard Sumner and Todd Terry remixes by name; original album producer is Jo Bogaert. `landmark=0` means not a defining studio album, but inclusion is justified by major documented remix collaborations.
- **Genre:** AllMusic applies club/dance and dance-pop to the whole release, BCM original issue electronic and Euro House. House and hip-house are attached to remix versions of the original recordings, not used to retroactively label every extra/alternate track. Six distinct genre tokens, not duplicate near-synonyms.
- **Instrumentation:** Album credit listing explicitly names keyboards (Merv De Peyer), percussion (David Morales), programming (several remixers), while the reworked base recordings contain documented original synthesizer and programmed drum timbres. Therefore `keyboards|percussion|synth|drummachine`; this does not imply every new remix recorded those sources anew.
- **Production:** `remixing|programming`; some credits for edits, reconstruction and post-production are not falsely translated to `digitalediting` or to a specific device. The column `producers` contains Jo Bogaert plus **only people with an explicit Producer credit in AllMusic**, not all remixers. Production credit is track-scoped for everyone after Jo; see the source CSV. Shep Pettibone and Todd Terry appear in the source notes as **remixers only** and are not included as principal producers.
- **Scores:** energy 4, electronic 5, groove 5, tempo 4, mood 4; 3 for experimental (radical club reconstructions relative to a conventional pop album); polish 4 because of polished professional remixes. Confidence medium, as versions differ and precise production credits vary across territories.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Retained MC Eric and Ya Kid K raps as well as sung elements, mostly party/sensuality English lyrics. Original Felly video credit is not transferred to actual source recordings without track-by-track verification.
- **Comparisons:** Black Box, S'Express and Inner City are similarity-only, not documented influences.

## 3. Body to Body (1991)

- **Identity:** The second studio album, NOT *Trip on This!*; first issued in 1991. Editions differ between nine-track LP and eleven-track CD. Current Fandome does not list this record.
- **Genres:** Belgian original pressings label Electronic, House, Euro House, Hip-House and New Beat. DJ centrum describes electronic dance-pop directly for this album. `eurohouse|house` lead due to sustained club emphasis; hiphouse applies particularly to Colt 45's featured rap tracks. New Beat is a lower-weight historical/track-level classification.
- **Instruments (important evidence gap):** The original 1991 packaging credits **programming**, not a named synthesizer or drum machine. A track-focused retailer write-up documents **programmed drums** and sampled voice textures; hence the only directly supported played/constructed sound under `instruments` is `drums` (programmed percussion). **Do not automatically add synthesizer, a sampler model, TR-909, or a real bass guitar** solely from the Euro-house genre, bassline descriptions or another album's equipment. This is a thin instrument list after additional research; preserve the limitation for future primary-credit checking. Production `programming|drumprogramming|sampling` is independently supported by liner metadata and samples on Voices/Yeh-Yeah.
- **Personnel:** Réjane 'Reggie' Magloire sings most vocals, with Riv and rapper Colt 45 on other tracks. Jo Bogaert is overall producer/programmer; Patrick De Meyer is an explicit producer on various tracks; Lucien Foort and Oliver Abbeloos are producers only on the title track on one LP pressing. Other programmed/re-mixed credits should not be promoted to full album production.
- **Ratings:** energetic Euro-house groove supports 4 energy/tempo, 5 electronic/groove; 4 mood from exuberant club presentation; 2 theatrical, 2 experimental, 3 polish are whole-record editorial readings. Confidence medium due to sparse professional reviews and exact instruments.
- **Lyrics:** party and sensuality lead, social at the tail from Money Makes the World Go Round (title alone is weaker; remove if a lyric check disputes this).
- **Sounds-like:** Black Box, Snap! and 2 Unlimited are comparisons only. Quadrophonia is a track collaborator and must not appear as a `sounds_like` or `influenced_by` entry; leave the collaborator in research notes. No verified musical influences.

## 4. Recall (1995)

- **Genre:** Jo Bogaert's interview associated with the original Japanese CD describes an explicit intention to make Belgian-influenced **Euro house**; specialist discography places the album in mid-1990s **Eurodance**. AllMusic tags Electronic, Dance-Pop and Club/Dance. Belgian new beat is only an antecedent texture, not dominant new 1995 music.
- **Instruments/technique:** AllMusic explicitly credits synthesizer to Jo Bogaert, Patrick De Meyer and Yannick Fonderie; booklet identifies Bogaert and De Meyer as programmers. `instruments=synth`, `production=programming` only; no specific sampler, drum machine or software asserted.
- **Credits disagreement:** AllMusic records Phil Wilde and Peter Bauwens as 'Producer', but original CD credits state **additional production and mixing on selected tracks**. Under Fandome rules, include **Jo Bogaert and Patrick De Meyer** as primary producers; no Phil Wilde / Peter Bauwens in producers column. The original Japanese-CD interview is a **collector translation**, not a verified official transcript; producer intent is considered medium-strength evidence and corroborated against genre/credit databases.
- **Voices:** Ya Kid K, Black Diamond and Daisy Dee; AllMusic per-track credits and original booklet transcriptions differ somewhat on exact tracks. Do not claim Ya Kid K fronts the entire album. MusicBrainz lists three instrumentals, useful for lyrics and score interpretation.
- **Scores:** danceable driving Euro-house 4 energy/tempo; highly electronic production and club beats 5 electronic/groove; sleek production 4 polish; brighter/energetic songs 4 mood; conventional 1995 Eurodance arranging 1 experimental and 2 theatrical.
- **Comparisons:** 2 Unlimited, Culture Beat and Haddaway are editorial musical comparisons, not artist-stated influences. The AllMusic user review shares some similar artists, but user reviews are not treated as professional evidence.

## Disagreements and abstentions

- New token `newbeat` is absent from the October 28 branch controlled genre list; ask Claude to add it and its family mapping, rather than collapsing it into `house` or `techno`.
- The 1990 remix set is counted once and distinguished from 1991 *The Best Remixes* and unrelated country collections. Its `producers` list is track-specific and should be rechecked by the importer if using the precise pressings.
- The 1991 album has no fully attributed instrument model from primary credits; do not invent one to make the row appear fuller.
- No verified named artist or producer directly claims **artist-wide musical influence** in the inspected interview evidence. Labels such as sampled tracks, collaborators and references to style/scene are not sufficient. The `influenced_by` column is consequently intentionally empty.
- Felly lip-syncing vs vocal credits on specific remix editions require edition-by-edition separation; no wholesale replacement of Ya Kid K's recording credit.
