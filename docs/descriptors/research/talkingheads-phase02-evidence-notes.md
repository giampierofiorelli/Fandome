# Talking Heads — Phase 02 / evidence notes

Cutoff: 27 October 2026. Scope is **original studio-album recordings**, not any live rendition, soundtrack compilation, 2005 remaster bonus material, or the 2018 film-soundtrack expansion. One phase 02 row per album. Ratings are **editorial interpretations of the guide’s eight 0–5 scales**, not externally reported measurements. The CSV deliberately leaves `influenced_by` empty for all four: no verified, record-specific claim identifies another musician as a direct influence; a reviewer’s comparison and a guest appearance are insufficient.

## Speaking in Tongues (1983) — `talkingheads-speakingintongues`

**Genres:** Pitchfork documents art-rock, pop, new-wave synthesis, funk and Afrobeat-inspired grooves; AllMusic gives the album-wide dance-rock classification. Afrobeat is marked a *texture*, gospel and blues only *some tracks*: William Ruhlmann identifies the call-and-response on “Slippery People” and the blues styling of “Swamp.” Formal AllMusic style tags are in the genre evidence file; they are secondary taxonomy labels and do not claim that every song is punk.

**Scales (energy/electronic/mood/theatrical/experimental/polish/tempo/groove): `4 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 5`.** Dance-oriented rhythmic force, repeated bass patterns and synths raise energy, electronic and groove. Mechanical precision and freer vocal behaviour coexist; tender “This Must Be the Place” prevents a uniform bright/dark mood value. Pitchfork and AllMusic substantiate these judgements.

**Voice / lyrics:** Byrne’s nervous, hoarse, semi-spoken delivery is described by Pitchfork; songs mix absurdity, selfhood and intimate affection. `love` is justified mainly by the closing song, rather than treated as the universal topic.

**Instruments:** Core guitar, bass, drums and keyboards from 1983 sleeve and MusicBrainz; synthesizers by Wally Badarou and Bernie Worrell, percussion by Steve Scales/Raphael DeJesus, violin by L. Shankar, and saxophone by Richard Landry appear on specific cuts. These are *not* all playing throughout. **Production `overdubbing`:** MusicBrainz credits Alex Sadkin as handling final overdubs and mixing; source neither warrants `sampling` nor a named drum machine. Original producer: **Talking Heads**. Qobuz's cataloguing repeatedly lists Lance Quinn as producer on tracks, unlike original sleeve and MusicBrainz; conservatively use band-only and flag inconsistency for Claude to check. Brian Eno did not produce this album. No record-specific artist `influenced_by` identified; Tom Tom Club belongs in `sounds_like` because a Pitchfork comparison is not evidence of a direct named influence.

Sources: [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-speaking-in-tongues/), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/speaking-in-tongues-mr0003118099), [MusicBrainz original credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/4835c737-9700-46fb-827d-1e6b51c43cca), [sleeve transcription](https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/speaking.html).

## Little Creatures (1985) — `talkingheads-littlecreatures`

**Genres:** Pitchfork reports a markedly more accessible pop-rock sound with country-western steel on “Creatures of Love”, hand drums, traces of funk/Afrobeat and the gospel ensemble on “Road to Nowhere”. These styles are tagged *some tracks* or *texture* rather than indiscriminately whole-album. AllMusic supplies New Wave, Dance-Rock, Post-Punk and other broad style names.

**Scales: `3 / 2 / 4 / 2 / 2 / 4 / 3 / 3`.** Warmer, melodic arrangements, comfortable dynamics and intimacy justify brighter mood and lower experimental/theatrical scores. Some synth bass and key parts persist, but guitar/bass/drums remain central. Carefully controlled studio sound is nevertheless not equal to late-1980s maximal pop.

**Instruments:** Original credits (MusicBrainz) identify Byrne's guitar, Weymouth's electric bass/synth bass, Frantz drums, Harrison keyboards, Steve Scales percussion, Eric Weissberg pedal steel, Lenny Pickett sax, Jimmy MacDonell accordion and Andrew Cader **washboard** (the latter only on “Road to Nowhere”). Hammond organ in the final recording was confirmed by engineer Thorngren. `washboard` is a **new instrument token** for Claude, not `percussion` silently substituted. A choir is vocal support, not `voice=choral` for Byrne’s lead.

**Production `analogtape|onetake|overdubbing|layeredvocals|noisegating`:** Eric Thorngren describes a **24-track Studer A800 analogue tape** setup, complete rhythm-band takes without assembling parts from different takes, overdubs including accordion and singers, **doubled vocal harmony** on “Road to Nowhere” and the specific **Kepex II noise gate** in his bass-drum chain. This does not mean every element on the album was one-take, and `onetake` refers to rhythm takes, not to final mixed songs. Harrison's **DX7** performed a guide accordion part that was *replaced* by real accordion for “Road to Nowhere”; do **not** list `dx7` as that track's finished arrangement. The same engineer lists an Emulator and DX7 in the studio, but presence alone is not proof of final audible contribution on an identified album track; neither model is included in `production`. Original producer: **Talking Heads**, not engineer Eric Thorngren. Disputed origin of “Road to Nowhere” choral intro (Byrne versus Thorngren) is noted in Sound on Sound: it does not affect the audio credit.

Sources: [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-little-creatures/), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/7a2ffc38-6d31-4849-8cdd-5fa3bb98f5f6), [Thorngren first-person technical account](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-talking-heads-road-nowhere).

## True Stories (1986) — `talkingheads-truestories`

**Release/format:** This is the **nine-track Talking Heads studio album** recorded by the group and sung chiefly by David Byrne, associated with the 1986 film. It is **not** the multi-performer film soundtrack nor the expanded 2018 soundtrack release. Therefore `type=studio`, not `soundtrack` or `compilation`. Original 1986 album *not currently in Fandome's catalog*; add exact row from audit before validating.

**Genres:** Apple Music explicitly describes the record as an exploration of gospel, soul, **New Orleans R&B**, **Tex-Mex** and especially country. Preserve `neworleansrnb` and `texmex` as new literal tokens (regional stylistic elements; some tracks only). AllMusic supplies dance-rock/new wave/post-punk and broader database styles. These labels should not be conflated with purely cinematic `soundtrack` genre.

**Scales: `3 / 1 / 3 / 3 / 2 / 4 / 3 / 3`.** Guitar, bass and live drums lead, with fewer clearly documented synthesizers than the band's early-1980s peak. Bright, melodic songs meet satirical or ambiguous lyrics, hence mixed mood. The theatrical score reflects film-derived characters, voices and gospel/choir scenes, not that every track is musical theatre. Commercial polish is evident without suggesting electronic maximalism.

**Instruments:** Byrne guitar, Weymouth bass, Frantz drums, Harrison guitar/keyboards, Paulinho da Costa percussion, Steve Jordan accordion on “Radio Head”, Tommy Morrell pedal steel on “People Like Us” / “City of Dreams” and Tommy Canfield **fiddle** on “People Like Us”; choirs on “Puzzlin’ Evidence” and “Hey Now” (original-sleeve transcription). `fiddle` new literal instrument token, deliberately not replaced with generic violin. **Production `overdubbing|onetake`:** original sleeve credits E. T. Thorngren as *overdub engineer*; Thorngren separately recalls tracking *True Stories* while finishing the prior album and keeping single complete takes, including guide vocal takes. Do not transfer 1985's complete studio machine list automatically to 1986. Producer credited **Talking Heads**. AllMusic's release page labels some formats “Soundtrack,” a distributor classification; recording is still an independent band studio album. Reception mixed: AllMusic critic Michael Hastings is dismissive; Apple Music regards stylistic diversity as the point. Confidence medium for subjective scales because these readings differ.

Sources: [Apple Music original album editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/true-stories/1644461295), [original sleeve credits](https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/true.html), [MusicBrainz credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/472165e0-8d28-42d8-a9d9-348cf1b91bf6), [Qobuz track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/true-stories-talking-heads/jhpk0vw059jnb).

## Naked (1988) — `talkingheads-naked`

**Genres:** African-oriented and Latin-oriented rhythmic idioms are documented in Apple Music's original-album commentary; AllMusic notes the split between a funky/danceable first side and darker second half. The *worldbeat*, *art pop* and *Latin funk* album labels appear in discographic secondary sources; `latinfunk` is new and is labelled *some tracks*. `afrobeat` describes identifiable influence/texture, not a uniform Fela Kuti composition style. Contemporary US rock/new-wave labels are in AllMusic's album metadata and should be seen as cataloguing taxonomy rather than per-track instrumentation.

**Scales: `4 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 3 / 4`.** Organic ensemble with elaborate Afro-Latin percussion and brass, not an electronics-led album. Dense syncopation supplies groove and energy. Darker political/identity-driven latter half lowers mood; expansive international ensemble and changing arrangements increase experimental and polish compared with the more direct albums.

**Instruments:** MusicBrainz track credits and the original sleeve list two guitarists besides Byrne (Yves N'Djock and Johnny Marr), drums/bass, horns (trumpet, sax, trombone, flugelhorn), percussion, Hammond, accordion (James Fearnley/Philippe Servain), Mory Kanté's actual **kora**, Arthur Russell cello, Eric Weissberg pedal steel/dobro, Phil Bodner English horn and Don Brooks harmonica. **Kora is a new instrument token**; do not replace it with harp just to pass current validator. These guests are performers, **not** credited album-wide influences. Credits sometimes render the English horn as *cor anglais* (same physical instrument); use existing `englishhorn` token.

**Production `hornarrangements|overdubbing`:** credited arrangers Lenny Pickett, Angel Fernandez and Jerry Harrison and overdub engineer Fernando Kral. Primary producers **Steve Lillywhite and Talking Heads**. Do not promote engineer Richard Manwaring, mixer Mark Wallis, Fernando Kral, or guest Angel Fernandez to producer. Album recorded in Paris at Studios Davout with vocals overdubbed in New York at Sigma Sound; neither sampling nor a specific drum machine is verified for the actual album. Documentary critics disagree: AllMusic sees an uneven, serious return to *Remain in Light*, while Connolly praises the musical reach; preserve that disagreement without an unsupported blanket five-star claim.

Sources: [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/naked/1644408228), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/naked-mw0000194659), [original credits](https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/naked.html), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/09706d10-ab53-4aff-9420-3222d13675c8).

## Classification and catalog cautions

- Existing New Wave/Post-Punk/College Rock/Punk–New Wave tags on older releases can be retrospective; genre evidence scope records their album-level metadata provenance, whereas instrumental textures refer to specific songs.
- `sounds_like` is editorial resemblance only. `influenced_by` must remain blank until a singer, producer or qualified album-specific critic names a musician as an actual influence. “Ray Barretto” is the bandleader whose salsa ensembles Angel Fernandez played in; **do not** infer Ray Barretto influenced this album merely from that fact.
- The `notes` field is a fresh description (15 words maximum), not copied review language. Values unknown or only inferable from generic band style have been omitted rather than fabricated.
