# Tupac Shakur / 2Pac — Fandome phase 01 evidence notes

Research date: 29 October 2026. Four draft rows only. Evidence keys refer to `tupac_phase01_sources.csv`. The 27-column Fandome scale is an **editorial album-level score**, not measured technical values. Album-level genre claims distinguish entire LP from single-track textures.

## tupac-2pacalypsenow — 2Pacalypse Now (1991)

- **Catalogue:** Original release **12 November 1991**. Fandome `tupac` already has this title. `allmusic_debut`, `interview_daveyd`.
- **Genre order:** AllMusic specifies West Coast, political, hardcore and gangsta rap; the album is hip-hop. `conscioushiphop` is a genre named by a specialist album taxonomy (`mlinusson_conscious`) and is supported by *Brenda's Got a Baby*, *Trapped* and Tupac's description of poverty, police brutality and teen pregnancy in his 1991 interview. `allmusic_debut`, `interview_daveyd`.
- **Scores:** 4 energy for confrontational delivery; 3 electronic for synthesized/key-based accompaniment and sampled backing; 1 mood for overwhelmingly dark social portraits; 3 theatrical for narrative character scenes and spoken interludes; 2 experimental for conventional hip-hop song forms with unusually stark topical specificity; 2 polish for early abrasive, less glossy mix; 3 tempo and 3 groove based on varied midtempo funk-derived beats. AllMusic review and primary interview underpin these judgments.
- **Voice and instruments:** lead aggressive rap, spoken/narrative elements; *The Piano Man* explicitly credited to **keyboards** by AllMusic. No drum machine is named in direct debut-album session credits consulted, so `drummachine` is **omitted** even if some sampled/beat-made material sounds electronically rhythmic.
- **Production:** sampling and groove construction heard in original recordings; no named sampler make/model or tape method verified. The artist's own first-person 1991 interview credits himself with co-production alongside Shock G/Underground Railroad. Atron Gregory is **executive producer** and therefore is NOT in producer field. The album contains group production credits overlapping individual producer names; retain distinctions only as original sleeve states them.
- **Themes:** systemic racial injustice, poverty, police conduct, teen pregnancy, Black youth identity, stories of survival. Documented by `interview_daveyd`, `history_wapo`. Producer list corroborated by AllMusic; "Jeremy" is listed as a producer in that database with no stable full identity. `influenced_by` left blank: political relatives and featured collaborators aren't proof of a named musician influencing composition.
- **Similarity:** Public Enemy and Ice Cube are editorial sonic parallels, NOT cited by Tupac as album influences.

## tupac-strictly4myniggaz — Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z. (1993)

- **Catalogue:** Correct original release **16 February 1993**, not a later reissue; currently **missing** from `catalog.ts`; add `tupac-strictly4myniggaz` before validation. `allmusic_strict`, `goodoldayz_strict`.
- **Genres:** hip-hop, West Coast, hardcore and gangsta styles are explicitly listed by AllMusic. `politicalhiphop` is explicitly named by the specialist taxonomy and describes protest songs like *Holler If Ya Hear Me*, substantiated by the artist's own track notes; `conscioushiphop` is listed by `mlinusson_conscious`; `poprap` comes from explicitly labelled retailer metadata (lower confidence, placed last), with *I Get Around* as the main crossover example. Claude should not treat it as source-named genre without additional documentation.
- **Scores:** 4 energy (urgent emphatic protest and fast articulation), 3 electronic (samples, key grooves), 2 mood (anger and bleak accounts alongside brighter hooks), 3 theatrical (personal and social character roles), 2 experimental, 3 polish (large-label beats, critics hear inconsistent middle section), 3 tempo, 4 groove (emphatic funk-derived dance/party cuts). Marisa Brown AllMusic and Jonathan Gold LA Times differ in appraisal; preserve distinction: AllMusic calls it developed, Gold criticizes vocal distinctiveness.
- **Instruments and production directly CREDITED:** *I Get Around* features **The Piano Man** keyboards and DJ Fuze custom **drum programming**, *Papa'z Song* features **Stan Franks guitar**. The *original 1993 sleeve transcription*, `goodoldayz_strict`, is precise. Sampled passages are not live performances by the sampled artists. `drummachine` follows DJ Fuze credited drum programming; a specific machine model is **not identified**. `sampling|drumprogramming`, not conjectural analogue recording.
- **Producers:** source lists Stretch, Underground Railroad, Big D, Bobcat, Live Squad, Special Ed and Akshun, Truman Jefferson, DJ Daryl, LayLaw, D-Flow Production Squad, Big Punisher and 2Pac co-producer. **"Big Punisher" is the credited 1993 name for a producer; do not assume this is later rapper Big Pun**. Atron Gregory was executive-only. `goodoldayz_strict`, `qobuz_strict`, `mb_strict`.
- **Sounds-like:** Contemporary critic Gold names Ice Cube and MC Ren sonic/flow similarity, and AllMusic compares early protest sound to Public Enemy/Bomb Squad. Do not promote such critic comparisons to `influenced_by`. `latimes_strict`, `allmusic_strict`.

## tupac-meagainsttheworld — Me Against the World (1995)

- **Catalogue:** **14 March 1995**, original 15-track Interscope release; already in Fandome. AllMusic and original MusicBrainz distinguish it from reissues. Original 1995 recording was made before prison release, including California and New York sessions.
- **Genres:** AllMusic West Coast rap, gangsta rap, G-funk. `conscioushiphop` is specifically named by Album of the Year and Acclaimed Music; Album of the Year also lists secondary `boombap`, placed last.
- **Scores:** 3 energy, 3 electronic, 1 mood, 2 theatrical, 2 experimental, 3 polish, 2 tempo and 3 groove reflect frequent sombre midtempo confession and soulful, restrained productions (versus outward force of later *All Eyez on Me*). Source: Huey's AllMusic appraisal, original recordings and contemporaneous Vibe interview.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic **Darrell Crooks / Eric Kenya Baker** on guitars, `mb_somany` **The Piano Man** on keyboards for *So Many Tears*; do not call a Stevie Wonder sample a live keyboard credit. `sampling` documented by original recording-level sampled-work attributions, including *So Many Tears*.
- **Producers:** AllMusic original personnel include Easy Mo Bee, Tony Pizarro, Soulshock & Karlin, Shock G, Johnny J, Mike Mosley, Moe Z MD, Sam Bostic, The D-Flizno Production Squad, Funky Drummer Tyler, Jill Rose, Brian Gallow, Master Tee, Jay-B. Check overlapping alias identities and credits before committing; some track versions credit different parties. **Afeni Shakur's executive producer credit does not create a producer entry**.
- **Direct artist influence:** Tupac said in the *Los Angeles Times* (25 October 1995), specifically while discussing **Dear Mama**, that Don McLean's *Vincent* deeply moved him and he wanted to make his songs feel that way. The song *Vincent* debuted on McLean's **American Pie** (1971); thus `Don McLean (American Pie)` is a source-grounded lyric-emotion influence for **one track on this album**, not a claim that the rhythm or sampled music imitates McLean. The Vibe interview says he feels close to Marvin Gaye, which is only affinity, so Marvin is **not** promoted into `influenced_by` without direct compositional evidence.
- **Similarities:** Scarface (*The Diary*) (dark introspection) and Nas (*Illmatic*) (first-person city narratives) editorial judgment only, not verified influence.

## tupac-alleyzonme — All Eyez on Me (1996)

- **Catalogue and form:** Original **13 February 1996** two-disc, 27-song Death Row album. No later remaster or bonus tracks. **California Love occurs as a remix** on the original release; original single version is not substituted. `allmusic_alleyez`, `pitchfork_alleyez`, `presskit_alleyez`.
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly lists G-funk, West Coast, gangsta, hardcore. `poprap` is explicitly a release-group genre tag on MusicBrainz. `rnb` is a tail-ranked track-level texture inferred from K-Ci & JoJo and other sung hooks; it is not tagged as an album-wide genre.
- **Scores:** 4 energy (hard-driving delivery), 4 electronic (producer-made drum machines, sampled/synthesized leads), 2 mood (aggression/paranoia offset by upbeat celebratory material), 4 theatrical (dramatic showmanship and broad double-album scope), 2 experimental (mostly conventional rap/funk structures), 4 polish (glossy studio mixes), 3 tempo and 5 groove (sustained danceable funk loops and propulsive rhythms). `pitchfork_alleyez` and AllMusic offer distinct critical perspectives on sprawl and focus.
- **Instruments and production directly attested:** Qobuz/AllMusic and MusicBrainz have **Sean Barney Thomas keyboards**, **Carl Butch Small percussion**, **Roger Troutman talkbox** on *California Love* (remix), and producer Mike Mosley recalls **Johnny J drum machine + keyboards** on these sessions. These are **song-level** facts, not a claim about 27 tracks. `sampling|drumprogramming|talkbox` is documented. `onetake` is **omitted** because evidence is limited to particular session anecdotes, not a reliable album-wide production practice. `mb_alleyez`, `mike_mosley_interview`, `all_eyez_making`.
- **Producers:** co-producer 2Pac, Johnny J, Daz Dillinger, Dr Dre, DJ Quik, DeVante Swing, DJ Pooh, Bobcat Ervin, Mike Mosley, Rick Rock, Doug Rasheed, QD3, LayLaw, David Blake (Dr Dre credit on both *California Love* and *Can't C Me*). **Suge Knight = executive producer only**; exclude as actual producer. Dave Aron **engineer/mixer only**, excluded even though SiriusXM lists him among broad credits. `allmusic_alleyez`, `qobuz_alleyez`, `mb_alleyez`, `djquik_interview`.
- **Themes:** prison freedom, paranoia, relationships, fame, sex, community, feud and identity. Critical writer Paul A. Thompson notes the record retains politics even when song themes are party/sex-oriented. No influence inferred from samples or guest performers; editorial `sounds_like` identifies fellow Death Row G-funk productions.

## Source disagreements and boundaries

- **Original catalogue missing 1993 album:** add first, or native validator flags the unfamiliar album ID.
- **Source-derived genres and confidence:** `conscioushiphop` is explicitly tagged for the 1991 and 1993 records by a specialty genre index and for the 1995 record by Album of the Year / Acclaimed Music. `poprap` is a low-confidence retail genre on the 1993 record but a MusicBrainz release-group tag for the 1996 record. `rnb` on 1996 denotes guest-vocal track texture only. The wording and evidence strengths are documented; do not imply all tracks share every genre.
- **Personnel:** engineering, executive production, featured guests and sample original performers must NOT be inserted in `producers` or `influenced_by` as shortcuts. Dr Dre as 1995 sampling/recording credit is NOT a 1995 album producer by default.
- **Makaveli** belongs in next phase, not omitted because its original release postdates Tupac's death. Other posthumous rebuilt albums require a separate eligibility decision before importing as original studio records.
