# Enigma — Phase 01: album-specific evidence and disagreement

**Approach:** describe original *studio* albums, not remix bonus discs. Scales are editorial **0–5 whole-album judgments**, compared to the anchors in Fandome's `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`. Token inclusion is narrower for instrument and production: explicitly named record-specific evidence only. A blank `influenced_by` is intentional unless a named influence is attributable to Cretu or qualified critical analysis.

## 1. MCMXC a.D. (1990) — `enigma-mcmxcad`

**Genre ranking:** AllMusic explicitly labels new age/electronic, with ambient, ethnic fusion and club/dance styles. 1990 sleeve-derived metadata supports downtempo. The official artist page supplies hip-hop rhythmic texture; critic Ned Raggett mentions Euro-disco crossover and the introductory Gregorian chants. A more general history characterizes the debut as worldbeat/pop crossover. Hip-hop and Euro-disco are intentionally later **textures** because this is not chiefly a rap or disco LP. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000320825) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/mcmxc-ad/) · [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/mcmxc-a-d).

**Scales:** energy 2 (laid-back with beat-driven peaks in “Sadeness”/“Mea Culpa”); electronic 4 (synth chordal beds and sampled historic performances coexist); mood 2 (sacred/sensual, hushed, often mysterious); theatrical 3 (ceremonial chant and suite concept, moderated by whispery vocal delivery); experimental 3 (unusual in context as a fusion, pop-accessible in structure); polish 4 (arranged, smoothed studio collage); tempo 2 (generally relaxed); groove 3 (club rhythmic core but ample slow soundscapes). These are judgments, not quoted numerical scores. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000320825) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/mcmxc-ad/).

**Voice:** soft (Sandra's breathy French voice), spoken (opening introduction and whisper-voice), choral (sampled Gregorian choir); soprano Maria Callas is sampled on “Callas Went Away”, but this is a localized performance, not representative of main voice typology. **Instruments:** `synth` only; Raggett directly specifies synths. Synthetic flute sonorities do **not** justify a credited real flute or a hardware model. **Production:** `sampling` is demonstrable through Gregorian and Callas excerpts. Do not claim named drum machines, heavy reverb parameters, or `analogtape` without album-specific sources. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000320825) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/mcmxc-ad/).

**Lyrics/languages:** religion, ritual, guilt and sensuality appear in the original album narrative. French spoken/sung phrases and English framing plus Latin chant (`fr|en|la`) make more sense than assuming one English lead language. For `producers`, 1990 sleeve Enigma group credit is resolved by official page and Qobuz identifying Michael Cretu, but the alias must be retained in metadata comment. **Influenced by:** Cretu himself invokes **Yes** and **Pink Floyd** as models for connected album sides and segues. He does *not* claim their timbres or synthesizers. **Landmark 1:** defining debut and global crossover. [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/mcmxc-ad/) · [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/ca-en/album/mcmxc-ad-enigma/0724381045459).

**Critical disagreement:** Raggett admires the hybrid's mass-cultural provocation while criticizing limited harmonic creativity, and the album's Gregorian sample clearance caused legal action. Separate popularity/originality from artistic endorsement, and do not give Gregorian performers an unsubstantiated 'influence' credit. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000320825).

## 2. The Cross of Changes (1993) — `enigma-thecrossofchanges`

**Genre ranking:** AllMusic gives new age/electronic with ethnic fusion and club/dance. Release-format style metadata adds downtempo and ambient. Official Cretu text explains world vocal sources set against sampled **1970s rock loops**, which justifies `worldbeat` for the full album and `rock` at the *texture* end of the list. The original album precedes its US 1994 release. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-cross-of-changes-mw0000107958) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-cross-of-changes/) · [Chartsurfer](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/enigma/the-cross-of-changes-album_fruncn.html).

**Scales:** energy 3 (stronger rhythm and guitars than debut); electronic 4 (loop/programmed foundations with guitar and human-voice recordings); mood 3 (the urgency of some tracks contrasts with affirmative/meditative moments); theatrical 3 (sweeping chants and dramatic guitar peaks); experimental 3 (new sampling sources with accessible song shapes); polish 4 (controlled Ibiza studio programming); tempo 3 (medium tempo, dance-motivated beats); groove 3 (marked beat patterns with many atmospheric interludes). [Raggett's review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/the-cross-of-changes-mr0005036205).

**Voice:** clear (male lead Angel X on “Return to Innocence” plus selected singers), soft (Sandra), spoken (Louisa Stanley). Crucial Ami vocal sample by Kuo Ying-nau and Kuo Hsin-chu is a traditional performance rather than Enigma-written material. It does not yield a two-letter language code: the Amis language typically uses ISO 639-3 `ami`; preserve in explanatory notes rather than forcing the validator. **Instruments:** specifically `electricguitar`, confirmed by Raggett and the documented guitarists Jens Gad and Peter Cornelius. Electric type comes from *review*, not inferred from the credits alone. No asserted model of keyboard or sampler. **Production:** `sampling`, documented by original liner notes for *Songs from the Victorious City*, *The Calling*, *The Crossing* and Taiwanese chant; critic also identifies Plant and Bonham source fragments. [Sleeve transcription](https://www.enigmamusica.com/the-cross-of-changes/) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/the-cross-of-changes-mr0005036205).

**Lyrics and affect:** life cycles, the sacred, self-reflection and universal love are described by official material; `identity` pertains especially to the return-to-innocence motif and so is lower-weight than `spiritual`. **Producer:** sole credited Michael Cretu; the guitarists are *not* co-producers. **Influenced by:** blank. Samples of Led Zeppelin and Anne Dudley/Jaz Coleman are documented musical content; Cretu did not establish those as consciously acknowledged artist influences for this record. **Landmark 1:** includes “Return to Innocence” and extends the defining crossover approach. [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-cross-of-changes/) · [Credits](https://www.enigmamusica.com/the-cross-of-changes/).

**Critical disagreement:** Raggett argues the second record's loop construction advances the grooves; he simultaneously notes it reuses the earlier successful concept. Cretu emphasizes active change to the recipe. Both can be true: method evolves without abandoning core template. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/the-cross-of-changes-mr0005036205) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-cross-of-changes/).

## 3. Le Roi Est Mort, Vive Le Roi! (1996) — `enigma-leroiestmortviveleroi`

**Genre ranking:** AllMusic: new age, electronic; ambient, ethnic fusion, club/dance. Official synopsis specifically says it combines Gregorian and worldbeat predecessors. Downtempo, ambient pop and trance come from **storefront/community tagging**, not multiple independent critics; later genres reflect weaker or song-level evidence and should be reviewed before affecting recommendations. Broad `pop` comes from Qobuz's album listing, not a claim of synthpop or electropop. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000425689) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi/) · [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/6e58ca05-c84f-38b8-b71d-5ba1296b1049) · [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/fr-fr/album/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi-enigma/0724384206550).

**Scales:** energy 2 (the record's slower and more reflective tone); electronic 4 (electronically assembled backgrounds containing sampled world vocals); mood 2 (pondering internal conflict, meaning and invisibility); theatrical 3 (ritual atmosphere, largely not bombastic); experimental 2 (returns to established Enigma formula); polish 4 (dense smooth studio mixes); tempo 2 (laid-back overall); groove 2 (some club propulsion but not a sustained dance record). [Anderson](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000425689) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi/).

**Voice:** clear/soft Michael Cretu and Sandra; spoken Louisa Stanley; sampled choral Gregorian passages. The original release credits name the women and three sampled sources (Pierre Kaelin, Rasa Ensemble Riga, and Mongolian performers). The Sanskrit chanting of Dr Bhagya Murthy on “The Child in Us” is discussed in a secondary sleeve analysis; `sa` retained as a sampled/performed language, not the majority language. **Instruments:** the original sleeve names Peter Cornelius **guitar** on “The Child in Us”, without subtype; hence generic `guitar` from the full current guide, not a guessed acoustic or electric guitar. **Production:** `sampling` from original credit notices; `heavyreverb` is literal Anderson review wording (plenty of reverb), not an instrument or a hardware claim. [Liner notes](https://www.enigmamusica.com/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi/) · [Anderson](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000425689) · [SongSuey](https://songsuey.com/artist/enigma/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi/).

**Lyrics:** existential anxiety, the unknowable and inner battles support introspection, spiritual and identity. **Producer:** Michael Cretu alone; David Fairstein has lyric credits, not production. **Influenced by:** blank; conceptual self-reference to the first two Enigma albums is not a new outside artist influence. **Landmark 0:** historically important studio entry but not as defining as the debut/second/fourth, an editorial prioritization and not a sales claim. [Official album page](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/le-roi-est-mort-vive-le-roi/).

**Critical disagreement:** Cretu presents it as a more integrated, tender step in the project; Rick Anderson argues it recycles aural formulas and devotes too much space to Cretu's own singing. This supports **medium** confidence for interpretation, while credits and sample sources remain strong. [Anderson](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000425689).

## 4. The Screen Behind the Mirror (2000) — `enigma-thescreenbehindthemirror`

**Genre ranking:** AllMusic names new age, ambient, ethnic fusion and reviews world music with dance rhythms. European record metadata labels electronic, ambient and downtempo. `classical` is restricted to a **texture** of sampled and reinterpreted Orff passages, not a recording of a classical performance by Enigma. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-screen-behind-the-mirror-mw0000670919) · [Chartsurfer](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/enigma/the-screen-behind-the-mirror-album_frfrgf.html).

**Scales:** energy 3 (rhythmic singles and atmospheric passages); electronic 4 (electronic backgrounds + prominent sample and guitars); mood 2 (enigmatic/sensual, dramatic or dark classical interjections); theatrical 4 (large Orff choirs and orchestral gestures); experimental 3 (classical sampling central to album architecture); polish 4 (continuous, engineered studio sequencing); tempo 2 (mid- and slow-tempo songs); groove 3 (dance-oriented rhythms in several singles, but reflective interludes dilute full-album danceability). [Phares](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-screen-behind-the-mirror-mw0000670919) · [Cretu](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-screen-behind-the-mirror/).

**Voice:** clear guest leads Ruth-Ann Boyle and Andru Donalds, plus Cretu; soft Sandra passages, spoken Elisabeth Houghton, choir/choral from Orff and other regional voice sources. **Instruments:** Jens Gad and Cretu credited on **guitar**, Gad specifically on **baritone guitar** in AllMusic's album credit table; AllMusic expressly identifies varied ethnic **percussion** and orchestral/choral **sample** presence, not original orchestra sessions. The `orchestra` token here describes audible sampled timbres; importer may omit if the product ontology excludes sample-derived instruments. **Production:** `sampling`, the original sleeve credits portions of Orff's *Carmina Burana*. **No specific named synthesizer model or software** has been proved for this album. [Liner](https://www.enigmamusica.com/the-screen-behind-the-mirror/) · [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r450728/review) · [Review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-screen-behind-the-mirror-mw0000670919).

**Lyrics and languages:** self-definition, convention breaking, concealed desires, the human condition. English main leads and Latin choir material from *O Fortuna* make `en|la`; Latin is a sample language. **Producers:** sole credited producer Cretu. Jens Gad co-wrote songs, performed and engineered; he is **not** credited as co-producer. **Influenced by:** `Carl Orff` based on Cretu's own explanation that *O Fortuna* was an instrumental conception tied to his idea of defying convention, beyond mere incidental borrowing. **Landmark 1:** Orff sampling is this album's defining sonic device, though the review calls its evolution limited. [Official](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-screen-behind-the-mirror/) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-screen-behind-the-mirror-mw0000670919).

**Critical disagreement and release chronology:** some retrospectives call the classical integration sophisticated; others think the Carmina Burana passages overshadow the Enigma material. Heather Phares detects little real formal development. The official site text mentions 1999, but its **release field and track credits** say 2000; European listings and MusicBrainz confirm January **2000**. The 1999 track is pre-album “Gravity of Love”. [uDiscover](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-enigma-the-screen-behind-the-mirror/) · [Artist](https://www.enigmaspace.com/music/the-screen-behind-the-mirror/) · [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9ac6be30-c568-398c-8a34-ac001cb73b53).
