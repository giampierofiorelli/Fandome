# Riccardo Cocciante — Phase 04: per-album evidence and safeguards

Source checking: 30 October 2026, repository `giampierofiorelli/Fandome`, branch `descriptors`. As observed, `docs/descriptors/cocciante.csv` has four phase-01 entries (Mu, Poesia, Anima, L'alba). The current `catalog.ts` has eight album titles through *Cervo a primavera* (1980). Phase 02/03 archives already delivered, **not** imported on that branch: exclude their eight albums from this ZIP.

All eight scales are **editorial estimates** on the documented 0–5 Fandome scale (not externally quoted ratings). Every scale is explained below. Do not infer exact recording methods from generic descriptions, and do not infer instruments from the artist’s reputation. `voice` is `powerful` across this batch only because sources including RAI characterize Cocciante’s recognizable intense / forceful delivery and original credits show lead-vocal performance. No operatic classification is suggested just because he later composed musical theatre.

## 1 — Cocciante (1991), `cocciante-cocciante1991`

**Edition and name:** Virgin VRC 91/261 669, original 10-track 1991 recording, not the 1982 album *Cocciante* and not the 1994 best-of *Il mio nome è Riccardo*. The slug of the 1982 LP has already been assigned `cocciante-cocciante` in delivered phase 03, therefore the **1991 disambiguation suffix is essential**. Sources disagree about exact first-issue date: a 1991 trade source reports March, AllMusic October 18, and Austrian charts October. **Only year 1991 is asserted in the CSV.**

**Producer:** **Patrice Cramer** is the producer on the original 1991 Virgin LP label/sleeve transcription, AllMusic and Sony’s phonogram credits, also recorded/mixed the LP. One *Se stiamo insieme* single pressing also names **Riccardo Cocciante as co-producer for that specific single** (Encyclopédisque), while album and several other single credit repositories list only Patrice. Because the guide asks for the album’s producer, row keeps `Patrice Cramer`; the single-level discrepancy is recorded, with a task for Claude to verify any actual full-album co-credit on the original inner sleeve. **Mogol is lyricist, not producer.** The band credited across tracks includes Basile Leroux (guitar), Evert Verhees (bass), Kirt Rust (drums), Jean-Philippe Rykiel (keyboards), and backing singers Jacques Mercier, Marie Ruggieri, Slim Batteux. Programming credit belongs to Patrice Rouillon; engineer/programmer is not producer.

**Genres:** whole-album accessible `pop`, energetic `poprock`, `songwriter` (cantautore and Mogol texts) and specifically a renewed rock/blues interest from Rockol; `blues` and `jazz` pushed after broader genres because critical descriptions concern *Jimi suona*, *Energia* and *1ª gita scolastica nel mondo del jazz, del blues e del rock*. The leading Sanremo hit supplies slower melodic/ballad material. AllMusic calls the recording Latin Pop as an indexing category; **no Latin-percussion instrumental credit or pervasive Latin rhythm was verified**, so the generic `latin` token is *not* asserted.

**Scales:** energy **3**: rock and blues pieces raised intensity, Sanremo ballad tempers it. Electronic **2**: programmed sections/keyboards alongside live drums/bass/guitar, not synth-dominated. Mood **3**: spirited rock and humorous school-excursion versus romantic yearning. Theatrical **4**: vigorous Cocciante vocal delivery and heightened ballad dynamics. Experimental **2**: jazz/blues/rock self-examination in largely conventional song forms. Polish **4**: major-label recording/mixing with full session band and programmer. Tempo **3**: driving rock passages alongside slow ballads. Groove **2**: rhythmically animated band, not a dance-targeted LP.

**Landmark = 1** for *Se stiamo insieme*’s Sanremo win, broad album chart recognition and the documented musical change (RAI/Rockol). **Influenced_by:** Cocciante directly describes revisiting rock music he loved at the start of his career, naming Otis Redding and then Jimi Hendrix. The names represent his *stated musical roots being revisited in this album*, **not** a track-specific guitar soundalike. If Claude decides the connection is too indirect, delete rather than infer additional influences. *Jimi suona*’s title alone would not qualify. **Sounds_like remains blank:** no critic explicitly compares the album sound to a named outside record. Paola Turci and guests are collaborators, not influences.

## 2 — Eventi e mutamenti (1993), `cocciante-eventiemutamenti`

**Edition:** original 10-track Virgin 8 39028 1/2, 1993 (Italian original). AllMusic metadata gives an October date; original chart entry was already registered in late August 1993 by HitParadeItalia. **Record year 1993 only; precise release day not established.** Recorded at Studio Forum Roma and Studio Mega Paris per AllMusic.

**Producer and arrangement:** The 1993 original CD sleeve transcribed by Recordsale directly credits **Riccardo Cocciante as producer/arranger** and names Paolo Carta (guitars), Francesco Puglisi (bass), Maurizio Dei Lazzaretti (drums), backing vocalist Alain Pewzner and Gruppo Corale Aquilano. Sony/Qobuz separately credits **Cocciante as producer** and **Leonardo De Amicis as arranger** on several songs; **do not automatically add De Amicis as producer**, which is the key distinction from the 1994/1997 records. Daniel DeShaime and Daniel Lavoie wrote *Amarsi come prima* and are not automatically producers or influences. Erminio Sinni wrote album material and later worked with Cocciante; he is neither a co-billed artist nor producer of this album.

**Genres:** RAI specifically characterizes 1993 as mixing *melodico e rock*, so broad `pop|poprock` plus Apple Music's Singer/Songwriter `songwriter` and AllMusic's Italian Pop `italianpop`. These are whole-album labels. Choral production is supported by original sleeve, but **do not invent gospel** merely because a choir participates. No whole-album jazz tag just because songs have lyrical allusions to Lucy/Diamonds.

**Scales:** energy **3**: electric rhythm section and rock side coexist with lyrical songs. Electronic **1**: electric band, but no specific electronic sound generator or programmed part in original accessible credits. Mood **3**: affection, social unease, language/identity themes and hopeful moments. Theatrical **4**: elaborate singing and documented choral feature. Experimental **2**: melodic-rock fusion conventional by 1993; varied literary texts but not avant-garde sound. Polish **4**: two international studios, credited mixed/mastered roles, choir. Tempo **3**: mid/active typical band songs, no track-BPM extrapolation from incorrect identical third-party BPM entries. Groove **2**: live drums and bass but no claim of club/dance focus.

**Landmark = 0**: reached Italian charts yet no independent source calls it career-defining. **Influenced_by and sounds_like blank:** French lyricists, Mogol, Gaio Chiocchio, Marco Luberti, Sinni, and the Gruppo Corale Aquilano are songwriting participants/performers, not automatically influences. **Language it** in this original album, regardless of collaborator French citizenship.

## 3 — Un uomo felice (1994), `cocciante-unuomofelice`

**Edition:** original Italian-language Virgin CD 8 40033 2 (10 tracks). Not the Spanish-language album *Un hombre feliz* (1995), which is an adaptation. And not the compilation *Il mio nome è Riccardo* (1994), which RAI expressly labels a collection. Guest duets occur within Cocciante’s own studio album: **do not introduce a new co-billed artist** for Mina, Mietta, Tosca, Cecilia Gasdia, Scarlett, Bellenis, or Baraonna.

**Producers:** both **Riccardo Cocciante and Leonardo De Amicis** explicitly listed on the original CD metadata (musicbase) and multiple Sony/Qobuz original 1994 song credits. AllMusic also registers both, though a derivative listing may use the pseudonym **Bozo Allegro** for De Amicis; use his civil/professional name, **never duplicate a pseudonym**. The 1994 release has arrangements and programming credited to De Amicis, with original players including Cocciante (piano), De Amicis (piano/keys), Paolo Carta (guitar), Roberto Gallinelli (bass), Maurizio Dei Lazzaretti / Manuela Borzi (drums), Giovanni Imparato (percussion), violin/viola/cello strings, Fabio Forte (trombone), and backing singers. `stringarrangements` derives from credited strings with co-arrangement; not an invented all-album symphony. Qobuz confirms *Sopra un preludio di Bach* original strings. `choirvocals` represents credited backing/ensemble vocals, **not** an uncredited gospel choir. Musical accompaniment by Mina/Mietta et al. is guest vocal involvement, **not** musical influence.

**Genres:** MusicBrainz original-1994 release group specifically registers `ballad|pop|poprock|schlager`; Apple categorizes record as Singer/Songwriter. `schlager` is specialist catalogue tag, not necessarily a whole-album German-language style; keep last among genre labels or ask Claude to remove if Fandome’s concept differs. None of the 10 tracks is an entire jazz LP just because one title is *Nel locale di jazz*. The English-language track **I'd Fly (per lei)** supports `it|en`; all other tracks sung in Italian.

**Scales:** energy **2**: intimate duet/ballad pacing, some full-band peaks. Electronic **2**: original programming/keyboards mixed with piano, strings and live rhythm section. Mood **3**: celebration of love alongside vulnerability. Theatrical **4**: multiple dramatic exchanges among singers and solo romantic crescendos. Experimental **2**: alternating pairings/pieces of diverse lyricist origin are unusual presentation, but traditional arrangements. Polish **4**: original professionally recorded multi-player sound, programmed keys, strings and famous collaborators. Tempo **2**: ballad-led ten tracks. Groove **1**: non-dance emphasis.

**Landmark = 0**: distinctive guest roster, modest chart success, no source specifically establishes album as career-defining. **No songs-like/influenced-by** based on the duets; the musicians are collaborators. `powerful` voice reflects the album’s dramatic duets and the singer’s established forceful vocal style, not opera-trained singing. **Confidence high** on original edition and producer roles.

## 4 — Innamorato (1997), `cocciante-innamorato`

**Edition/chronology:** Columbia COL 485393 2, 12 original 1997 Italian-language tracks. An earlier 1995 French-language *L'instant présent* includes several original bases and compositions, but **Innamorato** has additional tracks and revised versions and is a separately issued original Italian album in 1997. The French work is NOT to be inserted again as a concurrent Italian studio album, nor is 1998 *Istantanea* (live). The song title *Harry Loves Mary* is English but the actual song's verses are predominantly Italian: original record **language `it`**. Do not infer English language solely from the title or from composer/lyricist Luc Plamondon.

**Producer and musicians:** AllMusic lists **Riccardo Cocciante** and **Leonardo De Amicis** as *Artistic Producers*. This is corroborated by Sony/Qobuz **Producer** fields for original recordings *Ti amo ancora di più* and *Innamorato*. These are both in the album field. Beware metadata also calls **Boventoon B.V.** the corporate phonogram producer: it is a rights/legal label, not an additional musician-person producer. AllMusic and Sony confirm Cocciante piano, Paolo Carta/Michael Landau/James Harrah/Michael Thompson/Adriano Martino guitars, Roberto Gallinelli bass, Roberto Polito drums, Rafael Padilla percussion, Leonardo De Amicis and Tony Smith keyboards/programming, credited viola and orchestra conducting, Annie Robert/backing singers. **Do not assert a specific synthesizer model** or orchestral string arrangement when only generic conducting/viola are named.

**Genres:** AllMusic explicitly calls this album Italian Pop and broad Pop/Rock; retained `pop|italianpop|poprock`. The title track is explicitly analyzed as a **pop ballad** by pianist/music educator Alberto Moneti; it is one of 12, thus `ballad` placed fourth. Authorial songwriting by Cocciante and his main literary collaborators yields `songwriter`, supported by RAI's singer-songwriter framing. No Latin music texture is specifically documented even though AllMusic’s **retail shelf** says 'Latin'; token deliberately omitted.

**Scales:** energy **2**: intimate lyric ballads, some fuller electric band pieces. Electronic **2**: programmed keys amidst real bass/drums, acoustic/electric guitars. Mood **2**: yearning love, sensuality and separation/regret. Theatrical **3**: expressive high-register delivery but more intimate than the dramatic 1994 guest duets. Experimental **2**: cross-market translation/adaptation and alternate mixes, mostly familiar pop forms; do not call album a remix or experimental work. Polish **4**: multiple professional studios in Rome, Montreal and Los Angeles, Humberto Gatica mixes and multitrack programming. Tempo **2**: slower ballads dominate. Groove **1**: romantic songs not dance music.

**Landmark = 0:** important last Italian 1990s studio album, but that is chronology, not an independently established landmark. **No named influenced_by/sounds_like:** the link to 1995 French *L'instant présent* reflects reuse of the artist's own recording, not an outside influence; Luc Plamondon is a co-writer, Michael Landau a performer. **Lyrics:** love/heartbreak/nostalgia and sensuality, supported by lyric topics and RAI's album framing.
