# MARINA — Phase 01: evidence and review notes

Research date: 2026-10-26. All four rows are **draft**. Scope: original studio albums as first published, not later extended editions. Sources are identified by URL in `marina_phase01_sources.csv`, and each listed genre has one row in `marina_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Scales are *editorial estimates from album-specific descriptions*, not direct claims made by critics. Do not present these ratings as source quotations.

## marina-thefamilyjewels: The Family Jewels (2010)
- **Identity, genre and scope:** original *Marina and the Diamonds* credit, but Fandome artist is `MARINA`. AOTY describes alt-pop, art-pop, indie-pop, electropop, piano rock, chamber pop. AllMusic specifically assigns *left-field pop*. Drowned in Sound mentions ska and pop-punk *on “Girls”*, so those two go last with `some tracks` evidence. Guardian's contrast with taut new-wave guitars does **not** establish a new-wave classification; the tag is excluded.
- **Energy 4, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 5:** Alexis Petridis describes densely arpeggiated synthesizers, heavy bass/drums and extreme vocal personae; mix of anxious introspection and buoyant sarcasm. This is a theatrical, kinetically busy record, not a uniformly euphoric one.
- **Experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3:** adventurous vocal treatment and eclectic song forms over recognisable hooks; polished production varies from sparse piano to heavy rhythms. Moderately danceable, not a club album.
- **Voice (`clear|powerful|spoken`):** numerous sharp changes in character voice / declamatory delivery (Guardian). `spoken` is an album-level secondary mode, not rap. No `operatic` tag (guide reserves it for classically trained singing).
- **Instruments:** AllMusic and Amoeba credit Marina's piano/keys/organ/glockenspiel, Howe's synth, piano, acoustic/electric guitar, bass, mellotron, mandolin, recorder and jaw-harp, acoustic drums, bowed strings and diverse credited instruments; ordering is our editorial judgment across album. Minor instruments such as spoons are excluded rather than inventing unsupported importance.
- **Production:** `programming` (Liam Howe, Pascal Gabriel, Greg Kurstin and others), `protools` (Dougal Lott credit), `stringarrangements` (Lucy Shaw, Stephen Large), `autotune` (Guardian reports occasional audible Auto-Tune, track limited). No claim of a specific synthesizer model, and Pro Tools is an engineering tool, not proof of its use on every track.
- **Producers:** Album-level credited roles from AllMusic: Liam Howe, Richard “Biff” Stannard, Ash Howes, Pascal Gabriel, Greg Kurstin, Marina Diamandis and Starsmith. Starsmith is credited as producer on the album but some editions indicate *original* production on a specific song; retain with caution. Writers, engineers and mixers are not promoted to producer.
- **Themes:** identity, self-scrutiny, satire of success / social roles, romance. `sounds_like` is subjective editorial resemblance (Sparks, Lene Lovich, Kate Bush, Regina Spektor etc); not a documented collaboration.
- **Influenced_by:** Irish Times explicitly identifies Kate Bush, PJ Harvey and Fiona Apple as influential on Marina's early material; Line of Best Fit recounts her explicit Daniel Johnston reference for *The Family Jewels*. These are source-attributed influences rather than mere sonic parallels.
- **Landmark 1:** defining debut with notable “Hollywood”, “I Am Not a Robot”, “Mowgli's Road”, and “Oh No!”; high confidence for album profile, lower confidence for individual track-only ska/poppunk and some studio methods.

## marina-electraheart: Electra Heart (2012)
- **Scope:** original UK 12 tracks, *not* *Electra Heart: Platinum Blonde Edition* or every later deluxe track. “Radioactive” is explicitly omitted from the original album (NME). This avoids falsely counting remix/bonus producers. 2012 is secure.
- **Genres:** AOTY electropop/dance-pop/alt-pop primary; synthpop and pop rock secondary. Broad AllMusic/Metacritic `pop` and `adultalternativepoprock` exist, but are lower in order than the synthesised production's more specific descriptions. Avoid inferring `dubstep` simply from Pitchfork’s “Skrillex-lite” comparison for “Lies”.
- **Energy 4, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 5:** Pitchfork and Observer identify shimmering programmed bangers alternating with melancholy electronic ballads; conceptual archetype, feminist satire and theatrical character Electra Heart. Emotional darkness persists under glossy music.
- **Experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 4:** mainstream verse/chorus hooks with high-budget production by major-label pop collaborators; some tracks slow and understated. Theatrical concept ≠ high musical experiment score.
- **Voice:** powerful, clear soaring melodies; spoken/character passage in “Homewrecker”. Do not equate editorial comparison to Kate Bush with `operatic` training.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz original-release credits and AllMusic document synth, keyboard, piano, bass, drums, electric guitar, strings, mellotron and bouzouki; no drum machine brand is demonstrably credited.
- **Production:** credited programming, drum programming (Elite has drum producer credit; not counted as full record producer), string arrangements (David Campbell), and credited instrumentation; avoid `layeredvocals` (backing vocals alone do not prove layering techniques) and any claimed `tr808` / `autotune` without engineer corroboration.
- **Producers:** Rick Nowels; Dr. Luke; Cirkut; Greg Kurstin; Liam Howe; Devrim Karaoglu; Dean Reid (credited co-producer); Diplo (co-producer “Lies”). MusicBrainz supports actual producer/co-producer roles. *Stargate, Benny Blanco and Guy Sigsworth* mentioned in advance publicity (NME/VICE) cannot be confirmed as producers on original twelve tracks and are **not** included. `Elite` is credited a *drum producer*, not full album producer; excluded by user rule.
- **Lyrical themes:** personified romantic disappointment, fame, disempowerment, ironic social expectations. Marina calls much of the character an intentional satirical lens. Album closer is more vulnerable.
- **Influenced_by:** the FaceCulture interview names Madonna's *Ray of Light*, Depeche Mode and Britney Spears’ “Toxic” as references for the *musical* sound; the Glamour discussion further identifies Spears’ pop-star narrative. Marilyn Monroe and Marie Antoinette influenced the *visual persona*, not the music and are excluded.
- **Critic disagreement:** Pitchfork treats the pop polish as diluted originality, while other reviewers praise grand hooks. High confidence on production facts; subjective landmark judgment 1 because this is among the most identifiable artist eras.

## marina-froot: Froot (2015)
- **Genre:** AOTY identifies synthpop, pop rock, alt-pop, dance-pop, new wave and electro-disco; HiFi adds indie-pop; Apple Music says new wave and electro-pop. `disco` applies especially to title song and “Blue” by Marina's own interview. AllMusic names a *tango inflection* only in the title song; `tango` at the far end, with track-limited evidence, never a whole-album dance classification.
- **Energy 3, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 4:** mixed bright, rhythmic singles and slow piano/introspective songs. Compared with *Electra Heart*, less synthetic and more band-centered while retaining rhythmic electronic patterns. Theatrical vibrato and theatrical melody persist, but less persona-based staging.
- **Experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3:** ambitious, eclectic arrangements rather than avant-garde structure; pristine but band-performed studio pop. Grooves vary across 12 songs.
- **Instruments:** 2015 release credits identify Jason Cooper on kit, James Ahwai/Jeremy Pritchard on bass, Alex Robertshaw/Keith Bayley/Fyfe Dangerfield on guitars, David Kosten and Marina on keyboards, Fyfe Dangerfield on piano, Kosten on percussion. `synth` additionally appears explicitly in AllMusic's new-wave description. `guitar` generic is retained because MusicBrainz doesn't identify each guitar as electric or acoustic.
- **Production:** `programming` by David Kosten is credited **on tracks**; although Marina says she requested a band-like, live-sounding record, that does not prove all songs recorded simultaneously (`liverecording` intentionally **excluded**). No named model/software used.
- **Producers:** Marina Diamandis and **David Kosten only**, from original-release MusicBrainz track credits, VICE firsthand interview, TIME artist interview and Observer. **Important conflict:** musicOMH's review incorrectly attributes the album to Greg Kurstin. Greg Kurstin was active on the two earlier albums, **not** credited as producer on the original *Froot* tracks. Explicitly do not import his name for this record.
- **Influenced_by:** Marina expressly mentions The Distillers (Brody Dalle), Shirley Manson/Garbage as reasons she wanted a rougher sound, plus PJ Harvey / Patti Smith as models for treating a songwriter-driven recording like a band. This is production-method influence rather than claim of a direct melodic or compositional borrowing.
- **Themes:** personal recovery, self-direction, grief, attraction, sex and social critique; “Savages” comments on violence. Landmark 1 for her self-written / co-produced artistic consolidation.
- **Critic disagreement:** Guardian describes stylistic seams, Pitchfork admires intimacy but questions some songs; AllMusic emphasizes unity and maturity. Score is a composite—not a critical consensus score.

## marina-lovefear: Love + Fear (2019)
- **Credit:** released simply as **MARINA**; this is a single 16-track studio album divided into LOVE and FEAR, *not* two different catalogue albums. The track “Baby” has Clean Bandit and Luis Fonsi guest billing; that does not make the whole album a co-billed collaboration.
- **Genres:** AOTY names electropop/synthpop, dance-pop and alt-pop; AllMusic classifies pop and Adult Alternative Pop/Rock; ADM classifies indie pop. `tropicalhouse` / `afrobeats` are secondary AOTY labels for select cuts and appear last; their scope is limited, not assumed representative of all 16 songs.
- **Energy 3, electronic 4, mood 3, theatrical 3:** glossy, mostly programmed electronic arrangements with some piano/guitar and other live instruments. LOVE side trends brighter, FEAR darker; the combined album should not score as one of those extremes. Less eccentric/character-driven than the first three records.
- **Experimental 1, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 3:** orthodox contemporary pop construction with international songwriters and producers; occasional dance material but much of record mid-tempo, polished. NME and Pitchfork disagree over artistic impact, not actual instrument credits.
- **Voice:** soft/clear dynamic lead, with stronger lifts; Line of Best Fit explicitly mentions her falsetto in “Karma”, hence `falsetto` in production as an album-documented technique. No evidence for Auto-Tune brand or vocal effect chain.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic and MusicBrainz credit synthesizers, keyboards, programmed or live drums, piano, guitars, bass, percussion, violin, acoustic guitar, ukulele and mandolin. No inference about drum machine model.
- **Production:** `programming` and `drumprogramming` explicitly on MusicBrainz / AllMusic original-record credits. Avoid conflating producer, engineer and writer; the group Captain Cuts is credited for production.
- **Producers:** Joel Little, Sam de Jong, Captain Cuts, Oscar Görres, Oscar Holter, James Flannigan, Jack Patterson, Grace Chatto, Mark Ralph, Jonnali Parmenius, Alex Hope (AllMusic; MusicBrainz). Not credited here: Rick Nowels merely because co-writer on “Soft To Be Strong”; neither Luis Fonsi nor other featured singers are producers by virtue of recording a vocal. The label can supply later corrections if track-specific editions differ.
- **Influenced_by left blank:** references to Kübler-Ross's psychology, pop personae or to contemporaries as sonic comparisons do not establish a *musical influence* on *this album*. Source-based ARTIST INFLUENCES sit in CLAUDE_IMPORT, not forced into this row.
- **Landmark 0:** important credited-name transition but no compelling critical consensus that this is one of her defining sonically distinctive albums. AllMusic finds it encouraging, Pitchfork is more negative.
