# Michael Jackson — Phase 02: source reasoning and uncertainty

Scope: the **original studio albums**, not their expanded anniversary editions. Evaluation date: **25 October 2026**. 27-column rows are proposals (`draft`). Musical scales are researcher judgements anchored to the Fandome guide; they are **not** properties directly measured by the sources. In genre evidence, `whole album` describes a critical album-level classification, `some tracks` means identifiable excerpts, and `texture` denotes partial sonic resemblance or elements.

## Off the Wall (1979) — `mj-offthewall`

- **Genres and scope:** AllMusic describes the album as a combination of disco grooves, funk guitars, soul, pop and soft rock; its metadata additionally names quiet storm. Disco/funk/soul/pop are central, whereas quiet storm and soft rock describe the less dance-oriented side. `rnb` follows AllMusic's album classification. No arbitrary inflation into house, synthpop or electrofunk.
- **Scales (energy 4; electronic 2; mood 4; theatrical 3; experimental 1; polish 5; tempo 4; groove 5):** the primary rhythm section is acoustic drums, electric bass, guitar and percussion. Multiple track credits document synths, but they are supplementary, justifying electronic 2. The album leans to brisk joyous grooves while including reflective slower songs; high production polish is attested by separately arranged and recorded horns and strings. Theatrical 3 reflects animated rather than operatic vocal presentation; conventional late-1970s song forms justify experimental 1.
- **Voice:** `clear|soft|powerful` represents the nimble lead and both ballad singing and emphatic climaxes. Register/effects are not coded without track-specific documentary evidence.
- **Instruments:** Original Epic credits name Louis Johnson, John Robinson, Greg Phillinganes, Michael Boddicker, George Duke, David Williams and Seawind horn players. The `electricguitar` label is supported by the band's funk/wah-wah guitar context; `electricpiano`, `clavinet`, synth, strings, flute, trumpet, trombone and sax are credited. The instrument list orders the recurring rhythm section before less recurring parts. No specific drum-machine model is claimed.
- **Production:** `hornarrangements`, `stringarrangements`, `synthprogramming` are supported by the original album's track credits. Swedien's Acusonic process is described as a recording methodology, but is not encoded as a model-specific hardware token. `Michael Jackson` appears alongside `Quincy Jones` because the LP explicitly credits Michael as co-producer on three tracks, not as sole producer of the whole album.
- **Lyrics:** party, sensuality and love recur; heartbreak is audible in “She's Out of My Life.” The scene identifies historical context; `sounds_like` is a *similarity judgement*, not documentary influence. `influenced_by` deliberately left empty: no album-specific influence testimony verified.
- **Critical nuance:** The Guardian prefers the restrained, immediate effect of Off the Wall to some later blockbuster material; AllMusic likewise highlights focused musicianship. The high-spirited numbers do not erase the quieter side.

## Thriller (1982) — `mj-thriller`

- **Genres:** AllMusic supplies pop, R&B, post-disco, contemporary R&B, quiet storm and adult contemporary R&B; Erlewine's review identifies the additional funk, hard-rock and soul palette. Genres such as hard rock apply mainly to “Beat It,” and quiet storm to the ballads. `adultcontemporaryrnb` faithfully captures AllMusic's exact three-word style and is **a new token**, not a substitution with `adultcontemporary`.
- **Scales (4, 3, 3, 4, 2, 5, 3, 4):** album includes urgent rhythmic tracks, mid-tempo singles and ballads, so tempo 3/groove 4 rather than treating it like a continuous club album. Live drums and bass coexist with synthesizers and digital devices, hence electronic 3. Horror narration, contrasting personas, arrangements and hard-rock guitar support theatrical 4. Diverse styles increase experimentation compared with straightforward radio pop, yet structure remains accessible (experimental 2). Mood balances buoyancy and darker dramatic material (3).
- **Instruments:** Original Epic credits explicitly name synthesizers, electric bass, drums, guitars, Rhodes, piano, brass/winds, handclaps, strings, Synclavier, and a vocoder part performed by Michael Boddicker on “P.Y.T.” The voice `spoken` refers to Vincent Price on “Thriller”; he is a *featured spoken performer*, not the lead throughout.
- **Production:** `synclavier` (on “Beat It”), `vocoder` (on “P.Y.T.”), `linndrum` (MusicBrainz on the title track), `synthprogramming`, and formal horn/string arrangements are documented. The original liner credits Michael Boddicker on an **Emulator**, without version: new token `emulator` instead of incorrectly assigning `emulatorii`. This is a real hardware family, not a reference to the Album's title or a guessed software program. Quincy Jones produced; Jackson co-produced specified tracks. Bruce Swedien engineered and mixed, not included as producer.
- **Lyrics:** romantic love, seduction and heartbreak contrast with the title-track horror storytelling. Influence list is blank pending specific testimony.
- **Critic distinction:** AllMusic emphasizes stylistic breadth as core to the album; do not treat the guitar-rock sound of one track as its dominant genre.

## Bad (1987) — `mj-bad`

- **Genres:** AllMusic: contemporary R&B, dance-pop, pop and contemporary pop/rock. Album of the Year retrospectively tags synth funk, pop soul and new jack swing. AllMusic's **song** review names funk and post-disco for “Bad.” `newjackswing` has weaker confidence: it is a retrospective descriptor, not a claim that this album established the style. Contemporaneous Christian Science Monitor and Los Angeles Times reviews explicitly name gospel-flavored “Man in the Mirror” and hard-rock “Dirty Diana.” AllMusic additionally names adult contemporary balladry. These are **track-limited**, not album-core styles.
- **Scales (4, 4, 3, 4, 2, 5, 4, 4):** dense synthetic timbre/programmable rhythm plus live ensemble indicates electronic 4, short of all-electronic 5. Faster polished dance tracks balance “I Just Can't Stop Loving You” and “Man in the Mirror.” Theatrical 4 captures storytelling, vocal emphasis and dramatic contrasts; experimental 2 indicates sophisticated but mainstream structures.
- **Instruments and recording:** original LP explicitly lists drums, drum programming, Synclavier keyboards, synthesizers, Hammond B3 organ (“Bad”), saxophones, trumpets, guitars, strings (“Dirty Diana”), piano, bass and handclaps. The LP explicitly says **recorded digitally on Mitsubishi multitrack and stereo master machines**; this supports `digitaltape`, not `analogtape`, `protools`, `fairlight` or any specific LinnDrum. Digital processing of Jackson's heartbeat in “Smooth Criminal” supports `sonicmanipulation`; the title's digital samples should not be confused with ordinary field recordings.
- **Production credits:** Quincy Jones producer and Michael Jackson co-producer; engineers Bruce Swedien and Humberto Gatica are **not** inserted as producers. Horn/string arrangements and drum/synth programming are separately credited. Guest Stevie Wonder sings on “Just Good Friends”; the Andraé Crouch Choir contributes to “Man in the Mirror.”
- **Lyrics:** include romance, swagger and identity; “Man in the Mirror” supplies social and spiritual change; “Smooth Criminal” is narrative. `sounds_like` is editorial only, not an influence statement.
- **Disagreement:** Retrospective synthfunk/newjackswing classifications can overstate an emergent 1987 style; primary source supports sonic specifics more strongly than historical genre names.

## Dangerous (1991) — `mj-dangerous`

- **Genres:** contemporaneous Los Angeles Times review details the new jack swing section (first six songs plus closer), hip-hop and rap, then pop-rock “Black or White,” hard-rock “Give In to Me,” and gospel songs. Sessiondays additionally notes industrial, electronic, classical, funk, and rock *elements*, treated as textures or limited-track material rather than uniform album-wide styles. AllMusic supplies R&B, dance-pop and contemporary R&B. Genre ordering prioritises new jack swing, then R&B/pop, rather than giving one-off classical/industrial textures equal weight.
- **Scales (4, 4, 2, 4, 3, 5, 3, 4):** intricate sampled/programmed beats drive most of the first half; organic guitar, orchestra and gospel parts temper electronic score from 5 to 4. Mood 2 accounts for paranoia, confrontation, pain and social themes alongside uplifting songs. Theatrical 4: gospel crescendos, dramatic rock and spoken/narrative turns. Experimental 3: unusual mixed sound sources and genre partition, compared with more uniform standard pop. Tempo 3 and groove 4 average energetic tracks against long ballads and slower hymns.
- **Instruments:** track session roster specifically names Teddy Riley/Brad Buxer keyboards and synthesizers, Bottrell electric guitar, Terry Jackson bass, Bryan Loren Moog synth bass, assorted live drums, synths and percussion, piano on “Keep the Faith,” cello on “Who Is It,” and choirs. `drummachine`, `sampler` and `sequencer` are directly in producer/engineer interview. Organ is omitted because the inspected roster does not clearly credit it.
- **Production:** Teddy Riley explicitly says he used an Akai **MPC-60** and samples on the title track; label `mpc`. Bill Bottrell (Sound On Sound) describes analog Studer recording, subsequently compiled on Mitsubishi multitrack, drum sampling, MIDI sequencing, acoustic/electric guitar, Moog bass, his **Akai S1000** workflow, and layering backgrounds on released “Black Or White.” `akais1000` is anchored to Bottrell's description; not assigned to all 14 tracks. String arrangements on “Who Is It” documented. `analogtape` describes part of the initial recording process, not the final master medium.
- **Producers:** original notes list Michael Jackson (all 14), Teddy Riley (1–6,14), Bill Bottrell (8–10), Bruce Swedien (1). Do not attribute production to writers, arrangers, mixers, or Bryan Loren just for performing.
- **Lyrics and voice:** racism, social suffering and hope; romance, sexual tension and spiritual appeals. `rap` refers to featured Heavy D / rap sections, `choral` to choir-supported passages, `spoken` to included spoken delivery. These are album-level presence tags, not all Michael Jackson vocal types on every track.
- **Critic disagreement:** Chris Willman (LA Times) praised some Riley grooves while criticizing the sprawling mixture; AllMusic and Pitchfork give stronger credit to the production's boundary-crossing character. Bottrell personally preferred the simpler early “Black Or White” take to some subsequent layering; do not present that preference as universal.
- **Influences:** no names in `influenced_by` without a verifiable album-specific statement. The `sounds_like` comparator **Guy** is particularly grounded in LA Times' audible Riley comparison, and remains a resemblance rather than an influence.

## Verification/caveats shared by all rows

1. `landmark=1` for all four because they are defining solo-era records, **not** a quantitative quality score.
2. `status=draft`. Some scalar numbers are judgements tied to cited descriptions; they are not extracted facts. `confidence=high` means the *central* recording/genre history is well corroborated, not that every low-priority descriptor is indisputable.
3. Credit transcriptions are secondary mirrors of original packaging; prioritize original LP booklet if conflicting official credits later surface.
4. Original album sequence only; 2001/2008/2012/2022 deluxe remixes, interviews and bonus versions excluded.
5. Album IDs retained directly from the existing Fandome catalog; no additional catalog albums this phase. Note that the catalog title `HIStory` requires exact release-title resolution before the next phase.
