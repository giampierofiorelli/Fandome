# Trentemøller — Phase 01 | evidence, uncertainty, disagreements

Research cutoff: **24 October 2026**. The `descriptors` branch was read in view-only mode. Artist ID `trentemoller`, display name `Trentemøller`. Its catalogue entry contains *The Last Resort* (2006), *Into the Great Wide Yonder* (2010), *Lost* (2013), *Fixion* (2016), *Obverse* (2019), *Memoria* (2022). No `docs/descriptors/trentemoller.csv` existed at inspection. Every row in this package is new; no rows from previous artists or future phases are present.

**Interpretation:** Eight 0–5 numbers are independent editorial judgments against Fandome's guide, *not* instrument measurements. Genre evidence distinguishes whole record, substantial parts, selected tracks, reference styles and loose reviewer metaphors. This matters particularly here because a two-disc remix set can have two musical identities. More than one genre and more than one credit per field are allowed. A critic mentioning a musician does *not* by itself establish an influence. Unrelated remixed artists are not automatically sonic comparators. `landmark` reflects historical artistic importance, not a numerical review score.

## The Last Resort (2006) — `trentemoller-thelastresort`

**Scope / editions.** The **13-track original studio album** is an instrumental narrative with no sung lead, even though deluxe/bonus discs, later singles and reviews prominently feature Ane Trolle on *Moan* and Richard Davis on *Always Something Better*. GAFFA specifically distinguishes the instrumental album *Moan* from the later vocal bonus cut [L_BC, L_OFF, L_GA, L_MB]. Thus `voice=instrumental`; both `lyrics` and `language` are **empty** (not English inferred from a different edition). Original release date: 6 October 2006 per artist Bandcamp, with some physical market press dating 9 October.

**Scores.** `energy=2` and `tempo=2`: a mostly reflective downtempo sequence with a few actual dancing passages; Pitchfork stresses only two album tracks have obvious floor-moving force [L_PF]. `electronic=4`: synthesizers/programming central but the artist's own credits mention live drums, guitar, celesta, glockenspiel and melodica [L_OFF, L_MB]. `mood=1`: nocturnal isolation and melancholy are the sustained affect; RA pushes back against an exclusively depressing reading, judging it at times exhilarating [L_RA]. `theatrical=2`: broad cinematic flow but generally intimate, wordless miniatures [L_BC, L_OFF]. `experimental=3`: artful time/texture, detailed patterns and unlikely instrumental colour inside coherent structures [L_PF]. `polish=4`: intricate spatial arrangements, refined record finish [L_PF, L_RA]. `groove=2`: subtly syncopated bass/beat rather than a dancefloor compilation [L_PF]. 

**Genres / disputed scope.** Electronic, downtempo and ambient moods are core; trip-hop and dub are explicitly identified for specific tracks [L_OFF, L_PF, L_RA]. Tech-house, minimal techno, electro and electro-house appear in reviews, but especially in **comparisons with the preceding singles or bonus material**, not as descriptors of the whole original album [L_RA, L_PF]. `idm` is detailed rhythmic texture; `italodisco` is RA's *Italo* comparison for the opening track only. These peripheral tokens are last in `genres`, never treated as equal to the album's downtempo character. Producers: only **Anders Trentemøller** (not guests on later vocal cuts); physical liner credits confirm production/programming [L_MB]. `sounds_like`: Pitchfork explicitly compares Yorke's *The Eraser*, Kruder & Dorfmeister and Basic Channel-esque pulse [L_PF]. `influenced_by` deliberately **empty**: Trentemøller rejects single-source explanation for this album [L_HOT]. `landmark=1` as his defining debut [L_PF, L_RA].

## The Trentemøller Chronicles (2007) — `trentemoller-thetrentemllerchronicles`

**Scope / genre.** A landmark **two-CD compilation**, not studio album two. First disc mixes own singles, some new material and reworks; second collects commissioned remixes of third-party songs [C_RA, C_PF, C_NEWS]. `type=compilation`. Techno/electro, electro-house, minimal-techno, tech-house and house are expressly documented [C_RA, C_AM, C_PF]. Ambient, chillout and drum and bass are **disc/track-limited** (not whole-set genre averages) [C_RA, C_PF]. `experimentaltechno` is AllMusic's broad album taxonomic label [C_AM].

**Scores.** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=4`: rhythmic second half and later tracks of first disc offset the extended restrained opening [C_RA, C_PF]. `electronic=4`: primarily electronic production but many sampled/guest vocal sources and some live-track passages. `mood=2`: brooding early passages offset cathartic dance climax. `theatrical=3`: crescendos and dramatic remixes [C_RA]. `experimental=3`: variable editing and detailed minimal techniques; RA and Pitchfork find uneven or strained results rather than pure avant-garde innovation. `polish=4`: meticulous remix construction [C_RA, C_AM]. `voice=instrumental|soft|clear`: instrumentals dominate but several source vocals (Ane Trolle, Karin Dreijer, Robyn and others) remain across discs; do not interpret as a consistent lead performer. `language=en` concerns sung tracks only. Lyrics blank because tracks come from different writers [C_AM, C_Q].

**Producer credit semantics:** the CSV contains **all credited `Producer` personnel identified on AllMusic**, including source-track producers Klas Åhlund, Tomas Barfod, Torbjørn Brundtland, Ben Hart, Hubee Held, Jokke Ilsoe, Moby and Mathias Schaffhäuser, as well as Anders. These are **not** claims that everyone co-produced this compilation. The official physical release transcription independently credits Anders with producing originals and his own remix versions [C_AM, C_Q]. The field is explicitly multi-artist, multi-track; Claude should retain provenance rather than treat every name as an album-level co-producer. No unsupported `influenced_by` values. `sounds_like=The Cure|In the Nursery` are critics' selected-track comparisons [C_PF, C_AM]. `landmark=1`: extensively reviewed formative compilation documenting the transition from 2003–07 originals to high-profile remixes [C_PF, C_RA].

**Disagreement.** RA finds tension between the quiet and club sides attractive, particularly midway; Pitchfork finds significant padding and criticizes *Blood in the Streets*; AllMusic is more welcoming about the overview [C_RA, C_PF, C_AM]. 

## Into the Great Wide Yonder (2010) — `trentemoller-intothegreatwideyonder`

**Scope/credits.** Original **ten-track** second studio album, issued 31 May 2010 on In My Room [Y_Q]. Anders is explicitly sole credited producer on the published Qobuz ten-track listing, with guest singers and live players distinguished from producing credits [Y_Q]. The artist describes computer composition, then transfers through **analogue tape and vintage processors**, and a stronger use of guitar, drums, music box and theremin [Y_OFF]. This is neither an all-acoustic nor all-electronic album.

**Scores.** `energy=3`: wide swells and several aggressive guitar-driven peaks among a quieter record [Y_OFF, Y_PF]. `electronic=3`: substantial guitars, real drums, piano and vocalists alongside electronics and programmed pulses [Y_OFF, Y_IR]. `mood=1`: sustained dark, mournful, suspenseful tone [Y_GA, Y_SV, Y_IR]. `theatrical=4`: cinematic contrasts, noisy climaxes and tightly sequenced drama [Y_OFF, Y_NME]. `experimental=3`: cross-genre sonic palette but stronger song structures; Pitchfork criticizes the compositional cost of this diversity [Y_PF]. `polish=4`: layered analogue mastering plus high finish [Y_OFF, Y_IR]. `tempo=2`, `groove=2`: lyrical and soundscape material outweigh dancefloor tracks, though the electronic club pulse survives [Y_SV, Y_AM].

**Genre and voices.** Electronic, rock and indie/electro-rock are substantial hybrids; post-punk, surf-rock and dark pop are explicitly named by period critics [Y_NME, Y_SV, Y_IR]. Soundtrack-like is an *aesthetic*, not a released film score; no `soundtrack` genre. `gothabilly` is an NME headline **metaphor**, low weight and contested as established categorization. Techno, house and experimental-techno are AllMusic taxonomy labels less characteristic of the record's shift away from club structures [Y_AM, Y_IR]. `voice=instrumental|soft|clear|powerful` captures the many non-vocal tracks as well as distinct guests: Marie Fisker, Solveig Sandnes, Josephine Philip, Fyfe Dangerfield [Y_Q, Y_PF, Y_OFF]. **Vocal category types are interpretive**, and `powerful` refers mainly to Dangerfield rather than all vocals. `language=en` applies only to those vocal numbers. Album-wide `lyrics` remains empty: no source rigorously establishes a coherent thematic lyric category across mostly instrumental material. `sounds_like=UNKLE|Duane Eddy|Dan Sartain` is expressly NME's comparison [Y_NME]. `influenced_by=The Ventures` is **artist-confirmed for this album's surf guitar sound**, unlike imagined connections to cinematic artists [Y_INT]. `landmark=1` as the clearly documented shift to full-band, organic productions [Y_OFF, Y_SV].

**Disagreement.** Soundvenue admires the guitar-driven departure; Pitchfork hears uneven songs and misplaced guest vocals; GAFFA says imposing production sometimes depends on familiar film-score gestures; The Irish Times calls it melodramatic [Y_SV,Y_PF,Y_GA,Y_IR].

## Reworked / Remixed (2011) — `trentemoller-reworkedremixed`

**Scope.** The two-disc **remix anthology**, 2011 In My Room. One side features Trentemøller's remixes of other artists; another contains other producers reworking songs from *Into the Great Wide Yonder* [R_OFF, R_RA]. `type=remix`, not a studio LP; **do not mistake original recording artists or remixers for joint studio collaborators**. `landmark=0` because it is less defining artistically than the studio albums or first major retrospective; it is included for the *documented* multi-artist remixes, which Fandome's inclusion rule allows. Original 2011 releases differ by territory/format (October–November) but retain year 2011 [R_RA, R_Q].

**Genres / numbers.** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: both energetic beats and slowed atmospheric reinterpretations [R_RA, R_GA]. `electronic=4`: programmed electronic remixes dominate, but neo-folk, guitar, indie and source vocals are significant [R_OFF]. `mood=2`: a mostly dark, mournful palette with livelier club diversions [R_RA, R_GA]. `theatrical=3`: powerful dramatic remixes without continuous showpiece scale. `experimental=3`: the set alternates familiar material and adventurous mutations; some tracks are simply instrumental versions [R_RA]. `polish` intentionally **empty** because the album consists of heterogeneous recordings, and descriptions do not establish a comparable uniform finish. House/electro are RA's direct labels; other styles come from precise label notes and CD classification. `industrial` applies to *Dirty Snow*; `neofolk` to Fisker's reinterpretation; `shoegaze` to Chimes & Bells source material; all are narrowly scoped [R_OFF,R_RA].

**Voice / themes.** Instrumental variants and multiple guest singers: `instrumental|soft|clear` is a *collection-level mix*, not a primary lead singer. English is supported for the sung pieces sampled in the reviewed repertoire, not for every instrumental or every original artist. The anthology has no coherent lyric theme: `lyrics` **empty** [R_OFF, R_Q]. No direct album-level influence documentation: `influenced_by` empty. `sounds_like` also empty because listing artists being remixed as **resemblances** would manufacture an erroneous recommendation relationship. 

**Producer credit semantics.** The `producers` list transcribes **AllMusic credit roles `Producer`** for multi-artist source masters and reworks, including producers associated with some third-party recordings [R_AM]. This does *not* mean they were co-producers of Trentemøller's actual mixes. Critically, AllMusic also separately gives `Additional Production`, `Reworking`, and `Remixing` roles (e.g., Andrew Weatherall, Kollektiv Turmstrasse, I Blame Coco, Modeselektor members); those names were **not** silently promoted into `Producer`. The credited list keeps AllMusic's abbreviated name forms `G. Clark`, `J. Griffith`, `J. Lavelle`, and `Ben Hiller` rather than inventing a normalized identity; Claude may reconcile with original liner notes [R_AM, R_Q]. Strong caveat for recommendation engines: original source production does not equal Trentemøller's authorship.

**Disagreement.** RA regards the anthology as uneven and criticizes some reused instrumental versions; GAFFA emphasizes its engaging musical variety and more frequent successes; label text is promotional and should not outweigh independent criticism [R_RA, R_GA, R_OFF]. `confidence=medium` reflects source heterogeneity and credit semantics.

## Outside Phase 01, without pretending the selection is complete

**All studio albums released by the cutoff:** The Last Resort (2006), Into the Great Wide Yonder (2010), Lost (2013), Fixion (2016), Obverse (2019), Memoria (2022), Dreamweaver (2024) [C_DISC,C_ALM]. The catalogue needs the 2024 Dreamweaver studio entry (not yet imported in the `descriptors`-branch catalogue observed).

2009 *Harbour Boat Trips 01: Copenhagen by Trentemøller* and 2011 *Late Night Tales: Trentemøller* are primarily **DJ-curated selections of other musicians' records**, rather than Trentemøller album production. Their importance for **documenting influence** is real, but they are excluded from the album recommendation rows on present evidence; a separate *playlist/mix selection* feature is a better home. 2008 *Live in Concert: Roskilde Festival 2007* and 2013 *Live in Copenhagen* are live documents without independent landmark evidence and not slated as album rows. Earlier singles and EPs (e.g., *Physical Fraction*, *Polar Shift*) are formative, but covered by the 2007 early-career retrospective and do not meet the additional stand-alone landmark threshold. *Lost Reworks* (2014), *The Science of Fixion* (2016), *Harbour Boat Trips Vol. 02* (2018) and other reissues/remix collections should be screened during later phases for independently supported landmark/collaboration exceptions; no pre-emptive row is supplied.
