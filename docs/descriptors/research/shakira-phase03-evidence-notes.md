# Shakira — PHASE 03 evidence notes (original studio albums only)

## Method
Genre tokens come from identified critics, label releases, platform descriptions and track-level credits. Genre evidence distinguishes whole-album identity from single-track genres and textures. Numerical scales are editorial ratings supported by the described sound and cross-album context, not measurements from source documents. Leave any instrument or production field unsupported by a credit or specific review empty; absence here is not evidence of absence. The albums' `sounds_like` field is editorial, not a list of collaborators, and `influenced_by` stays deliberately empty unless explicit album-specific musical influence is verified. No live performance or compilation is included.

## Sale el Sol (2010) — shakira-saleelsol
**Genres:** Epic's 19 October 2010 label statement describes three overlapping sound families: romantic ballads, rock songs and tropical merengue/reggaeton. AllMusic confirms an eclectic pop setting, soft rock for the title track, new-wave pulse for Tu Boca and art-pop associations for the cover of the xx's Islands. The Washington Post expressly uses 'hip-hop merengue' for Loca; Rolling Stone names electronica mixed with rock guitar and Latin rhythms. Merengue is NEW and must not be silently renamed cumbia, dancehall or reggaeton. Art-pop and new-wave are song-level; do not present them as full-album dominant. Rock is recurring but not every song.

**Scales:** Energy 3 reflects merengue/dance beside several slow ballads; electronic 2 because accounts describe a hybrid dominated by singer/songwriting and band/pop sounds rather than She Wolf's club synthesis; mood 4 from sunny, buoyant contemporary reviews, moderated by heartbreak ballads; theatrical 3 for anthemic rock and softer intimate songs; experimental 2 because eclectic transitions stay accessible; polish 4 from commercially finished recording; tempo 3 owing to ballad/dance contrast; groove 3 because Loca/Rabiosa dance tracks are prominent but several other songs are not. `voice=powerful|raspy` reflects Stewart's description of growls and coos and Erlewine's emphasis on vocal identity.

**Instruments:** Qobuz track credits for Waka Waka name Sam Cohen (guitar) and Guy Licata (drums); nothing more specific is asserted. Strong guitar-rock commentary supports guitar at the album level. No keyboard model, drum machine or DAW inferred. **Production:** left empty; the credits confirm named producers but not the specific permitted treatment/technical vocabulary consistently across original recordings. A song's remix name is not evidence that original album was produced by a particular sampler or machine.

**Producers:** Original Qobuz and MusicBrainz disclose Shakira, Luis Fernando Ochoa, Lester Mendez, El Cata (co-producer), Josh Abraham (co-producer), OliGee (co-producer), Calle 13 (Gordita), John Hill, Lukas Burton (Islands), Gustavo Cerati (Tu Boca co-producer), Albert Menendez (Mariposas co-producer). Diplo and Gucci Vump are credited for **additional production** on parts of this release and are deliberately excluded. A few producer combinations differ on recording-specific editions; no version-specific guest feature is presumed to be an independent album.

**Lyrics/language/scene:** Contemporary reviews describe romance and other lyrical turns, plus a joyous dance side. Mostly Spanish, with English material (including Waka Waka's English counterpart depending on release). Critics compare Mariposas to New Radicals, Devoción to U2 and Islands to the xx; the latter is a **cover**, not proof that the xx influenced Shakira artistically. Influence column empty. Album is a defining post-She Wolf transition, so landmark=1.

## Shakira (2014) — shakira-shakira
**Genres:** AllMusic's central description is polished international mainstream pop; The National hears dance, rock, reggae and country; Billboard explicitly describes melodic pop-rock as the album's backbone. Country is focused on Medicine; Hot Press notes ska guitar on Can't Remember to Forget You and dub-reggae on Cut Me Deep. The National uses *baroque pop* for Empire's dramatic elements; acoustic/folk-like 23 and reggae/new wave are limited tracks. No subgenre inferred solely from being a track's featured vocalist. `dance` and `dancepop` coexist because the respective sources use each term.

**Scales:** energy 3; electronic 2 (prominent guitar/drum/keys arrangement alongside electro-oriented Dare); mood 4 (love, parenting and joy dominate even where heartbreak appears); theatrical 4 (Empire's soaring rock chorus and orchestra); experimental 1 (critics describe accessible format/radio crossover); polish 5 (glossy contemporary studio pop and high-end arrangements); tempo 3; groove 3 because dance singles coexist with numerous slower songs. `voice=powerful|raspy|soft` reflects critic observations of husky, big choruses and tender restrained passages.

**Instruments:** The album's label-supplied Qobuz per-track personnel supply guitar, bass, drum kit, keyboards, piano, strings (The Hossam Ramzy String Orchestra on Empire), electric guitars, steel guitar, accordion, mandolin and trumpet; a limited number of these (steel guitar, accordion/mandolin and trumpet) are one-song or few-song timbres. Instrument order prioritises recurring core guitar/drums/bass/keys over these markers. **Production:** Qobuz explicitly credits John Hill and Dave Clauss as programmers on relevant songs, and documentation of Steve Mac/string arrangements substantiates stringarrangements. No named synthesizer, Auto-Tune or software asserted.

**Producers:** Shakira plus only producer-credited personnel for the relevant original album tracks: John Hill, Kid Harpoon, Dr. Luke, Cirkut, Billboard, J2, Steve Mac, Adam Messinger, Nasri, Steve Lu, Greg Kurstin, Busbee, Luis Fernando Ochoa. Qobuz's *expanded edition* includes additional songs including Chasing Shadows; those bonus-only credits must not migrate to the original album. Kuk Harrell (additional studio production) and guest singers Rihanna/Blake Shelton are excluded as non-producers. MusicBrainz lists Hossam Ramzy as a producer for Empire, whereas Qobuz lists only additional studio production; his producer role is left unassigned pending booklet verification.

**Lyrics/scene:** Romantic love, motherhood, personal hope and spiritual transformation described by contemporary reviewers, mainly English with some Spanish-language recordings. Washington Post likens rootsy material to early Alanis Morissette; The National hears Seal/Tori Amos in Empire and Hot Press invokes Madonna in You Don't Care About Me. All these remain SONIC COMPARISONS, not verified Shakira admissions of album influences. Some critics praise range (AllMusic) and others fault fragmented demographics (Independent); reflected in experimental=1, not fabricated artistic innovation. landmark=0 relative to the 1990s and defining crossover records.

## El Dorado (2017) — shakira-eldorado
**Genres:** Apple Music directly identifies trap and reggaeton within Latin pop, while contemporary specialist Portal Vallenato and World Listening Post explicitly identify bachata and vallenato. AllMusic regards it as a controlled blend of electronic-rhythm dance numbers and glassy adult-pop ballads. `trap` is musical style, not a blanket label for each Maluma duet, and `vallenato`/`bachata` refer to specific tracks rather than the entire record. A tempting online genre list also includes cumbia, electropop and hip-hop, but the independent references checked here did not establish their album-specific scope precisely enough: these three are deliberately LEFT OUT pending a more direct source. `bachata` may already have been flagged in Phase 01 but is absent from the checked scenes.ts; still must be added.

**Scales:** energy 3, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 4. These come from Erlewine's compact, understated adult pop with danceable Latin songs, plus Apple Music's urban beats. The album features both lively reggaeton and low-energy ballads, ruling out dancefloor 5. The vocal palette spans raspy force and restrained quieter songs.

**Instruments:** Qobuz primary original-album track credits: Luis Fernando Ochoa guitar, bass and piano on Nada/Coconut Tree; Steve Purcell drums; various guitar, keyboard, accordion and flute credits on La Bicicleta; Dave Clauss whistle on Perro Fiel; Groovespin's transcription of physical CD credits includes bongos on Deja Vu and *guacharaca/caja* on La Bicicleta. These culturally specific instruments are NEW controlled tokens. The generic `percussion` applies to the credited rhythm section; isolated instruments deliberately appear at the end. **Production:** programmer credits on Me Enamoré, Chantaje, Perro Fiel, La Bicicleta etc confirm `programming`. Richer Sounds explicitly identifies vocal cuts in Me Enamoré, justifying `vocalchops` as a limited texture. No assumption about DAWs or trap drum-machine models.

**Producers:** Qobuz lists Shakira; Luis Fernando Ochoa; Rude Boyz; Supa Dups; The Arcade; Rayito; Saga WhiteBlack; Andrés Castro; Carlos Vives; Dany Synthé; Dadju; Nasri; D'Lesly Lora; Prince Royce (co-producer); Nicky Jam and Maluma (explicitly producer-credited on their tracks, not included just as guests). Afo Verde is executive only and excluded. Additional named producers vary by release provenance; do not automatically copy everything in liner's executive or mastering credit section. Note **La Bicicleta** and **Deja Vu** first appeared as collaborations separately: their presence on El Dorado does NOT make the album itself co-billed; `coBilled.ts` needs no entry for this record.

**Languages:** Most songs Spanish; three English cuts plus bilingual French/English on Comme moi/What We Said, as AllMusic and World Listening Post report. `sounds_like` is not evidence that the guests influenced the album; `influenced_by` blank. Mostly relationship material. landmark=0 within entire discography, while still commercially prominent; not a judgment on success.

## Las Mujeres Ya No Lloran (2024) — shakira-lasmujeresyanolloran
**Release caveat:** The 2024 record is classified by label as a NEW STUDIO ALBUM, but seven singles had already been published in 2022–23. Qobuz and AllMusic recognise that its back half functions partly like a hits recap. It is **not** indexed as a compilation, because it also contains new recordings, but the fact of reused singles is written openly. Do not describe Tiësto's remix or the repeated vinyl take of Puntería as new composition/album identities. For weighting similarity, consider whether Claude should de-emphasise duplicate tracks already catalogued as singles; never create duplicate album rows. It has 17 tracks on the standard digital listing, including variant tracks.

**Genres:** Pitchfork specifies album-wide mainstream pop and track-level EDM, disco, rap, Dominican bachata, ska/northern cumbia, Afrobeats and electropop/reggaeton, plus alt-rock on Tiempo Sin Verte and Cómo Dónde y Cuándo. The Guardian separately identifies pop-house and corrido El Jefe; El País specifies corrido tumbado. Rolling Stone en Español identifies regional mexicano. This is the clearest example of why Afrobeats plural must not be silently changed to Afrobeat singular (existing `afrobeat` token differs from documented `afrobeats`). `regionalmexican` is a genre-family label, distinct from the corrido song subgenre.

**Scales:** energy 4 (many assertive electronic singles); electronic 4 (club/drum-programming next to guitar/bass/accordion/piano); mood 2 (heartbreak and retaliation dominate despite liberated celebratory tracks); theatrical 3 (punchy confident delivery, also intimate Última); experimental 2 (genre mixtures across familiar commercial song structures); polish 5 (highly glossy multi-producer pop); tempo 4; groove 4. Compare the Observer's positive reading of emotions with the Guardian's judgement of generic production and Pitchfork's qualified praise: confidence high for **observed palette**, not consensus on quality.

**Instruments:** Qobuz official song personnel credit guitar (Tiempo Sin Verte, Entre Paréntesis), bass/drums/accordion/percussion (Entre Paréntesis), piano (Acróstico; Última is described by Guardian as piano ballad), trombone (El Jefe), synth/keyboards (Bizarrap Music Sessions, Vol.53). Those timbres are scoped per tracks. No source identifies a precise Roland/DAW/drum model; not inferred. **Production:** recording metadata explicitly says programmer Dave Clauss on Copa Vacía and multiple earlier collaborators, so only `programming` is coded; guest 'vocal producer' by itself is not a producer admission. Musical genres alone cannot establish vocalfilter/autotune.

**Producers:** The Qobuz label list documents Shakira, David Stewart, Bizarrap, Primo, Jota Rosa, Richi Lopez, Tainy, Albert Hype, Federico Vindver, Yeti Beats, Mr. Naisgai, Troy NoKA, DallasK, Edgar Barrera, Kevyn Moreno, AoD, Sam Homaee, Aldae, Ciey, Keityn, Ily Wonder, Lexuz, NUP, Slow, Ovy on the Drums. The producer list is unusually long **because the 2024 LP compiles separately produced 2022–24 original singles with new songs**. The Qobuz entry has guests and songwriters alongside producers; only individuals tagged Producer/Co-producer are admitted. Tiësto is credited on a **remix version**, which should be excluded from the standard-song producer list for consistency. Afo Verde and Rafa Arcaute are A&R, not producers in these Qobuz credits.

**Languages/lyrics:** Predominantly Spanish with English portions/features on Puntería and other songs. Breakup, identity, confidence, sexual autonomy, and labour criticism in El Jefe are established by the main reviews. Pitchfork's explicit comparisons to early Alanis Morissette-like alternative rock belong to `sounds_like`; no verified artist-declared album-specific influence. For other collaborations, `sounds_like` is an editorial reference with the caveat that featured artists' sonic signatures do not automatically become an influence. landmark=1 because the publicly framed artistic return after seven years is a defining Shakira era.

## Critical review differences and import guardrails
- Critics differ on El Dorado's inventiveness and 2024's cohesion; Fandome's experimental numbers are musical-structure judgements, not quality ratings.
- `influenced_by` is EMPTY in all four rows (no demonstrated album-specific musical influence). A cover, featured artist, producer, or sonic resemblance cannot establish influence.
- All musical instruments and production techniques in the CSV are grounded in identified album/track credits or a specific album review, with scope notes above; no hardware or software guessed by genre.
- Artist catalogue currently has El Dorado and Las Mujeres Ya No Lloran, but not Sale el Sol nor Shakira (2014); import two albums before descriptor validator, then register new tokens.
