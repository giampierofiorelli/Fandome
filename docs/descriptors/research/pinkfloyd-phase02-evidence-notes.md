# Pink Floyd — Phase 02: album-level evidence notes

Research date: 26 October 2026. This phase covers **only 1970–1973**, with original release programs. Genre metadata can encompass broad classifications and track-only episodes; scopes are recorded individually in `genre_evidence.csv`. Scores are editorial 0–5 judgments calibrated to the Fandome guide, not numbers directly supplied by critics.

## Atom Heart Mother (1970) — `pinkfloyd-atomheartmother`

**Sources / sound**: The [Pink Floyd official album page](https://www.pinkfloyd.com/albums/atom-heart-mother/) and [Ron Geesin's own audiography](https://www.rongeesin.com/audiography/40-pink-floyd-atom-heart-mother) describe the 23-minute side-one suite; Geesin says he composed the brass, choir **and cello** lines to a base tape supplied by Pink Floyd. MusicBrainz distinguishes his **orchestration** from the album's **production**, credited to Pink Floyd. The band is the sole value in `producers`. The John Alldis Choir and John Alldis as conductor belong in evidentiary notes, not producer values.

**Genres and disagreement**: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000195290) indexes Art Rock / Prog-Rock / British Psychedelia / Album Rock; [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/5251-pink-floyd-atom-heart-mother.php) also tags Symphonic Prog, Folk Rock, Experimental Rock and Psychedelic Rock. Symphonic Prog (`symphonicprog`, new token) strongly fits the suite but not the whole pastoral second side; genre ordering reflects both the suite's length and the rest of the album. AOTY also indexes “Field Recordings” as a genre, but this is principally a **recording practice**, documented separately in `production`, rather than a general sonic style. Erlewine finds the suite fascinating but difficult and structurally elusive; the band's subsequent negative assessment is not evidence that the record lacks a defined progressive-rock language.

**Instrument evidence**: [Geesin](https://www.rongeesin.com/audiography/40-pink-floyd-atom-heart-mother) establishes `brass|orchestra|cello` and the choir (represented by `choral` in voice); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/6571020d-6b25-4edd-9e82-44aba29827d0) identifies Gilmour acoustic/electric guitar, Wright Hammond/piano, Mason drums, Waters bass and orchestral brass on “Summer '68”. `percussion` follows suite and band credits. No unsupported synthesizer brand assigned.

**Production**: `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` reflect Geesin’s explicitly documented brass/cello scoring; `fieldrecordings|tapeeffects` refer narrowly to the recorded kitchen breakfast sounds in “Alan's Psychedelic Breakfast” (official account), not an assumption that sound sampling dominates the album. Because the guide distinguishes arrangement, recording and production credit, Geesin is not a co-producer.

**Scales**: energy **2** and tempo **2**: long measured passages and quiet songs balance more forceful suite sections; electronic **1**: band plus orchestra dominates, though sound processing appears; mood **3**: pastoral warmth and dramatic orchestral disquiet; theatrical **3**: the dramatic suite versus intimate second side; experimental **4**: long symphonic structure and domestic tape collage; polish **3**: ambitious but uneven assembly noted in contemporary and retrospective criticism; groove **1**: mostly atmospheric rather than dance-centred. `landmark=0`: major transitional work, but less representative of the group's signature classic era. `influenced_by` blank: sonically similar artists are editorial suggestions, not proof of direct album-specific borrowing.

## Meddle (1971) — `pinkfloyd-meddle`

**Sources / genre**: [Official page](https://www.pinkfloyd.com/albums/meddle/), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/meddle-mw0000650631), [Pitchfork retrospective](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/pink-floyd-meddle/), and [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/5252-pink-floyd-meddle.php) distinguish the folk/acoustic side-one numbers from “One of These Days” and the long, spatial “Echoes”. `ambient` is secondary and track-scoped; `blues` attaches only to the acoustic blues sketch “Seamus”, not the whole album. Genre tokens prioritize progressive and psychedelic rock. Some reviewers consider it the real beginning of the band's cohesive studio style; others, such as [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_album.php?id=5683), find the epic self-indulgent. Both positions are registered in sources.

**Instruments and production**: [MusicBrainz detailed release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0eb48051-e2ce-440f-a9f2-e4650265eeed) names electric and acoustic guitar, pedal steel, electric bass (including doubled tracks), drums, percussion, organ, piano and an **EMS VCS 3** on “One of These Days”; engineer [John Leckie and Nick Mason's interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/gw-archive/interview-pink-floyds-nick-mason-engineer-john-leckie-discuss-making-meddle) documents Wright's grand piano **through a Leslie** and the Cry Baby wah-wah guitar feedback on “Echoes”. Both effects are explicitly documented, but neither warrants inventing a software token. `emsvcs3|overdubbing|tapeeffects` records named device, double-tracked bass and backwards-cymbal manipulation. Note: the ambient section is guitar/organ-led; do not infer a sequencer in “Echoes” merely because the album includes an EMS on a different track.

**Scales**: energy **3** (One of These Days vs quieter songs), electronic **2** (mostly band but EMS/treatments), mood **2** (melancholic/reflective with lighter moments), theatrical **3** (sprawling dynamic Echoes), experimental **3** (collective studio improvisation, unusual treatments), polish **3** (clearer collaborative sound than 1970), tempo **2**, groove **2**. `landmark=1` for “Echoes” and the documented creative transition. `influenced_by` left blank absent album-specific artist statements.

## Obscured by Clouds (1972) — `pinkfloyd-obscuredbyclouds`

**Format**: [Official discography](https://www.pinkfloyd.com/albums/obscured-by-clouds/) explicitly identifies the score written for Barbet Schroeder's *La Vallée* and notes that “Mudmen” and “Stay” did **not** appear in the film itself. The album is nevertheless a 1972 original soundtrack release; use `type=soundtrack`, not studio, despite conventional songs and recording. The 10-track original sequence is the reference. Château d’Hérouville sessions were short (about two weeks), concurrent with early live development of *Dark Side*; exact dates differ between brief retrospective and MB tracking notes. The genre `soundtrack` remains last because it describes function, not timbre.

**Genre/critic disagreement**: [Erlewine, AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/obscured-by-clouds-r15241) recognizes Art Rock, Prog-Rock, psychedelic music and strong pastoral folk elements, but criticizes the unfocused atmospheric instrumentals. “The Gold It's in The…” is explicitly described as blues-rock; “Free Four” as pop. Thus `bluesrock|folk|pop` are placed after the broader album traits and marked track-only in evidence. Avoid calling the entire record 'ambient electronic' merely because EMS appears in some pieces.

**Instruments and production**: [MusicBrainz original French issue](https://musicbrainz.org/release/20008548-fbb0-3b2d-99aa-9c59632cc378/disc/1) and [track detail](https://musicbrainz.org/release/5e63659c-3bb5-4560-9394-1fc4a8ffca51) explicitly credit EMS VCS3, organ, Wurlitzer electric piano, Fender Rhodes electric piano, piano, vibraphone, guitar (acoustic/electric/slide), drums, bass, percussion, tambourine and handclaps. These justify `synth`, `electricpiano`, `mallets` and `emsvcs3`. Choral vocalisations on “Absolutely Curtains” derive from Mapuga Tribe performance credits and are only a track-level texture (`choral` comes after main lead voices). No specific multitrack workstation or samplers documented.

**Scales**: energy **3** (short heavy rock tracks vs drifts), electronic **2** (EMS timbres amid live band), mood **2** (dreamlike to dark), theatrical **2**, experimental **2** (conventional songwriting for film interludes), polish **3**, tempo **3**, groove **2**. `landmark=0`: important bridge, less defining than *Meddle* and *Dark Side*. No undocumented influences.

## The Dark Side of the Moon (1973) — `pinkfloyd-thedarksideofthemoon`

**Title/date/production roles**: The [official album page](https://www.pinkfloyd.com/albums/the-dark-side-of-the-moon/) gives March 1973 and credits **Pink Floyd** as producer. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/1a2a37a4-2ee5-3aa8-b1bf-4976dbb8007c) identifies Alan Parsons as **recording engineer** and Chris Thomas as **mixer**; neither becomes a producer merely because their contributions were significant. Distinguish the original 1973 recording from later remasters, quadraphonic mixes and live reworkings.

**Genres and disagreements**: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-dark-side-of-the-moon-mw0000191308) names Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Album Rock and Hard Rock; Erlewine's review explicitly moves from neo-psychedelic art rock to jazz fusion and blues-rock. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/4204-pink-floyd-the-dark-side-of-the-moon.php) also lists Psychedelic Rock and Space Rock. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/pink-floyd-the-dark-side-of-the-moon/) details modal jazz, soul-style backing vocals and the interaction with tape collage. `gospel` is *track-only*: Clare Torry's wordless improvised vocal on “The Great Gig in the Sky”, discussed by [Guitar Player](https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/pink-floyd-the-dark-side-of-the-moon-guide). Pitchfork emphasizes studio/concept formal unity more than hard-rock classification; `hardrock` is ordered late. **Musique concrète** in [Library of Congress discussion](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2023/03/happy-50th-dark-side-of-the-moon/) is a sound technique influence, not a claim that every song is avant-garde musique-concrète: it is documented in notes and production, not padded into the album's leading genre tags.

**Instruments**: Pink Floyd's rock quartet, Wright's Hammond, piano and electric piano, EMS VCS3, Synthi AKS, Minimoog and synthesizer sequencing, pedal steel on “Breathe”/“The Great Gig”, saxophone by Dick Parry (“Money” and “Us and Them”), drums and percussion are documented in [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/1a2a37a4-2ee5-3aa8-b1bf-4976dbb8007c) and [Guitar Player's Alan Parsons interview](https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/alan-parsons-pink-floyd-the-dark-side-of-the-moon). Piano leads “The Great Gig”. Instrument list prioritizes the rock core over rarer one-track contributions.

**Production**: Nick Mason and Roger Waters independently describe physical **tape splicing** to create the rhythmic cash-register and coin collage in “Money”, and Alan Parsons describes clocks sampled from his earlier recordings; see [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-making-of-pink-floyds-dark-side-of-the-moon) and [Parsons interview](https://thestrangebrew.co.uk/alan-parsons-abbey-road-dark-side-of-the-moon/). `tapeeffects|fieldrecordings` is strongly documented. `emssynthiaks|emsvcs3|moog` have documentary equipment credit; `overdubbing` reflects multitrack sessions. `belting` is Clare Torry's striking improvised vocal register; `layeredvocals` the overdubbed and arranged backing parts. Her improvised vocal was compiled from multiple takes; [Torry interviews](https://www.theartsdesk.com/new-music/dark-side-moon-clare-torrys-great-gig-sky) prevent treating her voice as a plug-in. No drum-machine model, DAW, computer sequencer or digital sampler may be imputed from the electronic sound effects.

**Scales**: energy **3** (Time/Money vs mellow songs), electronic **3** (electronic sequences and studio tape effects integral but band still central), mood **1** (mortality/alienation pervasive), theatrical **4** (full-arc concept; grand dynamics), experimental **4** (electroacoustic montage, sequenced electronics, time signatures), polish **5** (refined studio mix), tempo **2** (unhurried/mid-tempo); groove **2** (Money's unusual groove but not designed as club music). `landmark=1`; `confidence=high`. Direct compositional influences remain blank: critical formal comparisons are not documentation of intent.

## Uncertainties and scope safeguards

- The personal/studio recording accounts disagree about some exact equipment/guitar takes; prefer evidence of instrument families over exact model when not consistently corroborated.
- The origin of the “seagull” guitar sounds can be described as Cry Baby wah feedback; do not conflate those with a dedicated bird recording.
- *Atom Heart Mother*: field recording material refers to the breakfast track; recording at home versus at Abbey Road differs by source. Do not generalise field recording to the whole album.
- *Obscured by Clouds*: some EMI/AllMusic catalogues classify it as a 'studio album'; Fandome `soundtrack` is deliberate given film commission, even though a few tracks do not appear in the film.
- The notes distinguish album-wide genres from track-specific textures. The 0–5 scales are informed editorial assessments and should be reviewed by a second person before setting status `reviewed`.
