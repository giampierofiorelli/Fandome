# Sabrina Carpenter — Phase 02 evidence and numeric decisions (English)

**Scope:** This is an increment of EXACTLY three previously undelivered album rows: *Singular: Act II* (2019), *emails i can’t send* (2022), *Short n’ Sweet* (2024). No phase-01 records. Album titles and IDs follow the GitHub default-branch catalogue snapshot (2026-10-24), checked against the 25-field guide. Every `status` is `draft` pending independent review. No value-count ceiling on multi-valued columns; `notes` still has its explicit 15-word ceiling. Numeric 0–5 fields are transparent editorial judgments, never measured ground truths. The per-genre CSV records genre scope and weaker track-specific evidence; song-level styles must NOT be presented as characterizing a whole album.

## Singular: Act II — 2019 — sabrina-singularactii
**Primary audio-focused critics:** Matt Collar (AllMusic); PAPER editorial. Supporting: Hollywood Records, Universal Music Canada, track-specific interview (PopCrush), producer-role metadata (Qobuz/MusicBrainz).
- **Original identity:** 19 July 2019, **nine tracks**, Hollywood Records. A 2024 vinyl reissue is NOT its initial release. The two 'Singular' acts are separate publications, not variants of one dataset row.
- **Genre justification:** AllMusic's authored review identifies romantic pop and R&B-inflected dance-pop; PAPER describes the album's Pop&B sensibility, especially `Tell Em`. A Saweetie verse is sufficient for only a *track-local* pop-rap accent. This does not convert the entire album into a rap release.
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 4:** Some club-leaning bouncy singles, but the 9 tracks also include the exposed, slower *Exhale*; danceability slightly exceeds energy.
- **electronic 4 / polish 4:** Sequenced beats, synthesizer, programming and commercial mixing dominate many tracks, but strings, guitar, ballad elements survive.
- **mood 3 / theatrical 3 / experimental 2:** Confident flirting alternates with anxiety, adult vulnerability and self-questioning. Strong expressive vocal narrative but conventional pop arrangements.
- **voice clear|soft|powerful:** Clear lead, soft/intimate on Exhale, stronger runs elsewhere. Source distinction is about deliveries across the album, not separate vocalists.
- **lyrics identity|introspection|love|heartbreak|sensuality:** Source and artist testimony prioritize introspection and confidence in young adulthood, over and alongside relationships. No requirement to cap themes at 2.
- **producers:** Qobuz explicit `Producer`/`CoProducer`/`AdditionalProducer` credits: Mike Sabath, Oak Felder, Stargate, Andrés Torres, Mauricio Rengifo, Downtown Trevor Brown, Zaire Koalo, Sidney Swift, Dayyon Alexander, Johan Carlsson, Jonas Jeberg. MusicBrainz sometimes labels **Tim Blacksmith and Danny D** as both executive and producer on *I Can't Stop Me*, while Qobuz names them executive producers only; because an executive label is not necessarily hands-on production, they are **not** added to the top-level list pending credit resolution. Do NOT infer producers from writers (Saweetie/Steph Jones etc.).
- **sounds_like:** PAPER directly places the *Tell Em* Pop&B influence near Ariana Grande and Kelela: these names represent a **track-specific editorial comparison**, not documented influence over the whole album.
- **influenced_by left empty:** none of the consulted album-specific sources proved direct named artistic causal influences. `landmark=0` means a developmental record, not a dismissal of quality.

## emails i can’t send — 2022 — sabrina-emailsicantsend
**Primary:** Matt Collar (AllMusic), Liam Hess (Vogue interview), the artist's **official track-by-track credits**, original MusicBrainz issue; supplementary Sputnikmusic contributor review and Exclaim! retrospective.
- **Edition guard:** original **13 tracks released 15 July 2022**. The 2023 four-track `fwd:` expansion contains *Feather*, *Opposite*, *Lonesome* and *things i wish you said*. None is used to justify scores, genre distribution, production credits, or year. The artist's official credit webpage covers BOTH editions and must be filtered to first 13.
- **Genres:** `pop` umbrella; `songwriter` is a mapped category style for unusually confessional, close writing. Expert reviews discuss the contrast between acoustic singer-songwriter / folk-pop and buoyant pop/dance-pop. *Read Your Mind* provides a disco-specific accent, *Vicious* power-pop, *Tornado Warnings* pop-rock with spoken-like passages. Do not inflate this to an album primarily made of disco, power pop, or R&B.
- **energy 2 / electronic 2 / tempo 2 / groove 2:** acoustic and ballad-heavy repertoire weighs against several slick pop cuts. Critics disagree whether the stylistic jumps work; the description should preserve variation, not smooth it away.
- **mood 2 / theatrical 2 / experimental 2 / polish 4:** candid heartbreak and family rupture outweigh up-tempo levity, with generally polished but modestly staged productions, few structurally radical ideas.
- **voice clear|soft|spoken:** The title song and others emphasize intimate, clear articulation; critic specifically notes spoken-verse style on *Tornado Warnings*. This is a secondary color.
- **lyrics introspection|heartbreak|love|identity|irony|social:** the artist directly links writing to private pandemic emails, scrutiny and coping through humour (Vogue). Family betrayal, relationship pain and personal autonomy recur.
- **producers from artist's official credits:** Leroy Clampitt (5), John Ryan (4), Julian Bunetta (2), Jason Evigan, Jorgen Odegard, Ryan Marrone (one each), with occasional coproduction between Clampitt/Bunetta. Songwriters JP Saxe, Julia Michaels and Steph Jones are NOT automatically producers.
- **influenced_by Dolly Parton|Carole King|Carly Simon:** supported by the artist explicitly naming them as inspirations when explaining the record's simpler, timeless sonic direction in the Vogue interview, not invented from mere resemblance.
- **sounds_like Conan Gray:** AllMusic contextualizes the album alongside his contemporary songwriting; similarity is an *editorial comparison*, not causal influence.
- **landmark 1:** the major label transition and unusually personal authorship are widely recognized by Vogue/AllMusic. Lyrics more introspective than standard dancepop: this is not an error.

## Short n’ Sweet — 2024 — sabrina-shortnsweet
**Primary:** Quinn Moreland (Pitchfork), Charles Lyons-Burt (Slant), Matt Collar (AllMusic), Isabel Glasgow (Exclaim!), El Hunt (Evening Standard), Universal's official album announcement and Qobuz per-song credits.
- **Edition guard:** August 23, 2024; original **12-track 36-minute studio album**. Do not add material from later deluxe versions. The final single *Espresso* and country tracks should not alone decide the whole album profile.
- **Genres:** A pop umbrella unites disco/dance-pop and softer acoustic-country material. `nudisco`/`disco` specifically on *Espresso*; `countrypop`/`country` recur (*Slim Pickins*, *Sharpest Tool*, other arrangements); `rnb` on *Good Graces*; `poprock` particularly *Taste*; `folkpop` on *Lie to Girls* and *Slim Pickins*; `synthpop` is an Eighties-flavored accent. **`gfunk` only describes a trace of synth timbre on Bed Chem in Slant**; **`bluegrass` is a localized ELLE-described influence**. They appear last, with low strength. Slant describes *Taste*'s first bars as a `poppunk` fake-out, explicitly NOT a persistent stylistic passage, so `poppunk` is deliberately **excluded from genres**. Critics disagree whether *Espresso* is representative: the Evening Standard explicitly calls it an outlier compared with the country-pop album.
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** varied track pacing with dance peaks and laid-back country/folk and quiet romantic songs; do not give 5 simply because Espresso was a global hit.
- **electronic 3 / polish 5:** a hybrid of synthesizers/drum-programming and acoustic/live instrumentation by named producers, exceptionally polished and accessible.
- **mood 4 / theatrical 3 / experimental 2:** playful flirtation and humour interspersed with sharp disillusionment, no truly avant-garde song structures.
- **voice clear|soft|powerful:** hushed close-mic moments, clear rhythmic articulation and broader pop-rock belting across tracks.
- **lyrics sensuality|irony|love|heartbreak|introspection|identity:** sex-positive wit is a defining feature, plus romantic disappointment and self-reflection. These are ranked by prevalence; no arbitrary count limit.
- **producers:** Original-track `Producer`/`Programming` on official record-label feed Qobuz and MusicBrainz: John Ryan, Julian Bunetta, Jack Antonoff and Ian Kirkpatrick. Amy Allen is a core co-writer, not listed as producer solely on that basis. Serban Ghenea and Manny Marroquin are mixers, not album producers.
- **sounds_like Nancy Sinatra|Ariana Grande:** critics compare aspects of *Please Please Please* to Nancy Sinatra and R&B vocal timbres to Ariana Grande. These are track-specific editorial comparisons, NOT evidence of direct creative influence, so `influenced_by` is empty.
- **landmark 1:** large-profile career breakthrough, established through several critics' independent retrospectives. All scores are still `draft`.

## Critical caveats and disagreements
1. Qobuz reprints syndicated AllMusic/TiVo critic prose: count as one critic, never two independent reviews.
2. Spotify/Apple 'pop' record-level store genre is coarse. A song's arrangement cannot be generalized to 12 tracks. The genre evidence table separates scopes.
3. An artist naming an inspiration when discussing this album is direct evidence; a journalist comparing its sound to another artist is **not** evidence that the other artist shaped the album.
4. This research does NOT include Man's Best Friend (2025); next increment only. No GitHub repository write has been attempted.
