# Mina — Fandome Phase 03: evidence audit (English)

**Scope:** exactly four new 1970–74 descriptor rows. The user's editorial criterion for Mina is **important albums**, not a complete discography. This phase retrieves the critically important 1970 Brazilian album after Phases 01–02 covered the 1960s and 1971. Chronological ordering in `src/data/catalog.ts` is important even though phase number is incremental.

**Read-only branch audit 25 Oct 2026:** Fandome `descriptors` branch contains Phase 01 Mina rows only (1960 Tintarella; 1960 Il cielo; 1964 Mina; 1965 Studio Uno). Phase 02 1966–71 file was delivered separately but is **not yet imported at time of audit**. Do not drop Phase 02, or produce duplicate rows when importing these files later. `Frutta e verdura` is already in catalog; the other three titles are not.

**General methodology:** every genre has a matching row and source keys in genre-evidence CSV. Retrospective genre database labels are distinguished from core period-specific musical identities. The eight 0–5 axes are Fandome editorial judgments, never ratings of artistic merit. Unknown producers, especially people credited solely as *arrangers*, remain blank. No other 25-column field has length or item-count limit, **except notes, which is <=15 words**. `sounds_like` is comparison, not proof of artistic influence; `influenced_by` stays blank for all four because no independent documentation demonstrates direct *album-specific* inspirational borrowing beyond covering somebody's composition. Covering a writer is not automatically being influenced by them.

## 1 — mina-minacantaobrasil / Mina canta o Brasil (1970)

- **Original issue:** 11 Portuguese-language songs, not a compilation merely because Mina had performed **different Italian-language recordings** of some titles previously. The artist's primary discography [B01], Brazil's IMMuB catalogue [B04] and 1970 phonogram metadata [B05] align.
- **Critical status:** Rolling Stone Italia, Giorgio Moltisanti (21 Nov 2018), ranked it **#1 among ten forgotten Mina albums** [B02]. The SHFL describes her distinctive strong yet modulated performance over Brazilian guitars/percussion/horns [B03]; this makes `landmark=1` and `confidence=high` defensible.
- **Genres and scopes:** `pop`, `mpb`, `bossanova`, `samba`, `latin` explicitly distinguished in the specialist critical review [B03]. `tropicalia` is a contemporary/retrospective album description from Rolling Stone [B02] and a secondary discography [B06], not proof that all 11 recordings are Tropicália. `europop`, `adultcontemporary` are **broad indexing categories** from SHFL [B03] and weighted last. Five *new* tokens required across this phase: `mpb`, `samba`, `tropicalia`, `chanson`, `ballad`. `mpb` is Música Popular Brasileira, broader than bossa nova; do not silently alias these.
- **Scales:** energy 3 balances brassier spirited and gentle numbers [B03]; electronic 0 (horns, strings/guitars/percussion described, no synth evidence) [B02–B03], not conflating a 1970 recording with synthesized music; mood 3 balances festive and melancholic repertoire [B02–B03]; theatrical 3 broad dynamic vocal interpretation [B03]; experimental 2 for varied genre reworkings rather than unconventional structure [B02]; polish 4 for polished orchestral arrangement [B01,B03]; tempo 3 and groove 3 for alternation of sambas and slower bossa tunes [B03]. These latter comparative numbers remain editorial, not measured BPM.
- **Producer audit:** secondary discography specifically labels **Bob Mitchell as producer** [B06], but the first-party discography and reissue sleeve reliably credit him *as arranger* for all 11 songs [B01,B07]. Producer retained **provisionally**, Claude must verify an original-label credit; do not confuse arrangement evidence with independent proof of producer credit.
- **Language / voice / lyric themes:** `pt` original singing throughout [B01,B05,B06]. `powerful|clear|soft` reflects SHFL description of contrasts in delivery [B03]. Themes `love|social|sensuality` justified at track and repertoire level, not asserted as all-track uniform. Names of Brazilian songwriters on the sleeve are **composition credits**, not automatically influence declarations.

## 2 — mina-fruttaeverdura / Frutta e verdura (1973)

- **Original issue:** PDU PLD L 5069, ten song LP; issued initially together with, but separately labelled from, *Amanti di valore* [F01,F02,F05,F07]. Keep pre-existing catalog ID `mina-fruttaeverdura`. Hit *E poi...* recorded for this album; not a stand-alone album.
- **Artists and functions:** Mina's official primary credits list **Pino Presti arranging most tracks**, **Dario Baldan Bembo arranging Non tornare più** and **Natale Massara arranging Devo tornare a casa mia** [F01]. **Vittorio Buffoli coordinates**, **Nuccio Rinaldis records** [F01,F04]. None is independently established as album-wide producer. `producers` empty. WhoSampled identifies Presti as producer of *La pioggia di marzo* specifically, but this **track credit is not enough** to extrapolate to entire LP [see WhoSampled if needed].
- **Genres:** AllMusic uses pop, Italian Pop, Euro-Pop, Adult Contemporary [F03]. Bossa nova is **track subset** via Jobim's *Águas de Março*, rendered *La pioggia di marzo* [F01,F07]. Blues is **track subset** via the unusually structured *E poi...* [F02]. Chanson is an original record-shop style label, and musica leggera is contextual; do not promote them over the direct pop markers.
- **Scales:** energy 3, groove 2, tempo 3 for mixed rhythmic and ballad songs [F01,F02]; electronic 0 with Italian orchestrations and rhythm players, no substantial evidence for synths [F01,F06]; mood 2 due dominant disappointed/romantic material with some brighter episodes [F01,F02]; theatrical 3 versus intimate passages [F02]; experimental 2 because *E poi...* resists hook/chorus-centered pop while LP remains accessible [F02]; polish 4 for highly arranged professional PDU recordings [F01,F04]. `voice=powerful|clear`, love/heartbreak/identity nuance as track-level themes; `lyrics=love|heartbreak|introspection|sensuality` with primary track titles and analysis [F01,F02].
- **Landmark=1:** enduring hit *E poi...* and historical formal innovation, documented in Rockol historian account [F02].

## 3 — mina-amantidivalore / Amanti di valore (1973)

- **Original issue:** PDU PLD 5070 eleven-song LP, previously sold in a two-LP package with *Frutta e verdura*, not overlapping content [A03,A05,A08]. A 1974 Spanish adaptation is **not part of this album** [A08].
- **Creative concept:** Franco Califano composed **all eleven lyrics**, Carlo Pes composed music [A03,A05]; see also Rockol critical retrospective identifying the single-author experiment as historically significant [A04]. Corriere critic Giulia Cavaliere details desire, adultery, sexual expectations and irony; this is *strong album-wide lyric evidence* [A02]. Beware a **Rockol factual name typo** ('Franco Pes') in A03: sleeve [A05] specifically says **Carlo Pes**. Preserve the correct name.
- **Production roles:** Pino Presti and Carlo Pes are listed as **arrangers**, Nuccio Rinaldis as recording/mixing engineer, Carlo Pes as mixer on some issue credits [A05]; none is therefore automatically an album-wide producer. Producer deliberately blank. Songwriter = genre/tag for songwriter-led Italian artful vocal songbook; Mina herself does **not** claim writing these songs.
- **Genres:** primary pop [A07]; Chanson and ballad are original-pressing style labels [A09], not evidence of French-language singing; contextual Italian melodic canzone and adult contemporary remain lower confidence [A02–A07].
- **Scales:** energy 2, groove 1, tempo 2 for generally measured song-oriented album; electronic 0 because evidence of orchestrated 1973 PDU recordings rather than synthesizer-led production [A05]; mood 1 for emotionally painful and intimate songs [A02,A06]; theatrical 3 for expressive stage-worthy interpretation, not 5 [A06]; experimental 2 for concept/sexual candour, NOT an assertion of avant-garde sound [A02,A04]; polish 4 for refined arrangements [A05,A06]. *Carlo detto il mandrillo* has choral ironic presentation, not evidence the entire album has a choral lead. `voice=clear|powerful|soft`, lyric themes many and nonexclusive [A02,A06].
- **Landmark=1** cultural/creative significance even if not every recording was a mass hit [A04].

## 4 — mina-babygate / Baby Gate (1974)

- **Original issue:** **eleven tracks**, PDU PLD L 6006, combined commercially with *Mina®* as a **22-track two-disc 1974 package** [G04,G05]. Classify original *Baby Gate* independent side, never rate or duplicate the other LP. Despite the Baby Gate stage name's 1958–59 history, **1974 album is newly recorded covers**, not compilation of the 1958–59 masters [G03].
- **Repertoire:** Rockol identifies return to early American-pop persona and 1950s oldies/standards [G03]. Original first-party song list includes *Bird Dog*, *Mr. Blue*, *Flamingo*, *I Only Have Eyes for You*, *I'm in the Mood for Love* plus Italian songs *Amorevole* and *Non so* [G01,G04]. Hence original singing `en|it`, **not English only**. Jazz/vocal jazz covers as subset, rock and roll and R&B as repertory genres; `doowop` retroactive partial style [G06]. AllMusic explicitly tags Europop, Italian Pop and Adult Contemporary [G02]; original catalogue also calls the record Ballad [G10]; these remain broad or subset style labels.
- **Producer audit:** Pino Presti credited for orchestra and arrangements [G01,G09], but no original album-wide producer credit established; leave `producers` **blank**. Similarly do not add Elvis or doo-wop groups to `influenced_by` because Mina covering a song is not independently documented intention or influence. Comparison to The Platters etc belongs `sounds_like`.
- **Scales:** energy 3 and tempo 3 for upbeat rock-and-roll balanced with torchy standards; electronic 0 period big-band/live analogue arrangements [G01,G08]; mood 3 for bittersweet ballads and energetic oldies; theatrical 3 interpretation; experimental 1 deliberately retrospective, not radical; polish 4 carefully arranged/studio recorded; groove 3 thanks doo-wop and 1950s rhythmic songs [G01,G03,G08]. Album-wide voice expressive `powerful|clear|soft`. Lyric themes `love|heartbreak|nostalgia`, last interpreted as retrospective frame rather than literally sung all the way [G01,G03].
- **Landmark=0:** credible representative retrospective, but lower indispensability than the leading breakthrough albums; historic persona relevance explicitly recorded.
