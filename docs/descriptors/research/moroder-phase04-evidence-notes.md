# Giorgio Moroder — Phase 04, evidence notes (original editions only)
Research cutoff: 25 October 2026. All scores below are editorial, not measurements. Evidence references (AG-/FX-/CP-/SM-) resolve to the `sources.csv` file. `influenced_by` means a demonstrated influence on the work; collaborators and vocalists are not automatically influences. Numbers 0–5 follow the Fandome guide.

## American Gigolo (1980) — `moroder-americangigolo`
- **Album identity, production, voices:** eight-track original soundtrack. On A1 Blondie sings *Call Me*, on A2 Cheryl Barnes sings *Love and Passion*, and six remaining tracks are Moroder instrumentals. Moroder is the original sleeve's sole producer; Harold Faltermeyer arranged and played keyboards, and Keith Forsey played drums. Do not treat lyricist Paul Schrader, Debbie Harry, Faltermeyer or Forsey as album producer (AG-O, AG-L, AG-M, AG-Q).
- **Genres:** `soundtrack` and `electronic` apply throughout; `poprock`, `newwave`, `pop`, `disco`, `synthpop`, `electro` describe vocal/club tracks or selected score textures. AllMusic categorizes as Contemporary Pop/Rock, but calls the incidental cues synthetically generated. Moroder himself distinguishes Blondie's new-wave identity from his own disco rhythmic contribution. `newwave` is one prominent track, not the album's overall scene; `electro` is a record-retailer label and hence weaker (AG-A, AG-I, AG-S).
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** extended driving rock-disco A1 and *Night Drive* are counterbalanced by six incidental themes; avoid treating the full 36-minute record as a seamless club set. Score-wide ratings are inferred from the original 8-track LP structure plus firsthand descriptions of the disco beat and synth themes (AG-I, AG-L, AG-A).
- **electronic 4 / polish 4:** Moroder explicitly describes an electronically generated score; professional multistudio recording, arranging, synthesizers and percussion are documented. Not 5 because Blondie recorded a band performance and Forsey played percussion (AG-I, AG-L, AG-M).
- **mood 2 / theatrical 3:** critic hears a deliberately decadent artificial atmosphere; film composer situates work in nocturnal Los Angeles. Two pop vocals lift the mood, so not uniformly dark. Theatricality here denotes film-theme scale, not rock-opera bombast (AG-A, AG-I).
- **experimental 2:** sound design and a Mozart reworking are interesting, but melody/structures and tempo are readily accessible (AG-L, AG-I).
- **voice `instrumental|clear` / lyrics `sensuality|love` / language `en`:** most tracks are instrumental; Blondie and Barnes are the only recorded lead voices. Themes derive from the two vocal song titles and film context, not the instrumentals. Qualifiers like `raspy` and `powerful` were omitted because track-specific published voice evidence is thin (AG-O, AG-L, AG-A).
- **`influenced_by` = Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart:** the original LP states that *Hello Mr W.A.M. (Finale)* rearranges the Adagio of the Clarinet Concerto K. 622. This is directly documented **track-specific** borrowed composition, not a claim that the album overall sounds Classical or that Mozart influenced Moroder generally (AG-L, AG-M).
- **`sounds_like` editorial only:** Donna Summer is similar for Moroder's disco motifs, Tangerine Dream for nocturnal electronics, Blondie for A1's performance. None is asserted as a documented historical influence.
- **landmark 1 / confidence high:** defining soundtrack and major hit *Call Me*, with original sleeves, interview and independent critic concordance; genre scopes and interpretations remain draft (AG-O, AG-A, AG-I).
- **Exclusions:** Film appearances of John Hiatt and Smokey Robinson are not on original Polydor LP (AG-F); do not use their sounds/credits to infer LP descriptors.

## Foxes (1980) — `moroder-foxes`
- **Original LP discrepancy:** Moroder's website displays **11** tracks, while independent catalogues of the original Casablanca NBLP-2-7206 double LP display **12**, including a side-D *Valley of the Dolls (Slow Instrumental)* (FX-O, FX-V, FX-D). Use 12 songs. Do not score cinema-only music not on the record (FX-F).
- **Producer:** official artist page names Giorgio Moroder for soundtrack; this doesn't imply he composed every performance. Angel's two songs have other writers, and specific sessions may have other production involvement. The row lists **credited soundtrack producer** Moroder only, and Claude should inspect original packaging for any additional individual-track production credits (FX-O, FX-F, FX-V).
- **Genres:** pop and disco are called out together in Turner Classic Movies; electronic instruments and synth-led instrumental *Valley of the Dolls* are a significant strand, but not the entire program. The two Angel performances justify rock and track-specific hard rock; they are not Moroder disco compositions. Do not add funk or synthpop merely because Moroder produced the collection (FX-T, FX-R, FX-H, FX-V).
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** extended Donna Summer dance track, Cher's *Bad Love*, Brooklyn Dreams, and Angel rock are interspersed with reflective Janis Ian and numerous instrumental versions; these scores deliberately avoid weighting *On the Radio* as if it were the whole LP (FX-V, FX-T, FX-R).
- **electronic 3:** a sizeable Moroder instrumental/dance component uses electronics, while live-band rock and other vocal material make it a stylistically mixed record; no 4/5 based only on Moroder's studio reputation (FX-V, FX-O, FX-R).
- **mood 3 / polish 4:** content varies from romantic/heartbreak narratives through bright disco and rock to somber film cues. Multi-performer polished studio LP; not a uniformly euphoric or dark release (FX-V, FX-T).
- **theatrical/experimental intentionally empty:** no sufficiently specific album-wide source evidence supports numeric separation from other professionally made 1980 soundtracks.
- **voice `instrumental|clear|powerful`, lyrics `love|heartbreak`, language en:** LP contains lengthy instrumentals plus distinctive performances by Donna Summer, Cher, Janis Ian and Brooklyn Dreams. Voice set is conservatively limited to broadly supported kinds; do not mark choir (`choral`) for a vocal group. Song themes principally relationship/longing. These themes **do not** apply to instrumentals (FX-O, FX-V, FX-T).
- **`sounds_like`:** Donna Summer, Sparks (a Moroder-produced eclectic disco-pop comparison), Angel capture different sides, editorial only. `influenced_by` empty: appearing on a various-artists LP or using Moroder as producer is not an influence claim.
- **landmark 0 / confidence medium:** historically important singles, but highly heterogeneous soundtrack is not automatically a solo defining studio statement; discrepancy and absence of a comprehensive contemporary stylistic album review temper confidence.

## Cat People (1982) — `moroder-catpeople`
- **Album:** original ten-track MCA/Backstreet LP has one David Bowie vocal theme followed by nine Moroder instrumental cues. The song was co-written (music Moroder, lyrics Bowie), while the release credits only Moroder as producer. No later 1983 *Let's Dance* remake is included (CP-O, CP-M, CP-C, CP-R).
- **Genres:** score is electronic film music. Wax Poetics recounts studio treatment and ominous ambience. `ambient` / **new `darkambient`** mark sustained ominous cues. AOTY explicitly tags Dark Ambient, weaker than the firsthand tape story. AllMusic explicitly lists Disco and Dance-Pop as album styles, yet those occur in the rhythmic/theme strand, not all nine instrumentals. `rock` relates to Bowie's introductory track. `synthpop` is the electronic-pop strand and requires follow-up review of album vs song scope; if judged insufficient, remove from row and genre evidence (CP-A, CP-W, CP-D, CP-R).
- **energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 2:** measured suspense cues, pulsing sequences and *Paul's Theme (Jogging Chase)* rhythm are a minority relative to eerie, restrained incidental underscoring (CP-F, CP-A).
- **electronic 4 / polish 4:** Moog, Prophet-5, Synclavier, synthesizer programming and sound effects; also real drums, guitar and Lee Sklar bass, so not fully electronic. Professional multistudio soundtrack recording (CP-M, CP-A).
- **mood 1 / theatrical 3:** review describes dread and atonal tension; recurring melodic cues retain atmosphere, sometimes cinematic grandeur, without cheerful euphoria. Use 1 instead of 0 due to melodic/romantic passage contrast (CP-F, CP-W).
- **experimental 3:** Moroder's firsthand claim of reverse tape work to make the sound uncanny, plus manipulated animal noise and unusual synthesized timbres, supports unusual techniques without suggesting full avant-garde structures (CP-W, CP-M).
- **voice `instrumental|powerful|raspy`:** more than four-fifths instrumental; Bowie is the only main album lead singer. Moroder himself calls his performance powerful and AllMusic notes growling vocals. No other singer is claimed lead; backing vocals do not imply choral album (CP-W, CP-R, CP-M).
- **lyrics `sensuality`, language `en`:** thematic coding derives only from Bowie title song and erotic narrative; most tracks are instrumental and should not be assigned lyrical topics. 
- **`sounds_like`:** Tangerine Dream, John Carpenter and Vangelis are editorial analogues for electronic film-scoring textures, *not documented sources Moroder studied*. `influenced_by` empty despite Bowie as collaborator.
- **landmark 1 / confidence high:** one of his distinctive score albums with well-attested recording techniques, credited producer and reputable independent discussion. Genre `darkambient` is **medium-strength metadata** within an otherwise high-confidence row.
- **Critic disagreement:** Steven McDonald (AllMusic) finds the cues repetitive; Henrik (Danish Film Music Society) praises their effective variation and development. Avoid using either critic's taste to infer quantitative scores (CP-A, CP-F).

## Solitary Men (1983) — `moroder-solitarymen`
- **Artist and edition:** an actual jointly credited **studio album** by Giorgio Moroder and Joe Esposito, mapped to Moroder's Fandome room with `artist=Giorgio Moroder` as the required single-artist room label. Eleven-track original Oasis LP, not the 14-/18-track Repertoire variants. The title song can be printed *Solitary Man* on sleeve and *Solitary Men* on labels. *Lady, Lady* had soundtrack exposure; do not score the entire *Flashdance* soundtrack as a track here (SM-O, SM-R, SM-B, SM-C).
- **Producer and musicians:** official Moroder page explicitly credits him producer. AllMusic also tags Lauri Kanner 'Producer, Production Coordination' on its reissue metadata; because her exact original LP production attribution is unclear, she is **not yet** appended to `producers`, pending original sleeve proof. Joe Esposito is lead singer/arranger, **not producer** based on official album. Richie Zito guitar/bass, Keith Forsey drums, and Sylvester Levay keyboards are neither assumed producers nor influences (SM-O, SM-A).
- **Genre disagreement:** Repertoire's label copy emphasizes disco dance workouts, whereas independent critic Leon MacDuff describes synth-based soft rock and an AOR-heavy ballad set. AllMusic says Electronic / Club-Dance, Apple Dance. Preserve **both** strands with `softrock|synthpop|electronic|disco|aor|dance|pop`, scopes limited. `synthpop` is derived from synthesizer arrangements/period pop form and AllMusic's user account, so weaker than direct label Electronic; optional second-review removal (SM-R, SM-A, SM-V, SM-P).
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 3:** *A Love Affair* and dance workouts balance several mid-tempo ballads and a Moody Blues cover; neither a relentless club album nor a uniformly slow AOR album (SM-R, SM-V, SM-O).
- **electronic 3 / polish 4:** documented synths and keyboard arrangements over actual guitars, live bass and drums; careful production at Musicland, Weryton and Carla Ridge supports substantial polish but not synthetic-only 5 (SM-A, SM-V).
- **mood 3 / theatrical 3:** love-related ballads sit beside upbeat disco tracks; powerful, husky vocal performances are dramatic but not rock-opera scale (SM-R, SM-V).
- **experimental 1:** critic highlights familiar romantic ballads and normalized studio song structures, rather than the unusual sequencing of earlier Moroder synth work (SM-V, SM-R).
- **voice `raspy|powerful`:** source reissue calls Esposito husky and powerful. Not instrumental; later instrumental bonus tracks are out of scope (SM-R, SM-B).
- **lyrics `love|sensuality`, language en:** original album contains relationship and physical-attraction songs including *A Love Affair*, *My Girl*, *Too Hot to Touch* and cover *Nights in White Satin*. No social, political or narrative lyrics established (SM-O, SM-B).
- **`sounds_like`:** Brooklyn Dreams, Laura Branigan, Berlin are only interpretive 1980s analogues, never `influenced_by`. The Moody Blues tune is a documented cover, not proof that their aesthetic broadly influenced the album.
- **landmark 0 / confidence medium:** historically legitimate collaboration, but not a widely accepted defining Moroder record and critic/label dispute about album identity remains.

## Shared inclusion policy
- One phase, exactly these four new albums. Prior phase rows remain untouched.
- No `influenced_by` from undocumented stylistic comparison, co-production or vocalist collaboration.
- `darkambient` is the only proposed new genre. Keep its specific evidence strength visible before adding it to `scenes.ts`, `genreFamilies.ts` and validator.
- Original year and edition outrank reissues/streaming metadata. Cinematic incidental songs excluded from official LPs are not represented.
