## Bandido (1979) — `labionda-bandido`

Original Baby Records LPX 30 (Italian sleeve ℗ 1978; 1979 LP release). The independently archived 1979 Spanish and German editions corroborate eight tracks. The Italian LP credits **Producer – La Bionda**, with Baby Records also a producing entity. The brothers credited Angelo/Michelangelo and Carmelo are the artists, so the producer field expands the joint name to both brothers; music writers and engineers are not individually credited as producers. Charly Ricanek arranges first five tracks, Thor Baldursson last three. Harry Thumann engineers; Juergen Koppers records/mixes. Keith Forsey plays drums/percussion/noises; Charly Ricanek piano/Polymoog; Mats Björklund guitar; the Pop Strings of Munich appear. **Deserts of Mars explicitly credits Thor Baldursson on Minimoog and Polymoog, Pepe Solera on soprano sax**. *Bandido* and *Baby Make Love* rhythm tracks were recorded at Country Lane Studios, while other material involves Musicland/Stone Castle; do not flatten to one recording setup. The specialist retrospection contrasts title-track American-style disco, restrained *Black and White*, futuristic *Deserts of Mars* and nostalgic *Will She or Won’t She*. It gives meaningful differentiation but is a single interpretive source. Spanish issue indexes Disco/Electro/Synth Pop: that does NOT justify calling all eight songs synthpop.

**Scales:** energy 4/tempo 4/groove 4: disco album with softer/narrative alternatives; electronic 3: credited analog polysynths over live rhythm section; mood 3: retrospective identifies mixed calm/nostalgic/upbeat material; theatrical 3: strings and voices; experimental 3: programmed/noise/synth textures within commercial disco; polish 4: multiple professional studios and orchestral players. Each remains an editorial qualitative reading. `voice` abstained: backing vocals are documented but lead voice qualities are not consistently described. `lyrics` left blank: lyrics were not independently checked. `sounds_like` / `influenced_by` intentionally blank.

**Original track audit:**
1. **Deserts of Mars** — Futuristic extended cut. A1 directly credits Minimoog, Polymoog and soprano-sax solo; supports electronic texture, not automatically ambient, space disco or synthpop as a unique track genre.
2. **Welcome Home** — A2 original album cut. No independently verified track-specific style: retain broad LP-level disco only.
3. **Moonlight Palais** — A3 writing attributed to arranger Charly Ricanek and a solo guitar credited to Sigi Schwab. This does not establish a guitar-rock genre.
4. **Baby Make Love** — A4 disco single; Gary Unwin bass and Billy Long guitar solo specifically credited. Romantic lyric domain suggested by title; lyrics not independently audited.
5. **Bandido** — B1 directly called American-style disco in specialist retrospective; rhythm instruments separately credited to Gary Unwin, Martin Harrison, Sigi Schwab and percussionists.
6. **Black and White** — B2 called calmer/restrained in retrospective, so do not represent all songs as maximum-energy dance.
7. **Never Gonna Let You Go** — B3 full length 6:36, but duration alone does not establish a particular subgenre.
8. **Will She or Won’t She** — B4 described as nostalgic; additional songwriting credit Thor Baldursson; no proven swing/rock genre.

**Genre disagreement:** general music databases simplify to Disco, original Spanish issue uses Electro/Synth Pop, and one archival collection labels the whole LP Pop/Rock. All retained with qualifications; studio personnel ground the electronic/live hybrid, but not track-by-track extrapolation.

## High Energy (1979) — `labionda-highenergy`

Original 1979 Italian Baby Records BR 56001 and Ariola 201 198-320 LP, **six tracks** with extended 5–7 minute cuts. A detailed reproduction of the inner sleeve expressly says “Produced by Michelangelo & Carmelo La Bionda”; another catalogue abbreviates to “Producer Angelo La Bionda” and is less specific. Geoff Stradling: synths/keys/melodica/trombone; Mats Björklund: guitars; Günther Gebauer: bass; Pat Gammon: acoustic piano on Disco Roller; Todd Canedy drums on High Energy / Listen To My Heart / Save Your Energy; Evert van der Wal drums I Got Your Number / Disco Roller. String/horn arranger Geoff Bastow and horn arrangements Geoff Stradling, not producers. Recorded across Carimate, Milan, Munich, London, May–Oct 1979. **Do not misread track title High Energy as modern hi-NRG genre**.

An independent specialist retrospective explicitly differentiates a more rock-based *I Got Your Number* from a more disco-based *Disco Roller*, and places the album in the wake of Donna Summer’s *Hot Stuff* rock-disco crossover. `influenced_by=Donna Summer` reflects this **qualified critic interpretation**, not a direct quote from the brothers. `sounds_like` is similar sonic comparison, not a personnel attribution. Both require editorial caution.
**Scales:** 4 energy, 4 tempo, 4 groove with extended dance grooves; electronic 3 for real synth alongside acoustic drums, bass and horn/string overdubs; theatrical 3 for arranged horn sections; experimental 2 for familiar rock/disco crossover, polish 4; mood 4 provisional; voice blank because session/backing personnel do not resolve lead type.

**Original track audit:**
1. **High Energy** — Seven-minute opening track with Geoff Stradling horn arrangement, synths and Todd Canedy drums. Album genre disco; not enough evidence to tag it hi-NRG.
2. **I Got Your Number** — Six-minute track called the more rock-oriented cut by specialist source. Evert van der Wal drums, Les Hurdle bass.
3. **Tune It Up** — Original A3 track. No independently verified style beyond album-classified disco; title alone does not establish funk.
4. **Disco Roller (Come on the Rock and Roll)** — Nearly seven minutes, disco-focused according to specialist source. Patrick Gammon credited acoustic piano, Evert drums; track title alone not evidence of rock.
5. **Listen to My Heart** — Original B2; Todd Canedy drums and Björklund co-writing; exact ballad-vs-dance treatment not independently resolved.
6. **Save Your Energy** — Original B3; Todd Canedy drums; no independent evidence of a slower groove despite title.

## I Wanna Be Your Lover (1980) — `labionda-iwannabeyourlover`

Original nine-song 1980 Italian Baby Records BR 56018, issued in other countries 1980/1981; do not substitute the 1981 variants or singles. 1980 7-inch *I Wanna Be Your Lover* runs approximately 3:12, while LP lead song is ~4:18. Credits say **Produced by La Bionda**; two brothers have single-level producer credits. Keyboardists Thor Baldursson and Hermann Weindorf, guitarist Mats Björklund, Günther Gebauer bass, Curt Cress drums/percussion, Strings of Munich, three backing singers. Recorded/mixed by Cedric Beatty at Union Studios in October 1980. Curtis Drake credits specifically **recording the “alien voice” on title track**, not producing the whole album. Arrangers: Charly Ricanek and Thor Baldursson on differing tracks; neither is necessarily a producer.

Original release databases agree on Disco/Italo-Disco/Electronic; 1980 German LP separately identifies **Pop Rock**. That album taxonomy is retained instead of erasing stylistic breadth, although no individual track has yet been independently classified as pop-rock by an accredited critic. `voice` remains blank: sound-effect treatment is not a traditional lead-voice type and credited backing singers cannot establish lead timbre. `electronic=3` provisional for synth-keyboard/live-rhythm synthesis and alien-voice manipulation; theatrical 3, experimental 2, production polish 4, tempo 4, groove 4, energy 4, mood 3. `lyrics` left blank pending lyric-by-lyric verification.

**Original track audit:**
1. **I Wanna Be Your Lover** — Disco title single, longer LP version; explicitly documented processed alien voice recorded by Curtis Drake; not proof of whole-album sci-fi theme.
2. **Johnie Don’t Go Away** — Original A2 5:30; Thor Baldursson arranges; sleeve also prints Johnny in one variant. No substantiated song-specific subgenre.
3. **Action** — Original A3 ~3:57; Charly Ricanek arrangement; 1980 B-side single. No credible specific electro-funk claim.
4. **A Moment of Sunshine** — Original A4 4:52; Thor Baldursson-arranged; do not infer mood from title only.
5. **Night Flight** — Original B1 4:22; Thor Baldursson arrangement. Aviation title not evidence of space disco.
6. **You’re So Fine** — Original B2 3:55; Charly Ricanek arrangement; no validated special subgenre.
7. **One Thing’s for Sure** — Original B3 3:48; Charly Ricanek arrangement. No independent lyrics evidence.
8. **Cyclone Rock** — Original B4 ~3:24; Charly Ricanek arrangement and Timothy Touchton lyric credit; explicit Rock in title alone is not genre evidence.
9. **Don’t Cry Now** — Original B5 3:14; Thor Baldursson arrangement. Title suggests heartbreak but does not establish ballad sound.

## In Beatween (1998) — `labionda-inbeatween`

Universal CD UMD77566, recorded January–May **1998**, Logic Studios Milan (one archive spells it “Logis”; treat as same venue only after sleeve review). Important: record database files it as **Electronic / Euro House**, with 10 core songs plus two program mixes and hidden reprise/acapella content. It has substantial fresh material **and new recordings of historical La Bionda and D.D. Sound songs**, e.g. One for You One for Me, Bandido, Café and 1-2-3-4 Gimme Some More. It is not a straightforward original-composition studio album. Because reworkings feature **new lead vocalists, rap and new programmed music**, `type=studio` is defensible rather than `compilation`, but the importing editor may prefer `remix`. Keep distinction explicit and do not treat cover repertoire as wholly original 1998 compositions.
**Production credits:** Carmelo La Bionda expressly producer; Rolando Alberti arranger, keyboards and programming (not explicitly producer), Dario Mazzoli bass/guitar/keys on credited cuts, sax Giancarlo Porro on tracks 7 & 11, castanets Carmelo on track 3, guest voices D.D. Klein, Sandra Gunn and Teacher Mike. Rap specifically Sandra Gunn on track 6 and Teacher Mike on 9. This is the basis of voice token `rap`, **minority/secondary lead**, and NOT a blanket genre `hiphop` or `poprap` without song-style corroboration. `electronic=4` tied to programmed keyboards; stylistically 1998 Euro House rather than 1970s Disco. New remakes keep their **1998 audio identity**. `language=en` relies on English track names and known vocal songs; needs checked lyrics for Señor Alfredo/Chico title. Only electronic 4 is entered, grounded in documented extensive keyboard programming. Other scale fields are left blank because club-genre metadata alone cannot justify numerical judgments across a hybrid studio album.

**CD track audit, original program plus mixes:**
1. **Eeah Dada** — New 1998 composition with several listed lead vocals; contemporaneous 1999 single remixes must not replace 1998 track.
2. **One for You One for Me** — Reinterpretation of 1978 La Bionda hit; 1998 lead Teacher Mike, new house programming; do not double-count as 1978 original recording.
3. **Señor Alfredo** — New 1998 track; Sandra Gunn lead, Carmelo on castanets. Castanets alone do not prove flamenco, Latin house or Spanish-language vocals.
4. **Justify Emotions** — New 1998 track; D.D. Klein lead. No independent subgenre beyond album-level Euro House.
5. **She’s Not a Disco Lady** — Revisits D.D. Sound repertoire; guest lead vocals. The word Disco in a title of a 1998 rework does not mean original disco recording.
6. **Bandido** — 1998 revisitation of 1979 track; Carmelo and D.D. Klein lead; Sandra Gunn rap. Rap supports a minority voice type, not automatically hip-hop genre.
7. **Chico Is Back in Puerto Rico** — New track; multiple guest vocals and credited saxophone/percussion; song title cannot by itself justify reggaeton or salsa genre.
8. **1-2-3-4 Gimme Some More** — Re-records/reworks 1977 D.D. Sound song. The liner metadata supplies no separate track 8 performer credits.
9. **Café** — D.D. Sound repertoire reworked in 1998, includes Teacher Mike rap; singer identities separately documented.
10. **Believin’** — 1998 contemporary song with Sandra Gunn lead; later hidden remix is not another album.

## Cross-album qualifications and disagreements

- Source catalogues frequently give only release-level genre labels. All six unusual styles in Bandido are retained if source-labelled even if not individually proven; the scope and strength must accompany recommender reasoning. A 1998 title sounding Spanish is never enough to add salsa or Latin genre.
- 1979 High Energy must not be tagged `hinrg` merely because of its title; the actual record classifications found are Disco/Electronic, with a specialist describing rock/dance crossover.
- There are alternate spellings `Desert on Mars` / `Deserts of Mars`, `Johnie`/`Johnny`, `Cyclone Rock`/`The Cyclone Rock`, `InBeatween`/`In Beatween`. Canonical album labels follow primary release metadata; importer must not create extra albums.
- `influenced_by=Donna Summer` on High Energy is **only** a critic's explicit Hot Stuff comparison; it does not imply a verified statement by either La Bionda brother. If importer requires a direct musician statement rather than qualified-critic inference, leave it blank.
- `producers` has only people credited in production role. Original 1979 Bandido says `Producer – La Bionda`; High Energy an independently reproduced Italian sleeve says both Michelangelo and Carmelo; some abbreviated databases say only Angelo. 1998 only Carmelo credited. Do not substitute arranger, engineer or writer for producer.
- Source coverage is primarily sleeve/collector reconstructions and one Hungarian specialist critical retrospect. Track audit marks unknown sonic character explicitly rather than inventing fine-grained subgenres or filling `sounds_like` speculatively.
- All numerical dimensions are non-measured editorial estimates rooted in specific album personnel, genre metadata and retrospective comments. `confidence=medium` recognizes detailed release evidence but limited independent song-by-song reviews. Importer may blank uncertain scales rather than treat them as laboratory data.
