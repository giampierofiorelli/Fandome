# Kylie Minogue — Phase 03 evidence notes

Research/audit date: **26 October 2026**. Exactly three NEW descriptors (2003, 2007, 2010). Original standard-tracklist studio albums only; reissues, mixes, bonus tracks, compilations and live recordings do **not** count. Values are independent anchored editorial assessments based on the **entire original album**, not calculated from genre. The genre evidence CSV records each source/scope separately. Source URL keys below resolve to the sources CSV and to the working links in this document. Do not treat the number of independently indexed genre tags as musical dominance.

## `kylie-bodylanguage` — *Body Language* (2003)

**Crediting basis:** 12 original tracks; [Qobuz/Parlophone credits](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/body-language-kylie-minogue/5099990767456) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/d2093e09-dcb6-4133-9139-6cf07b868385). [AllMusic (Chris True)](https://www.allmusic.com/album/body-language-r675507/review), [Slant (Sal Cinquemani, 2003)](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-body-language/), [The Guardian (Helen Pidd)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/nov/14/popandrock.shopping4), [Apple Music editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/body-language/693183065), [SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2004/07/kylie-minogue-body-language-emi/), [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/reviews/album-kylie-minogue-78216.html).

**Genre scope and balance:** Dance-pop with 1980s synth-pop, disco and modern electro; greater R&B presence than preceding disco albums. Sal Cinquemani specifically calls *Slow* minimal electro-pop/disco, *Still Standing* nu-disco, *Red Blooded Woman* hip-hop-flavoured and *Chocolate/Someday* wistful/soft. AllMusic uses electroclash. AOTY adds synth funk/electro-disco/downtempo as secondary descriptors ([genre metadata](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/1802-kylie-minogue-body-language.php)); keep them after well-referenced whole-album genres. `electronic` describes texture, not proof of an electronic dance subgenre. The guest on *Someday* (Green Gartside) is not a producer.

**Scales:** energy **3** (unusually restrained versus *Fever*, but still a set of pop grooves: Guardian and Slant); electronic **4** (synth-centric rhythmic language with some guitar, keyboard and orchestra: credits); mood **3** (sensual/fun songs counterweighted by melancholy *Chocolate/Someday*); theatrical **2** (mostly close, breathy studio singing, little large-scale belting); experimental **3** (unusually spare *Slow*, hip-hop borrowing, off-centre chord movement); polish **5** (commercial studio synth and elaborate production); tempo **3** (*Slow* and other midtempo tracks dilute club tempo); groove **3** (several danceable tracks but Guardian explicitly finds most songs more home-listening-oriented). These are qualitative judgments, not BPM calculations.

**Voice:** `soft|clear|rap`: intimate and breathy delivery; Slant and the Guardian identify rapping on *Secret (Take You Home)*. No `operatic` or `powerful` classification. **Lyrics:** `sensuality|love|party|heartbreak`; the lyrics and reviewer commentary emphasise flirtation and relationships, with minor longing; no unsupported political themes.

**Instruments:** `synth` (the electro-synth layers described by Independent/Slant), `keyboards` (Julian Gallagher, Biff Stannard and Dave Morgan on *Loving Days*; Dave Clews on *Red Blooded Woman*), `electricpiano` (Guardian explicitly identifies electric piano on *I Feel for You*), `guitar` (Dave Morgan *Loving Days*; muted riffs on *I Feel for You*), `orchestra` (London Session Orchestra, *Loving Days*). We deliberately do **not** infer a specific drum machine or synth model from the electronic genre, and do not enumerate string instruments that the credit calls an orchestra. *Johnny Douglas / Cathy Dennis / Chris Braide "all instruments"* is NOT evidence for a specific instrument.

**Production:** `programming` (Dave Clews, Gallagher and Dave McCracken); `synthprogramming` (Dave Clews credited keyboards programmer in label credits); `sampling` (Lisa Lisa & Cult Jam, *Secret*, as explicitly noted by Apple Music; sampled song itself is **not** `influenced_by`); `vocalfilter` (SPIN describes filtered vocals); `overdubbing` (Slant explicitly calls out vocal overdubs on *Slow*); `stringarrangements` (Simon Hale, *Loving Days*); `protools` ([original 2003 physical-release credit transcription](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/kylie-minogue/body-language-album_frvcun.html) calls Dave Clews Pro Tools technician/editor on tracks 3, 6, 7, but **not** an inferred mix-system for the entire album). No unsupported synth/drum model tokens.

**Producers by original songs:** Sunnyroads (*Slow*); Baby Ash (*Still Standing*, *Sweet Music*, *Someday*); Rez (*Secret*); Kurtis Mantronik (*Promises*, *Obsession*); Johnny Douglas (*Red Blooded Woman*, *Chocolate*); Electric J (*I Feel for You*); Richard “Biff” Stannard and Julian Gallagher (*Loving Days*); Chris Braide and Cathy Dennis (*After Dark*). **Exclude** Johnny Douglas from *Promises/Obsession* and *Secret*: credited only as **additional producer** there. Exclude Baby Ash on *I Feel for You* where he is only additional producer. In the CSV producer lists are album-wide, not shares of authorship. Credited vocal producers/editors/masterers are excluded unless also fully produced elsewhere.

**Similarity vs influence:** *Still Standing* evokes Prince; Goldfrapp/Jamiroquai comparisons in Slant; other sonic comparisons from Apple Music/criticism. The Guardian describes Prince and Kool & the Gang as actual inspirations **for this album**, sufficient for `influenced_by`; sampled Lisa Lisa, Green Gartside guest, and lyric references to Chaka Khan/Janet are **not** influence tokens. Mood and the level of stylistic originality are contested: AllMusic praises its innovation and cohesion; The Independent faults artificial vocals, while Guardian questions danceability.

**Landmark 1:** central Kylie artistic shift and signature *Slow*, not a claim of universal critical acclaim. **Confidence high** for core style and recording, not for every secondary index genre.

## `kylie-x` — *X* (2007)

**Crediting basis:** [Parlophone October 2007 original press release](https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=50353) specifies 13 standard songs and most production credits; [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/x-kylie-minogue/vapqzkxcp19ba) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/6eccc5aa-d013-4935-96a9-d6a6999a9b2a) expand individual musician roles. [Pitchfork (Tom Ewing)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/10947-x/); [Slant (Dave Hughes)](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-x/); [AOTY tags](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/1804-kylie-minogue-x.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r1265366/review). USA/late reissue extras (*Magnetic Electric*, MIMS remix of *All I See*) are **not** used.

**Genre scope and balance:** Electropop and dance-pop govern the album; AOTY lists electroclash, electro-disco, synthpop and pop-rock as secondary styles. Pitchfork expressly names electro-disco, **cosmic disco** and disco, alongside 2000s R&B and 1980s street-dance nods. `cosmicdisco` is a **new literal genre token**; do not substitute `spacedisco` (different term). *2 Hearts* is electro-rock, *Stars/The One* show pop-rock/disco-rock, *Speakerphone/Nu-Di-Ty* hip-hop flirtations; these go later. `electronica` is a low-confidence literal tag sourced from a secondary discography synthesis ([Wikipedia musical styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_%28Kylie_Minogue_album%29)), not a primary Pitchfork genre statement; omit if reviewers require qualified criticism. Hip-hop/R&B are only partial. `poprock` specifically uses the AOTY string, not a deduction from having guitars.

**Scales:** energy **4** (high, kinetic singles across the album, some quieter tracks); electronic **4** (synth-heavy and programmed but documented live guitars, bass, sax, theremin and drums); mood **4** (extroverted pop fun against contemplative *No More Rain*); theatrical **3** (regular glossy pop phrasing, a touch of sung/spoken play); experimental **2** (stylistically promiscuous, but much of the material conventional or trend-following according to both critics); polish **5** (intensive contemporary commercial studio processing); tempo **4** (mostly rapid dance numbers, with downtempo exceptions); groove **4** (majority geared to club/pop dancing). Experimentation is **not** scored 4 merely because there are multiple genres.

**Voice:** `clear|soft|spoken`, with half-spoken lead on *Heart Beat Rock* according to Pitchfork. The vocoder on *Speakerphone* is a **production** marker not an `operatic` or robotic voice type. **Lyrics:** `love|sensuality|party|introspection`; *No More Rain* is cited by Pitchfork as reflective on life's beauty. Do not infer a cancer concept album; treatment isn't a blanket subject of the 13 lyrics.

**Instruments:** Qobuz explicit: `synth` (Jim Eliot *2 Hearts*), `keyboards` (Jeberg/Bloodshy & Avant/Wiltshire etc), `bass` (Moray McLaren/Bloodshy & Avant/Peake), `guitar` (Stars, Speakerphone), `drums` (Jim Eliot *2 Hearts*), `percussion` (Mich Hansen, Russell Small), `electricpiano` and `theremin` (Jim Eliot *2 Hearts*), `harp` (Slant/Pitchfork *All I See/Speakerphone*), `saxophone` (Ian Kirkham, *The One*), `acousticguitar` (Henrik Jonback *Speakerphone*), `electricguitar` (Paul Stanborough *Sensitized*), `handclaps` (Jim Eliot, *2 Hearts*). A **theremin is credited**, not guessed from an electronic tone. Some are one-track instruments and therefore appear last. No uncredited hardware-specific models.

**Production:** Qobuz credits generic `programming` and keyboard programming (`synthprogramming`); Slant specifically says *Speakerphone* uses a `vocoder`, Pitchfork specifically hears sliced vocal effects on *Nu-Di-Ty*, validating `vocalchops`; Pitchfork records Serge Gainsbourg sample on *Sensitized* (`sampling`). We **do not** invent TR-808/TR-909, software DAWs, Auto-Tune or sidechain. Musical allusions to Justice/Daft Punk are only sonic.

**Producers:** Official Parlophone publicity: Kish Mauve; Cutfather and Jonas Jeberg; Richard “Biff” Stannard; Calvin Harris; Bloodshy & Avant; Guy Chambers and Cathy Dennis; Freemasons; Greg Kurstin; Eg White. Qobuz adds Mich Hedin Hansen (Cutfather), Karen Poole (*No More Rain/Wow*), and in some original track credits individual Paul Harris and Julian Peake (*Stars*). **Conservative standardized row:** the release-group name Cutfather stands for Mich Hansen, who is not duplicated separately; *No More Rain*/*Wow* have Karen Poole listed as producer by Qobuz, so included. We do **not** add Matt Prisne on *Cosmic* even though later digital metadata says producer: the contemporaneous Parlophone release specifies **Eg White alone**; this conflict is explicitly left for human review. The contemporaneous release says only Stannard produced *In My Arms* but digital credits also Calvin Harris; include both as the later official metadata calls both producers. *Nu-Di-Ty* has an Eg White co-production credit on MusicBrainz versus release-only B&A; Eg White is already securely credited for *Cosmic*. All this is song production, not songwriting. Read the cited press release before changing.

**Influence vs resemblance:** *2 Hearts* compared to Goldfrapp; *The One* to New Order, *In My Arms* to Justice; other sounds-like comparisons to Gwen Stefani, Britney Spears and Sugababes explicitly arise in Pitchfork/Slant. These are **not** independently documented formative influences; leave `influenced_by` blank. Critics agree *X* lacks sonic cohesion but dispute whether its genre breadth is refreshing or opportunistic.

**Landmark 1:** defining comeback after the artist's cancer treatment, with major singles; not a claim it is her best album. **Confidence high** for credited instrumentation and main genre, caveats on producer discrepancies and fringe genres.

## `kylie-aphrodite` — *Aphrodite* (2010)

**Crediting basis:** Original 12 songs in [Qobuz/Parlophone](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/aphrodite-kylie-minogue/5099964290355) verified against [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/8a1892ba-0d1a-4b8e-9c78-2655cd45c2e7). Core reviews: [AllMusic (Tim Sendra)](https://www.allmusic.com/album/aphrodite-r1814540/review), [Slant (Sal Cinquemani)](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-aphrodite/), [The Observer/Guardian (Kitty Empire)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2010/jul/04/kylie-minogue-aphrodite-review), [The Guardian (Caroline Sullivan)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2010/jul/01/kylie-minogue-aphrodite-cd-review). [AOTY style taxonomy](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/1801-kylie-minogue-aphrodite.php); [Metacritic Uncut extract](https://www.metacritic.com/music/aphrodite/kylie-minogue).

**Genre scope and balance:** Cohesive glittery disco/dance-pop is the repeatedly documented core, with synth-pop/electropop and electronic production. The AOTY secondary tags `electrohouse|nudisco|electrodisco|progressivehouse` apply to **some tracks**, not to the album's every passage. `house` as family only where subgenre supported. Guardian hears R&B energy on the title song (one track only). `poptrance` is explicitly a **weak secondary** genre: Uncut's description in Metacritic summary; conservative import may remove if reviewers deem a short critical analogy insufficient, but do not rename it to `trance`.

**Scales:** energy **4** (pounding euphoric dance with *Everything Is Beautiful* as calmer release); electronic **4** (synth-forward studio production, but credited guitar and piano contributions prevent a fully electronic 5); mood **5** (exuberant happiness and love, despite some wistful tracks); theatrical **3** (bright diva pop drama but vocals consistently restrained); experimental **1** (cohesive and intentionally classic; Slant calls it safe); polish **5** (finely unified high-gloss Price mixes); tempo **4** (upbeat majority of original 12 songs); groove **5** (nearly every track aims at dancefloor, AllMusic). These ratings are not tempo/instrument measurements.

**Voice:** `clear|soft` for lighter tenor, playful whispery timbre; no evidence of belting. **Lyrics:** `love|party|sensuality|identity` (last subordinate, title track boasts persona/return; do not call it autobiographical). AllMusic explicitly says most songs are love songs or happy dance anthems.

**Instruments:** `synth` (Pallot/Chatterley *Aphrodite*, *Better Than Today*, Richard Phillips *Can't Beat the Feeling*), `keyboards` (numerous credited), `piano` (Jim Eliot; Rice-Oxley; Pallot/Chatterley), `bass` (Jim Eliot; Maime Hladiy), `acousticguitar` (Pallot on title track / Better Than Today), `electricguitar` (Pallot and Ben Vella *Better Than Today*), `guitar` (Fraser T. Smith *Everything Is Beautiful*; Davidsen *Get Outta My Way*), `percussion` (Mich Hansen *Get Outta My Way*). A harpsichord-LIKE **synth** does not justify `harpsichord`; fake string timbre does not justify `strings`. Do not manufacture a drum machine simply because drums are programmed.

**Production:** `synthprogramming` (Wallevik/Davidsen keyboard programming; explicit credit); `drumprogramming` (Jim Eliot and Andy Chatterley); `programming` (broad credit above); `digitalediting` (Pete Hoffman, *Get Outta My Way*). No specific computer, synth model or effects plug-in confirmed. Stuart Price credited as executive producer **and** actual full producer on several tracks; include for the latter, not simply the executive credit.

**Producers on original twelve:** Qobuz credits Stuart Price (many tracks), Jim Eliot (*All the Lovers*), Damon Sharpe, Lucas Secon, Daniel Davidsen, Peter Wallevik, Mich Hansen (*Get Outta My Way*), Fin Dow-Smith / Starsmith (*Put Your Hands Up*), Fraser T. Smith (*Everything Is Beautiful*), Nerina Pallot and Andy Chatterley (*Aphrodite/Better Than Today*), Calvin Harris (*Too Much*), Sebastian Ingrosso and Magnus Lidehäll (*Cupid Boy*), Pascal Gabriel and Børge Fjordheim (*Can't Beat the Feeling*). Some release-era sources specify **additional production** only for Stuart on *All the Lovers/Aphrodite/Better Than Today*; he indisputably has full producer status elsewhere, so remains included. Qobuz lists named individuals even where industry credits use 'Cutfather' grouping: use named credited co-producers and no duplicate group label. Jake Shears and Tim Rice-Oxley are writers/musicians but **not album producers** according to original per-track credits.

**Influence vs resemblance:** Slant compares *Closer* with Cerrone/Daft Punk; Guardian compares title song energy with Bananarama's *Venus*; Price also worked with Madonna and Scissor Sisters. None of these latter facts documents the **album's musical influence**, so keep `influenced_by` empty and store sonic resemblance in `sounds_like`. Critics disagree: AllMusic admires cohesion and hooks; Slant calls it safe, Caroline Sullivan weak lyrically. Distinguish strong evidence about sound from critical value judgment.

**Landmark 1** for the cohesive dance-pop era, led by *All the Lovers* and *Get Outta My Way*. **Confidence high** for recording facts/core genres; progressive house/pop trance are individually low-strength.

## Cross-record methods and caveats

- Original 2003/2007/2010 first-release studio albums, without expanded bonus cuts. All releases already appear in `src/data/seed.ts` on the queried `descriptors` branch; the currently committed `docs/descriptors/kylie.csv` contains the **four 1988–1991 rows only**, even though earlier phase-02 deliverables exist as a local ZIP. Claude should merge Phase 02 before Phase 03 if it has not already done so.
- Avoid co-billing due to guest appearances, samples, writer credits, remixers, or exec producers. All three original albums are billed to Kylie Minogue only.
- Instruments/production are grounded in **specific** original song credits; not all every-song ubiquitous. A synth with harpsichord-like tone does not imply a real harpsichord, synth strings do not imply string instruments, and use of a sample does not create an influence claim.
- Genre source-scope and secondary genre confidence are deliberately separate from the whole-row confidence. Some distributor record-shop parent genres classify *Body Language/X* as “Rock”; this is a store browsing bin, contradicted by specialist criticism, not evidence that the albums are rock-dominant.
- The `notes` CSV column stays at 15 words maximum; all other list fields may be as long as needed.
