# Evidence notes — Ennio Morricone / Phase 03

Scope: original commercially released **soundtrack albums**, not all film scores ever composed. This research is about the **original LP programmes**, never a modern expansion. Research checked on **26 October 2026**. Values are editorial Fandome judgments anchored in specific sources below; citations and links also appear in `morricone_phase03_sources.csv`. Empty fields are intentionally unknown, not zero. Prior Phase 01/02 rows are not present.

## 1. Il clan dei siciliani — `morricone-ilclandeisiciliani` (1969)

**Original issue and title.** The film is the French *Le clan des Siciliens* (1969), but the Fandome canonical record uses the established Italian film/title **Il clan dei siciliani**. SoundtrackCollector identifies a French **20th Century Fox 940 006** 11-track LP released in **1969**, with A-side *Tema Italiano*, *Snack Bar*, *Mostra dei gioielli*, *Dialogo N°1*, *Jeanne e la spiaggia* and six B-side cues. CAM Sugar explicitly labels the first disc of its 2025 expanded *Archivio Segreto* release **The Original 1969 Soundtrack Album**. The 2025 edition also contains **outtakes, alternates and eight previously unreleased themes**, all excluded. The 1970 US and Japanese LPs are later regional variants. Some later reviewers refer to 1996 as the first soundtrack release; original pressing evidence supersedes this claim. Sources: CLAN_STC, CLAN_CAM, CLAN_BROX, CLAN_JAP.

**Genres.** The dominant theme is described by specialist Jonathan Broxton as having the sound of Morricone's spaghetti-Western scores; this is **sound resemblance in a gangster thriller**, not a claim that the film is a Western. `spaghettiwestern` is thus a major motif/texture, not a film classification. `jazz` and `lounge` are limited primarily to *Snack Bar*, described as contemporary lounge jazz. `soundtrack` is last and describes the format. Source: CLAN_BROX.

**Instruments, individually sourced.** `electricguitar` and `jawharp` are specified in the original recurring *Tema italiano*, as are `percussion`, `strings` and orchestral support (`orchestra`). `whistle` is specifically identified in *Dialogo N°1* (Alessandro Alessandroni's whistling). All come from CLAN_BROX and are mapped to existing Fandome tokens; no synth or recording effect is inferred. The publisher mentions original analogue master tapes but not a verified **original recording or mixing chain**, so `analogtape` is omitted from `production` as a non-specific reissue disclosure.

**8 scales.** Energy 3 (some chase surges, mainly mid-intensity themes); electronic 1 (amplified guitar but fundamentally acoustic ensembles); mood 2 (coolly threatening nocturnal tone); theatrical 3 (thematic orchestral crime drama rather than spectacle); experimental 2 (jaw harp / whistle juxtaposed with conventional orchestration); polish **empty** (no defensible original production-quality benchmark); tempo 3 (mixed languor and chase cues); groove 2 (a jazzy cue, not continuous dance music). Entire 11-track sequence considered. Sources: CLAN_STC, CLAN_BROX.

**Other fields.** `instrumental`; no verified lyric themes or sung language. Bruno Nicolai appears in historical credits as *conductor*, not independently validated album recording producer; `producers` blank. `influenced_by` blank: resemblance to other Morricone Westerns does not establish an influence. `sounds_like` Lalo Schifrin and Piero Piccioni are transparent editorial comparisons of noir-jazz orchestration, **not** claimed documented influences. Landmark=1 for iconic theme and identified historical significance according to CAM Sugar and specialist review. Confidence high for album date and acoustic signature; no engineer's original session breakdown.

## 2. L’assoluto naturale — `morricone-lassolutonaturale` (1969)

**Original issue.** Film (Mauro Bolognini) and original soundtrack LP **1969**, as independently stated by contemporary rights holder Cinevox/Beat, Quartet's detailed 2013 notes and Jonathan Broxton. The full original programme comprises **13 cues**; a more recent 14-track vinyl adds *Il profumo della tua pelle (Ballata per organo)* and 2013 editions list six further bonus cues. Do not ascribe those added takes to the 1969 first LP. The collector's listing of Cinevox MDF 33-23 has incomplete release date metadata, so date is supported principally by label statements. Sources: ABS_BEA, ABS_QUA, ABS_BROX, ABS_STC, ABS_AMS.

**Genres.** Cinevox describes *classical, jazz, pop and lounge* components; the contemporary *jazz* / *lounge* combo predominates among the initial LP cues. `classical` particularly applies to orchestral romantic music and *Assalito dalle rondini*, while `pop` denotes accessible romantic theme settings; all have explicit named support. No 1970s-style `beat` inserted based solely on later bonus track *Sempre più verità (Sviluppo in Beat)*. Format `soundtrack` is last. Sources: ABS_BEA, ABS_BROX.

**Instruments, individually sourced.** Reviewer Broxton describes the opening main motif in `flute` (specifically bass flute), `organ` (Hammond; **no brand-specific production token**), and `strings`; recurring `piano` ostinatos; audible `drums` including hi-hat pulse; `trumpet` with muted timbre on *Studio di colori*; and the final textural climax for agitated layered `strings`. `orchestra` confirmed by record label. Source ABS_BROX; cross-check ABS_BEA. The word 'layers' describes musical arrangement rather than a verified overdubbing process: do not assign `overdubbing`. The later added organ bonus is **not** sole support for organ because the original main theme itself is documented using it.

**8 scales.** Energy 2 (generally gentle, one turbulent closing cue); electronic 1 (Hammond organ in predominantly acoustic group); mood 3 (romantic sunshine with anxious strings); theatrical 2 (intimate lounge over occasional drama); experimental 3 (dissonant final *Assalito dalle rondini* disrupts mild opening); polish **empty** (no original mastering comparison); tempo 2 (mostly languid); groove 2 (jazz pulses rather than sustained dancing). Sources ABS_BROX/ABS_BEA.

**Other fields.** Album described as largely instrumentals, thus `voice=instrumental`; lyrics/language blank. Nicolai is conductor only. No original recording producer evidenced, `producers` blank. `sounds_like` Piero Piccioni/Armando Trovajoli is stylistic editorial judgment and not *influenced_by*. The film-score remains culturally notable yet not among Morricone's defining major albums; `landmark=0`, confidence high for the verifiable descriptor facets.

## 3. Metti, una sera a cena — `morricone-mettiunaseracena` (1969)

**Original issue.** Cinevox **MDF 33/16**, Italian **December 1969**, 9-track program (Side A 5, Side B 4). The 1970 CBS *Love Circle* UK album adds *Hurry to Me*; the 2015 AMS reissue also adds it as an explicitly labeled **bonus**. The English-language song by **The Sandpipers**, despite being linked to the film, **does not belong to the nine original LP tracks** and supplies neither language nor lyric nor guest artist values in this descriptor. The later 25-track edition includes unused/alternate *Ric Happening* organ/sitar combinations; those are not sole evidence for original edition instrumental tags. Sources MET_STC, MET_AMS, MET_BEAT, MET_BROX.

**Genres.** Original label explicitly lists `lounge`, `bossanova`, `jazz`, `beat` with orchestra; Broxton describes `cooljazz` (his phrasing *cool-jazz*) and occasional light `rock` rhythmic figures in the nine originals. All named tokens kept distinct rather than collapsing cool jazz to general jazz. `cooljazz` is **NEW**, requires addition to `scenes.ts`, `genreFamilies.ts`, and the validator's genre whitelist; `beat` already exists on current GitHub descriptors branch. `soundtrack` format last. Jazz/loungy settings dominate; late genres are limited to individual tracks. Sources MET_AMS, MET_BROX.

**Instruments, individually sourced.** `orchestra` / `strings`, `drums` (record label's jazz drum emphasis), `percussion`, `guitar`, `keyboards`, prominent `organ` (*Terrazza vuota* Hammond sound), `piano` (staccato motifs), `brass` (jazz-noir motifs in *Nina* and *Croce d'amore*), `oboe` (*Uno che grida amore*), `sitar` (*Ric Happening* original score, explicitly described by Broxton): MET_BROX and MET_AMS. No synthesis/drum machines extrapolated from organ/tape or 1970s beat. Some later credits identify Thomas organ models used by conductor Nicolai, but manufacturer/model is **not independently secure for the nine 1969 LP cues** and no exact machine token is added.

**Voice and lyrics.** Edda Dell’Orso provides wordless airy lead vocalise; choir-like vocal textures occur on *Alla luce del giorno*; most cues are instrumentals. Use `soft|instrumental|choral` for this combination. No lyric story/thematic column assigned because vocal sounds are nonlexical on selected release. No singing language. `Hurry to Me` English lyrics not counted. Sources MET_ALL, MET_BROX, MET_BEAT.

**8 scales.** Energy 3 (relaxed buoyancy interrupted by forceful noir brass); electronic 1 (electric organ among acoustic instruments); mood 3 (sensual yet bittersweet); theatrical 2 (near-intimate sensual scoring); experimental 2 (psychedelic organ/sitar color, still melodic); polish **empty** (unverified original sound-finishing); tempo 3 (bossa/beat mixed with slower cues); groove 3 (frequent bossa/beat and lightly danceable cues). Sources MET_AMS, MET_BROX. Composer credited Morricone, conductor Bruno Nicolai, but no authenticated original phonogram **recording producer**; leave blank. `sounds_like` editorial comparison to contemporaneous Italian lounge scoring (Piccioni/Trovajoli), no demonstrated personal per-album influence. Landmark=1 based on historical visibility/Nastro d’Argento 1970 (MET_AMS), confidence high.

## 4. L’uccello dalle piume di cristallo — `morricone-luccellodallepiumedicristallo` (1971)

**Original issue — major date/edition hazard.** Dario Argento film **1970**. Cinevox/AMS (the original Italian label) states explicitly that the original **Italian 10-track MONO LP arrived March 1971**, reproduced in its AMSLP36 reissue. There **was a 1970 US Capitol ST-642 LP** (11 differently named sequenced cues) with an **entirely different album program**, verified independently in SoundtrackCollector and Film Music Site. The two historical country-specific albums must **not** be merged into one first-year master. This Fandome `album_id` follows the original Italian title and the **1971 Cinevox original ten-cue sequence**. Put 1970 Capitol in the caveats as a separate American release, not this row's year or its source of instrumental tags. The 20-track digital presentation marked 1970 is a later ℗2008 issue, not a first vinyl date. Sources BIRD_AMS, BIRD_BAN, BIRD_STC, BIRD_US.

**Genres.** Label explicitly describes contemporary avant-garde/improvisation-derived modernist writing set against simple, identifiable melodic themes. Critic Jonathan Broxton details an experimental/dissonant side together with `jazz`, `lounge`, some `pop`, a specific `bossanova` rhythm on *Non rimane più nessuno*, and light `rock` guitars on *Violenza inattesa*. Use `avantgarde` and `experimental` as essential score qualities but **not as every cue's exclusive genre**; the remaining specific styles use some-tracks scope. Do not add `giallo` as a sonic genre without source treating it as such; here it is cinema context. Sources BIRD_AMS, BIRD_BROX.

**Instruments, individually sourced.** On the canonical original ten cues, Broxton specifies `organ`, vibraphone (`mallets` Fandome token), wind chimes (`chimes`), jazz `drums`, muted `trumpet`, frantic `keyboards`, trembling `strings`, jazz `saxophone`, rock guitars (`guitar`) and other `percussion`. No `synth` is claimed without specific instrument credit, no `tapeeffects`/`sampling` inferred merely from metallic sounds, and no `overdubbing` deduced from Edda's vocal layers. Source BIRD_BROX. The reviewer covers an expanded later pressing but all cited cue titles are **1–10 of the original Cinevox 1971 LP**, independently confirmed by BIRD_AMS. Do not base tags on 1998 bonus cues 11/12.

**Voice/lyrics.** Edda Dell’Orso's wordless lullaby-like and breathy/distressed vocalise appears amid instrumental score, with low vocal grunts and gasps reported on *Silenzio nel caos*. `instrumental|soft` captures predominantly instrumental cues and wordless light soprano; nonlexical grunts and gasps are **not** tagged `spoken`, because no intelligible speech is documented. The very brief *Se sei stonato...* is described as a drinking song but without a verified original 1971 LP lyrical transcript; `language` and `lyrics` are blank instead of inferring Italian from its title. Sources BIRD_BROX, BIRD_AMS.

**8 scales.** Energy 3 (punctuated drum attacks amidst meditative lullabies); electronic 1 (amplified organ and guitars but no demonstrated synthesizer); mood 1 (ominous violence with deceptively warm melodic cues); theatrical 4 (sharp terror gestures); experimental 5 (radical abstract/noise and vocal timbre juxtaposition); polish **empty** (no benchmark from original recording); tempo 2 (many slow suspense textures); groove 1 (some bossa/pop, predominantly non-dance tension). Sources BIRD_AMS/BIRD_BROX. No original producer documented. `sounds_like` Penderecki and Bruno Nicolai is an **editorial similarity hypothesis**, not documented influence; `influenced_by` blank. Landmark=1 as Argento's first directorial giallo score and widely discussed modernist work.

## Cross-phase and chronological audit: 1981 LP anomaly

The previously previewed *Indagine su un cittadino al di sopra di ogni sospetto* (1970 Elio Petri film) has an important distinction: original **1970 Cinevox MDF 016 was a 45rpm with only two themes**, while specialist publisher **Quartet Records (2025) expressly confirms there was NO standalone LP before simultaneous French and US 1981 releases**. Do not fabricate a 1970 album from the film or later digital release dating. Place its `morricone-indaginesuuncittadinoaldisopradiognisospetto` soundtrack row in the chronological **1981** batch. For Phase 03, substitute the independently attested original **1969** soundtrack album *L'assoluto naturale*, which falls within the correct period and satisfies the four-album phase size. Quartet source: https://quartetrecords.com/product/indagine-su-un-cittadino-al-di-sopra-di-ogni-sospetto/ ; SoundtrackCollector LP discography: https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/22117/Indagine%2BSu%2BUn%2BCittadino%2BAl%2BDi%2BSopra%2BDi%2BOgni%2BSospetto .

## Cautions about research quality

- Historical 1969 scores with unspecified release **months** are chronologically tied at year precision; **the intra-1969 ordering does not assert an exact day**. *Metti* has December independently attested.
- Audio-based claims are from specialist published cue descriptions; this is not an independent full-session score transcription. `confidence=high` concerns support for the selected main values, not the completeness of studio session documentation.
- Composer / orchestrator / conductor / vocal guests are not recording producers. Empty `producers` and `production` are **deliberate abstentions**, not missing work.
- An expanded posthumous reissue may reveal more instruments or machine models but does **not** make those additions valid for original-era album programmes.
- `sounds_like` suggests sonically close composers for algorithmic similarity. These are editorial comparisons and must not be reclassified as `influenced_by`.
- No collaboration/co-billed album credit verified. The current branch already contains phase 01 and 02 rows; this file strictly adds four distinct albums.
