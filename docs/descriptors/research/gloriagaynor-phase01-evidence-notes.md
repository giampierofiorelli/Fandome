# Gloria Gaynor — Phase 01: evidence and editorial decisions

Research date: 28 October 2026. Original album editions only; recordings from later remasters and additional single mixes do not define the original LP. The numerical descriptors are Fandome editorial judgments, not empirical values derived from reviews. The genre evidence sheet records the scope and ranking of every token. Artist-level influences are documented in CLAUDE_IMPORT but not silently copied into individual album rows.

## Never Can Say Goodbye (1975)

- **Sound and editions:** The original MGM LP has eight songs: *Honey Bee*, *Never Can Say Goodbye*, *Reach Out, I'll Be There*, then five shorter tracks. AllMusic praises side A's three-track uninterrupted mix as an early disco-album innovation, while characterising side B as more conventional R&B and Northern soul. The producer/mixer's continuous sequence should not be confused with a later dance remix, remaster or an album recorded live. `disco|soul|rnb|northernsoul|downtempo`: the final two genre terms describe secondary material; `downtempo` rests on UK pressing metadata rather than a description of the whole LP.
- **Credited performance:** Schwartzberg (drums), Babbitt (bass), Mironov, Friedman and Quinn (guitars), Rebillot (keys/electric piano), and Martin (congas) are named in original-release credit transcriptions and the album's personnel compilation. No explicitly documented synth or drum-machine model. `electronic=0` means the cited source instrumentation is predominantly live/acoustic or electrically amplified instruments, NOT a claim that the record was never electronically processed.
- **Producers:** The track-level Qobuz label credits settle an otherwise confusing four-producer list. **Paul Leka produced *Honey Bee* (A1) only; Tony Bongiovi, Meco Monardo and Jay Ellis produced the remainder.** Original-pressing databases sometimes omit Paul Leka because most tracks carry the other trio. Since Fandome lists every actually credited producer, all four names belong in the album row. **Tom Moulton is credited for mixing**, not production. `production` is empty: continuous mixing is clear, but `remixing` would misleadingly imply a subsequent remix, and no specific allowed technology is documented.
- **Songs and relationships:** Tracks sample no known record as a documented mechanism; versions of Jackson 5 and Four Tops songs are **covers**, not evidence that those artists directly influenced Gaynor's recording style. `sounds_like` contains limited editorial disco-era parallels; `influenced_by` remains empty absent first-person album-period evidence.
- **Scores:** High dance-floor groove and energy on side A, more sentimental side B. `experimental=3` for uninterrupted LP sequencing, not a claim of avant-garde composition. `polish=4` for coordinated studio arrangements; `mood=3` averages exuberant and yearning material. The album is a landmark owing to the historically early nonstop disco medley.

## Experience Gloria Gaynor (1975)

- **Scope:** Eight original songs, not 2010 remastered bonus singles. Side A runs *Casanova Brown*, *(If You Want It) Do It Yourself* and *How High the Moon* as one continuous disco suite. Side B includes *What'll I Do*, *I'm Still Yours* and *Walk On By*. AllMusic explicitly contrasts the disco suite with slower **Northern soul** material. The origin of *How High the Moon* as a jazz standard does **not** automatically add a jazz album genre; this is a disco interpretation rather than a jazz arrangement.
- **Instruments:** Source personnel lists the electric rhythm section (Schwartzberg, Babbitt, guitar players and Rebillot), Martin's congas, Maelen percussion, Tony Posk string section and a brass/reed group (Rubin, Russo, Andre, Taylor, Del Gatto). `production=stringarrangements` only because both strings and credited arrangements are documented; not `hornarrangements` without a separately specified horn arranger. No exact synth or effects-model proof.
- **Production:** Meco Monardo, Tony Bongiovi and Jay Ellis are the producers. Tom Moulton made the mix, not a producer. Recording locations (Mediasound, Dimensional Sound, Track Recorders) are supported by AllMusic and original pressing listing. `electronic=0` indicates a live ensemble with modern studio mix; avoid extrapolating synthesizers, digital technology, or drum machines.
- **Genre/scales:** `disco|soul|rnb|northernsoul`; long up-tempo first side balanced by slower, generally melancholy songs. `groove=4` rather than 5 since half the album is explicitly less danceable; tonal average `mood=2`. `sounds_like` are comparators, not documented album influences.

## I've Got You (1976)

- **Scope:** Third original album, first under Polydor after MGM. Eight original tracks; no 2015 reissue extras. The continuous side A places *Let's Make a Deal*, *I've Got You Under My Skin* (Cole Porter) and *Be Mine* together, whereas side B includes less club-driven soul performances. AllMusic calls it disco-soul and explicitly **Northern soul** for the five second-side cuts. A Porter standard is a **cover**, not a sign that Cole Porter is the artist's direct musical influence.
- **Instruments:** From published personnel: drums (Schwartzberg/Young), bass (Babbitt), guitar players (Quinn/Friedman/Mironov), keyboards (Rebillot), congas (Martin), trumpet, trombone, French horns and reeds, plus Tony Posk strings. Thus strings/horn instrumentation is evidence-backed. The unusual *dishpan drums* credit for Bongi refers to percussion, but a literal new instrument token is unnecessary until source corroboration of its sound; no machine brand was added.
- **Production:** Meco Monardo, Tony Bongiovi, Jay Ellis, with Moulton credited only as **mixer**. String arrangements are included conservatively given credited ensemble and arrangers. Original mixes stay separate from later remastered CD material. No verified specific tape, sampler or drum machine.
- **Scores:** Disco side remains rhythmically forward but mood mixes romance and reflective slow performances. Moderate theatrical score and lower experimental score than the debut: this repeats a recognised formula, not a further technical innovation. The AllMusic review is lukewarm about the album's consistency; the sound descriptors need not duplicate its judgment.

## Glorious (1977)

- **Scope:** Fourth studio LP, eight original tracks. The nine-track display on some AllMusic versions adds the **single edit of *Most of All***; exclude that duplicate when judging the original LP. Unlike the previous three, this album **does not** divide its first side into a continuous dance medley; ballads and faster tracks are interleaved.
- **Producers and instrumentation:** **Gregg Diamond and Joe Beck only** are credited producers. Original Polydor LP credit transcription identifies electric piano/Fender Rhodes, clavinet, keyboards, acoustic piano, guitar, bass, drums, percussion, saxophones, trumpets, trombones and string players, plus female backing singers. `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` comes from the documented orchestration and brass/strings ensembles; avoid naming any synthesizer or production unit not in sources. Joe Beck is a **guitarist and producer**, not an influence, and Gregg Diamond's prior projects are also not influences.
- **Genre placement:** AllMusic specifically labels **disco** and broader **R&B**, and describes **disco-soul** songs. Secondary genre `dance` is broader metadata from another album-specific entry; not equal to a newer dance-pop or electronic dance genre. A dealer's genre family *Funk / Soul* is a marketplace umbrella and is **not** sufficient to assert whole-album `funk`, so it is omitted. Jazz musician Joe Beck participating does not make the record a jazz album.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic critic Alex Henderson finds the new songwriting less compelling than the previous LPs, but recognises the altered arrangement and format; differences in taste do not alter band/producer facts. Midtempo *Most of All*, orchestration and ballads justify an energy and groove below peak disco material, while strong singing keeps theatricality high.

## Research limits / review actions

- The public GitHub branch has no Gloria Gaynor catalog or seed listing and no `docs/descriptors/gloriagaynor.csv` (as checked). These rows propose identifiers; Claude must reconcile with any private, unmerged work before import.
- `sounds_like` are independent editorial suggestions based on era and genre; sources do **not** assert one-to-one album sonic equivalence. `influenced_by` remains empty on all four albums because the direct Gaynor interviews document singer-level formative influences, not these four particular records.
- Avoid genre-inflation from generic *Funk / Soul* Discogs category, or adding `jazz` because a singer covered a jazz standard. Original track sequence and label-level producer credits outrank later compilation/reissue databases.
- Vocal registers are a listening-informed descriptor consistent with the cited reviews; vocal timbre itself is partly subjective. No attempt to claim exact studio signal processing or source-specific reverb.
