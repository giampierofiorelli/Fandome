# Kraftwerk — Phase 02: album-specific research notes

**Research date:** 2026-10-25. **Scope:** exactly four new studio-album rows (1975, 1977, 1978, 1981), no repetition of Phase 01's Kraftwerk (1970), Kraftwerk 2 (1972), Ralf und Florian (1973), or Autobahn (1974). Source snippets paraphrased, not copied. 0–5 numbers are editorial judgments with sources about each **whole LP**; genre vocabulary covers all album-named categories while documenting weak and track-only cases. `influenced_by` requires source-backed musical inspiration, never confused with later artists influenced BY Kraftwerk or a producer credit.

## Radio-Activity (1975) — `kraftwerk-radioactivity`

**Edition**: 1975 German *Radio-Aktivität* and international *Radio-Activity* carry equivalent **bilingual original recordings**, not a new studio album for each sleeve language [R-LANG, R-SLEEVE, R-DIS]. Use existing catalog ID and English catalog title. Original running order: 12 tracks; short radio intermissions count. Do not substitute the 1991 *The Mix* anti-nuclear revision with names of nuclear incidents and different lyrics. Album 1975 nuclear/radio-broadcast pun does **not** automatically authorize `political` as the dominant theme; `social|irony` is a cautious editorial interpretation. Note disagreement: some 1975 lyrics can sound ambiguous or celebratory, others hear implicit anti-nuclear caution [R-DIS,R-LANG].

**Credits**: Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider produce, Karl Bartos and Wolfgang Flür on electronic percussion. Peter Bollig technical engineer; Walter Quintus sound engineer and mixing. **Conny Plank is not a producer on this record**, unlike early albums [R-SLEEVE,R-DIS].

**Genres / sources**: electronic and experimental electronic whole-record [R-AM,R-DIS], avant-garde and krautrock AllMusic categorization; original title track electro-pop and Antenna proto-synth-pop [R-AM,R-DIS]. `dance` and `poprock` are database umbrella genres, annotated low. `progressiveelectronic` and `minimalsynth` are retrospectively named AOTY genres. Ghostly ambient texture is described but **ambient was not included as a genre without explicit classification**. Do not overrepresent isolated moments.

**Scores** (album-wide editorial): energy 2, slow sections/collages predominate over Antenna; electronics 5 because original LP dispenses with guitars, flute and conventional instruments; mood 1 based on unusually melancholy, eerie atmosphere [R-PF,R-DIS]; theatrical 2 because the vocals are distant and detached, with robotic special effects; experimental 4 for static/silence, shortwave collage, brief song structures; polish 3 for deliberate textural haze, not smooth studio sheen; tempo 2 and groove 2 because there are some clear electronic pulses but much of the LP is not a continuous beat [R-PF,R-DIS]. `voice=soft|spoken|instrumental` captures subdued Hütter voice, robotic speech and multiple instrumental/interstitial tracks. Both German and English appear on originals. The influence column stays blank; critics comparing track silence to John Cage support `sounds_like` at most, not a documented source of compositional inspiration.

## Trans-Europe Express (1977) — `kraftwerk-transeuropeexpress`

**Edition**: 1977 German *Trans Europa Express* with **eight indexed titles** (including separate `Abzug`) and international *Trans-Europe Express* with **seven indexed titles** (the equivalent passage combined into `Metal on Metal`); these are the same underlying studio album and must not generate duplicate descriptors. German original title is **not** a 2009 re-recording [T-ORIG,T-LP,T-DISCO]. `language=de|en` records original variants, not simultaneous bilingual vocals in every song (in contrast to *Radio-Activity*). Maintain existing normalized ID from `catalog.ts`.

**Credits**: producers and album concept Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider. Peter Bollig, Thomas Kukuck and Bill Halverson are **engineers**; Karl Bartos and Wolfgang Flür electronic percussion, Emil Schult lyricist/artwork [T-ORIG,T-LP]. No Conny Plank production.

**Style / scales**: album largely synthesis and electronic percussion, so electronics 5; energy 3 and tempo 3 for consistent moderate chug of train suite balanced by slow reflectiveness; mood 3 as *Europe Endless* optimistic and nostalgic but *Hall of Mirrors* melancholy, self-alienated; theatrical 2 for restrained detached voice; experimental 3 as repetitive/minimal structural invention is paired with lucid melodic hooks; polish 4 for refined conceptual studio detail; groove 3 because drive is pronounced on suite but not 5 dancefloor [T-AM,T-PF,T-SONG]. The album is no louder/more danceable than *The Man-Machine*. `voice=spoken|clear|instrumental`, includes robotic repetitions, partly sung lyrics and instrumental sides. Lyrical domains: `identity|introspection|social|irony` for public self-image, glass/mirrors, European travel/unity and mannequin irony; **not** songs about modern social media. `sounds_like` sonic comparisons remain editorial.

**Genre scope**: AllMusic `electronic|experimentalelectronic|krautrock|avantgarde|poprock`, original LP genre catalog `electro|synthpop`, and lower-scope `industrial` on some retroactive issue tags [T-AM,T-LP]. `progressiveelectronic` and `minimalsynth` are retroactive AOTY album-level tags. Ambient and disco analogies apply to passages, but these tags were **excluded** for lack of reliable album-specific genre naming [T-AOTY,T-PF]. `influenced_by=Franz Schubert` is **only** justified as a direct named composer tribute in album track 'Franz Schubert' [T-AM]; it is **not** proof of harmonic plagiarism or the sole artist influence on other six songs. Claude can blank it if the stricter song-specific influence requirement is applied.

## The Man-Machine (1978) — `kraftwerk-themanmachine`

**Edition**: 1978 *Die Mensch-Maschine* original Kling Klang six tracks versus *The Man-Machine* international 1978 six-track album with rerecorded English lyrics. Preserve one Fandome album ID, title *The Man-Machine*. Do not double the discography with *Die Mensch-Maschine*. Original 1978 credited LP producers Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider [M-ORIG,M-LANG]. Joschko Rudas and Leanard Jackson are engineers/mixers, **not** producers; Karl Bartos musical coauthor, not credited producer [M-LP].

**Sound**: AllMusic documents more pop hooks and a more danceable arrangement than 1977, heavily electronic percussion/synth architecture and vocoder sounds [M-AM]. Pitchfork instead calls this the *lushest* and somewhat less driving of the group’s albums, with `Neon Lights`, `Spacelab` and the title track providing restraint; both accounts inform moderate **energy 3** and groove **4** rather than a universal club-driven 5 [M-PF]. `electronic=5` fully electronic sources; mood 3 contrasts playful mannequin pop, tender night cityscapes and mechanical chill; theatrical 3 includes robot personas and staged voices but impersonal singing; experimental 3, as formally conceived futurism remains unconventional but pop songs are accessible; polish 4 for lush, synthetic recording; tempo 3 across six tracks [M-AM,M-PF]. `voice=spoken|clear|instrumental`, including vocoder chant, conventional simple sung Model and significant instrumentals.

**Lyrics**: identity and social relationship between man and machine, cynicism and `irony` of modelling/celebrity, and modern city life; not a literal advocacy of artificial intelligence as an ethically neutral concept. `Fritz Lang`/1920s film *Metropolis* inspired the imagery and thematic environment [M-PF] but the guide's influence column requires documented musical artist influence; leave `influenced_by` blank. `spacedisco|progressiveelectronic` retrospectively sourced by AOTY, weaker scope; AllMusic `experimentalrock|protopunk` are **database style tags only**, and cannot be taken as evidence of punk guitar. These two are new token proposals, and Claude must decide whether the very weak album-level taxonomy meets source standard [M-AM].

## Computer World (1981) — `kraftwerk-computerworld`

**Edition and credits**: 1981 original seven tracks; *Computerwelt* German album and *Computer World* English album same underlying release, do not create two descriptors [C-ORIG,C-RA]. Produce credit Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider (original/2009 metadata), with Karl Bartos as electronic percussion and credited composer, **not an album producer** [C-AOTY,C-ORIG]. Original sleeve's 'Hardware'/'Software' listing is not the credit for musicians or computer program developers. Avoid adding 2009 remasters or 1991 live/remix edits.

**Sound/scales**: electronic=5 on synthesizers, vocoder, primitive voice synthesis, sampled calculator and fully electronic percussion [C-AM,C-RA,C-EAM]. `energy=3` for pulsing and more forceful Numbers balanced against gentle Computer Love; `mood=3` for warm, mischievous calculator and techno-optimism, contrasted with data-surveillance anxiety and lonely love song; `theatrical=2` deadpan, processed rather than stage-filling projection; `experimental=3` minimal synthesis and speech technology inside short accessible song structures; `polish=5` due unusually sharp, clear and meticulously sculpted sonic definition explicitly highlighted by expert electronic musician Kirk Degiorgio; `tempo=3`, `groove=4` because the rhythmic core has a strong b-boy/electro drive yet also non-dance wistful sections [C-RA,C-PF,C-DIS]. Voices are mainly robotic/spoken, occasional soft melody and many instrumental/effects passages. `lyrics=social|love|irony|introspection` includes computing's surveillance, loneliness/Computer Love, calculator humor. Do not use generic political just because 'Interpol and Deutsche Bank' are named.

**Genres**: RA expressly tags Electro/Experimental/Pop and AllMusic electronic/electro/synthpop/electronic-experimental/club-dance. AOTY adds minimal synth and progressive electronic; RA critic describes a Moroder-like disco groove and funky robots **as sound texture**, not evidence of an influence credited by Kraftwerk. The term techno-pop appears for Kraftwerk more generally but was **excluded** without album-specific corroboration. AllMusic/RA and critics provide strong album-wide evidence for `electro`, `synthpop`, `electronic`, `dance` and `pop`, weaker support for `disco` and `funk`. `influenced_by` blank: **Planet Rock** sampled/was influenced by Kraftwerk *after* this LP, so Bambaataa is not a Kraftwerk influence [C-RA].

## Risks / follow-up validation

- German vs international English vocal versions: `language=de|en` for catalog-level album concept, but tracks sometimes differ. Do not infer each song was bilingual. *Radio-Activity* is a distinct special case, already bilingual in original tapes.
- `ambient` on *Radio-Activity*, `ambient|disco` on *Trans-Europe Express*, and `technopop` on *Computer World* are only descriptive extrapolations without strong album-specific source genre naming and are deliberately **not** included. R/T receive source-named `minimalsynth|progressiveelectronic` from retrospective AOTY album taxonomy; these are explicitly not contemporary genre labels.
- `experimentalrock` and `protopunk` on *The Man-Machine* derive **solely** from AllMusic broad database style labels, not an LP actually played with punk instrumentation. Very low-confidence; maintain user's exhaustive-genre rule, but these two may need review before inclusion in enum.
- Mood/energy/theatrical categories can be interpretive; there is no numerical expert survey. The conservative scale anchors matter more than single cut extremes.
- `Franz Schubert` appears in one track on *Trans-Europe Express*, with AllMusic explicit tribute. If a recording-specific rather than composition-homage test is required for influences, delete this token. Fritz Lang is a cinematic/visual *Man-Machine* reference, deliberately not inserted into music-artist `influenced_by`.
