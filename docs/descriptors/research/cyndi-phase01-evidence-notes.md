# Cyndi Lauper — Fandome Phase 01: evidence and reasoning

Research freeze: **2026-10-25**. Source URLs and provenance are in `cyndi_phase01_sources.csv`; each genre is independently indexed in `cyndi_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Values are **editorial album-level judgements, not measured acoustics**. A source documenting a particular track does not automatically establish album-wide dominance.

## 1983 — She’s So Unusual (`cyndi-shessounusual`)

- **Genres:** Pitchfork (Arielle Gordon, 2024) explicitly calls it new-wave pop and describes Lauper's ambition to combine synth-pop with ska and reggae, as well as a synthesizer-led disco sound and a rockabilly-derived single. Herb Music identifies new wave, pop rock and synthpop. Reggae, ska, rockabilly and disco are ordered after dominant pop/new-wave because the evidence is localized to songs or sound components. Pitchfork's Pop/R&B top-level label justifies R&B at low weight; it is not an album's dominant identity.
- **Production:** Rick Chertoff credited as producer; Qobuz/Sony describes William Wittman as **co-producer**, though MusicBrainz's release-level record says *associate producer*. Keep both in `producers` provisionally and ask Claude to prioritise original LP credits. **Lennie Petze is an executive producer, not a producer** here. Pitchfork specifically identifies the *Memorymoog* keyboard on “Girls Just Want to Have Fun” and forceful belted singing, justifying `memorymoog` and `belting`. This does not prove that Memorymoog was present throughout the LP. Do not assert Auto-Tune, LinnDrum, DAW, gated reverb or sampling without a contemporary album source.
- **Instruments:** Synthesizers, keys, guitars, bass, drums, percussion and saxophone documented by Sony/AllMusic credits; a melodica/“hooter” is identified for Rob Hyman/Eric Bazilian in liner-related musician credits and Pitchfork. Piano is described in the “Time After Time” composition/arrangement account. Treat these as participants / album textures rather than every-track lineup.
- **Lyrics / voice:** Songs shift from independence and pleasure (“Girls Just Want to Have Fun” / “She Bop”) to separation (“Time After Time”). `raspy` reflects the sometimes biting rough edge in her delivery; her upper-register strength is documented by contemporary accounts and critical retrospectives.
- **Scales (E4/Elec3/Mood4/Theat4/Exper2/Polish4/Tempo3/Groove4):** Frequent upbeat full-band and synth-driven songs set energy and groove high, tempered by ballads. Album is neither acoustic nor fully synthesised. Distinctive performance is theatrical; songs remain structurally accessible. Polished mainstream multitrack production.
- **Influences:** Pitchfork explicitly attributes Lauper's sought mix to **The Clash** and **Grace Jones**, and reports **Roy Orbison** as inspiration for the hiccuping vocal attack on “Girls”. These are not inferred from a cover or similarity. **Sounds-like** comparisons are independent editorial proposals, not cited influence claims.
- **Disagreement:** Credit databases disagree on William Wittman's production role; archive/label co-producer credit is favored while flagging it. “Rockabilly” is partly an origin/track reference, not an album-wide genre.

## 1986 — True Colors (`cyndi-truecolors`)

- **Genres:** AllMusic assigns new wave and adult contemporary; Album of the Year lists pop rock and synthpop. Official Sony history documents soul repertoire (“What's Going On”, “Iko Iko”); the record remains predominately pop/new wave rather than an entire soul album. Dance-pop relies on secondary genre metadata and is comparatively lower certainty.
- **Production / instruments:** **Cyndi Lauper and Lennie Petze** produced. David Wolff is credited **executive producer only**. Sony/Qobuz credits keyboards, guitars, bass, percussion and Jimmy Bralower's drum parts; detailed musician credits also attribute **LinnDrum programming** and Peter Wood synth bass. `linndrum` is included on personnel-derived evidence, **not** from a generic 1980s sound assumption; a secondary album personnel list is the strongest currently accessible explicit model reference. The Bangles, Billy Joel, Aimee Mann are *guests, not producers*.
- **Lyrics / voice:** Personal authenticity, tolerance and acceptance in title track; relationship introspection elsewhere; social commentary in cover of Marvin Gaye's “What's Going On”. `spiritual` denotes affirmative moral / emotional conviction rather than religious doctrine, and is tentative. `soft` describes its quieter moments; `powerful` still captures the overall delivery.
- **Scales (E3/Elec3/Mood3/Theat3/Exper2/Polish4/Tempo3/Groove3):** Energetic tracks plus slower emotive tracks make this more balanced than debut; a polished hybrid ensemble with drum programming and substantial live players.
- **Disagreement:** Terry Atkinson (Los Angeles Times, 1986) criticised busy, percussion-heavy arrangements; AllMusic (Eugene Chadbourne) finds the ambitious stylistic excursions mixed but sometimes effective. No attempt to disguise the disagreement as consensus. Marvin Gaye is **covered**; no verified direct statement that he specifically shaped production of the album, so `influenced_by` is empty.

## 1989 — A Night to Remember (`cyndi-anighttoremember`)

- **Genres:** AllMusic calls it **adult contemporary** and Pop/Rock; secondary album genre listing uses **pop rock**; TiVo/Stephen Thomas Erlewine on Qobuz describes deliberate mainstream polished sound. Avoid treating a synth part or isolated funk guest as enough to label the whole record synthpop/funk.
- **Production / instruments:** Sessiondays transcription of liner notes credits **Cyndi Lauper and Lennie Petze** on most songs and **Phil Ramone** on selected tracks. **Eric Thorngren is credited as associate producer**, and is therefore excluded from the producer column even though another summary calls him a producer. AllMusic credits keyboards, guitar, bass, drums, drum machine, accordion, saxophone and **Coral Sitar** (specific electric sitar-style guitar). Sessiondays explicitly assigns Linn-drums to Joe Bellia. Use `coralsitar`, not an ungrounded conventional `sitar`, and flag as a new token. Sax and accordion are localised colors, not driving forces.
- **Lyrics / voice:** Dominantly relationship / breakup / yearning, with “I Drove All Night” as the energetic outlier. `powerful`/`clear` applies to adult-pop vocals; no evidence to use autotune or any specific electronic vocal treatment.
- **Scales (E3/Elec2/Mood2/Theat3/Exper1/Polish4/Tempo3/Groove2):** Strong ballad and guitar/keyboard-pop emphasis, elaborately polished but less rhythmically club-oriented than early material. Electronic-ness is conservative because instrument credits indicate heavy acoustic/live presence.
- **Disagreement:** AllMusic's Erlewine criticises over-elaborate arrangements and a strained claim to sophistication, despite praising “I Drove All Night”; the singing and musicianship themselves are supported by source credits. Standard international editions vary: short opening “Intro” and extra “Hole in My Heart” are not on all original issues. The descriptor refers to the **core 1989 LP**, not a later bonus configuration.

## 1993 — Hat Full of Stars (`cyndi-hatfullofstars`)

- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly highlights **folk** and **soul**, while Album of the Year classifies the full album in pop, contemporary R&B, adult contemporary, dance-pop and hip-hop soul. Los Angeles Times notes a sophisticated accessible pop core. Lower-weight genres should not be treated as evidence of a pure R&B or folk project. `poprock` is secondary, supported by AllMusic's broad Contemporary Pop/Rock classification; treat as low weight. `rnb` is a broader genre appearing alongside contemporary R&B in classification sources.
- **Production:** Sony/Qobuz attributes producing to **Cyndi Lauper**, **Junior Vasquez** and **William Wittman** (Qobuz metadata can roll up album-wide credits across individual tracks; Junior Vasquez is not necessarily the producer of every title). Drum programming is explicitly credited to Jimmy Bralower, Eric Bazilian, Peter Wood and Joey Moskowitz. `drumprogramming` is a NEW technique token and is deliberately chosen in preference to assuming a specific drum machine. No unverified sampler/DAW or analog-tape claim.
- **Instruments:** Label credits list keyboards, guitar, bass, acoustic drums, piano, organ, accordion, mandolin, saxophone, harmonica, percussion, flugelhorn and dulcimer; last two are NEW `flugelhorn` and `dulcimer` tokens. Do **not** map flugelhorn to trumpet or generic dulcimer to hammered dulcimer without information. Detailed musical contributors include Hugh Masekela and Bakithi Kumalo; neither is a producer solely because of their contributions.
- **Lyrics / voice:** Songs deal with racism (“A Part Hate”), abuse, women's reproductive choice (“Sally's Pigeons”), and personal vulnerability. Not every track is a political manifesto. Strong expressive lead and some roughened intensity remain, with restrained passages.
- **Scales (E3/Elec3/Mood2/Theat3/Exper3/Polish4/Tempo3/Groove3):** A deliberate mix of organic ensemble and dance-production loops, uneven in mood, diverse in genre; higher experimental score reflects stylistic combinations rather than extreme avant-garde technique.
- **Critical disagreement:** **Holly George-Warren (Rolling Stone, Sept 1993)** praised its maturity, range and topical concerns; **Mike DeGagne (AllMusic)** criticised the stylistic sprawl and didactic tone. **Jean Rosenbluth (Los Angeles Times)** welcomed its tuneful, mature songwriting. These assessments are kept distinct. Critical reception must not be conflated with audience sales.

## Cross-album constraints

- Do not copy genre tags wholesale across the catalogue: later albums are stylistically different.
- `sounds_like` entries are proposals made on sonic proximity, never sourced historical influence. `influenced_by` is blank without an explicit supported artistic or production link.
- No claim about specific instruments or production technologies is derived solely from a style label.
- Production credit may be different on an original edition, a 2010s reissue, or streaming metadata; the caveats above are import-review priorities.
- The sole `notes` column is under the 15-word limit on every row; evidence descriptions here are intentionally longer.
