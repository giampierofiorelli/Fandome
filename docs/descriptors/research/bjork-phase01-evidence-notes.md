# Björk — Phase 01: evidence notes

## Research / scoring conventions
- Four records, 1977–1995, assessed against the Fandome guide. Eight scales are **editorial album-wide judgments** grounded in cited record descriptions; they are not objective measurements. In contrast, **instruments and production require positive attribution** from credits, interviews or accounts.
- Genre evidence CSV distinguishes **whole album**, **some tracks**, and **texture**; track-only genres are intentionally ranked after dominant styles. Records/sources labelled as reissues or secondary are explicitly flagged.
- `sounds_like` is independent comparative editorial judgment, **not documented influence**. `influenced_by` is empty unless a qualified primary account links musical taste/influence to the specific album/period. Labels such as ‘produced’ are not inferred from engineering/arranging.

## Björk — *Björk* (1977), `bjork-bjork`
- **Status/discography**: first solo album under Björk Guðmundsdóttir, originally Icelandic Fálkinn 1977 vinyl/cassette, often excluded from the adult discography beginning with *Debut*. MusicBrainz original release [B], AllMusic [A]; unofficial CD releases and uncertain versions [C]. `landmark=0` means not a defining *adult-era* album, not that it is unimportant biographically.
- **Genre**: AllMusic explicitly gives *Children's Pop* [A], mapped to new `childrenspop`, its broader family `childrensmusic` and `pop`. `novelty` is secondary [D] and must receive lower evidential weighting. Do not claim this is her mature art-pop idiom. The genre inclusion follows the user’s rule: sources' specific genre even when not predefined.
- **Instrumentation**: Original-release track credits [B] identify piano (Kristján Guðmundsson), guitar (Björgvin Gíslason / Sævar Árnason), bass (Pálmi Gunnarsson), drums (Sigurður Karlsson), organ (Pétur Hjaltested), flute (Björk), harmonica (Guðmundur Ingólfsson), xylophone (mapped `mallets`), and percussion; **no synthesisers or specific recording techniques claimed**.
- **Production**: uncredited/uncertain as *music producer* in consulted original-release data [B]; **empty producer and production cells**, even though Sævar facilitated the album. Arranging, composing or facilitating does not qualify as producer.
- **Voice**: `clear|soft` reflects 11-year-old singer (AllMusic [A]), not adult voice range; comparisons and specific influences left empty.
- **Scales**: energy 2, electronic 0, mood 4, theatrical 2, experimental 0, polish 2, tempo 2, groove 1 reflect children’s arrangements with band and mainly melodic covers; these are tentative; confidence medium. `lyrics=storytelling` from children's repertoire, not single canonical narrative. Language Icelandic, based on original tracks.
- **Critic disagreement**: AllMusic [A] finds it primarily historical/juvenile, while a discography lists it as a studio album. Preserve both facts, not a ‘real debut vs fake debut’ binary.

## *Gling-Gló* (1990), `bjork-glingglo`
- **Credit and edition**: Exact co-billing *Björk Guðmundsdóttir & Tríó Guðmundar Ingólfssonar*. Original 14-track studio album, mostly Icelandic standards [F,G], distinct from later 16-track editions adding **two English rehearsal recordings** from 1989 [F]. `language=is` applies to **original issue**; if importing 16-track edition, language becomes `is|en` and note edition accordingly.
- **Genre**: AllMusic [F] explicitly calls vocal jazz, jazz and standards. Discogs UK reissue [H] also tags swing and polka, so those genres are **some tracks**, low relative weight; do not turn every song into polka.
- **Music/production**: Trio credited piano (Guðmundur Ingólfsson), bass (Þórður Högnason), drums/percussion (Guðmundur Steingrímsson) [G]. Björk harmonica and occasional tambourine are documented in credits [G]. No machine/software is attributed, and being recorded together in a studio does not automatically prove a particular `liverecording` workflow. Hence blank `production`.
- **Producer**: Tómas Tómasson is attributed as producer in [I], while recording/mixing role independently appears in [G]. This **producer role is secondary-only** and should be checked against original sleeve before promotion to ‘reviewed’; we do not promote engineers on that basis alone.
- **Voice**: elastic, jazz-adjacent improvisational singing described by AllMusic [F]; main `clear|powerful`; no timbral rasp classification asserted without strong listening/criticism support. Mood 4, electronic 0, groove 3, experimental 1 and low-to-moderate polish are album-wide editorial judgments from review and ensemble form [F]. `landmark=0`: crossover career milestone but not one of her defining electronic solo albums.

## *Debut* (1993), `bjork-debut`
- **Edition**: `Debut` = 1993 international career solo debut, distinct from 1977 childhood record. Main standard album; Bandcamp [J] has additional *Play Dead* as a 12th track, so note release/territory. *There's More to Life Than This* is explicitly subtitled recorded live at the Milk Bar toilets [J], permitting `liverecording` as a **one-track** production tag.
- **Genre evidence**: Artist label [J] explicitly gives electronic pop, house, jazz, trip-hop. AllMusic [M] gives alternative dance, electronica, club/dance. [N] adds ambient-like atmosphere and world-music touches. `artpop` is supported by the artist/label genre tagging [J]; distinct from `electropop`. Jazz/ambient/world are **limited textures or tracks**, not dominant electronic club rhythm.
- **Instrumentation**: [K,L] credit programmed keyboards (Marius de Vries, Martin Virgo, Garry Hughes), live drums and percussion (Luis Jardim, Bruce Smith, Hooper), bass (Jardim), brass (Oliver Lake, Gary Barnacle, Mike Mower), harp (Corky Hale), tabla (Talvin Singh), Hammond organ, guitar, orchestral strings. `synth` and specific samplers left unasserted as session devices: programming does not automatically establish model/type.
- **Production**: `sampling` grounded by *Human Behaviour* sample relationship [K]; `liverecording` confined to track on standard listing [J]. No invention of Moog, Akai, Pro Tools etc. [L] notes Nellee Hooper primary and Björk on specific tracks; both credited producer/co-producer [J,K]. Excludes musician/programmers from producer list.
- **Influences**: Björk describes preferring DAF and Brian Eno around her formative electronic interests and her break with rock, interviewed retrospectively in [L]. This attests general musical taste but **not** clearly specific influence on *Debut*, so `influenced_by` remains empty. Her admiration for London club music [O] shapes context, not documentary proof each named act influenced songs.
- **Scales**: energy 3, electronic 3, mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. Contrasts between rhythmic *Big Time Sensuality* and acoustic/jazz ballads explain moderate rather than maximal dance score [J,M,N]. Critics historically disagreed about electronic production: [N] recounts early Rolling Stone scepticism versus retrospective praise.

## *Post* (1995), `bjork-post`
- **Genre**: Independent official listing [P] art-pop, electronic/dance plus techno, trip-hop, IDM, house, ambient, jazz, industrial; secondary discography [Z] explicitly lists avant-pop and experimental pop; review [T] highlights avant-dance and swing big-band exception. `bigband` is chiefly *It's Oh So Quiet*, `industrial` is concentrated in *Army of Me*/*Enjoy*; not uniformly distributed. It remains one highly eclectic album rather than multiple albums.
- **Instruments**: [Q] directly credits keyboards (Björk/Marius de Vries/Graham Massey/Tricky), strings/viola/cello, orchestral ensemble, brass/trumpet and saxophone, organ, Talvin Singh percussion, Guy Sigsworth harpsichord, Jim Couza hammered dulcimer, double bass on big-band track. [U] in Björk’s own words names additional bagpipes, saxophone, harpsichord, dulcimers, brass and orchestra. Novel instrument tokens: `hammereddulcimer`, `bagpipes`. The special strings and big-band arrangements are documented, not a generic genre inference.
- **Production**: `sampling` explicitly credited on *Possibly Maybe* (Scanner sound sample) [Q]. Vocal recordings and studio work in the Bahamas/outdoors [T,U] are **not automatically `fieldrecordings`**, and do not establish an ‘entire album live’ process. Producers exactly Björk, Nellee Hooper, Graham Massey, Tricky, Howie B [P,R,Q]. Marius de Vries is programmer/arranger, not unambiguously album producer in reviewed credits; exclude. Eumir Deodato is strings arranger/conductor, not producer; exclude.
- **Documented influence**: Pitchfork critic [T] directly positions the album against melodic phrasing of Joni Mitchell and voice techniques of Meredith Monk. They qualify as critic-attributed lineage (`influenced_by`) **only**; this is not a statement that Björk said they directly inspired a particular song. *Edith Piaf* is a descriptive simile in the review and is not inserted as an influence.
- **Sounds_like**: editorial proximity to Tricky/808 State/Aphex Twin/Massive Attack reflects album-specific club/trip-hop textures; no source claim of direct influence. Compare them as sonic counterparts only.
- **Scales**: energy 3, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 4, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3; contrast intensive *Army of Me*, swing big-band *It's Oh So Quiet*, airy *You've Been Flirting Again*, *Possibly Maybe* and *Headphones* [T,V,W]. Note a historical disagreement: LA Times [W] praised hybrid imagination but questioned theatrical gestures; Pitchfork [T] sees this multiform theatricality as central to the record. No glossing over variation.
