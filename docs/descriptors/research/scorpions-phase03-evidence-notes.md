# Scorpions — Phase 03 — Album evidence and editorial decisions

Research date: 28 October 2026. Four released studio albums, strictly incremental after Phases 01 and 02. Follow the Fandome 27-column descriptor guide; numerical scores are **editorial judgments**. **Artist-wide influences do not carry over into `influenced_by` without album-specific first-person evidence.** This file contains no reproduced song lyrics.

## Love at First Sting (1984) — `scorpions-loveatfirststing`

**Release and producers.** [Official album discography](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/love-at-first-sting/) dates it 27 March 1984, with producer **Dieter Dierks**. He is not to be conflated with engineers Gerd Rautenbach / Mike Beiriger or mastering credits. Songwriting does not confer a producer credit. Recording and mixing took place at Dierks Studios; [original record annotation](https://musicbrainz.org/release/16e036d6-61b3-4532-bf70-f1740f704897) documents **32-track digital recording**. This warrants `digitalrecording` in production. The official original personnel also credit **Francis Buchholz with a Moog synthesizer** (Metal Archives specifies Moog Taurus), supporting subsidiary `synth` and named `moog` production hardware, not all-electronic music. `electronic=1` acknowledges this minor layer beneath dominant live rock instruments.

**Arrangement.** Electric lead/rhythm guitars dominate with bass and live drums, while acoustic guitar and slide guitar appear in [MusicBrainz track credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/dac357c1-899a-42c1-94df-49029ab6ba5d). [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/love-at-first-sting-mw0000195649) explicitly lists `hardrock|heavymetal|albumrock|arenarock|contemporarypoprock|hairmetal`; later styles are largely retrospective classifications. Rock You Like a Hurricane, Bad Boys Running Wild and Big City Nights offer hard, concise hooks. Still Loving You and Coming Home supply contrasting emotional/musical space. `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=3`, `polish=5`, `experimental=1`, `mood=3`, `theatrical=4` reflect the whole LP rather than one ballad.

**Qualifiers.** [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2014/10/1984-scorpions-love-first-sting/) notes both sonic precision and some fan dislike of the metallic/digitally polished sound; recording technology does not prove using a specific digital machine, and no such model was inserted. Some editions list uncredited Jimmy Bain and Bobby Rondinelli during aborted/early sessions. Their appearance on the finished masters is not established here: do not overwrite credited band personnel. `landmark=1` reflects the record's signature hits/role in the group's history. `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` empty absent sufficiently firm album-specific evidence.

## Savage Amusement (1988) — `scorpions-savageamusement`

**Release, players and effects.** Original date **16 April 1988** and last Dieter Dierks-produced Scorpions album: [official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/savage-amusement/). Matthias Jabs gets **voice box** guitar effect credit, normalized `talkbox` (not `vocoder` or a treated lead vocal). Main instruments include electric, acoustic and slide guitars, live bass and drums, with Lee Aaron guest backing vocals and Peter Baltes cameo intro vocals; those guests are neither producers nor `influenced_by` artists. Electric/acoustic guitar layering is documented in band credits. No reliable album-specific synth or drum machine credit, therefore `electronic=0` and no invented 1980s synth hardware.

**Genre and critical distinction.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/savage-amusement-mw0000195133) lists Hard Rock, Heavy Metal, Album Rock, Pop-Metal, Arena Rock. Apple Music [retrospective](https://music.apple.com/us/album/savage-amusement/1440766479) explicitly uses **hair-metal** and praises the ballad Believe in Love; AllMusic critic [Barry Weber](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r17533/review) describes a more formulaic, high-polish turn toward pop melody relative to Blackout. `sounds_like=Def Leppard` is **a professional critic's sonic analogy**, not a directly documented influence. Thus `influenced_by` is empty; `polish=5`, `experimental=1`, `energy=3`, `groove=3` distinguish it from the punchier 1984 predecessor. `landmark=0` is a comparative career-impact judgment, not a claim of insignificance.

**Token caveat.** `popmetal` is specifically named in AllMusic and is **not in** current `scenes.ts`, as it was already raised for Phase 02 Animal Magnetism. Add this token once to `src/data/scenes.ts` and `genreFamilies.ts`, or apply only after source/taxonomy confirmation. `hairmetal` already exists. No other new token is needed here.

## Crazy World (1990) — `scorpions-crazyworld`

**Producer correction.** Producer credit is **Keith Olsen and Scorpions**, supported by [MusicBrainz primary musician relations](https://musicbrainz.org/release/a2f422af-195f-4f83-9b31-73963e14b800) and by songwriter [Jim Vallance's archive](https://www.jimvallance.com/01-music-folder/songs-folder-may-27/pg-song-scorpions-crazy.html). The [official Scorpions page](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/crazy-world/) accidentally spells the same person **Keith Olson** once in the producer line and **Keith Olsen** in the next line: use the latter, standard real producer name. Do not add writer Vallance as album producer. Original date 6 November 1990. This ends the band's long Dieter Dierks partnership and is the final studio record with bassist Francis Buchholz.

**Instruments.** Official credits specifically include Jabs **talk box**, Koen van Baal and Jim Vallance on **keyboards**, acoustic/slide and electric guitars, bass, drums and numerous backing singers. So `production=talkbox`, `instruments` includes keyboard; the main lead remains Klaus Meine. Some contemporary release liner metadata records digital D.A.S.H. recording using Trident DI-AN and SSL consoles ([edition transcription](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Crazy_World/1388176)); `digitaltape` reflects a **reported recording medium** and should be reconfirmed against original sleeve before publication; no machine brand token inferred. `electronic=0` indicates predominantly amplified acoustic/electric rock performance, not absence of keyboard technology.

**Genre and themes.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/crazy-world-mw0000309561) gives hard rock, heavy metal, album rock, arena rock, contemporary pop/rock, hair metal. [Discographic overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazy_World_%28Scorpions_album%29) separately names pop metal; hence `popmetal` appears in long-tail position. AllMusic reviewer considers it heavier and less polished in character than Savage Amusement, yet melodic; Tease Me Please Me remains brisk, while Wind of Change and Send Me an Angel are major soft-song counterparts. The **social/political** classification comes principally from Wind of Change. According to [the band history](https://www.the-scorpions.com/band-2/), the song was conceived after the 1989 Moscow Music Peace Festival, **before** the November 1989 fall of the Berlin Wall. Do not claim the band wrote it about seeing that event. `landmark=1`.

## Face the Heat (1993) — `scorpions-facetheheat`

**Date and producers.** [Official group record](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/face-the-heat/) gives **21 September 1993**, produced by **Bruce Fairbairn and Scorpions**, engineered/mixed by Erwin Musper. AllMusic's broad May 1993 date is inconsistent: use the original release date the band documents. **Ralph Rieckermann** replaces Francis Buchholz on bass, and this is **Herman Rarebell's final studio album** with the group.

**Unusual credits.** Band's list names Rudolf Schenker **12-string acoustic guitar, sitar, Ebo (E-Bow)** as well as electric guitar; Jabs has lead/rhythm/slide/acoustic guitar and **talk box**; John Webster and Luke Herzog **keyboards**; Helen Donath is an **opera guest**, with other backing vocalists. Use subsidiary `sitar` in instruments and `ebow|talkbox` in production (both *guitar* techniques); do not classify lead vocal as `operatic` because Meine remains lead and Donath is a credited guest. A sitar credit on the record does **not** make the overall album Indian music, and the opera voice cameo does **not** make the album opera or classical crossover. Official list does not identify a specific track for these instruments: do not invent one. `electronic=0` because no synthesis/drum machine production is documented as a core sound, despite keyboard appearances.

**Genres / response.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/face-the-heat-r189267) provides four whole-album styles (`hardrock|heavymetal|albumrock|arenarock`) and a particularly negative review alleging a noisier/less melodic record. This is a named reviewer's assessment, not critical unanimity; compare explicitly in notes to the band's own [personnel and tracklist](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/face-the-heat/). Alien Nation and Under the Same Sun provide social commentary, alongside more familiar romance and resilience themes. The balance of abrasive energy, slower material and guest instrumentation motivates `energy=4`, `experimental=2`, `mood=2`, `polish=4` and `landmark=0`. Do not label the record grunge simply because it was released in 1993.

## Across all four

- Studio albums only. Live **World Wide Live** (1985), compilation releases, and reissue bonus tracks excluded.
- Genre rows each have a named source and scope. Hair metal and pop metal are **retrospective album descriptions** in some reference taxonomies, not necessarily labels used at original release.
- `influenced_by` is blank unless an artist/producer explicitly names a musician shaping that particular album. The Def Leppard parallel is only `sounds_like`; guests and producers are not influences.
- Original performers outrank speculation about early-session substitutes. All note text and 27-column rows are independent editorial synthesis; no lyric quotations.
- Source links were located through indexed website results; network availability can change. Claude should re-open sources during import before declaring any row `reviewed`.
