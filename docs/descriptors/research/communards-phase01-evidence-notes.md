# The Communards — Phase 01, traceable evidence and interpretive decisions

Scope: The Communards' **original two studio LPs**. Genre strengths recorded separately in `communards_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Sources in `communards_phase01_sources.csv` have traceable URLs. The eight integer descriptor scales are researcher assessments from 0–5 anchored in the Fandome guide, not externally measured data.

## communards-communards — *Communards* (1986)

**Main LP vs reissues:** The ten-song original 1986 vinyl (ending with “Forbidden Love”) is described. The London/Bandcamp digital issue adds “Breadline Britain” and an extended “Disenchanted [Dance]” mix: excluded from sound-profile inference. https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/the-communards-communards-lp-222813/ ; https://communards.bandcamp.com/album/communards

**Genres and weights.** Dance-pop (London Records Bandcamp label and AllMusic), synth-pop (original LP pressing and label), and alternative dance (AllMusic) are album-wide. Hi-NRG and disco refer primarily to the single “Don't Leave Me This Way” and dance-floor material, also verified by London Records and an original 7-inch's genre styles. General `pop` and `electronic` reflect label/pressing classifications. `jazz` is limited principally to the classic “Lover Man” and Coles's supper-club arrangements: the LP should not be shown as predominantly jazz, even though a reviewer notes jazz/blues detours. Original LP and cover track roots: https://londonrecords.co.uk/artists/the-communards/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/communards-mw0000193376 ; https://www.caprarecords.com/release/1460893/the-communards-communards ; https://www.hiddensounds.co.uk/release/9415416/Communards-With-Sarah-Jane-Morris-Don%27t-Leave-Me-This-Way ; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/richard-coles-communards/. Do not substitute `rnb` or `soul` merely because a cover originated from American soul.

**Instruments (each supported by LP liner credits).** `piano|organ|synth` belong to Richard Coles; `doublebass` to credited upright bassist Tony Garnier [some transcriptions vary the surname spelling]; `saxophone` to Crispin Cioe and Arno Hecht; `trumpet` and `trombone` to credited horn players; `percussion`, `congas`, `bongos` to Roger Squitero and Jimmy Maelen; `strings` and `cello` supported by arrangement/conductor and Jesse Levy. `drummachine` is supported by Coles's contemporary comment on Jimmy's Linn rhythm programming. DO NOT transfer *touring* drummer June Miles-Kingston or touring violinists into this 1986 studio row. https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/the-communards-communards-lp-222813/ ; https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/communards-manifesto/5127?theme=1. The bass player appears as Tony “Gagnier” in one collector's transcription and “Garnier” in other releases; the instrument *doublebass* is not in dispute.

**Production.** Main credited producer Mike Thorne on LP and Qobuz song metadata. `falsetto` widely characterized by AllMusic; the contrast between Somerville's treble and Sarah Jane Morris's husky style supports second voice `raspy`, but NOT a claim of rasp on every track. `drumprogramming` and `programming` grounded in 1986 musician interview and LP credits. `stringarrangements` is expressly printed on original LP. A “Linn” is discussed, but *specific LinnDrum model* is NOT specified and thus `linndrum` is absent. Do not add synthesized bass when original credited acoustic upright bass is explicit. https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/the-communards-communards-lp-222813/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/communards-mw0000193376 ; https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/communards-manifesto/5127?theme=1.

**Scales.** Energy **4** / tempo **4** / groove **4**: hi-NRG single and dance pop are conspicuous, but the LP has quiet piano numbers. Electronic **3**: synth and drum programming balanced by live brass, acoustic bass and strings. Mood **3**: buoyant musical surface next to serious political/queer themes. Theatrical **4**: soaring falsetto and large arrangement, supported by strong duet framing. Experimental **2**: stylistic fusion with accessible songs; recorded jazz/classical/acoustic contrasts are unusual within a dance-pop LP. Polish **4**: Mike Thorne's session recording is carefully arranged, not all-machine gloss.

**Lyrics and scene.** London Records' history calls the project openly gay and political; Richard Coles's retrospective describes intentional political disco. `identity|political|social|love|heartbreak` is based on lyrical subjects across group songs, avoiding any unsupported line-level interpretations. Scene specifically British **political queer hi-NRG/disco**, rather than assigning a generic “Bronski Beat” subgenre. https://londonrecords.co.uk/artists/the-communards/ ; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/richard-coles-communards/.

**Sounds-like vs influenced-by.** London Records explicitly says duo reimagined Thelma Houston's hit. That is a covered recording, hence Houston is a SONIC comparator only; linked to the 1976 album *Any Way You Like It*. The same label says their sound extended Bronski Beat's electronic pop, supporting a sonic comparison to *The Age of Consent*, **not** an influence because Somerville belonged to both bands. `Fats Waller|Art Tatum|Count Basie` in `influenced_by`: Coles calls all three jazz piano heroes during a **February 1986** interview while describing this group's music, so these are explicit performer influences relevant to the debut's piano/jazz material. They should not be used as evidence the album samples or quotes those artists. https://londonrecords.co.uk/artists/the-communards/ ; https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/communards-manifesto/5127?theme=1.

**Disagreement:** AllMusic evaluates the album less favourably than *Red*, yet recognizes its dancefloor material/piano breaks. Other contemporary assessments stress the broad jazz/blues songbook element; this diversity drives `experimental=2`, not an unsupported blanket high experimental score. 

## communards-red — *Red* (1987)

**Original scope:** Only the original ten 1987 LP tracks from “Tomorrow” to “C Minor”. The 2022 35th anniversary edition includes bonus demos/B-sides and remixes, and is **not** the album used for these descriptors. https://communards.bandcamp.com/album/red ; https://store.thecommunards.com/product/red-35th-anniversary-edition-colour?cur=GBP ; https://www.theturntable.uk/product-page/the-communards-red-35-year-anniversary-edition.

**Genres and weighting.** `dancepop|synthpop|alternativedance` are repeated by original-pressing metadata, AllMusic and London Records' digital official tags. `disco` and `hinrg` describe “Never Can Say Goodbye” and club material more than the ballads. `pop` album-wide; `electronic` is broad record-store categorization not a claim the album contains no acoustic instruments. AllMusic applies **“Euro-dance”** retrospectively, hence `eurodance` lower than core genres (do not conflate it with the 1990s-defined scene). `ballad` is limited to tracks such as “For a Friend” documented in contemporary review. https://www.allmusic.com/album/red-mw0000651576 ; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/the-communards/red-album_ffhrhg.html ; https://communards.bandcamp.com/album/red ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-03-06-ca-684-story.html ; https://store.thecommunards.com/product/red-35th-anniversary-edition-colour?cur=GBP.

**Instruments.** Period UK/US/Australian 1987 sleeves list `piano`, `organ`, `keyboards` and piano/string arranger Richard Coles; `synth`/`synthbass` and additional keyboards/programming Rob Fisher; `drums` June Miles-Kingston; `bass` Dave Renwick; horn section Simon Clarke, Tim Sanders, Gary Barnacle (saxophones), Roddy Lorimer and Steve Sidwell (trumpet), Annie Whitehead (trombone); `violin`, `viola`, `cello` credited Anne Stephenson, Sally Herbert, Jocelyn Pook, Audrey Riley; `percussion` Pandit Dinesh; `harmonica` Judd Lander. `drummachine` appears in personnel listings for Coles, while sleeves credit rhythm programming. Every instrument has track or album credits. `sequencer` is not added because the source detailed original credits do not consistently label a model/part; avoid unsupported exact unit IDs. https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/the-communards/red-album_ffhrhg.html ; https://store.thecommunards.com/product/red-35th-anniversary-edition-colour?cur=GBP.

**Production / producers.** Richard Coles and Somerville both credited `programmed`; synth-bass/keys parts linked to Rob Fisher production work support `synthprogramming`; `stringarrangements` credited to Coles; `hornarrangements` credited to Kick Horns; `falsetto` is Somerville's documented defining vocal technique. No synth model asserted. **Stephen Hague** original album tracks 1,2,4,5,6,8; **The Communards** original tracks 3,7,9,10. **Alvin Clark** typically billed assistant producer, **Shep Pettibone** additional production/mixing on a track, neither given full album producer entry. A minority pressing calls Clark co-producer; this inconsistent role is flagged in import notes and excluded pending image/credit confirmation. https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/the-communards/red-album_ffhrhg.html ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/87860dde-99e8-41c5-899c-7579db3c815b.

**Scales.** Energy **4**, tempo **4**, groove **4**: high-tempo dance hits amid slow piano elegies. Electronic **3**: prominent drum programming/synthesizer/bass synth, yet acoustic string section, horns and drums. Mood **3**: joy and defiance in dance tracks, but real grief on “For a Friend”. Theatrical **4**: dramatic strong delivery and orchestrated textures. Experimental **1**: tighter, more conventionally polished arrangements than the first record. Polish **5**: Stephen Hague's tightly realized club arrangements and carefully engineered acoustic/electronic sessions. AllMusic explicitly calls the result tighter, more melodic and more polished. https://www.allmusic.com/album/red-mw0000651576.

**Lyrics / scene / comparisons.** `political|identity|social|love|heartbreak|introspection` supported by London Records' commentary on the album's activist themes, including a moving song about Mark Ashton; `Never Can Say Goodbye` transforms Gloria Gaynor's disco arrangement, so comparison `Gloria Gaynor (Never Can Say Goodbye)` is a SONIC analogue and NOT `influenced_by` (covered song). Producer Stephen Hague's association with the Pet Shop Boys and New Order is a personnel fact, **NOT** automatically an audible likeness to those albums. `Bronski Beat (The Age of Consent)` is supported by the label's claimed stylistic continuity; since Somerville was a member, exclude from influences. Per-album `influenced_by` deliberately **blank**: retrospective general dance influences do not show which specific artists shaped these 1987 sessions. https://londonrecords.co.uk/artists/the-communards/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/red-mw0000651576 ; https://store.thecommunards.com/product/red-35th-anniversary-edition-colour?cur=GBP.

**Critics disagree.** AllMusic reviewer Andy Kellman is very positive about the stronger melodies/tighter songwriting and pop-disco sequencing. LA Times critic Cary Darling, writing in March 1988, finds this album less eclectic than the debut and more conventional aside from the elegy. Those are attributed critical positions, not facts to declare as universal judgments. https://www.allmusic.com/album/red-mw0000651576 ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-03-06-ca-684-story.html.

## Data-validation caveats

- All descriptor rows use **exactly 27 columns**, 0–5 integer scales and 15-word-or-shorter original notes; allowed voice and lyric tokens only, with sources per album.
- All genre, instrument and production tokens already appear in the checked Fandome lists and require **no additions**.
- Album-level link to *Any Way You Like It* is for Thelma Houston's song, not a cover as an actual influence; comparable *Never Can Say Goodbye* is Gloria Gaynor's 1975 LP.
- This research does **not** represent audio forensic listening or hardware session confirmation. Where a device model is not explicitly credited, leave it absent.
- Sources were checked using the public pages listed; Fandome artist + album IDs are **proposed new** and should be matched against current HEAD at import time.
