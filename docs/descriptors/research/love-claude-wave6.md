## beatles-pleasepleaseme
- Sources: Pitchfork (Tom Ewing, 2009, 9.5) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/13419-please-please-me/ ; BBC Music (Mike Diver, 2010) https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/6jwx/ ; Rolling Stone (Jordan Runtagh, 2018) hour-by-hour session https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-beatles-marathon-please-please-me-session-hour-by-hour-128271/
- genres: rockandroll: Pitchfork "the sound of rock'n'roll finding a suddenly large, new audience"; beat: Pitchfork "local scenes like the Mersey Sound the Beatles dominated" (Merseybeat mapped to existing `beat`, used on Rubber Soul's scene too); pop: BBC "a career that would effectively define pop itself", "The self-contained pop group was born".
- energy 4 / tempo 4 / groove 4: Pitchfork "A raw, high-energy run-through", "More than any other of their records, Please Please Me is a dance music album", "the most kinetic, danceable tracks they ever made". mood 4: "There's rawness here... but a lot of sweetness too". polish 2: recorded "live to a two-track BTR machine" in under 10 hours (RS); BBC "essentially 'as live' recording". experimental 0: BBC "Their way with composition is relatively simple".
- voice: left empty (only Lennon's "ragged-edged" Twist and Shout is described).
- instruments: MusicBrainz credits (electric/acoustic guitars, bass, drums, harmonica, Martin's piano and celesta, handclaps, tambourine, maracas, Andy White's bongos); RS: harmonica overdub on There's a Place, Martin "playing celeste" on Baby It's You, hand-clap overdubs.
- production: liverecording (RS "Songs were recorded live"), overdubbing (RS "vocal overdubs", "harmonica overdubs", "hand-clap overdubs"), falsetto (RS "the first of their famous falsetto 'oooohs'").
- lyrics: love (RS on There's a Place: "youthful yearning"; McCartney "kiss and a cuddle"); thin, medium support.
- producers: George Martin; Ron Richards (MusicBrainz credit, Love Me Do/P.S. I Love You session).
- influenced_by: Chuck Berry [1/14]: McCartney (RS, quoting Many Years From Now) "nicked" the I Saw Her Standing There bass line from Berry's "I'm Talking About You": "I played exactly the same notes as he did". Covers (Arthur Alexander, Shirelles, Cookies, Isley Brothers) are not influences.
- landmark 1: BBC "marks a vital moment in the career of The Beatles"; Pitchfork on their debut.
- reception: importance 5 (debut that launched them, BBC), acclaim 4 (Pitchfork 9.5 vs BBC "relatively simple"), mainstream 3.

## dusty-dustyinmemphis
- Sources: BBC Music (Peter Marsh, 2002) https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/8vzf ; Rolling Stone (Greil Marcus, 1 Nov 1969) reposted https://greilmarcus.net/2014/08/25/dusty-springfield-dusty-in-memphis-110169/ ; P60s #19 (Jillian Mapes); RS500 #83; PopMatters Counterbalance No. 103 (2012) https://www.popmatters.com/post/164369-dusty-springfield/
- genres: blueeyedsoul: BBC "the perfect frame for Dusty's blue eyed soul"; soul: RS500 "the result was blazing soul", PopMatters "her 1969 excursion into soul music"; cinematicsoul: P60s "a dreamier approach to cinematic soul". Greil Marcus dissents: "Dusty is not a soul singer" (noted).
- voice soft: Marcus "a soft, sensual box", BBC "relying on understatement, timing and delivery rather than vocal firepower".
- energy 2 / theatrical 2: BBC understatement; Marcus "evocative rather than overwhelming". polish 4: PopMatters "lush string arrangements that seem to fill every possible space"; BBC "Mardin's sensitive blend of Bacharach poise and Memphis funk". mood 2: ballads of "vulnerability", Newman's "denial and resignation" (PopMatters). tempo 2, groove 2: mostly ballads, "Son of a Preacher Man" "funky" intro (Marcus). experimental 1.
- instruments: MusicBrainz (Reggie Young guitar and sitar, Tommy Cogbill bass, Bobby Wood piano, Bobby Emmons organ/electric piano, Gene Chrisman drums, congas); Marcus: "counterpoint between bass and vibrating guitar", "Gene Chrisman's woodblock" (percussion), "Piano cues Reggie Young's sizzling guitar"; PopMatters "little organ stabs"; strings: PopMatters.
- production: stringarrangements (PopMatters); overdubbing: RS500 "Her vocals were overdubbed later, when the sessions moved to New York"; P60s "re-record the bulk of her vocals in New York".
- lyrics: sensuality (PopMatters "far and away the sexiest album"; P60s "extol the benefits of morning sex"), love, heartbreak (P60s "lament the loneliness of lurid affairs"; BBC "vulnerability and knowing desire").
- producers: Jerry Wexler, Tom Dowd, Arif Mardin (BBC, PopMatters, MusicBrainz).
- sounds_like: empty. Critics compare her only negatively to Aretha Franklin (BBC "resists any temptation to do an Aretha"; Marcus "Unlike Aretha"); the Bacharach note concerns songs she covers. influenced_by: empty (her quoted wish to sing like Black singers names no artist; RS's "her favorite Aretha Franklin and Otis Redding hits" is the magazine's narration about the session players, not her statement).
- landmark 1: BBC "its status as a classic record".
- legacy 4: PopMatters (template for Adele/Duffy/Rumer; roots of trip-hop).

## vanmorrison-astralweeks
- Sources: The Guardian (Laura Barton, 2011) https://www.theguardian.com/music/2011/aug/03/van-morrison-astral-weeks-review ; The Independent (Andy Gill, 2015) https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/reviews/van-morrison-astral-weeks-album-review-an-album-so-timeless-it-re-casts-its-spell-each-time-you-hear-a6706026.html ; RS500 #60; P60s #24 (Sam Sodomsky); NPR (Josh Gleason, 2009) https://www.npr.org/templates/story/story.php?storyId=101249415 ; RTÉ (2015) https://www.rte.ie/entertainment/music-reviews/2015/1107/740327-van-morrison-astral-weeks ; New Yorker (Jon Michaud) https://www.newyorker.com/culture/culture-desk/the-miracle-of-van-morrisons-astral-weeks
- genres: Guardian "It marries folk and rock and blues and jazz and gospel"; Independent "Van Morrison's folk-jazz classic", "If we're talking baroque pop... the landmark work"; RS500 "connecting the earthy poetry in Celtic folk and American R&B", "a superior jazz quartet". folkjazz is a NEW token (no folkjazz/jazzfolk in the list).
- voice raspy: Guardian "dour and swarthy and half-grunted, barking and nickering". production scatsinging: RS500 "his extended poetic-scat singing".
- experimental 4: NPR (Joe Levy) "a different way of organizing music. It's otherworldly", "Where are the songs?"; RTÉ "markedly different from pretty much anything that went before". polish 2: RTÉ "so little thought out, so big on spontaneity"; Morrison (NPR) "The approach was spontaneity". mood 3: P60s "happy just to be alive", Guardian "heavy with yearning". energy 2, tempo 2, groove 1, theatrical 2, electronic 0 (acoustic jazz band with strings).
- instruments: MusicBrainz (Jay Berliner acoustic/classical guitar, Richard Davis double bass, Connie Kay drums, Warren Smith vibraphone/percussion, John Payne flute/soprano sax, Larry Fallon harpsichord); P60s "Acoustic guitars and pianos, flutes and harpsichords, string arrangements and saxophone solos"; Guardian "flute, harpsichord, vibraphone"; RTÉ "double bassist Richard Davis", Young Lovers Do "whose brass".
- production: liverecording (RS500 "recorded their basic backing tracks in one three-hour session"; RTÉ), stringarrangements and overdubbing (Independent "overdubbed strings"; New Yorker, Morrison: "They added strings").
- lyrics: nostalgia (Guardian "an aching for the streets of Belfast"), spiritual (Independent "spiritual yearning"), love (Independent "romantic reminiscence"), introspection, storytelling (Morrison via Guardian: "a story with definite characters, a song-cycle").
- producers: Lewis Merenstein (RTÉ, New Yorker, MusicBrainz).
- sounds_like / influenced_by: none found (Lead Belly is only named in a lyric).
- landmark 1: Independent "the landmark work"; P60s "made his masterpiece".
- legacy 4: P60s "massively influential".

## zombies-odesseyandoracle
- Sources: Pitchfork (Liam Singer, 2004, 9.3) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/8959-odessey-and-oracle/ ; P60s #47 (Jazz Monroe); BBC Music (Chris Jones, 2008) https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/d8p2 ; Sound On Sound, Classic Tracks: Time of the Season (Rod Argent interview) https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-zombies-time-season ; PopMatters (Dominic Umile, 2013) https://www.popmatters.com/166784-mods-and-mellotrons-the-zombies-at-abbey-road-2495787881.html
- genres: baroquepop: BBC "mellotron-assisted baroque pop of the highest order"; psychedelicpop: Pitchfork "a psych-pop treasure", P60s "psych-pop group", PopMatters "cornerstone of English psychedelic pop"; blueeyedsoul (Time of the Season, the hit single): BBC "the blue-eyed soul shuffle of Time Of The Season".
- voice soft: BBC "the exquisite breathy vocals of Colin Blunstone".
- experimental 2: Pitchfork "not so far-out", yet "notable for its experimental bend", "jarring cut-and-paste thematic shifts". polish 3: PopMatters "polished", but SOS "£1000 budget", tracks cut in one three-hour session. electronic 2: band with Mellotron, Hammond organ, piano. mood 3: P60s "a sweet, tonally complex meditation on the insularity of sadness and the dizziness of bliss". energy 2, tempo 3, groove 2.
- instruments: MusicBrainz (Rod Argent Mellotron/organ/piano, Chris White bass, Paul Atkinson guitar, Hugh Grundy drums); SOS "Hammond L100", "Mellotron Mark II", Rickenbacker/Vox guitar, "the clap" overdub, trumpet on This Will Be Our Year; PopMatters "percussive hand drums" (percussion).
- production: overdubbing and layeredvocals (SOS "I can hear another harmony on the top there... and just overdub it"; Argent via PopMatters "a mass vocal thing like we did on 'Changes'"); fourtrack (Pitchfork "four-track recordings"; SOS two synced four-track machines).
- lyrics: storytelling (Pitchfork "Direct, narrative threads", "the dramatic third person feel"), nostalgia and heartbreak (P60s "music of chances missed and summers past"; BBC "tales of lost hope, subtly tinged with nostalgia"), love.
- producers: Rod Argent, Chris White (MusicBrainz; SOS, Argent: "So we wanted to produce an album ourselves"; Pitchfork "no producers").
- sounds_like: The Beach Boys (Pet Sounds): P60s "an autumnal twist on Pet Sounds" (Pitchfork 2004 also: Care of Cell 44 "strongly recalls" Wouldn't It Be Nice); The Kinks (The Kinks Are the Village Green Preservation Society): Pitchfork "its complex arrangements and baroque instrumentation-- much like The Kinks' Village Green Preservation Society".
- influenced_by: Johann Sebastian Bach [1/12]: PopMatters, BBC Mastertapes: "a brief discussion of the Zombies' baroque era influences. Rod Argent demonstrated where Bach had figured in by way of a live piano rendition of the classically driven bridge section of Odessey's 'Hung Up on a Dream'". Not used: Ben E. King (Argent: the bass line "does echo 'Stand By Me'... I didn't think about it at the time", not intended); the Beatles only left the Mellotron behind.
- landmark 1: Pitchfork "the band's fully realized statement of intent".
- legacy 3: Pitchfork "harbingers of today's indie pop", seeds of prog.

## love-foreverchanges
- Sources: Los Angeles Times (Richard Cromelin, 2001) https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-mar-09-ca-35270-story.html ; Pitchfork (Stephen M. Deusner, 2008, 8.6 for the reissue) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11429-forever-changes-collectors-edition/ ; P60s #33 (Deusner); RS500 #180; Library of Congress NRR essay (Ted Olson, with Bruce Botnick's account) https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Love-Forever-Changes.pdf
- genres: folkrock: LA Times "a folk-rock, chamber-pop opus", RS500 "a pioneering folk-rock"; chamberpop: LA Times; psychedelia: P60s "psychedelia at its darkest and most apocalyptic"; folk, rock, pop, classical, mariachi: Pitchfork 2008 "draws from rock, pyschedelia, folk, pop, classical, and even mariachi" (RS500 "mariachi-brass drama of 'Alone Again Or'").
- voice soft: Pitchfork "the calm in his voice, the matter-of-factness with which he delivers those lines" (low weight; Lee and MacLean both sing leads).
- mood 2: Pitchfork "paranoia-soaked", P60s "darkest and most apocalyptic", LA Times "longing and a sweet melancholy". theatrical 3: RS500 "symphonic sweep". experimental 3: LA Times "unconventional structures"; Pitchfork "circuitous arrangements", yet "surprisingly accessible". polish 4: Pitchfork "a rank perfectionist"; LOC "spirited, seamlessly crafted". tempo 3: LA Times "propulsive". electronic 1, energy 2, groove 1.
- instruments: LA Times "a foundation of acoustic guitar and string orchestrations"; P60s "Trading electric guitars for acoustics and chamber strings"; LOC Echols' "electric guitar leads", strings and brass overdubs, trumpet (Pitchfork "the trumpet solo"); MusicBrainz: bass (Forssi, Carol Kaye), drums (Stuart, Hal Blaine), piano/harpsichord (Don Randi, Wrecking Crew session).
- production: stringarrangements, hornarrangements (LOC: "Overdub Strings and Horns", arranger David Angel), overdubbing (LOC session diary: vocal and guitar overdubs).
- lyrics: P60s "takes a cynical view of hippie piousness"; Pitchfork "the Vietnam War, his friends' drug addictions, and the end of the world"; LOC "ponderings about mortality, hypocrisy, and psychic freedom" -> social|political|introspection.
- producers: Bruce Botnick (LOC "enlisted to oversee the production"; LA Times "producer Bruce Botnick"), Arthur Lee (MusicBrainz credit).
- sounds_like: LA Times "string orchestrations that evoke Simon & Garfunkel and Burt Bacharach", "a 'Pet Sounds,' a work to which it's often likened" -> Simon & Garfunkel | Burt Bacharach | The Beach Boys (Pet Sounds). (Bacharach did not work on this album.)
- influenced_by: Herb Alpert & The Tijuana Brass [1/11]: co-producer Bruce Botnick (LOC): "the Tijuana Brass were ruling the airwaves, and I thought that we could stylistically borrow the flavor without doing a direct copy, more of a Spanish influence than Mexican." The brass was added to "a few of the songs"; share set to the one song critics tie to mariachi brass (Alone Again Or); could be 3/11 (RS500 names Alone Again Or, Andmoreagain, You Set the Scene). Not used: Jac Holzman (label head) suggested an acoustic approach, no artist named; Benny Goodman only "recogniz[ed] the jazz influences".
- landmark 1: RS500 "Love's third record is his crowning achievement"; Pitchfork "third and best album".
- legacy 4: LA Times and LOC quotes (legacy.csv).
