# Mike Oldfield — phase 02: research / evidence notes

These are original editorial judgments for the **original studio albums**, not measurements or descriptions of bonus discs. The cited album-focused sources and original sleeve-credit transcriptions support the instrumentation and producer fields; genre evidence is mapped explicitly, one term per row, in `mikeoldfield_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`. `sounds_like` is an editorial acoustic comparison, **not** a claim of historical influence.

## 1979 — *Platinum* (`mikeoldfield-platinum`)

**Composition and genre.** The 19-minute four-part title suite combines guitar-led progressive rock with a steady dance-like motion and a passage described by AllMusic's Dave Thompson as neo-disco. AllMusic indexes Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Progressive Electronic and (retrospectively) New Age; a secondary album overview identifies dance-rock and pop rock. The latter and `disco` are **not** descriptions of the entire album; their placement after core styles is intentional. `Punkadiddle` parodies punk but is not therefore labelled `punkrock`. The North Star section draws directly on Philip Glass's composition, but **covering/adapting music is not evidence for `influenced_by`**. [AllMusic album](https://www.allmusic.com/album/platinum-mw0000650590); [Dave Thompson](https://www.allmusic.com/song/platinum-mt0012116925); [official](https://www.mikeoldfieldofficial.com/discography/); [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_(Mike_Oldfield_album)).

**Evidence for eight numeric values** (energy 3, electronic 2, mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3): guitar/drum propulsion on much of side one, but the quieter `Woodhenge` and vocal/novelty tracks restrain whole-album energy and speed; many electric and acoustic players limit the electronic proportion; juxtaposed styles and studio assembly raise experimentation to 3, while New York/UK professional recording and tight ensemble control support polish 4. Side-one pulse lifts groove to 3, not a dance-only 5. Avoid scoring by the disco-like segment alone.

**Instruments, production, people.** Album credits explicitly name electric/acoustic guitars, piano, synthesizer, vibraphone/marimba (both canonical `mallets`), basses, several drummers, congas, horns, tubular bells and keyboards. Tom Newman is credited as **producer**; Kurt Munkacsi and Renate Blauel were engineers, not producers. The tubular.net equipment list identifies Polymoog, Prophet V (canonical `prophet5`) and Roland SH-2000 (`rolandsh2000`, proposed token), plus an Ampex multitrack tape machine. Section production includes horn arrangements. The rare original `Sally` uses Newman's narrow-band vocal EQ (`vocalfilter`); that effect is **pressing-specific**, so it is retained with explicit edition caveat. The commonly encountered replacement `Into Wonderland` has Wendy Roberts on vocals, as does `I Got Rhythm`. `spoken` reflects brief utterances in the suite, not a spoken-word album. [Sleeve transcription](https://tubular.net/discography/Platinum); [Dark Star](https://www.mikeoldfield.org/platinum).

**Disagreements / exclusions.** Earliest UK LP copies had the song `Sally`; later pressings substituted `Into Wonderland` without promptly updating the sleeve. The US *Airborn* changes tracks (including `Woodhenge`/`Guilty`): never score its `Guilty` addition as if present on the original *Platinum*. `Punkadiddle` does not establish a punk style classification and Gershwin and Glass are covers/quoted music, not documented personal influences on this album. `sounds_like`: Jarre for melodic synth structures, Gong for instrumental jazz-rock touches, Glass for repeating choral figures; **editorial parallels only**. `lyrics` blank: the lyric-bearing track differs by pressing, and standardised themes are not consistently evidenced for the album as a whole.

## 1980 — *QE2* (`mikeoldfield-qe2`)

**Composition and genre.** This is a largely short-form instrumental album, with `Taurus 1` and the title piece the longer constructions; AllMusic styles are Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Progressive Electronic, plus retrospective New Age. Its biography explicitly calls this phase progressive pop. The ABBA `Arrival` and Shadows `Wonderful Land` are **covers**, not collaborator credits or documented influence claims. There is no need to apply synthpop just because the arrangements feature synths. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/qe2-mw0000191286); [biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/mike-oldfield-mn0000489520); [official](https://www.mikeoldfieldofficial.com/releases-archive/qe2-deluxe-edition/).

**Scales** (3,3,3,3,3,4,3,2): rhythmic drums and electric-guitar peaks offset lighter miniatures; synthesizers, a drum machine and several vocoder passages justify electronic 3 rather than 5 because guitars, pipes, harp, orchestral strings and horns remain prominent. At 2, groove acknowledges rhythmic titles without claiming dance-floor focus. Mood 3 reflects a mixture of bright, stately and reflective passages. The arranged horn-and-string sessions and Hentschel production support polish 4. Experimental 3 captures vocoded textures and changing instrumentation; pop-instrumental formats reduce extremes.

**Instruments / production.** The detailed original sleeve transcription explicitly names electric and Spanish/acoustic guitar, synths, bass, drum machine, piano, vocals, bagpipes, harp, banjo, mandolin, marimba, vibraphone, timpani, congas/African percussion, strings, trumpets, trombone, sax, gong and tambourine. The electronic `Claptrap`/Syndrums are encoded under the existing broad electronic percussion/drum-machine instruments, not a speculative new custom token. `vocoder` is both heard as an instrumental timbre and credited as an effect, therefore in instruments and production. `stringarrangements` (David Bedford) and `hornarrangements` (David Hentschel) are explicitly credited. The Yamaha CS-80 was played by Hentschel. An analogue Polymoog and Prophet V are inventoried in the instrument source. [Tubular.net](https://tubular.net/discography/QE2); [Dark Star](https://www.mikeoldfield.org/qe2).

**Credits and caution.** Mike Oldfield and David Hentschel are co-producers and engineers, not David Bedford, Phil Collins or Maggie Reilly, whose roles are arranger/drummer/vocalist. Specialist sources refer to both a Roland-style vocoder and a Barth Musicoder around this period: the `vocoder` effect is certain, **which model is heard on which *QE2* track is not**. No exact vocoder machine is asserted. `influenced_by` is blank: ABBA and the Shadows are present via cover versions only, which the rules explicitly exclude. `sounds_like` comparisons to Vangelis, Jarre and Sky refer only to atmosphere/arrangement; they do not claim personal influence. `lyrics` left blank: much of the album instrumental or vocoded, text themes insufficiently documented.

## 1982 — *Five Miles Out* (`mikeoldfield-fivemilesout`)

**Composition and genre.** Side one is the approximately 25-minute `Taurus II`; side two moves among `Family Man`, `Orabidoo`, `Mount Teidi` and the aircraft-themed title song. AllMusic indexes Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Progressive Electronic and New Age. The artist's official catalogue **specifically** describes the record as rock and pop electronica, hence the separate `rock`, `pop`, and `electronica` descriptors, mainly for the shorter pieces. Acclaimed Music calls **the song** `Five Miles Out` Art Pop and Progressive Pop; these are scoped to `some_tracks` rather than whole album. No Celtic genre is claimed solely because there are uilleann pipes. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/five-miles-out-mw0000195162); [official](https://www.mikeoldfieldofficial.com/releases-archive/five-miles-out/); [Acclaimed Music](https://www.acclaimedmusic.net/artist/Mike%20Oldfield.htm).

**Scales** (3,3,3,4,4,3,3,2): the orchestral-band peaks and elaborate structure of `Taurus II` raise theatrical and experimental to 4, while gentle sections and a lullaby keep energy 3. Synth textures and vocoder are interleaved with a substantial acoustic folk/jazz-rock ensemble, making electronic 3 appropriate. The original 1982 *High Fidelity* review calls the arrangements elaborate but is sceptical of their emotional depth; this disagreement counsels mood 3 rather than implausible universal triumph/sorrow. Tempo 3 and groove 2 distinguish brisk sections from dance music. [Contemporary review](https://tubular.net/articles/1982_07b/High-Fidelity-Five-Miles-Out-Review).

**Instruments and machines.** Jacket credits identify Oldfield's guitars, bass, keyboards and vocals; Reilly's vocals; Pert's percussion and keys; Fenn's guitar; Cross's keys; uilleann pipes by Paddy Moloney on `Taurus II`; Carl Palmer on `Mount Teidi`; and Graham Broad's drums on the title song. The detailed contemporary review actually names further *Taurus II* sounds including trumpet/brass, trombone, oboe, flute, recorder, bodhran, banjo, organ and vocoder. `strings` is justified by the title-song arrangement credit to Morris Pert (conducted by Martyn Ford). Tape work is evidenced by the 24-track Ampex ATR-124; a DN70 delay is listed in the same studio equipment inventory. Vocoder is attested but no exact device is tagged, even though a Barth Musicoder is photographed in the studio: photograph alone does not prove each recorded sound. `overdubbing` inferred with high confidence from the 24-track sheet and contemporary description of individually stacked parts. [Tubular.net](https://tubular.net/discography/FiveMilesOut); [Dark Star](https://www.mikeoldfield.org/five-miles-out); [High Fidelity](https://tubular.net/articles/1982_07b/High-Fidelity-Five-Miles-Out-Review).

**Producers/lyrics.** Mike Oldfield produced all tracks; **Tom Newman** co-produced only the song `Five Miles Out` (and was its engineer). Richard Manwaring was engineer on `Mount Teidi`, not a producer. The autobiographical flight-risk story gives `storytelling`; `Family Man` introduces seduction/temptation (broad `sensuality` tag, not a claim about *all* lyrics). The instrumental is central, so `voice` starts with instrumental. Editorial `sounds_like` compares the progressive/folk interplay to Camel and Jethro Tull, with densely produced band passages reminiscent of Alan Parsons Project; not documented influences. A contemporary critic admires melody and layering but finds the work emotionally thin, while AllMusic regards the title track as standout: these opinions are left as disagreement rather than treated as objective score.

## 1983 — *Crises* (`mikeoldfield-crises`)

**Composition and genre.** The 20-minute title suite uses synth washes and expressive guitar; `Moonlight Shadow`, `Foreign Affair`, Jon Anderson-fronted `In High Places` and Roger Chapman-fronted `Shadow on the Wall` favour pop/rock songwriting. AllMusic uses Prog-Rock, Art Rock and Progressive Electronic plus New Age umbrella. Album of the Year identifies Progressive Pop / Pop Rock / Art Rock; both distinctions are retained, but ranked after the progressive suite styles. Song-level `poprock` is independently supported by Acclaimed Music. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/crises-mw0000191172); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/11934-mike-oldfield-crises.php); [Acclaimed Music](https://www.acclaimedmusic.net/artist/Mike%20Oldfield.htm).

**Scales** (3,4,3,3,3,4,3,2): Fairlight/synths and DMX machine are pervasive, yet guitar, acoustic mandolin and live drummer Simon Phillips limit electronic to 4. A long exploratory suite and several concise singles justify experimental 3; polished sessions and vocal hooks justify polish 4. Mood 3 reflects contrasting suspenseful title track, tuneful pop and heavy rock. Groove 2: drums underpin rock but the album is not dance-oriented. `storytelling|heartbreak` rests on the *Moonlight Shadow* account of love and death; no undocumented John Lennon dedication is added. [AllMusic single review](https://www.allmusic.com/song/moonlight-shadow-mt0047803191).

**Specific documented gear.** Original inner-sleeve detail and studio history identify Fairlight CMI (sampled keyboard), Oberheim OB-Xa synth, Oberheim DMX drum machine, Prophet-5, Roland VP-330 **used for strings** (not as a credited vocal vocoder), and Quantec Room Simulator. Oberheim DSX is documented as the sequencer interface with Fairlight and analogue synths. The last two models require new optional validator tokens `quantecroomsimulator` and `oberheimdsx`; machine names are kept distinct. The recording source identifies Ampex ATR-124 and Neve 8108, hence `analogtape` is factual despite the digital Fairlight. The instrumentation list uses `sampler` because Fairlight is a sampler and is explicitly credited; `drummachine` for DMX plus Simmons drums; `mallets` for Pierre Moerlen's vibraphone. Album-level `voice` includes Maggie Reilly's close-miked soft delivery, Jon Anderson's clear high register and Roger Chapman's rasp. [Sleeve and gear](https://tubular.net/discography/Crises); [Dark Star](https://www.mikeoldfield.org/crises).

**Artist's words and disagreements.** Oldfield says the lead vocal on `Moonlight Shadow` was deliberately recorded extremely close to Reilly's microphone to produce a quiet, almost whispered delivery; do **not** infer `vocalfilter` or `autotune` from this performance decision. This direct interview is reproduced in *Prog* (20 January 2026 publication). Some popular accounts relate the song to John Lennon; Oldfield has described non-musical inspirations differently. Neither assertion establishes musical `influenced_by` under Fandome rules. [Mike Barnes / Prog](https://www.loudersound.com/music/tracks-singles/mike-oldfield-moonlight-shadow); [Dave Thompson / AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/song/moonlight-shadow-mt0047803191). `sounds_like` analogies (Alan Parsons Project, Yes, Genesis) are editorial comparisons grounded in art-rock and high-vocal synth textures, **not** guest participation or historical influence. Credits list Mike Oldfield **and Simon Phillips** as album producers; Nigel Luby was engineer, not producer.

## Cross-phase / original-edition handling

- All four are **studio**. The earlier *Exposed* (1979) is a **live** album and remains excluded. A tour live disc bundled with the expanded *QE2* is also excluded.
- No duplicate or phase 01 rows. `mikeoldfield` remains a **proposed** artist ID as of the repository read-only check on 27 October 2026; it must be registered in the catalogue for a successful Fandome import.
- Influences directly stated by Oldfield **for his career** are documented in the phase 01 packet and are **not** repeated here. No album-specific artist-or-producer-named musical influence was verified for these four albums; all four `influenced_by` fields intentionally remain empty. Covers, paraphrases, guests and critics' comparisons do not qualify.
- `newage` is a retrospective AllMusic classification across this stretch of Oldfield's career, not the sole contemporary style.
