# ABBA — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes (1973–1976)

Research date: 2026-10-25. English-only documentation; **four original studio albums only**. No live album, best-of, soundtrack, reissue, or later-phase album. Source citations use the IDs in `abba_phase01_sources.csv`. Every genre in the import is represented in `abba_phase01_genre_evidence.csv` with an evidence scope. `genre` presence is **not** a statement of album-wide dominance. All 0–5 values are editorial ordinals using Fandome guide anchors, not published acoustic measurements; remain `draft` for Claude review.

## 1. Ring Ring (1973) — `abba-ringring`

- **Identity/edition.** The first Swedish LP predates the permanent ABBA name: credit was *Björn & Benny, Agnetha & Frida*. Some first pressings have the Swedish version of “Ring Ring” first and the English version later; the more widespread international edition differs. Record `en|sv`, predominantly English, not a Swedish-language album. Sources O-DISC, O-RING, D-RING and M-RING.
- **Genres.** Mainstream early `pop|europop`; rock/guitar tracks plus `folkpop`, `glamrock`, `countryrock`, some `traditionalpop` and `bubblegumpop`. `schlager` is a documented Swedish songwriting **background** in O-RING, not evidence that every track is schlager; second reviewer can remove it.
- **Scales.** energy **3**, tempo **3**, groove **2**: the title track and other singalongs alternate with introspective ballads and acoustic material. electronic **1**: electric instruments, Mellotron and recorded layering are *not* synonymous with electronic programming. polish **3**, theatrical **2**, experimental **1**: studio overdubs and developing pop formula. mood **3** (mixed): buoyant melodies alongside the emotional loss in “Disillusion” and “I Saw It in the Mirror.”
- **Voices and themes.** `clear|soft|powerful`: both lead singers plus intermittent male-fronted songs, not a uniform vocal delivery; “powerful” lower certainty. Love and heartbreak are recurrent; some nostalgia/storytelling songs. `social` is based on “He Is Your Brother” and “People Need Love”, track-specific.
- **Production and influence.** Benny Andersson and Björn Ulvaeus: verified producers; engineer Michael B. Tretow is not a producer. `influenced_by=Phil Spector` explicitly means the *Ring Ring title track’s production method*: ABBA official article O-RING recounts Tretow's adaptation of Spector layering; **not** a claim Spector wrote, produced, played on or influenced every song on the album.
- **Importance.** Formative debut, `landmark=0` reflects relative critical assessment, not a dismissal of historical significance.

## 2. Waterloo (1974) — `abba-waterloo`

- **Identity/edition.** ABBA's first album under the four-letter band name; the original Swedish LP contains the Swedish and English title recordings. `language=en|sv` conservatively records the local first pressing; the international LP was effectively English (D-WATER). `Waterloo` is already in the Fandome catalog. Do not include later bonus singles.
- **Genres.** `pop|europop|rock|glamrock` the core, with the signed AllMusic review describing folk-rock and reggae variety; Classic Pop notes a baroque-pop song, disco-like bass and funk touches. `folkrock`, `baroquepop` new tokens only if Claude maintains the documented distinctions. Neither reggae nor disco should rank as a dominant sound.
- **Scales.** energy **4** for rock-driven peaks, typical tempo **3**, groove **3** because of mixed ballads and rhythm-focused titles; electronic **1** despite organ/Moog and multi-track tape. mood **3** because threatening/dark songs and sweet melodies coexist. theatrical **3**, experimental **2**, polish **3**.
- **Voices.** Strong contrasting female leads with intermittent male lead sections, not all songs female-fronted (`clear|powerful|soft`). Spector-like dense instrumentation can sound large without making the entire album theatrical=5.
- **Influences.** Glossy glam-rock sonic similarities to Sweet et al are **not** entered as causal influences: ABBA's own focus piece describes Eurovision staging and critical comparisons, not a proven album-level influence. Leave `influenced_by` blank.
- **Importance.** Historic Eurovision leap, `landmark=1`. Sources O-WATER, A-WATER, C-ALBUMS, D-WATER.

## 3. ABBA (1975) — `abba-abba`

- **Original edition.** **11 songs**: “Mamma Mia”, “SOS”, “I Do, I Do, I Do, I Do, I Do”, “Intermezzo No. 1” and others. Songs appearing as CD bonus tracks or on regional compilations are not included (D-ABBA). New catalog entry required.
- **Genres.** `pop|europop|poprock|rock` core. Glam hard riffs (“Hey Hey Helen”, “So Long”), reggae pastiche (“Tropical Loveland”), proggy instrumental (“Intermezzo No. 1”), retro 1950s pop (“I Do...”). Funk texture “Man In The Middle” requires editorial verification on its *track scope*; no claim album is funk-based. Vocal and musical contrasts reflect the whole LP.
- **Scales.** energy **4**, electronic **2** (Mellotron/synthesizer yet predominantly played band), groove **3**, tempo **3**, mood **3** (melodic brightness vs troubled romantic text), theatrical **3**, experimental **2**, polish **4** as multitrack arrangements consolidate.
- **Voices/lyrics.** Both female singers with intermittent male leads: `clear|powerful|soft`, varying song by song. Romance/breakup prominent, with narratives and “Hey Hey Helen” identity/social commentary; no claim of a whole political statement.
- **Comparisons.** `sounds_like=Elton John` is a **track-limited** comparison of “So Long” cited in C-ALBUMS, not an established blanket resemblance. `influenced_by` blank: Billy Vaughn mentioned as a critical sound reference, not directly attested as formative influence.
- **Producers.** Andersson and Ulvaeus; Michael B. Tretow credited only engineer (D-ABBA).

## 4. Arrival (1976) — `abba-arrival`

- **Original edition.** Original Swedish album has **ten tracks**, excluding “Fernando”; that single was included in 1976 Australian/NZ variants and subsequent deluxe editions. Scope and evaluations use original Swedish tracklisting only. Existing catalog ID.
- **Genres.** `pop|europop` dominant, prominent `dancepop|disco`; signed critics additionally cite AOR/adult pop, Philadelphia soul, retro-rock/blues and folk touches. `phillysoul` is proposed as a **secondary influence descriptor**, NOT a claim ABBA became a Philadelphia soul act. The stylistic diversity remains bounded at track level in genre evidence.
- **Scales.** energy **3** (bouncy pop and several dramatic ballads), electronic **2** (live instrumentation plus studio processing/keyboards), mood **3**: “Dancing Queen” and “When I Kissed the Teacher” contrast heartbreak in “Knowing Me, Knowing You” and “My Love, My Life”; a high mood would conflate beat with emotion. theatrical **3**, experimental **2**, polish **5** for conspicuous layering and crafted mix. tempo **3**, groove **3**, since the LP is not wall-to-wall dance music.
- **Voice.** `clear|powerful|soft` describes shifting front leads and ballad singing; the vocal layering is not necessarily a choir, so omit `choral` despite doubled harmonies.
- **Directly evidenced inspirations, not collaborators:** `George McCrae` from “Rock Your Baby” rhythmic model for “Dancing Queen” (C-ARR, P-ARR); `10cc` vocal arrangement inspiration for “My Love, My Life” (C-ARR); `Fats Domino` retro R&B/rock reference for “Why Did It Have To Be Me” (C-ARR). **These are specific songs**. Do not promote them to causation for every track.
- **Sonic comparison vs influence.** Pitchfork likens ABBA's ornate sonics to Phil Spector; this is editorial comparison not enough to set `influenced_by=Phil Spector` for Arrival.
- **Review differences.** The album is widely praised; detailed interpretations of “Dancing Queen” vary greatly, notably Pitchfork's possible sadness against its surface joy. Retain mixed mood.

## Genre token additions requested (review before coding)

1. `schlager`: early Swedish pop/song form. Evidence O-RING; categorise under `traditionalpop` or `pop` as appropriate, and review its borderline album applicability.
2. `folkrock`: explicitly invoked in AllMusic's Waterloo review and Pitchfork history. Parent `rock|folk`.
3. `baroquepop`: one Waterloo cut, “Suzy-Hang-Around” (C-ALBUMS). Parent `pop`.
4. `phillysoul`: Philadelphia soul influences noted by Classic Pop for Arrival. Parent `soul|rnb`, track/texture scope; spelling normalised.

Do not turn the above into a blanket claim of studio-album genre prevalence. Source IDs can be resolved to URLs in the sources CSV. Expert classifications and writer credits are not producer credits; production engineer Michael B. Tretow must remain out of producer field.
