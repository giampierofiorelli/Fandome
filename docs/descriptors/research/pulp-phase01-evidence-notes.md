# Pulp — Phase 01 | source reasoning and uncertainty

Scope: original editions of four chronological releases, not reissue extras. All scales are independent editorial 0–5 judgements anchored to Fandome's album-descriptors-guide; `status=draft` until human second-person review. Album genre evidence is per-token, with retrospective taggings or track-level styles explicitly marked as such.

## It (1983) — `pulp-it`
- **Identity**: Seven-song **mini-LP**, officially counted as first studio album. Original Red Rhino 18 Apr 1983. Do not ingest Cherry Red / Fire reissue bonus tracks as a separate studio album. [PulpWiki](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/It), [Pulp discography](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/Albums).
- **Genres**: [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15708-it-freaks-separations/) calls it indie-pop and pastoral/folky; [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r47327/review) describes mainly acoustic folk-like songs. [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/10885-pulp-it.php) prints twee pop, jangle pop, baroque pop and neo-acoustic (aggregate tags; medium-strength, not independent critical album evidence). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_(Pulp_album)) explicitly names indie folk, a lower-authority secondary genre label; review its appropriateness before adding it globally.
- **Scales**: energy **2** and tempo **2**, restrained songs; electronic **1**, mostly acoustic despite keyboards/Rhodes; mood **3**, comparatively bright and pastoral; theatrical **2**, early low-key delivery with some Walker-like ballads; experimental **1**, simple compositions; polish **2**, modest early recording; groove **1**, little dance emphasis. Pitchfork and AllMusic support comparisons; these numbers are authored judgement.
- **Instruments**: original notes list guitar, bass, drums, piano, mandolin, keyboards and trombone. The detailed [1982–83 sessions](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/ItLPSessions?action=print) specifically include acoustic guitar, Rhodes, flute, clarinet, organ and hand percussion; track-level minor instrumentation, not present continuously.
- **Production**: empty: original source credits Simon Hinkler but do not establish a specific microphone, console, tape method or vocal effect. Cocker retrospectively states the goal was a [Leonard Cohen-type sound](https://www.pulpwiki.net/Pulp/It), so this is *documented influence*. Scott Walker comparison is the critic's sonic likeness, not an artist admission.
- **Caveat**: AllMusic's catalogue tags Britpop and post-punk retroactively; their review describes predominantly acoustic music with no synthesisers or dance sound. Those retrospective umbrella tags are omitted as misleading album-wide evidence.

## Freaks (1987) — `pulp-freaks`
- **Identity**: Recorded June 1986 but released 11 May **1987**. Original sleeve [PulpWiki](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/Freaks) lists Pulp as producer. [Sessions](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/FreaksLPSessions) mentions Jonathan Kirk alongside Pulp, while the sleeve says mixing assistant; discrepancy deliberately excluded from definitive `producers` field pending original printed producer proof.
- **Genres**: Explicit post-punk / alternative indie-rock tags and gothic/dark-cabaret character in [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/freaks-mw0000027134), [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/10886-pulp-freaks.php) and [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15708-it-freaks-separations/). AOTY subgenre tags are lower strength. `rock` is a broad umbrella.
- **Scales**: energy **3**, often forceful guitars; electronic **1**, organ, with no documented electronic beat programming; mood **0**, unusually claustrophobic/bleak; theatrical **4**, dramatic and agitated singing; experimental **3**, jagged structures and noise; polish **1**, AllMusic explicitly calls production muddy; tempo **2**, mostly slow/mid; groove **1**, not aimed at dancing.
- **Instruments**: [1986 session list](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/FreaksLPSessions): semiacoustic/electric guitars, Farfisa, piano, violin, bass, drums and percussion. No instrument is guessed from gothic genre.
- **Production**: blank intentionally: specific production hardware/process is not documented well enough. Do **not** treat dark/distorted aesthetics as proof of a particular effect unit.
- **Influences**: Pitchfork's Joe Tangari explicitly describes Cocker's delivery in relation to Ian Curtis (Joy Division) and Mark E. Smith (The Fall). This is *critic-attributed vocal influence* rather than first-person testimony; weaker than the Cohen citation for It.
- **Caveat**: Do not treat database's retrospective Britpop classification as contemporary 1987 genre.

## Separations (1992) — `pulp-separations`
- **Identity**: Recording 1989–1990, original Fire issue June **1992**; reissue extra tracks are not included. Album producer **Alan Smyth and Pulp**, engineer Alan Fisch. [PulpWiki](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/SeparationsAlbum) / [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/86ad123c-7b3a-4978-ac54-217a30f53404).
- **Genres**: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r228574/review) specifically reports an album-wide dance/pop/rock blend, acid-house influence and disco on Countdown/This House Is Condemned. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15708-it-freaks-separations/) says the **second half** is the major disco breakthrough; preceding material is gloomier guitar indie. Period [Q/NME reviews](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/SeparationsAlbum) mention electro-pop and cabaret. These are album subsets, marked in genre-evidence CSV, not dominant album identity.
- **Scales**: energy **3**, mixed ballads and dance; electronic **3**, substantial Casio sampler and drum-machine tracks but not exclusively electronic; mood **2**, romantic failure and tense songs offset by dance thrust; theatrical **4**, spoken/narrated dramatic verse; experimental **3**, dividing folk/rock with acid-house experimentation; polish **2**, budget-conscious production; tempo **3**, mixed but extended dance tracks; groove **3**, concentrated in second half.
- **Instruments/production**: [session evidence](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/SeparationsLPSessions): Casio FZ-10 sampler, Alesis drum machine, Korg Delta/Jen SX1000 synths, Yamaha keyboard, electric guitar, bass guitar, synth bass, Farfisa, violin, piano, drums and timpani. Sampling, drum programming and overdubs explicitly identified, unlike many general genre reviews. No Alesis model number is given; do not invent one. `casiofz10` is a new specific-machine token.
- **Influences**: band's house-music turn is documented but no *named musical artist* shown to have directly shaped this album. `influenced_by` deliberately empty; listed `sounds_like` are transparent editorial similarities.
- **Caveat**: AllMusic's retrospective Britpop database tag deliberately omitted because the 1989/1990 recordings predate the scene and genres are addressed with more precise source descriptions.

## His 'n' Hers (1994) — `pulp-hisnhers`
- **Identity**: 18 Apr 1994 original UK Island issue, despite some AllMusic digital release listings showing US June. Ed Buller producer. Original UK vinyl excludes the remixed 'Babies', whereas the original CD includes it. [PulpWiki](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/HisNHersAlbum), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/30a94519-0c6e-41ab-8a4e-77cccd91f477).
- **Genres**: Album-wide Britpop, indie/alternative rock, synth-pop and glam pop-rock from [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/his-n-hers-mw0000625094), [SHFL](https://www.theshfl.com/album/His-n-Hers), [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/3603-pulp-his-n-hers.php). Artist [AllMusic biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/p19990/biography) describes glam, disco, new wave, acid-house and Europop threads in the sound; mark disco/acid-house/europop as partial stylistic threads, not the full LP. Retrospective critical tags have lower strength than contemporary sleeve facts.
- **Scales**: energy **3**, guitar pop alternating slinky ballads; electronic **2**, pervasive keyboards but live rhythm section; mood **2**, bittersweet narratives of awkward sex and relationships; theatrical **4**, talk-sung dramatization; experimental **2**, retro hybrid with accessible songwriting; polish **4**, established studio and detailed timbral palette; tempo **3**, much midtempo, some upbeat; groove **3**, danceable material alongside slower narratives.
- **Instrumentation**: original [sleeve/session credits](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/HisNHersLPSessions) list **EMS Synthi A**, **Korg Trident II**, **Stylophone 350S**, Farfisa, Rhodes, Wurlitzer, grand piano, Clavinet, guitar, bass, drums, Russell Senior's violin and Nick Banks's timpani. `timpani` added as instrument token; treated cymbals and fire-extinguisher sound remain track-level notes rather than invented instrument tokens.
- **Production**: overdubs on She's a Lady and album mix of Babies [session source](https://pulpwiki.net/Pulp/HisNHersLPSessions); sample in David's Last Summer [MusicBrainz recording credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/30a94519-0c6e-41ab-8a4e-77cccd91f477). Named analogue synth models recorded as new production tokens, **not** inferred digital music software. `emssynthia`, `korgtridentii`, `stylophone350s` newly proposed.
- **Influences**: David Bowie/Roxy Music lineage comes from professional [AllMusic biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/p19990/biography), not producer status. `sounds_like` is editorial judgement. Lyrics cover desire, insecurity, irony and social detail; the precise scale scores are editorial not machine-derived.

## Uncertainties and verification queue
- **New artist**: `pulp` is absent from current `src/data/catalog.ts` on descriptors; create new artist before descriptors. Existing `docs/descriptors/pulp.csv` is missing. IDs use `<artist-id>-<slug>`.
- **New genres**: `indiefolk`, `tweepop`, `neoacoustic`, `gothicrock`, `darkcabaret` (and check against current genre list before adding). Some are weak AOTY taxonomy; Claude can flag for editorial review before adding globally.
- **New instruments**: `timpani`. Others all in current allowed labels.
- **New production**: `casiofz10`, `emssynthia`, `korgtridentii`, `stylophone350s`. Sources describe machine models directly. No machine is inferred from decade or genre.
- Pulp's earliest recordings may have different US-release dates, or extra tracks; the original UK versions, not reissues, set identifiers and years. The 1983 `It` mini-LP is a real studio album in standard Pulp discographies, not an additional EP to double-count.
- Review the distinction between PulpWiki original sleeve credit and session producer for *Freaks* before adding Jonathan Kirk.
- User's recommendation engine should use genre evidence scope (`whole album`, `some tracks`, `influence`) rather than treating every token as equal album-wide presence.
