## Rondò Veneziano (1980)
- Identity: official artist tracklist has nine titles; original Italian Baby Records BR 56011 pressing dates to 1980. The UK alias is *The Genius of Venice*. Nine tracks, no lyric-bearing singer indicated; `instrumental` identifies primary performance.
- Styles: LP vendor's original Italian edition gives `Electronic` / `Modern Classical`; music listings describe `classical crossover`, `easy listening`, and Italian `pop barocco` / pop. Place crossover first because it best captures the acoustic and popular-style hybrid, and tag lower-confidence umbrella electronic later. Avoid `synthpop` (not explicitly named for this original album).
- Production: German and Italian LP listings document Varirecording Studios Milano, and mixing at Varirecording/Country Lane. Credited *producer* Gian Piero Reverberi; named recording/mixing personnel including Harry Thumann and Plinio Chiesa are **not co-producers**. Specific gear/process unverified => production blank.
- Instruments: Original album credits name the performing ensemble; official history explicitly calls it an orchestra. Therefore only generic `orchestra` is supported. Neither a synthesizer model nor per-session strings, drums, reeds can be established simply from group description.
- Scales: modest energy 3, electronic 2, theatrical 3, experimental 2, tempo 3, groove 2 are tentative judgments of the documented classical/pop hybrid, **not measured acoustic features**. Mood, polish lack album-specific critical evidence and remain blank. If stronger scrutiny is required, drop uncertain scores rather than impute from artist reputation.
- `sounds_like`: James Last / Richard Clayderman offered as editorial crossover analogies, not a producer-claimed influence. Influenced_by left blank; official lifetime influences do not prove linkage to these nine songs.
- Landmark 1: debut that established the project and entered album charts; artist history and contemporary first album release.

## La Serenissima (1981)
- Original album is ten tracks, Baby Records BR 56027 (MusicBrainz original 1981 release). In Britain and Australia reissued as *Venice in Peril* (1983); not a second record. The UK 1983 disco-mix **single** is not proof that the 1981 album as a whole is disco.
- Styles: classical crossover, baroque pop and easy listening recorded in album reference material; `pop` explicitly named in Italian metadata. Does **not** warrant `disco` or `synthpop` from its later single/remix.
- Producer: Gian Piero Reverberi. MusicBrainz records Laura Giordano as *composer* on songs; this is not evidence she produced the LP. Group credited performance and official orchestra identity support only `orchestra`. No per-album model, multitrack format or specific instrument sublist corroborated.
- Scale omissions are intentional: no defensible album-specific machine ratio, mood, polish, tempo or groove. The theatrical 4 and other populated numbers remain tentative arrangement-focused assessments; human listener should adjust under validator review.
- `sounds_like` is analogy only; `influenced_by` is blank because Bach is historically a composer influence on Reverberi's formation, not documented as a specific direct source for the 1981 arrangements.
- Landmark 1: title piece became international breakthrough and defined the project in the UK (AllMusic); no assertion that foreign remix belongs on original 1981 LP.

## Scaramucce (1982)
- Original Italian 1982 *Scaramucce* LP BR 56044 has ten tracks ending **Trasparenze**, not the list on current official website with **La Serenissima**. MusicBrainz confirms original 1982 Italian and French editions. This edition mismatch is tracked rather than silently overwriting.
- Genres: MusicBrainz groups `baroque pop`, `classical`, `electronic`, `modern classical` at release group scope; original pressing calls `Electronic` and `Modern Classical`. `classicalcrossover` / `easylistening` are specific album description classifications. A weak Italian tertiary source additionally calls it `pop` and `rock progressivo`, added as `prog` **last** due lower confidence; no extrapolation to every track.
- Sound: available overview says more classically oriented than its first two predecessors, yet largely rhythmic. This supports relative `electronic 1`, `energy 3`, `tempo 3`, and moderate `groove 2`; all remain editorial proxies and not tape/audio analysis. Other scale blanks reflect unavailable album-specific qualified reviews.
- Producers vs engineers: original 1982 pressings explicitly credit Gian Piero Reverberi as producer; Cedric Beatty as mixer and Plinio Chiesa as recording engineer. Do not add mixers as co-producers. No listed instrument or hardware models attributable to these album sessions beyond ensemble orchestra.
- Editorial sounds_like examples: Richard Clayderman, Sky; no directly evidenced artist influence specified.
- Landmark 1: original early catalogue title with distinct classical emphasis; `confidence medium` given genre-tag uncertainty and edition discrepancy.

## Venezia 2000 (1983)
- Type `compilation`, **not** a fourth set of fresh compositions. Original 1983 LP (e.g., BR 56052) comprises a reassembled, continuous two-side collection (`Symphony Part 1` and `Symphony Part 2`) drawn from 1980–1982 pieces. The original 15 short segments are not fifteen wholly new songs.
- Genre values apply to the **collected historical program**; LP metadata explicitly calls it `Electronic`, `Modern Classical` and even `Synth-pop` (specific 1983 country editions). Preserve exact `synthpop` token, **not electropop**; demote to last, scope `texture`, as album is predominantly orchestral crossover rather than conventional vocal synthpop. Album overview calls it classical crossover / easy listening. Baroque-pop derives from documented source repertoire; editorial compilation-level category rather than independent 1983 new-recording criticism.
- Production `remixing`: release-format 'Mixed' and credited LP mixers Gian Piero Reverberi, Massimo Noè; nonstop sequencing / shortened excerpts demonstrate a remix/collage assembly of previously released works. Caution: no specified machine, new overdubbing, drum programming or analog tape confirmed; human importer may elect to remove this tag if `remixing` is reserved for full-scale sonic remixes rather than continuous mixes.
- Producer: Gian Piero Reverberi credited on original release context. Credited **mixing** Massimo Noè does not make him a co-producer. Performer is credited orchestra; no fine-grained instrumental attribution.
- Landmark 1: Chartsurfer historical chart has Swiss #1 for four weeks in 1983/84 and German #2; compilation with major release impact, not an arbitrary best-of.
- Region/edition conflicts: different countries have differing printed catalog numbers; French version *Venise de l'an 2000*. Do not create separate duplicate album IDs. All scales tentative because program recycles tracks already described; no per-1983 performances should be invented.

## Artist influences vs documented album influences
The official artist history explicitly names Bach and Strauss among formative classical figures. Unspecified 'Strauss' should not be automatically resolved to Johann or Richard. Neither should appear in album `influenced_by` without direct album-specific evidence. Reverberi's interview mentioning a request for an instrumental Branduardi-type project describes an initial concept, **not** a claim that these records directly imitate Branduardi. Same for period-level 'Venetian Baroque': not itself proof of specific Antonio Vivaldi influence.
