# Review of the first three studio albums

## Madonna (1983) — `madonna-madonna` [PROPOSED ID]
- Pitchfork's Jill Mapes describes club-derived dance-pop centered on synthetic rhythms, with LinnDrum/Oberheim timbres and contrasting piano/guitar parts. The official Madonna catalogue confirms the early dance-club singles.
- `electronic=4`, `groove=4`, `energy=4`, `tempo=4`: relative editorial readings of the central electronic rhythm section and dance-club origin, **not measured bpm or objective loudness**.
- `mood=4`: predominantly upbeat material with emotional exceptions; `theatrical=2`: more direct delivery than later theatrical albums; `experimental=1`: focused accessible pop structures with technologically modern-for-1983 production. `polish=3`: cohesive professionally produced debut, not yet the lavish production of later records.
- Genre: `pop|dance|synthpop`. `voice=clear`, lyrics `love|sensuality`, scene `New York dance-pop`; `landmark=1` as a defining debut. No specific expert-supported sound-alike or influence artist recorded.
- Primary sources: https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-madonna/ ; https://www.madonna.com/products/madonna ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/madonna-mr0000541854

## Like a Virgin (1984) — `m-virgin` [EXISTING ID]
- Pitchfork identifies additional polish versus the debut and tracks mixing electro-pop with soul ballads, irony and melodic hooks. The Guardian emphasizes sexuality, materialism and danceable pop. Engineer Jason Corsaro, interviewed by BMI, describes Nile Rodgers' work with the live players Tony Thompson and Bernard Edwards plus electric bass/guitar and room-recorded drums.
- `electronic=2` rather than copying a synthetic-club stereotype: a significant live-rhythm foundation defines the record. This does **not** mean electronic equipment is absent.
- `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4`: dominant propulsion, despite the slower ballad `Love Don't Live Here Anymore`. `mood=4`: pop brightness mixed with a melancholy ballad; `theatrical=3`: playful character performance; `experimental=1`: largely accessible pop; `polish=4`: polished and muscular studio sound.
- Genre: `pop|dance`. `voice=clear`; lyrics `love|sensuality` (materialistic satire is an additional theme beyond the two allowed fields). `landmark=1` for the defining breakthrough. **Nile Rodgers / Chic are credited collaborators, not asserted as an influenced_by edge without direct support.**
- Primary sources: https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/the-200-best-albums-of-the-1980s/ ; https://www.bmi.com/advice/Tips_From_the_Top_The_Making_of_Madonnas_Like_a_Virgin ; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2011/jun/11/madonna-like-a-virgin ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/r12204

## True Blue (1986) — `m-trueblue` [EXISTING ID]
- AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine highlights dance-pop, classic girl-group echoes, Latin rhythms, political/social subject matter and the pronounced ballad `Live to Tell`. Classic Pop documents a mixed palette, collaborator testimony from Patrick Leonard and Stephen Bray, sequenced bass in `Open Your Heart`, and large differences between individual tracks.
- `electronic=3`: sequenced/synthesized rhythm and acoustic or orchestral contrasts. `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: a heterogeneous set with high-energy singles and a major slow ballad; do not assign the entire album the dance score of one hit. `mood=3`: mixed joyful and serious themes. `theatrical=3`, `experimental=2`, `polish=4`: wider arrangements than the first two albums but accessible studio-pop craftsmanship.
- Genre: `pop|dance`; `voice=clear`; lyrics `love|social` (pregnancy/autonomy, humanitarian concern alongside love). `landmark=1` for her central 1980s breakthrough work.
- Critical nuance: Robert Christgau's less favorable verdict on the album's artistic quality is noted; the Fandome numbers describe sound, not whether the album is good.
- Primary sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/true-blue-mw0000190762 ; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/classic-album/classic-albumadonna-madonna-true-blue/ ; https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?id=862

## Outstanding methodological cautions
- No `sounds_like`/`influenced_by` edges without direct specialist support. Similarity is a reasoned judgment, causality must be independently evidenced.
- The genre taxonomy currently lacks genre terms such as house, trip-hop and art pop. `scene` may temporarily hold subgenre descriptions, but a deliberate taxonomy revision should be considered before later albums.
- Vocal timbre can vary *within* one album; one voice label is a lossy summary. The same problem applies to mood and tempo. Do not make the 0–5 album profile look like acoustic measurement.
- A `landmark=1` means defining in the artist's catalogue, not guaranteed critical acclaim and not a claim about the popularity of every song.
- Source review pending: additional independent specialist corroboration, systematic full-track assessment, independent second rater of qualitative scores.

## Ray of Light: revision (Oct 2026, owner)
The owner flagged that Ray of Light is mostly not a dance record, is close to trip-hop and orchestral, and that Massive Attack's influence is documented. Changes, with sources:
- `influenced_by` Massive Attack, Björk: Classic Pop, "Madonna: The Second Coming" (https://www.classicpopmag.com/?p=15604): she leaned towards the sound of "I Want You" (her 1995 Massive Attack collaboration) and the Björk-written "Bedtime Story". Tricky, Goldie, The Prodigy: same article, she offered them to work on the album (they turned it down).
- `producers` + Marius De Vries ("Massive Attack associate", four tracks, SPIN 1998 cover story, https://www.spinmagazine.com/2016/03/madonna-ray-of-light-cover-story-1998/) and Patrick Leonard (album credits).
- `instruments` + strings: string arrangements by Craig Armstrong (liner notes, tracks 9–10); "electronic rhythms combined with orchestral textures" on "Frozen" and "Drowned World" (Wikipedia, Ray of Light). `production` sampling: San Sebastian Strings sample (liner notes).
- `genres`: electronica, trip hop and new age added/moved up (Wikipedia: "electronica, trip hop, techno-pop, and new-age sound"; The Guardian 2019: "convincing, idiosyncratic trip-hop"); house and drum and bass as elements.
- `groove` 3 → 2: only a few tracks are for dancing (title track, Nothing Really Matters); Madonna called it fundamentally a pop record; SPIN: "haunted singer/songwriter introspection and beat-savvy electronic exotica".
