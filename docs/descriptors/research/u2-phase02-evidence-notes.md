# U2 — Phase 02: album-level evidence notes (27 October 2026)

This ledger covers **only the three new phase-02 records**. Numeric 0–5 ratings are Fandome research judgments grounded in the described material, **not** numbers asserted by a critic. Empty `sounds_like` and (for the first two) `influenced_by` are intentional; no cross-artist resemblance or album-specific external musical influence was independently supported strongly enough. Source URLs below are repeated in the machine-readable source and genre ledgers.

## `u2-underabloodredsky` — *Under a Blood Red Sky* (1983)

**Release and exceptional inclusion.** U2 dates the original eight-song live album **21 November 1983** and credits **Jimmy Iovine** as its album producer. Original sleeve and MusicBrainz location-level credits confirm the audio combines **Denver Red Rocks (5 June), Boston (6 May) and St. Goarshausen / Loreley (20 August)**. This is **not** the separate longer 1984 *Live at Red Rocks* concert video, which has different audio/track scope. Although marketed as a mini-LP / EP, Fandome `type=live` is the more informative classification because the recordings are live; do not duplicate it as an EP. U2 records a UK No. 2 chart peak and unusually extended chart life, and contemporary Hot Press reviewer Bill Graham describes the band's emerging headline command. These are concrete reasons for `landmark=1`, the live-album exception.

- [Original album](https://www.u2.com/products/under-a-blood-red-sky); [original sleeve / venue breakdown](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_under_a_blood_red_sky_album_original_release); [1983 Hot Press criticism](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/bill_graham_in_hot_press_1090).

**Genre ranking.** `postpunk` and `bigmusic` identify the concert's period and large-scale dynamic most directly. `alternativerock`, generic `rock` and the AllMusic style variants follow, each indexed in `u2_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`. `bigmusic` is a genre string used by Album of the Year; it requires explicit addition to Fandome instead of silently converting to `arenarock` or `albumrock`. Broad catalog genre families are retained because the user requests every sourced name; they are lower-ranked, not ten independent claims about the sound.

**Scales (energy 5, electronic 1, mood 3, theatrical 5, experimental 1, polish 2, tempo 4, groove 2).** Hot Press highlights stage command and Edge's simultaneous melodic/rhythmic roles, and AllMusic describes faster, harder concert takes than studio recordings. The high energy and theatricality contrast with limited rearrangement or new studio experimentation. Mood = 3 reflects both rousing audience-facing performances and politically grave numbers rather than uniform euphoria. Polish = 2 acknowledges location recordings mixed for an LP while retaining conspicuous performance texture. Electronics = 1 registers guitar amplification/effects, not synth-led production. Groove = 2 describes pulse-driven post-punk/arena rock rather than a dance album.

**Voice, lyrics, instruments and production.** Bono's projected concert voice is `powerful|clear`. `electricguitar|bass|drums` are the documented core quartet instrumentation. The Edge's piano part is known for *New Year's Day*, but the sources checked do not decisively identify the keyboard performance on this particular eight-track live master; **piano is therefore omitted rather than inferred from the studio recording or a different concert video. Do not confuse instruments credited for the separate Red Rocks film with this LP.** The one certain production token is `concertrecording`. The original LP identifies Iovine as album producer; **Shelly Yakus is mixer**, **Bobby Cohen is assistant engineer**, **Steve Lillywhite is location recorder on Denver tracks**, and DIR / EDR had radio-location production roles. They are not additional overall album producers in this descriptor. Lyrical subjects (`political|social|spiritual|love|introspection`) are inherited from these *performances of the songs*, not imagined newly written 1983 material.

**Edition conflict.** Original *The Electric Co.* contained short quotations from Sondheim's songs that subsequent pressings removed. No samples, covers, other artists or incidental theatrical quotations are transferred to `influenced_by`. The official album page's simplified “recorded at Red Rocks” credit should not be interpreted as the provenance of all eight tracks. Original source details are more granular. AllMusic calls the record an EP; U2 calls it their first live album. Here `type=live` and notes preserve both positions.

## `u2-theunforgettablefire` — *The Unforgettable Fire* (1984)

**Release, sessions and production.** Released **1 October 1984**. Original sleeve and U2 official page assign **Brian Eno and Daniel Lanois** production/engineering; **Kevin Killen** is an additional engineer, not a producer, and string arranger **Noel Kelehan** and credited Fairlight player **Paul Barrett** are not producers. Slane Castle rooms and Windmill Lane Studios are the recording sites. Contemporary 1984 reviews and later engineering interviews show a deliberate break with *War* toward less declarative, more fragmentary, atmosphere-first music. This is a landmark artistic pivot (`landmark=1`).

- [Official credits](https://www.u2.com/products/the-unforgettable-fire); [1984 sleeve transcription](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_the_unforgettable_fire_album_original_release); [Daniel Lanois interview](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/10/daniel-lanois-interview/); [original producer discussion](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/the-magic-of-daniel-lanois/2506).

**Genre evidence and scope.** AOTY names `alternativerock`, `postpunk`, `artrock`, `dreampop`, `bigmusic` and `ambientpop`; AllMusic retains mainstream umbrella tags. The SHFL lists `rock` and `artrock`. The first (`artrock`) emphasises this LP's compositional experiments, while `ambientpop` and `dreampop` are marked **some tracks** for the atmospheric or near-ambient passages. The source says *ambient pop*, **not automatically ambient or synthpop**. No new instrument token is needed; `bigmusic` is the new genre addition shared with the other records.

**Scales (energy 3, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 4, polish 3, tempo 3, groove 2).** *Pride* and *Wire* push intensity upward, balanced against the sustained, quiet or wordless *4th of July* and *MLK*. Mood is darker/reflective because *Bad* and *Elvis Presley and America* carry unease; uplifting moments prevent 0–1. Electronic = 2 denotes conspicuous keyboards/Fairlight and treated recordings inside a rock band. Experimental = 4 reflects documented tape slowing, control-room improvisation and ambient reconstruction; polish = 3 contrasts atmospheric intent with audible looseness and unfinished vocal sketches. Theatrical = 3 recognises the anthems but not uniform arena presentation. Groove = 2 retains some danceable beat-led passages without club focus.

**Instrument provenance.** The original sleeve specifically credits The Edge's guitar and keyboards, Adam's bass, Larry's drums, the string arrangement and a **Fairlight** played by Paul Barrett. Hence `electricguitar|bass|drums|keyboards|synth|strings`. The Fairlight is placed in the `production` column under the precise named machine; synth reflects that credited electronic instrument rather than inference based on art rock. `drummachine` is **not** added: The Edge describes the title track as originally composed over a drum-machine backbeat but also describes Larry recording the basic drum part. There is no sufficient proof the original programmed beat survives in the final album mix. Neither a particular pedal nor a tape recorder model other than the original session equipment is guessed.

**Production provenance.** `roomambience` is supported by Lanois describing the unusably reverberant ballroom and the denser, book-lined library; `delay` by The Edge's first-person remarks about playing into his own echoes on the record. `tapeeffects|sonicmanipulation` map to Lanois's first-person account of making *Elvis Presley and America* by slowing an existing band performance and recording improvised vocal takes; the separate Edge interview describes treatments on *4th of July*. `overdubbing` is explicitly mentioned by Lanois for vocals and harmonies. `analogtape` rests on the Stephens 24-track tape setup documented by Vintage King. `stringarrangements` and `fairlight` are named on the original sleeve. The sleeve does not support a specific Fairlight generation, so **`fairlight` rather than a guessed `fairlightii`**. Some descriptions say *Elvis Presley and America* is an inspired fragment; [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/the-making-of-a-legend-551586) regards it as underdeveloped. This disagreement helps explain `experimental=4`, but does not imply a wholly lo-fi LP.

**Other fields.** Main voice `powerful|soft|clear` reflects Bono's force on *Pride* / *Bad* and hushed, wordless delivery elsewhere. Lyrics cover Martin Luther King tributes, addiction and spiritual identity: `political|social|spiritual|introspection|identity`. Influences and sonically similar acts are blank: Eno's presence as a collaborator/producer is **not** a free licence to list him as an album influence. The producer credits are not the same as artist inspiration.

## `u2-thejoshuatree` — *The Joshua Tree* (1987)

**Identity and personnel.** Original release **9 March 1987**, **existing Fandome ID `u2-thejoshuatree`** (do not create a new album). Original sleeve: **Brian Eno and Daniel Lanois produced**, **Flood recorded**, **Dave Meegan and Pat McCarthy additionally engineered**, **Steve Lillywhite mixed four tracks**, and **Mark Wallis was mix engineer**. The four latter names **must not be placed in the `producers` cell**. Sessions involved Danesmoate and a temporary home studio as well as Windmill Lane; the official U2 page gives the simplified Windmill Lane credit. Lanois describes room low-midrange and dynamic acoustic contribution. U2's US No. 1 and its lasting cultural importance are well evidenced (`landmark=1`).

- [U2 official discography](https://www.u2.com/products/u2-the-joshua-tree); [1987 sleeve transcription](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_the_joshua_tree_album_original_release); [Hi-Fi News room and mixing history](https://www.hifinews.com/content/u2-joshua-tree-production-notes).

**Genre hierarchy.** AOTY names `poprock`, `alternativerock`, **`bigmusic` and `heartlandrock`** (both new tokens), `postpunk`. AllMusic styles include `albumrock`, `alternativepoprock`, `alternativeindierock`, `collegerock`, `contemporarypoprock`; the latter broad labels are kept after the descriptive core. Classic Rock documents folk, blues and country influence on this album. `folk|blues|country` are placed last as selected passages or texture, **not** whole-album pure folk/blues/country. The Edge explicitly identifies gospel listening while making *I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For*, so `gospel` is a **some-tracks** stylistic tag, not a claim of an actual gospel choir on the 1987 studio LP. The 1988 *Rattle and Hum* Harlem choir performance is different.

**Scales (energy 4, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 4, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 2).** *Where the Streets Have No Name*, *Bullet the Blue Sky*, *In God's Country* are forceful, while *Running to Stand Still* and *Mothers of the Disappeared* restrain the average energy, speed and vocal projection. The political and yearning content makes `mood=2`, despite melodic uplift. Large-scale guitar crescendos are theatrical (`4`), but stripped-down, intimate songs stop it being `5`. The record is carefully produced while retaining deliberate room sound (`polish=4`). Electronic = 2 acknowledges Eno's **DX7** textures and processing within conventional rock-band instrumentation. Groove = 2 because its tempos are song-driven, not dancefloor-driven. Experimental = 3 for complex *Streets* arrangement and novel Infinite Guitar timbre within relatively conventional memorable songs.

**Instruments.** Core band electric guitar, bass, drums plus the album's acoustic guitars / slide parts, keyboards and synthesizer. **Bono's harmonica**, **Brian Eno's keyboard / DX7 programmes**, **Daniel Lanois's tambourine, omnichord, additional guitar**, **the Armin family's strings** and **Arklow Silver Band brass** are unambiguous in the original sleeve; The Edge discusses slide on *Bullet the Blue Sky* and piano on *Running to Stand Still* is confirmed by [Lanois and The Edge's recollections](https://mmusicmag.com/m/2012/03/u2/). `omnichord` already exists in the live Fandome instrument list although omitted from the user's older example enumeration. The Infinite Guitar on *With or Without You* was an actual Michael Brook device, documented by Brook and Edge, **not an E-Bow**: include under `electricguitar`, not `ebow` in production. Avoid the candidate `drummachine`: Lanois discusses a Yamaha beatbox initially used for the song's guide, but does not establish that its output survived the final LP mix. This should be reconsidered only with exact final-track evidence.

**Production.** `dx7` and `synthprogramming` are straight original sleeve credits. `roomambience` comes from Lanois's remarks about the particular Danesmoate living room and separation window. `overdubbing` comes from his discussions of recording guitar passes on *With or Without You* and rebuilding *Streets*. `hornarrangements` is supported by Paul Barrett's credit for *Red Hill Mining Town*, not an inferred orchestral effect. `delay` is the source-documented Edge guitar treatment; the rig's exact model varies in retellings, so **no guessed manufacturer/model token**. `analogtape` and sampler model names are omitted because they are not sufficiently established in the 1987 sleeve and cited production recollections.

**Lyrics.** Central America and *Mothers of the Disappeared* support `political|social`; *I Still Haven't Found* and *Where the Streets* support `spiritual|identity`; *With or Without You* is `love|introspection`. The language is English, with a predominantly Dublin band working through American musical imagery rather than recording in the US.

**Influences: narrowly documented, not a personnel list.** The 1987 [Guitar World interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/u2-edge-joshua-tree-1987-interview) has The Edge explicitly naming **Jimi Hendrix, Willie Dixon, and Archie Edwards** for *Bullet the Blue Sky*. On [U2's official gospel single page](https://www.u2.com/products/i-still-haven-t-found-what-i-m-looking-for), he names **The Staple Singers, the Mighty Clouds of Joy and Reverend James Cleveland** as music they listened to during *Joshua Tree* sessions. On [U2's official With or Without You page](https://www.u2.com/products/with-or-without-you), Bono cites **Scott Walker’s *Climate of Hunter*** as an influence on the LP, with **The Walker Brothers** and **The Righteous Brothers** reported as particular references for that song. The spreadsheet stores **artists only**, never a sampled track, covered tune or literary inspiration. Note that the U2 page phrases two of these as “are said to have inspired” rather than direct quotes: those two names are somewhat weaker than Bono's explicit Scott Walker statement, so their provenance is transparent in the sources ledger.

**No unearned extra credits.** Michael Brook *invented* the Infinite Guitar but does not thereby become an album producer, guest player or `influenced_by` act. Steve Lillywhite mixed specific songs but was not album producer. The gospel choir heard on a later concert/film recording is not part of this 1987 studio album. `sounds_like` remains blank because general roots-rock adjacency with a band like Bruce Springsteen is not a documented track/album-specific sonic comparison in these sources.
