# Franco Battiato — Phase 09: album evidence & editorial reasoning

**Date:** 2026-10-26. Only 2012, 2014, 2015 and 2019 albums. Sources in `battiato_phase09_sources.csv`; exact per-genre reasoning in `battiato_phase09_genre_evidence.csv`.

## `battiato-apritisesamo` — *Apriti sesamo* (2012)

**Provenance/release.** Universal original CD issued 23 October 2012, ten songs. Qobuz lists 1 October for some digital territorial metadata: prefer label's Italian physical date. Artist and Universal present this as a last new pop-song collection. Long-term artistic collaboration with lyricist Manlio Sgalambro does **not** imply an extra producer credit.

**Genres.** Album pop is explicit in Universal and OndaRock, and Qobuz gives pop. AllMusic's Pop/Rock broad metadata motivates low-priority `poprock`. OndaRock calls out electronic surfaces, most particularly *La polvere del branco* and the title track. `classicalpop` is editorial composite for the critic's explicit **pop plus classical passages**, not a verbatim label; keep after electronic. Universal invokes *canzone d'autore* in framing.

**Instrument and production audit.** The original CD-credit transcription lists bassist **Faso**, drummer **Gavin Harrison**, piano **Carlo Guaitoli**, strings **Nuovo Quartetto Italiano**, guitar **Simon Tong**, organ **Carlo Boccadoro** (particular track), cello **Luca Simoncini**, percussion **Gianluca Ruggeri** (particular track), and Pino Pischetola as programmer of rhythmic patterns. Independent period report on Rockol corroborates Tong and Hammond. **Source-specific instrument `guitar` is deliberately generic**: OndaRock explicitly says the album avoids *electric guitar*. `stringarrangements` is inferred narrowly from explicitly credited arrangement/strings and described classical passages; `drumprogramming` is directly credited. Named software or synth models are unverified and absent.

**Producer list.** CD credit: **Franco Battiato (producer/arranged by)** and **Angelo Privitera (producer)**. **Francesco Cattini** is executive only, **Pinaxa** recorded/mixed/programmed, **Sgalambro** lyricist. Qobuz's simplified credits only show Battiato; full original physical credits take precedence for second producer.

**Text/voice/language.** Ten close/clear melodic vocals, compact arrangements; Battiato's recurring themes as reviewed by Rockol/OndaRock: metaphysics and death (`spiritual|introspection`), social breakdown (`social|political`), childhood and remembrance (`nostalgia|identity`). OndaRock specifically describes **Italian, Sicilian, Latin, English** in *Caliti Junku*. Other references are quotes and sources of ideas, not necessarily vocal languages. `energy=2`, `groove=1`, `tempo=2` reflect the review's short measured melodic songs, despite powerful live bass/drums acknowledged by the artist. `electronic=2`/`experimental=2` reflect modest electronic layers amid conventional songwriting; `polish=4` documented finished studio production. `mood=2` is restrained existential theme and gentle treatment, `theatrical=2` occasional grand classical passages but not a vocal stage work.

**Musical influence rule.** `Stefano Landi` (the adapted *Passacaglia della vita*), `Christoph Willibald Gluck` (*Orfeo ed Euridice* quotation in *Caliti Junku*), and `Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov` (*Scheherazade* source in the title track) are reported as borrowed MUSIC by Rockol and OndaRock. Do not infer musical influence from **Santa Teresa, Dante, Ibn Hamdis**, whose cited contributions are literary/religious texts. `sounds_like` Alice and David Sylvian are editorial sonic analogies only, not documented debts. `landmark=1`: final all-new song-cycle after his pop career, corroborated by Universal and Rolling Stone.

**Critical disagreement.** Rockol (Paola De Simone) rates album 9/10 and praises coherent high-minded sophistication; OndaRock calls the record an appreciable hybrid, whereas Rolling Stone's retrospective notes refined arrangements but limited exceptional tunes. Quantitative scales are judgments; artistic evaluations not forced into false consensus.

## `battiato-joepattissexperimentalgroup` — *Joe Patti's Experimental Group* (2014)

**Release and billing.** September **16, 2014** Universal original, NOT date of 2024 10th-anniversary vinyl. This is a co-billed **Battiato / Pino “Pinaxa” Pischetola** project, both named producers in Universal track metadata, original disc credit transcription and original licensed release. Fandome `artist` remains Franco Battiato to associate this descriptor with his ID, but add co-billing record; don't mislabel the album as solo.

**Genre evidence.** Universal's electronic-and-experimental formulation supports `experimentalelectronic|electronic`. OndaRock reviewer Michele Palozzo explicitly characterises it as **elettronica psichedelica** and describes ambient passages, ambient-techno detours, and **kosmische** ancestry; `kosmische` is new exact token. Brian Eno/Harold Budd/Popol Vuh are _critical sonic comparisons_; Stockhausen is _Stockhausen-like collage_, a qualified critic's documented reference plus Battiato's known early experimental legacy. The `influenced_by` field includes Stockhausen at _critic-supported but not new direct artist interview_ level. Licensed Qobuz variant contains the flat genre `Pop` which clashes with both serious reviews and label: preserve as last, metadata-only with low evidentiary strength.

**Instruments.** Rockol explicitly names **Battiato at piano, keyboards and synthesizers**; the 2014 pressing credits the same. Pinaxa credited for programmed computer rhythms; nothing in those original **studio** credits identifies model VCS3. Thus `synth|keyboards|piano` and `programming|drumprogramming`. Rockol says Battiato's voice is sung, declaimed and reversed on some tracks: `instrumental|spoken|soft`, `backmasking`. Its references to reverb/echo do not justify `heavyreverb` as a specific processing intensity. No DAW names copied from the wholly separate 2014 concert technical interview; no confirmed VCS3, Pro Tools or analogue tape.

**Themes and voice.** Much of album is instrumental, with recycled sung/spoken lyric scraps; `spiritual|introspection` is weak as album-wide generality and rests on Giordano Bruno/existential references; removing these terms at import if stricter threshold is reasonable. *L'isola elefante* contains German passage recycled from *Shackleton*; thus `language=it|de` with de minority. `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=1`: usually atmospheric with a few violent programmed-percussion peaks. `electronic=5` nearly all sourced sonic architecture electronic apart from piano; `experimental=4` unusual non-pop structure (critic observes experiments less radical than 1970s); `polish=4` carefully placed studio details by Pinaxa; `mood=2`, `theatrical=2` sparse/lisergic imagery. `landmark=1` because of unusual co-billed return to seminal experimental idiom and OndaRock year-end recognition.

**Critics' disputes.** OndaRock finds album aesthetically interesting but not genuinely unprecedented experimental technique (6.5 score); Rockol finds strong genuine return to 1970s Battiato. Keep subjective evaluation separate from acoustic evidence.

## `battiato-anthologylenostreanime` — *Anthology - Le nostre anime* (2015)

**Scope and eligibility.** Universal **standard 3CD 6 November 2015**, 52 tracks; the **6CD+4DVD mega 13 November 2015**, 112 songs and films. The official site and Rockol call it the one retrospective specifically chosen by Battiato. Includes fresh title track, *Lo spirito degli abissi*, new English Mika duet version of *Centro di gravità permanente*, cover *Se telefonando*, remixes and remasters. Qualifies as *landmark artist-curated anthology*, not a new studio album. `type=compilation`. `album_id` uses the FULL official title to differentiate from same-name song.

**Genres and scales.** SentireAscoltare explicitly tags pop/cantautori; MusicBrainz genre tags for entire release are pop, electronic, new wave, rock; latter tags refer to SOME musical periods, not homogeneous sound. The 3CD version mostly surveys pop hits, later spiritual pieces and covers; **do not copy avant-garde electronic/classical genres from the 6CD deluxe extras**. As reviewer Franco Zanetti says, it is a multi-era retrospective, and assigning a typical `energy`, `electronic`, `mood`, `theatrical`, `experimental`, `polish`, `tempo` or `groove` by averaging decades would be spurious. All **eight scales deliberately blank**, and `sounds_like` blank (unhelpful eclectic anthology).

**Specific 2015 recording instrumentation.** For the newly recorded title track, period Radio Italia, SentireAscoltare and original official site name **Royal Philharmonic Orchestra strings, Gavin Harrison drums, Jakko Jakszyk guitars, Carlo Guaitoli piano/keyboards** and Pinaxa as **mixer**. `instruments` is these confirmed title-track instruments, placed in order of title-track importance, **not a summary of 52-track entire CD**. Production `remastering|remixing` reflects the many explicitly labelled new 2015 remasters and mixes, with `stringarrangements` confined to title track. Two new production tokens are required in `production.ts`. No invented CD-spanning synth machine list or mixing producer credited. `producers` left EMPTY because no album-wide retrospective producer credits were documented; having chosen the tracklist doesn't make Battiato producer of all old performances.

**Themes and languages.** Official site calls new songs spiritual, Rockol highlights anthology of social and personal classic repertory. `spiritual|introspection|love|social|political|nostalgia` are general **curated material** themes, rather than claims that all tracks are consistent. Standard disc contains Italian, Sicilian *Stranizza d'amuri* and English *Center of Gravity*; `it|en|scn`. Do not include French based only on deluxe track *J'entends siffler le train*. Vocal types `clear|soft|spoken` span much older recorded epochs and are weak, not newly sung uniform palette. `landmark=1` artist's final chosen career collection.

**Edition uncertainty.** Universal catalog explicitly indicates different UPCs and release days. Battiato official text discusses three inediti, deluxe marketing calls four; count differs over whether cover is called previously unreleased. Sources agree on the actual new-recording list. Keep relative ratings `confidence=medium` because many values are weak for a compilation.

## `battiato-torneremoancora` — *Torneremo ancora* (2019)

**Type and co-billing.** Sony released it **18 October 2019** as **Franco Battiato / Royal Philharmonic Concert Orchestra** (licensed Sony/Qobuz and AllMusic listing as both main artists), with Carlo Guaitoli conducting. `type=live` under the user's *landmark live exception*, and `landmark=1`. It is **NOT** a 15-track new studio album: fourteen catalogue songs and only **one newly issued original**. Rockol and SentireAscoltare clarify most were recorded at **2017 tour rehearsals**; one bonus *L'era del cinghiale bianco* is an actual audience-attended concert excerpt. Contemporary Sony promotion more loosely describes all as recorded during concerts. `concertrecording` is explicitly *partial* (one live crowd song); for the rest live-multitrack/rehearsal origin.

**Instruments and engineering.** Rockol's press conference quotes Pischetola and describes **concert grand piano and Royal Philharmonic orchestral recording with separated tracks**. Its liner notes credit **Carlo Guaitoli piano and conductor**, **Angelo Privitera keyboards and programming**, **Pino Pischetola engineering**, and orchestra. `orchestra|strings|piano|keyboards`, `concertrecording|programming|overdubbing|stringarrangements`. `overdubbing` specifically applies to title song: Battiato's *2016/2017* guide voice used with Royal Philharmonic orchestra taped separately in London in **May 2019**. `stringarrangements` refers to re-scored concert repertoire and string additions. No per-machine software assertion. No named **whole-album producer** credit from those original sources, so `producers` stays empty; 2022 Universal later notes Battiato producer on the *title track only*.

**Vocal/themes/scale judgments.** Rolling Stone's Claudio Todesco explicitly calls new lead voice fragile/tremulous; reviewer is evaluating 2019 title song rather than claiming Battiato's historic voice has changed on old classics. Since `raspy` describes a different quality, use `soft|clear`. Themes: new song spirituality, repeated existence and inward focus, plus other selected songs love/social critique; not all lyrics newly written. Predominantly Italian performances. Low drive `energy=1`, `tempo=1`, `groove=0`; strongly orchestral but still some programmed keyboard passages `electronic=1`, `polish=4`, `experimental=1`, restrained elegiac `mood=1`, concert-scale `theatrical=3`. These relate to THIS orchestral issue, **not to the originals**. Sounds-like analogies are merely editorial: Alice / Antony and the Johnsons for intimate symphonic arrangements. Do **not** treat composers of included covers (Endrigo, Adamo) as Battiato's album-level influences.

**Genre evidence and disagreement.** AllMusic labels `Classical Pop` style and top-level `Pop/Rock`. `classicalpop` is primary genre; `poprock` is weak metadata, NOT evidence of a loud rock backing. Italian canzone d'autore is inherent repertoire and explicitly discussed by Rockol, editorial classification. SentireAscoltare reviewer questions album's independent artistic purpose, while Rockol calls it a moving coda. Its final-release significance motivates landmark despite the mixed rehearsal/live format.

## Citation method

Every source has a **literal, verified public URL** recorded with what it supports, and each genre has a separate source/scope row. High confidence means strong release/credits evidence, not unanimity about aesthetic value. The output has no copied review prose. Links to original studies, audio engineering and media are not replaced by unreproducible guesses.
