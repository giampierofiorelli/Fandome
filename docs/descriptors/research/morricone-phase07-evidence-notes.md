# Ennio Morricone — Phase 07: album-specific evidence notes

**Research date:** 26 October 2026. This is an original-LP-centric continuation, not an inventory of every film score or digital release. Original track orders are supported in `morricone_phase07_sources.csv`; per-genre justifications live in `morricone_phase07_genre_evidence.csv`. All scale values are anchored **editorial assessments of sourced album-specific descriptions**, not sound measurements. No recording producer, session machine or processing technique is asserted without a corresponding album-specific source. All `production` values are therefore left blank and `polish` left unknown.

## Il serpente — 1973; General Music ZSLGE 55495 (10 tracks)

**Release:** EIL describes a 1973 original ten-track General Music LP with exact catalogue number; the French BnF 1973 RCA two-track 45 rpm is a distinct issue. The 2009 21-track GDM issue is expanded, not the first album. The original Italian record is **Il serpente**, whereas the French film is *Le serpent*.

- **Genres:** `experimental` for original tracks *Astrazione con ritmo* and *Esplicitamente sospeso* (RSD); `musiqueconcrete` for atonality and film's sound-design-like cues (Gianmarco Diana, and corroborating Reece Crothers LP-side review); `jazz|psychedelic` for jazzy psychedelic passages described by Crothers; `rock` secondary for track *Nadine*'s rock guitars; `march` for *Parata militare*; `soundtrack` as format last. `psychedelic` is a **new exact token** distinct from existing `psychedelia`. Do not infer jazz genre from a generic spy-film association: these are album-specific descriptions.
- **Instruments:** RSD documents `organ|drums|electricguitar`; Crothers reviews the original LP with `strings|keyboards|piano|flute|percussion`. The LP is clearly mixed acoustic and electrically amplified, but sources do not identify a synthesizer, specific machine or production chain.
- **Voice/lyrics/language:** Edda Dell’Orso and Cantori Moderni voices are documented; use `instrumental|choral`, leaving language and lyrics blank because words are not independently confirmed. Do not infer Latin from the track *Orazione*.
- **Scales:** `energy=3`, `mood=1`, `theatrical=4` follow brooding suspense plus bursts of driving rhythm; `electronic=2` is a modest rating given electric keyboards/organ and sound-design textures (not a claim of synthesizer technology). `experimental=4` is supported by avant-garde sonorities; `tempo=3` reflects mix of languid pieces and march/drive; `groove=2` is limited to the funky danceable *Nadine*-type passages. `polish` absent: no studio-process evidence. `landmark=0`: distinctive but no verified broad historical landmark claim.
- **Credits and comparisons:** composer Ennio Morricone only; no named production credit. `sounds_like=Lalo Schifrin|Piero Piccioni` is an **editorial sonic analogy**, not an influence. Empty `influenced_by`.
- **Limits:** Diana's remarks on radio-source pop cues describe the film; those cues may be absent from the LP; therefore no unverified film-only pop genre was added. The 2009 expanded-cue attributes are not imported into the original LP.

## Sepolta viva — 1973; Beat LPF 021 (13 tracks)

- **Release:** SoundtrackCollector and first-press collectors agree on the 1973 13-track Beat vinyl. Later combined releases with *L’Anticristo* and added bonus cues do not define the 1973 album. Score is solely Morricone's; Bruno Nicolai orchestrated/conducted but this is **not** production or co-billing.
- **Genres:** MMI specialist calls it symphonic and classical, dominated by chamber-like lyrical themes; only `classical|soundtrack` assigned. The music's gothic film setting is not itself a genre. No unrelated jazz or horror token.
- **Instruments:** Detailed critical listening to original cues names `strings`, `piano`, `violin`, `woodwinds`, `organ`, `harp` and `harpsichord`. All appear on the original soundtrack repertoire; woodwinds should not be atomised into oboe/flute without identification in this recording.
- **Voice/language:** the critic identifies *Asperges Me Vidi Aquam* as the **exceptional female a cappella Latin cue** on original LP track 12; hence `instrumental|choral` and `la`. The rest is predominantly instrumental. `lyrics` left empty: a single liturgical title does not warrant assuming the overall album's lyrical subjects.
- **Scales:** `energy=2`, `tempo=1` and `groove=0` for delicate predominantly slow romantic writing; `mood=1` for sustained melancholic colour; `theatrical=3` for orchestral swell; `experimental=1` because largely conventional thematic harmony. `electronic` and `polish` blank, not assigned from period. `landmark=0` relative to canonical Morricone albums; high evidence confidence for instruments.
- **Conflict:** A 2020 MMI article says broadly no choral work, but its later 2022 specialist revision specifically identifies an a cappella track; retain `choral` as a **minor** aspect with track scope, not album-wide choir.

## Giordano Bruno — December 1973; RCA OLS 21 (11 tracks)

- **Release:** SoundtrackCollector gives the original December 1973 RCA OLS 21, eleven tracks; Quartet identifies its RCA source. The 2009 GDM / 2025 Quartet expansion has 21 tracks, including material never on the 1973 LP.
- **Genres:** Pugliese explicitly distinguishes avant-garde contemporary writing from Gregorian/Palestrina-like Latin polyphony. Use `avantgarde|sacredmusic|classical|soundtrack`, with `sacredmusic` on choral tracks and `avantgarde` important in sound architecture. Don't label religious film themes alone as spiritual lyrics: here sung liturgical texts are documented.
- **Instruments:** Critic names `englishhorn` (corno inglese on original theme), `strings`, church `organ`, `woodwinds`, `mallets` (vibraphone), `percussion`, `piano`, `trumpet`, `violin`, `drums` (march drum), `flute`, `clarinet`, `bells` as part of original-track descriptions. Some later alternate versions use related parts; only original-track-identified instrumental families included. **`englishhorn` is new; do not misname as ordinary `oboe`.**
- **Voice/language/lyrics:** Latin texts in *Super flumina Babilonis*, *Domine quando veneris* and *Alleluja*, chorally sung. `voice=choral|instrumental`, `language=la`, `lyrics=spiritual`.
- **Scales:** `mood=0` for accusatory tragic severity, `energy=2` for slow choral soundscapes interspersed with march sections, `theatrical=4` for epic ritual weight, `experimental=4` for modernist composition, `tempo=2`, `groove=0`. `electronic` blank (modernist does NOT mean electronic) and `polish` blank.
- **Influence criterion:** Pugliese directly says modern reinterpretations of Palestrina-style polyphony; record `influenced_by=Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina` as specifically sourced period-compositional influence. The reviewer compares dissonant sonorities to Ligeti and Penderecki, but these belong in `sounds_like`, not influence. `landmark=1` editorial selection supported by review's exceptionally strong reception; no named producer credit.

## Il sorriso del grande tentatore — 1974; Beat LPF 026 (6 tracks)

- **Release:** The 1974 six-track LP is corroborated by soundtrack archives and original pressing registry. Reissues in 2010 and 2024 contain **16 tracks**, and Roundtable's 2015/2019 issue adds two formerly unissued. Score may be classified in 1973 by the Morricone foundation based on commission/film-production year; 1974 is the **LP year**.
- **Genres:** Six Latin liturgical movements make `sacredmusic` central, qualified by experimental avant-garde choral orchestration (`avantgarde|experimental|classical`). Roundtable's publisher tags give `electronic|jazz|psychedelic` as secondary **sonic textures**, not verified synthesis equipment or a fully electronic album. Specialist reissue comment notes rhythmically funky moments (`funk` is limited to some tracks). `soundtrack` comes last. The label's **library music** tag is not used as a genre because this title is a commissioned film score, not demonstrated stock/library music.
- **Voice, language, lyrics:** Liturgical Latin collage performed by choir; exact singers described inconsistently (female choir vs children's Arcum choir) so `choral`, `la`, `spiritual` are supportable, not the choir demographics. Some syllables are unusually clipped or distorted-sounding, but no proven production vocal processing, so `production` stays blank.
- **Instruments:** Orchestra as a unit is credited by Stabat Mater reference and Beat, but detailed string/keyboard/percussion session instrumentation on **the 1974 original LP** is not reliable enough to claim individual instruments. `orchestra` only. No fabricated organ/synth or machine models. Do not equate a word in the movement title with instrumentation.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` balances hypnotic stillness with explosive staccato chant; `mood=0` troubled/devotional menace; `theatrical=5` and `experimental=4` from choral dissonance, `tempo=2`, `groove=1` because rhythmic passages are exceptional, `electronic=1` cautiously based on publisher tag, `polish` blank.
- **Landmark and comparisons:** `landmark=1` is an editorial attribution supported by the reissue's report that Morricone considered it especially significant; label expressly compares the sonics to The Electric Prunes (*Mass in F Minor*) and William Sheller (*Lux Aeterna*), hence `sounds_like` ONLY. This is **not** an influence statement and remains blank in `influenced_by`.

## Systematic uncertainty rules

- Scale values are human judgments grounded in specific descriptions; unsupported studio polish, electronics, processing, and producer identity are empty.
- `psychedelic` is a new **literal** genre token, not a silently normalised synonym of `psychedelia`. `englishhorn` is a documented instrument distinct from `oboe`. Both must be registered in source lists, genre families and validator before tests.
- Every genre has a source and a scope; album rows contain no tracks from earlier phases.
