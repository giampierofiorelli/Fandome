## IMPORTANT: album credits and co-billing

1. *Apriti sesamo*: original 2012 CD credits list **Franco Battiato** and **Angelo Privitera** as producers; Francesco Cattini is *executive producer* only. Pino Pischetola's role is audio recording/mixing and rhythm programming, **not** an unambiguous album producer. Simon Tong appears simply as guitarist: **do not infer electric guitar** (OndaRock says no electric guitar).
2. *Joe Patti's Experimental Group*: **co-billed release**, EXACT historical artist credit **Franco Battiato / Pino “Pinaxa” Pischetola** (also shown by licensors as `Franco Battiato, Pino "Pinaxa" Pischetola`). Both are explicitly named **producers** in original and official 2024 reissue metadata. Add to `src/data/coBilled.ts` using this phase album_id and exact credit. Pischetola is the co-artist, not merely a featured guest. No VCS3, Fairlight, DAW or hardware model is asserted for the 2014 **studio** album; the Rockol critic explicitly says its VCS3 use is unverified. **Do not transpose Pro Tools / Space Echo from the separate 2014 live-tour article into the studio production field.**
3. *Anthology - Le nostre anime*: `compilation`, artist-supervised 3CD, with 2015 remixes/remasters, new recordings and a few previously unreleased songs. The **standard 3CD UPC 00602547374318 was issued 6 November 2015**; the **6CD+4DVD UPC 00602547594983 13 November**. Many press items claimed 13 November for the whole project. We describe *standard triple-CD* as the default album; its 52 tracks span multiple eras. `producers` is deliberately EMPTY: artist-curated sequencing does not make Battiato the credited producer of every track. `instruments` and `stringarrangements` are sourced mainly to the **new title track** and are not an exhaustive analysis of historic masters. Eight 0–5 scales left empty: not meaningful to average across decades/editions from the reviewed sources. The single `Center of Gravity` with Mika is a **track guest**, not co-billing of the anthology.
4. *Torneremo ancora*: **co-billed** with **Royal Philharmonic Concert Orchestra** per Sony-licensed Qobuz and AllMusic (artist credit **Franco Battiato / Royal Philharmonic Concert Orchestra**); add `coBilled.ts` record, even if the orchestra has no independent Fandome artist room. It is **not** a wholly new studio record: the **14 repertoire performances** stem mainly from **2017 rehearsals** with one clearly identified actual-concert track `L'era del cinghiale bianco`. One new song, whose **2016–17 guide voice** was augmented with Royal Philharmonic strings recorded **May 2019**, justifies its inclusion as a **landmark live exception**, type `live`. Music press sometimes labels it an album in studio or a live album despite mixed provenance; do not silently claim that all tracks are concert recordings. `concertrecording` applies to a narrow part of the record, `overdubbing` specifically to new title track. Carlo Guaitoli (piano/conductor), Angelo Privitera (keys/programming) and Pino Pischetola (mixing/engineering) are **not credited album producers** in the available original sources; leave `producers` empty rather than misassign. The 2022 Universal anthology credits Battiato as producer of the title track only, NOT necessarily whole 2019 album.

## Evidentiary caveats

- *Apriti sesamo*: OndaRock highlights classical passages and largely absent **electric** guitar; the artist directly describes a stronger live bass/drums/guitar presence than *Il vuoto*. These are compatible: no electric-guitar inference. The baroque composer **Stefano Landi**, classical composer **Gluck**, and **Rimsky-Korsakov** are **documented musical borrowings** (not merely lyric allusions) on **specific tracks**, represented in `influenced_by`. **Santa Teresa d'Avila, Dante and Ibn Hamdis** are textual/religious sources, **not necessarily musical influences**: do not add them there. The exact composer credit alignment differs in a few non-Italian Qobuz metadata orderings; use the verified 2012 press release/critics for tracks.
- *Joe Patti*: in the 2014 OndaRock review Stockhausen is a qualified critic's musical comparison, not Battiato's direct attribution to this album; the interpretation is recorded in evidence notes. Brian Eno, Harold Budd and Popol Vuh are **sounds_like**, never asserted as confirmed direct influences. A Qobuz variant surprisingly classifies the album as generic `pop`; retained in `genres` LAST with metadata-only/low scope, not presented as its musical essence.
- *Le nostre anime*: MusicBrainz metadata tags `electronic|new wave|pop|rock` for the release; they are retrospective category tags, **not true of every one of its 52 tracks**. The 6CD edition adds classical/experimental works not in the standard version, so do not import those genres by transferring deluxe content to the 3CD row. Production techniques `remastering` and `remixing` apply only to marked tracks, strings to title track.
- *Torneremo ancora*: AllMusic explicitly says **Classical Pop**, whereas Sony and some journalists use `live` generically, and Rockol/SentireAscoltare clarify rehearsal recordings. `poprock` is AllMusic's broad parent metadata and is not a claim that 2019 orchestra played a rock band. `landmark=1` is a judgment based on final project/new song and orchestral singularity; reviews disagree as to artistic meaning and vitality.
- Empty values intentionally signal **unverified**. Do not fill missing scales/instruments/producers from the artist's familiar sound. All scales are **editorial estimates anchored to this album**, not measured values.
