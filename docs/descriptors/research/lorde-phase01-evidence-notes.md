# Lorde — Fandome Phase 01 evidence notes

Scope: *The Love Club EP* (2012), *Pure Heroine* (2013), and *Melodrama* (2017). Sources are retrievable in `lorde_phase01_sources.csv`. Numerical scales are **our interpretative album-level scores**, not facts attributed to critics. `sources.csv` records the external factual basis; where that basis is too weak a token is omitted. Main chronology: EP initially distributed free in November 2012; commercial release March 2013. All other dates are original studio releases.

## `lorde-theloveclubep` — The Love Club EP (2012, ep)

- **Genres:** Album of the Year indexes this EP as alt-pop/synthpop; AllMusic explicitly describes its five songs as electro-pop and categorises Pop plus Alternative/Indie Rock. Rock is a **secondary editorial style**, not a claim of dominant rock guitar. The album-level electronic profile is justified by the electro-pop review and independent accounts of Joel Little constructing beats. No genre is inferred from later Lorde albums.
- **Scales:** energy 3 and tempo 3 reflect moderately active rhythms without consistently dance-floor-focused arrangements; electronic 4, groove 3 reflect electronic rhythms and melodic pop choruses. Mood 2 covers social unease, tentative bravado and youth alienation. Theatrical 2 avoids projecting later stage persona; experimental 2 credits manipulated vocal samples without comparing it to truly avant-garde records. Polish 3 reflects a sparse but intentionally shaped studio EP. These estimates rest primarily on AllMusic, SPIN and the 2014 interview republished by Junkee; **medium** confidence.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Warm, close and occasionally husky solo delivery in AllMusic and SPIN; themes of youth, love, social belonging and status are directly described in the reviews. `raspy` is lower-confidence than `soft`/`clear` and remains a secondary descriptor.
- **Instruments:** `synth|percussion` represents synthesised electro-pop texture and beats described in AllMusic/SPIN/Junkee. Do **not** invent individual drum-machine brands, software, an EP-specific guitar part, or a physical sampling keyboard. No supporting machine or synth model credited.
- **Production:** The detailed joint interview cited by Junkee explains manipulated samples of Lorde's voice on “Million Dollar Bills” and Joel Little constructing beats. The Joel Little interview with *NZ Musician* specifically describes recording and stacking EP vocals before chopping parts into the arrangement. Hence `layeredvocals|vocalchops|sampling|sonicmanipulation`; these are **album-attested techniques**, not third-party-song samples or confirmed physical sampler hardware. They need not occur on every EP track.
- **Production personnel:** Qobuz and MusicBrainz credit Joel Little as producer on EP tracks. Lorde is a writer/performer but has no credited album-level producer role on these sources.
- **Influences:** Left blank album-level: AllMusic's Lana Del Rey, Sky Ferreira, Grimes and Florence + the Machine references are **sonic comparisons**, not direct influence declarations. Accordingly all four appear only in `sounds_like`. Artist-wide influences appear in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` and must not be automatically inherited into album rows.
- **Landmark:** 1 because this breakout EP introduced “Royals” and was the first original release, supported by GRAMMY/SPIN. However *Royals* recurs on *Pure Heroine*; this overlap is flagged to avoid interpreting the two albums as wholly independent collections of songs.

## `lorde-pureheroine` — Pure Heroine (2013, studio)

- **Genres:** Album of the Year records alt-pop, synthpop, electropop, indietronica and alternative R&B. AllMusic lists Pop, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock and Alternative/Indie Rock. R&B and the rock-family labels are **secondary styles/textures**, not a claim that the record is principally live guitar rock. Pitchfork and the Guardian independently confirm minimalist, beat-centred pop. The critic's phrase “trip-pop” in Pitchfork is discussed here but **not silently converted** into `triphop`; it would require a distinct token and stronger album-style evidence.
- **Scales:** electronic 4 and polish 4 reflect fully shaped, precise sparse electronic arrangements; tempo 2 and groove 2 reflect deliberate pulse rather than consistent dance rhythms. Energy 3 covers bass-heavy tracks and quiet verses. Mood 1 is dark, disaffected suburban youth, while introspective moments retain emotional variety. Theatrical 1: restrained, close vocal delivery; experimental 2: unconventional negative space within approachable pop structures. High confidence in qualitative directions, numerical placements remain editorial.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Pitchfork describes low, tight/growly delivery and its contrast with gentler passages; `raspy|soft|clear` records these vocal facets. The lyrics critique status, consumerism and life as an adolescent, alongside anxiety and nostalgia; `party` is secondary to `identity|social` rather than a uniformly celebratory album.
- **Instruments:** Guardian directly names bass and minimal clicks, and Pitchfork discusses the electronic pulse; synthesizer/processed keyboard texture is described in contemporary accounts of the album's production. Producer Joel Little specifically names synths and a small **electric guitar** loop on one track in his *NZ Musician* interview. Thus `synth|bass|percussion|electricguitar`, with guitar last because only one track is confirmed. MusicBrainz credits Joel Little generically with “instruments” but **does not identify models**; don't infer a LinnDrum, TR-808 or Ableton from a live performance rig.
- **Production:** Producer Joel Little specifically describes creating the album beats from his own **drum sample library**, down-pitching Lorde's lower vocal parts, and using layered vocal stacks (*NZ Musician* interview): `drumprogramming|sampling|pitchedvocals|layeredvocals`. The live keyboardist separately states the studio album contains substantial **side-chaining** (*Live Design*), hence `sidechain`. No specific hardware or studio DAW is established by this evidence; the live rig is **not** studio gear.
- **Production personnel:** Recording Academy, Qobuz and MusicBrainz confirm Joel Little produced the original standard album; Lorde co-wrote the songs but is not credited as an album producer.
- **Influenced by:** Guardian producer profile explicitly discusses how Little shaped Lorde's debut productions in relation to James Blake, Burial and The Neptunes. Those are **producer-side documented aesthetic reference points**, not unqualified claims Lorde personally named them in the album sessions. Kept distinct from `sounds_like` (editorial resemblances: The xx, Lana Del Rey, The Weeknd, BANKS).
- **Disagreement:** Pitchfork admired its distinctive minimalism but found repetitive formulas across tracks; AllMusic emphasised its crafted theatrical construction. Both views are compatible with low theatrical presentation, moderate studio polish and a narrow sonic palette.
- **Edition:** Original 10-track 2013 studio album; excludes extended-edition EP tracks, so overlap with EP is *Royals* alone.

## `lorde-melodrama` — Melodrama (2017, studio)

- **Genres:** Album of the Year identifies alt-pop, synthpop, electropop, art-pop, dance-pop, singer-songwriter. Pitchfork and NME substantiate pop and piano-house and 1980s New Wave colour. NME: `house` applies primarily to “Green Light”; Slant: `industrial` applies to “Hard Feelings”; SPIN: `dancehall` is an attributed *Sober* hybrid. Those minor genres appear last and carry **track-scoped** evidence, not a whole-album claim. AllMusic adds secondary Adult Alternative Pop/Rock and Alternative/Indie Rock classifications.
- **Scales:** energy 4 describes the peak “Green Light”/“Supercut”/“Perfect Places”, balanced by ballads; electronic 4 (synths, programming alongside piano/real brass and strings), mood 2 (grief and nighttime uncertainty despite euphoric moments); theatrical 4 (deliberately dramatic party and breakup staging); experimental 3 (unconventional song forms, noise elements, vocal manipulation); polish 5 (complex multi-producer studio craft); tempo 3 and groove 3 represent an album with both piano ballads and dance tracks. Understood as judgments about **whole album**, not only lead single.
- **Voice and lyrics:** Pitchfork, NME, The Independent and Slant describe an expanded range: husky/clear delivery, higher-register strong singing, spoken refrains, and private ballad softness. NME explicitly calls it a conceptual party with relationship rush and aftermath; heartbreak, identity, party and intimacy dominate.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic personnel specifically credits drums, keyboards, trumpet, saxophone, trombone, bass trombone, tuba, viola, violins and cello. NME describes piano-house and strings; Pitchfork describes synthesizers. General group `strings` is included with credited violin/viola/cello; `percussion|bass` supported by record analyses and credited musical roles. No unsupported vintage synth or named drum machine is included.
- **Production:** Qobuz confirms Malay and Dukes with programming roles; the Grammy studio oral history explicitly mentions use of **Pro Tools setup in the sessions**. `protools` means employed during album sessions, **not** sole DAW. The Independent compares singing in “Writer in the Dark” to a falsetto approach. `layeredvocals` reflects reviewed vocal harmonies/digitized masks; there is no substantiated exact autotune/processor machine, so none listed.
- **Producers:** Qobuz track-credit audit identifies six production credits: `Lorde|Jack Antonoff|Frank Dukes|Malay|Joel Little|Andrew Wyatt`. Keep Joel Little **only** because he is credited producer on “Supercut”, not for writing “Green Light”. Exclude Flume, S1, Jean-Benoît Dunckel, T-Minus (additional-only); Kuk Harrell (first-verse vocal production/additional only); all engineers/mixers and Tove Lo (co-writer only). The MusicBrainz role for Kuk Harrell on one track labels him producer while qualifying the task “first verse vocal”; the more precise Qobuz album credit and other liner material justify exclusion under Fandome's vocal-producer-only rule.
- **Influenced by:** In a direct iHeart interview Lorde specifically names **Robyn** as a major influence on this record's dance-through-sorrow songwriting, **not** a sample or guest contribution. In triple j, Lorde described **Kate Bush** as the template for “Writer in the Dark”'s keening vocal approach. A Guardian qualified critic connects a **David Bowie** chord progression in “Liability”; this third link is critic-attested rather than an artist confession. None are listed as production collaborators.
- **Disagreement:** Some reviewers call this a house-party record; others emphasise heartbreak and reflective balladry. The numeric `mood=2` and `groove=3` reflect **both**, instead of using the high danceability of “Green Light” as the album-wide value.
- **Edition:** Original 11-track album only; no remix of “Homemade Dynamite” and no reissue.

## Source quality and withheld claims

AllMusic/Qobuz credits are used for personnel and release facts; contemporaneous criticism for sonic classification; producer/artist interviews for intent and influences; genre aggregation is clearly distinguished from primary reviews. No claims about a specific DAW, synth or drum-machine model have been made for the first two releases without album-specific verification. The Joel Little producer interview directly documents early recording techniques; touring Ableton and live keyboard models are explicitly excluded. The 2014 *Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 1* soundtrack is primarily a multi-artist record curated by Lorde, not a Lorde solo album and not included. The 2021 *Te Ao Mārama* EP contains Māori-language re-recordings of *Solar Power* songs and is excluded as a duplicate-song release, to be reviewed in Phase 02 caveats.
