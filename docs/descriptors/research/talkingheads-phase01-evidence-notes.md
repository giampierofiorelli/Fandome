# Talking Heads — Phase 01 / track- and album-specific evidence notes

Rows are drafts. Scales: energy / electronic / mood / theatrical / experimental / polish / tempo / groove (0–5), anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`. Genre scope is machine-readable in `genre_evidence.csv`. `sounds_like` is editorial judgement, while `influenced_by` requires explicit evidence.

## Talking Heads: 77 (1977) — `talkingheads-talkingheads77`

**Scales `3 / 1 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 2 / 3 / 3`.** Andy Cush's Pitchfork analysis covers the tight, nervy but surprisingly buoyant and melodic grooves, a mostly conventional band setup interrupted by unexpected piano, synth and percussion. Mid-to-high energy and danceability are tempered by jittery performance, audible 1977 studio limitations and slower/more uneasy numbers; polish is 2, not later Eno-period 4. The record alternates anxiety with upbeat pop; mood 3. Theatrical 2 captures Byrne's exaggerated phrasing without claiming arena spectacle; experimental 2 for arrangement surprises but still short song forms.

**Instruments:** Original sleeve transcriptions specify guitars/keys (Byrne/Harrison), electric bass (Weymouth) and drums (Frantz); Pitchfork describes steelpan on "Uh-Oh, Love Comes to Town", toy synth on "Don't Worry About the Government", piano on "The Book I Read" and mallets/sax on "First Week/Last Week ... Carefree". `steeldrums` and `mallets` are existing tokens, positioned after the core guitar/bass/drums. https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/77.html ; https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-talking-heads-77/ .

**Production:** Left empty. Tony Bongiovi, Lance Quinn and Talking Heads are credited as producers in original-release database; Ed Stasium is an engineer/mixer **not** a producer for this field. Thin/raw production is reflected in polish, but not enough evidence supports a named production *technique*. https://musicbrainz.org/release/baef36ef-64d7-421d-9362-813bc11ac9b2 ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/talking-heads-77/120135545 .

**Voice/lyrics:** `clear|spoken` reflects Byrne's clean, angular yelp and spoken conversational phrasing; no unsupported specialist singing technique. The songs combine social discomfort, ironic roles, paranoia, interpersonal love and outsider identity, supported by the Cush review. The lyrics are mostly English. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-talking-heads-77/ .

**Genre dispute:** Art-punk is a retrospective MusicBrainz release tag, while AllMusic describes the debut mainly as new wave and punk/new wave. These are not collapsed into `punk` alone; metadata-level American Punk/album rock is subordinated in order. Critics who describe a funk *feel* don't necessarily claim this debut is an album of funk music, so `funk` was deliberately not added. https://www.allmusic.com/album/talking-heads-77-mw0000650867 ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/57be3031-d63c-4c31-9be0-1d773a43eee9 .

**Documented album influence:** Frantz explicitly recalls Roxy Music in the band's early formation. Do **not** turn the former Modern Lovers member Jerry Harrison into a musical-influence claim; Modern Lovers goes in the editorial resemblance column. https://www.loudersound.com/features/talking-heads-prog .

## More Songs About Buildings and Food (1978) — `talkingheads-moresongsaboutbuildingsandfood`

**Scales `4 / 2 / 3 / 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 4`.** Compared with the debut, Pitchfork documents stronger funk/disco pulses, greater weight and detail from Eno's sound design, and danceable but tense repeated motifs. The record is not dominated by synthesis, so electronic remains 2; Eno's recording craftsmanship justifies polish 3 rather than glossy 5. Byrne's witty, sometimes bitter lyrics balance the emotional score at 3. Complex interlocking parts and processing justify experimental 3; groove 4 is based on album-level coverage, not the Al Green cover alone. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/ ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/302108628 .

**Instruments:** Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's library reproduction of the album notes credits Byrne on guitars and synthesized percussion; Harrison on piano, organ, synth, guitar; Eno on synthesizers, piano, guitar, percussion; Weymouth bass; Frantz drums/percussion. Genre does not supply any instruments. https://axiell.rockhall.com/rrhof-ais/Details/fullCatalogue/200001708 .

**Production:** Pitchfork names loudspeaker-style vocal treatment on "Warning Sign" and prominent reverb bouncing from drums, warranting `vocalfilter` and `heavyreverb` but only as selected-track effects. The same review describes dubby echoes, but an echo sound does not prove *tape*-delay technology, so `tapeeffects` is omitted. Arrangements being layered alone does not verify overdubbing workflow. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/ .

**Voice/lyrics:** Byrne sings directly but with a strained upper-register falsetto on "Take Me to the River" (specific Pitchfork discussion), so `falsetto` is correctly represented in `production`, while `voice` stays `clear|raspy`, using allowed voice types only. Social ambitions, artists' work, relationship fights, and irony described by Mapes. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/ .

**Documented influence vs cover:** James Brown is a band-level musical influence named by Byrne and Weymouth; `influenced_by=James Brown` represents rhythm-source evidence, not a falsely inferred relation from the Al Green cover. That Al Green song is a cover, *not by itself proof* of influence in this column. https://pitchfork.com/features/5-10-15-20/david-byrne-on-the-music-that-made-him/ ; https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/ .

**Genre dispute:** Pitchfork specifically says the album straddles art-rock and dance music with disco patterns and dubby echoes. These last two are `texture` in genre_evidence, not blanket electronic-dance identity. AllMusic's college rock and American punk tags are retrospective database categories and accordingly rank lower. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-more-songs-about-buildings-and-food/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/more-songs-about-buildings-and-food-mw0000650866 .

## Fear of Music (1979) — `talkingheads-fearofmusic`

**Scales `4 / 2 / 1 / 3 / 4 / 3 / 3 / 3`.** Pitchfork identifies fear, warm-less groove, fast percussive outbursts, surreal writing and more radical editing/arrangements. The emotional colour is dark (1) while energy is high (4). Experimental 4 reflects extended structures/treatments beyond the previous LP; polish 3 acknowledges deliberate studio complexity without glossy finish. Although "I Zimbra" grooves hard, much of the remainder is uneasy or slow; whole-album groove 3. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-fear-of-music/ .

**Instruments:** Byrne/Harrison guitars, Harrison keyboards, Frantz drums, Weymouth bass, Eno electronic instruments/treatments, conga guests and Fripp guitar on "I Zimbra". Source is original sleeve plus MusicBrainz release track-level credits. `guitar|bass|drums|keyboards|synth|percussion` only; no specific synth model is invented. Congas are represented as generic `percussion`, not erroneously as `gobletdrum`. https://musicbrainz.org/release/33b207bf-6364-459b-8d8a-22b256fd4ec7 ; https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/fear.html .

**Production:** Original notes give Eno *treatments*; contemporary and retrospective critics discuss the album's treated guitar/bass and altered sonics, supporting `sonicmanipulation`. We intentionally omit `distortion` as a named method because no specific distortion treatment was documented for this album. Avoid `vocalphasing` because Fripp's *guitar* is phased; avoid `sampling` because no third-party sample is documented. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-fear-of-music/ ; https://cultfollowing.co.uk/2024/04/06/talking-heads-fear-of-music-review/ .

**Voice/lyrics:** Byrne's barked and half-spoken lyrics create rasp/angst and purposeful tension. Anxiety about air, animals, cities, war and the physical environment informs `social|introspection|political|irony|identity`; political describes "Life During Wartime" rather than each track. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-fear-of-music/ .

**Genre / influence:** The 1979 album as a whole is not best tagged Afrobeat. "I Zimbra", in particular, takes inspiration from Fela Kuti's approach, confirmed by the retrospective and original group interviews; its `afrobeat` position is deliberately last and scoped "some tracks". Similarly disco on "Life During Wartime" and "I Zimbra" is not album-wide. https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ ; https://cultfollowing.co.uk/2024/04/06/talking-heads-fear-of-music-review/ .

## Remain in Light (1980) — `talkingheads-remaininlight`

**Scales `4 / 3 / 2 / 4 / 5 / 4 / 3 / 5`.** Frantic polyrhythms, layered funk and Afrobeat arrangements dominate the opening side, balanced by the slower, shadowy closing side. Energy 4, electronic 3 (many electronics layered with live funk players), mood 2 (both uplift/unease), theatrical 4 (Byrne's pulpit-like voice and call-and-response structures), experimental 5 (iterative nonstandard writing/arrangement), polish 4 (deliberate complex production), tempo 3 (varied), groove 5 (rhythmic motive structures album-wide). Each is an editorial guide judgement tied to critic/engineer evidence. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ ; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ .

**Instruments:** Sleeve/MusicBrainz list Byrne/Harrison guitars, bass, keys/percussion; Frantz drums/percussion/keys; Weymouth bass/keys/percussion; Eno keys/bass/percussion; Belew guitars; Hassell trumpet and horn arrangement; additional percussionists; Hendryx vocals. `synth` is additionally supported by critic descriptions of the electronic sound, but no model is inferred from unverified general synth anecdotes. Specific `trumpet` and generic `percussion` are documented. https://musicbrainz.org/release/7953fbfc-070e-448b-8ac7-66394e394629 ; https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/remain.html .

**Production:** David Byrne says the band played recurring parts live from beginning to end while recording to analogue tape, not literally splicing loops of these underlying rhythm parts. Dave Jerden describes extensive track editing and added takes from Adrian Belew; Jerry Harrison confirms delay, echo and flanging. This supports `analogtape`, `overdubbing` and `sonicmanipulation`, but not literal `tapeeffects` looping. Jon Hassell has an explicit horn arrangement credit on "Houses in Motion", supporting `hornarrangements`, scope some tracks. The album's expanded backing voices (Eno/Hendryx) justify `layeredvocals`. There is no claim of computer sampling or hardware model based only on sonic resemblance. https://tapeop.com/interviews/86/dave-jerden-Talking-Heads-Stones-Herbie-Hancock ; https://tapeop.com/interviews/79/david-byrne ; https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/interview-talking-heads-jerry-harrison-adrian-belew-remain-in-light/ ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/7953fbfc-070e-448b-8ac7-66394e394629 .

**Producer dispute:** MusicBrainz and the original sleeve identify **Brian Eno** as *producer*. Some retrospectives refer to "produced by Eno and Talking Heads" in a broad collaborative sense. Keep `producers=Brian Eno` to follow formal credits, while all four Heads remain composers/arrangers/players; don't promote these other roles to producer. https://musicbrainz.org/release/7953fbfc-070e-448b-8ac7-66394e394629 ; https://www.connollyco.com/discography/talking_heads/remain.html ; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ .

**Album influence:** The group and producer explicitly identify **Fela Kuti**'s Afrobeat as a primary construction model. In a GRAMMY interview Chris Frantz pushes back on the suggestion that they encountered Fela solely through Eno; he says they knew that music previously. The disagreement concerns *how* the band discovered Fela, not whether he influenced their work. Other bands in `sounds_like` are editorial resemblances only, not claims that they inspired the album. https://www.grammy.com/news/remain-light-turns-40-artists-weigh-talking-heads-genre-defying-dance-floor-classic/ ; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/mar/27/brian-eno-david-byrne .

**Vocal and lyric sources:** Pitchfork discusses the spoken delivery and chanted/choral responses, including the preacher-like manner of "Once in a Lifetime", juxtaposed with alienation, self-scrutiny, and themes of uncertainty. `storytelling` and `political` cover narrative "Listening Wind" and political images on selected tracks; they are not the primary focus of every song. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ .

**Genre conflicts:** Pitchfork centres Afrobeat/art-rock/funk, while AllMusic adds generic album-rock, punk/new-wave, alternative/indie, and college-rock styles. Rank the first source's sound descriptions ahead of library classifications and explain genre family overlaps. `experimentalrock` is explicitly AllMusic; not a guess from experimental scale 5. https://www.allmusic.com/album/remain-in-light-mw0000192118 ; https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/talking-heads-remain-in-light/ .

## Review flags for Claude

- `voice` must use only `soft|clear|powerful|raspy|rap|spoken|choral|operatic|instrumental`. For the 1978 falsetto documented on "Take Me to the River", `production` includes `falsetto`, while `voice` stays inside the guide's allowed values.
- Genre metadata from AllMusic is inclusive and partly retrospective; new tokens (`americanpunk`, `punknewwave`, `collegerock`) are preserved as named per the user rule even though the styles may be broad. Claude may assess confidence but should not silently remap.
- No album in Phase 01 has an undocumented machine model or fake co-billed credit.
