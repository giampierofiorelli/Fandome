## Essential release-identification checks

1. `Robyn` (2005) — Use *original Swedish* 2005 album and not later 2007/2008 international version: `With Every Heartbeat` was added later, and other reworked tracks change genre/mood/tempo. R05_MB has original 13-track CD; R05_SL explicitly details US changes. Some credited Kleerup production is from later album issue; **do not assign his late-reissue collaboration to the 2005 edition**. Track-specific original-pressing producer lists remain incomplete in accessible first-hand materials: independently review less prominent production credits before final promotion.
2. `Body Talk Pt. 1` (2010) — stand-alone 8-song mini-LP, with *acoustic* `Hang With Me` and Swedish traditional `Jag Vet en Dejlig Rosa`, so `language=en|sv` and whole-record ratings must account for quiet songs.
3. `Body Talk Pt. 2` (2010) — stand-alone 8-song mini-LP, features *dance* `Hang With Me` but *acoustic* `Indestructible`. Its tracks are not those of Pt. 1.
4. `Body Talk` (2010) — 15-track final album, blending 10 earlier recordings/versions and 5 newly introduced songs; not a reissue of Pt. 1 or Pt. 2 and not simply an EP. **There is no separately titled `Body Talk Pt. 3` release in the 2010 final trilogy**: Pitchfork contemporary correction F_NEWS specifically renames it `Body Talk`.
5. AllMusic & Pitchfork call Pt. 1/2 mini-*albums*, Qobuz's licensed review calls them EPs: `type=studio` is a provisional deliberate choice reflecting separately charting mini-LPs in album discography. If Fandome taxonomy mandates 8-track projects as `ep`, Claude should review the project's classification scheme; do not silently recast.

## Robyn (2005) — robyn-robyn

**Whole-album character**: Jess Harvell [R05_PF] emphasizes a playful but guarded synth-beat-driven pop record; Sasha Frere-Jones [R05_NY] documents dominant synthetic sound, 80s musical quotations, hip-hop and R&B cadence, and compares its character with Missy Elliott and Gwen Stefani. Slant [R05_SL] notes its genre-hopping personality and distinction from the later US mix/reissue. Some rock/acoustic songs and ballads temper overall electronics and speed, but electronic production remains dominant.

**Scales (editorial, 0–5):** energy **3** (assertive hooks but ballads); electronic **4** (programmed synths central, varied instrumentation); mood **3** (sass and bright dance music counterbalanced by wounded heartbreak); theatrical **2** (direct playful delivery rather than spectacular arrangements); experimental **3** (unusual synth ideas, genre blends, interludes); polish **4** (tightly programmed, radio-ready independent studio production); tempo **3** (mix of driving tracks, slower songs); groove **4** (dance-pop backbone, not continuous club mix). [R05_PF,R05_NY,R05_SL]

**Voice** `clear|spoken`: sung voice typically bright and direct, spoken/rap-adjacent rhythm and `Curriculum Vitae` spoken/MC framing. Do not assign `rap` as primary solely because of Swingfly's cameo or a rhythmic vocal phrase. **Lyrics** heartbreak, identity, love, irony and introspection supported by reviews' `Be Mine!`, `Handle Me`, `Who's That Girl?` analysis. **Similar to** Missy Elliott/Gwen Stefani are a contemporaneous expert critical analogy, not causal proof [R05_NY]. **Influenced by** Prince, Visage, The Cure, Bronski Beat: [R05_NY,R05_NY10] note sonic connections / specific 80s stylistic quotations; this is **qualified critic inference**, not an artist confession. Claude can prefer blank influenced_by if only directly stated first-person influence is permitted; avoid confusing track producer The Knife with influence attribution. **Producers** original album's high-confidence Klas Åhlund and The Knife; additional names cross-checked against R05_PRESS and R05_SN, see original edition caveat above. `landmark=1` stylistic autonomy; signed reviewers note its career impact, not automatically a chart assertion. `indiepop`, `dancehall` are less reliable whole-album labels: both explicitly flagged low / song-only in genre evidence.

## Body Talk Pt. 1 (2010) — robyn-bodytalkpt1

**Whole-album character**: Pitchfork [P1_PF] finds a dancefloor-friendly production balancing fear, assertiveness and deep isolation. AllMusic [P1_AM] contrasts hard-hitting opening and `Fembot` with intimate acoustic final two cuts; song `Dancing on My Own` is aching rather than euphoric. Sleng Teng / dancehall references are from one track, not whole LP.

**Scales:** energy **4** (driving electronic majority but intimate close); electronic **4** (programmed synth music, acoustic and vibraphone closer); mood **2** (strong undercurrent tension/loneliness despite exuberant peaks); theatrical **3** (big vocal hooks but some close performances); experimental **3** (stark electro-house talky track, dancehall detour, Swedish traditional arrangement); polish **5** (meticulously produced pop); tempo **4** (fast dance songs majority); groove **4** (dancefloor function, two acoustic tracks). [P1_PF,P1_AM,P1_Q]

**Voice:** `clear|spoken|soft` covers full-voice pop, talky first track, quieter acoustic ending. **Lyrics:** heartbreak/identity/introspection/love/irony/party; track-level themes and contradictions documented in both critical pieces. **Language:** `en|sv` due to Swedish *Jag vet en dejlig rosa*, a real sung piece in the original edition; no instrumental-only classification. **Producers:** Klas Åhlund, Patrik Berger, Diplo, Röyksopp and Robyn (co-producer explicit Qobuz). **sounds_like** Gwen Stefani and Santigold are stylistic analogies expressly raised by Pitchfork about a specific track; editor should be aware they are *not album-wide comparator proof*. No documented causal `influenced_by`.

## Body Talk Pt. 2 (2010) — robyn-bodytalkpt2

**Whole-album character:** Pitchfork [P2_PF] explicitly contrasts Pt. 2's streamlined, club-friendly synthpop with Pt. 1's greater sonic variety; AllMusic [P2_AM] finds it warmer and more focused on trust, company and reassurance, while still containing heartbreak material and kinetic hip-hop features. Avoid scoring entirely from the euphoric `Hang With Me` or the guest raps.

**Scales:** energy **4** (several kinetic club tracks counterweighted by acoustic closer); electronic **4** (dominantly programmed synthpop); mood **3** (reassuring/comic material with heartbreak); theatrical **3** (club pop delivery with reflective endings); experimental **2** (cleaner and more conventional pop hooks than Pt1); polish **5** (dense polished programming); tempo **4** (upbeats predominant); groove **4** (strong dance orientation). [P2_PF,P2_AM,P2_Q]

**Voice:** `clear|soft|spoken` for melodic refrains, acoustic ending, talky `We Dance to the Beat`; Snoop Dogg is feature, not Robyn lead `rap`. **Lyrics:** love/heartbreak/identity/party/introspection, all song-specific. **Producers:** Klas Åhlund, Kleerup, Savage Skulls, Diplo, Niggaracci plus Robyn documented `AdditionalProducer` on opener [P2_Q]. AllMusic lists each [P2_AM]. **Sounds like:** Pitchfork explicitly compares the sound of `Love Kills` to La Roux/Lady Gaga; this is a *track-level analogy only*. `influenced_by` blank: no evidence of direct causal influence. `landmark=0` *relative to the whole Body Talk cycle* only, not a low rating.

## Body Talk (2010) — robyn-bodytalk

**Whole-album character:** This is the final 15-song studio LP as released Nov 2010, not `Pt. 3` [F_NEWS,F_MC,F_Q]. Pitchfork [F_PF] describes emotional vulnerability under pounding beats; Guardian [F_GU] stresses the tension between curated older songs and four/five newly recorded entries; AllMusic's licensed review [F_Q] considers full album a curated cohesive culmination. Differences in descriptions of the number of *brand-new* songs reflect whether the new dance re-recording of `Indestructible` is counted; **do not present a numerical contradiction as a factual error**. Final album includes newly produced `Time Machine`, `Call Your Girlfriend`, `Get Myself Together`, `Stars 4-Ever` and the new `Indestructible` version.

**Scales:** energy **4** (punchy electronic pop balanced by tender moods); electronic **4** (synths/drum machines dominate, strings and acoustic features); mood **3** (heartbreak and togetherness/euphoria share space); theatrical **3** (open club sonics, expressive direct delivery); experimental **3** (slotted electro-house, dancehall and hip-hop sounds into pop structures, curated unconventional three-release project); polish **5** (precision high-level production); tempo **4** (majority driving); groove **4** (dancefloor pulse with changes of pace). [F_PF,F_GU,F_Q]

**Voice:** `clear|spoken|soft` characteristic song-level delivery, not Snoop guest classification. **Lyrics:** heartbreak/love/identity/party/introspection/irony from reviewers' songs. **Producers:** from full 15-track licensed Qobuz song credits [F_Q] include Klas Åhlund, Patrik Berger, Diplo, Röyksopp, Kleerup, Savage Skulls, Niggaracci, Max Martin, Shellback, Billboard, Joakim Åhlund, and Robyn co-producer. `Billboard` is Mathieu Jomphe-Lépine, **do not duplicate as two separate credited producers**. Producer count exceeds arbitrary limits by design. `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` empty absent compelling album-wide documentation. `landmark=1` is an editorial legacy judgment anchored by major reviews; the separate Pt. 1 also carries `landmark=1` because Dancing On My Own was first released there.

## Genres and interpretability

Each genre CSV row has its claimed scope and source keys. Tokens are not generated merely from all songs' instrument inventory; labels such as `dancehall`, `hiphop`, `eurodance` and `electrohouse` may be **single-track or subset**. The ranked order is the overall sonic significance, not genre prominence judged from one hit. No arbitrary cap on producers, voice tags, lyrics, sounds_like or influenced_by; producer names only from credit evidence. `notes` max 15 words per current guide.
