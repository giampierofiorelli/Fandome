# Marlene Kuntz — Phase 01: editorial and technical evidence notes

**Method**: original releases only (1994–1999). The eight 0–5 scales are editorial judgments against the Fandome guide anchors, based on reviews of each **album**, not a band-wide assumption. The `genre_evidence` file preserves the difference between whole album, a song, and texture. Nothing below purports to be a listening transcription. Research originally published in Italian is summarized in English without reproducing review prose. `sounds_like` is editorial sonic proximity, not influence. Credited producers are distinguished from executive producers, engineers and remixers.

## Catartica (1994) — `marlenekuntz-catartica`

- **Identity**: The [band’s official page](https://marlenekuntz.com/catartica/) gives **13 May 1994**, 14 original tracks. Later *Catartica + Demosonici* is a reissue/companion, not a new original studio album. [OndaRock's landmark review](https://www.ondarock.it/recensioni/pietremiliariitaliane/marlenekuntz_catartica/) gives a direct alt-rock label and detailed noisy/melodic song contrasts. [Indie for Bunnies](https://www.indieforbunnies.com/2024/05/13/oggi-catartica-dei-marlene-kuntz-compie-30-anni/) calls it noise rock / alt-rock / post-grunge, but grunge vs post-grunge is contentious as a primary identity and those tags rank behind noise/alternative. Post-punk has qualified [OndaRock album-specific text](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) describing occasional rhythmic/attitude textures; hard rock relates especially to 1° 2° 3° ([MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-catartica/)).
- **Credit dispute / correct producer roles**: [MusiCulturA's original credit reproduction](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-catartica/) assigns artistic production to **Marco L. Lega**, **Gianni Maroccolo only executive production**. [Label-supplied Qobuz track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/catartica-marlene-kuntz/0724384911553) explicitly credit *Marlene Kuntz* and Lega as producers. The row lists Lega + band, never Maroccolo. Some later retrospectives loosely call Maroccolo producer; do not treat an A&R role or executive supervision as album co-production without a full producer credit.
- **Instruments / production**: Godano & Tesio guitars, **Gianluca Viano bass**, **Luca Bergia drums** from [original personnel](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-catartica/). Deliberately list **electricguitar|bass|drums**, not synths or keyboards guessed from post-punk. [The same source](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-catartica/) explicitly states **3M tape** for recording sessions and **digital editing**; these substantiate `analogtape|digitalediting`. The critics emphasize distorted guitars, supporting `distortion` ([SentireAscoltare](https://www.sentireascoltare.com/recensioni/marlene-kuntz-catartica/)). No brand/model of recorder established.
- **Scales**: `4/0/1/3/3/2/4/1` energy/electronic/mood/theatrical/experimental/polish/tempo/groove. Fierce guitars alternate with slower **Lieve** and **Nuotando nell’aria**. 0 electronic characterizes instrumental sources, not the existence of digital editing. Strong but not maximal intensity; performance expressive rather than Queen-level spectacle, relatively dry/non-glossy recording, fast average but not a dance album. Review support: [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/), [SentireAscoltare](https://www.sentireascoltare.com/recensioni/marlene-kuntz-catartica/).
- **Vocals/lyrics/influence**: Descriptions of growled, strained/shouted singing and vulnerable ballads support `raspy|soft|powerful`. Poetic inwardness, anger and occasional irony are [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) and [SentireAscoltare](https://www.sentireascoltare.com/recensioni/marlene-kuntz-catartica/) findings. **Sonic Youth** explicitly acknowledged by [Godano](https://www.ondarock.it/interviste/marlenekuntz/) and identifiable in the track Sonica. **Nick Cave** noted by the critic and [Godano's early interview](https://www.distorsioni.net/canali/interviste/un-ventennio-con-i-marlene-kuntz); **CCCP Fedeli alla linea** cautiously included for Sonica vocal delivery alone, from [Godano's direct 2017 attribution](https://www.spaziorock.it/marlene-kuntz-cristiano-godano-intervista-2017/), not because he endorses a wholesale C.S.I. sound influence.
- **`sounds_like`** sonic resemblance only: Sonic Youth and Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds are common qualified comparisons; C.S.I. reflects timbre/Italian spoken-sung vocals and label scene but is not band-wide proof of being influenced by C.S.I. Critical enthusiasm differs about whether the Sonic Youth debt is too direct; never claim a hidden original style completely separate from documented affinities.

## Il vile (1996) — `marlenekuntz-ilvile`

- **Identity and people**: [Official page](https://marlenekuntz.com/il-vile/), **26 Apr 1996**, 11 original tracks. [MusiCulturA original credits](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/) verify **Dan Solo on bass** (replacing Viano), Godano & Tesio guitars, Bergia drums, voice recording by **Bruce Morrison** (an engineer, *not* producer). Label **producer**: **Marco L. Lega except track 10**, which credits the band; hence `Marco L. Lega|Marlene Kuntz`. **Gianni Maroccolo is executive producer** and not entered as album producer. [Maroccolo's own detailed interview](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/intervista-al-musicista-e-produttore-gianni-maroccolo/) corroborates the continued producer relationship with Lega and changing rehearsal locations.
- **Sound/genres**: [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) links the heavier/darker bass-driven approach to Dan Solo; [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/) names **noise rock** for 3 di 3; grunge for L’agguato; metal tones for Cenere and Ape Regina, explicitly *particular songs*, ranked last. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/3fde3f3f-7024-3c64-b003-3f16ee60850b) additionally tags alternative/indie rock. The retrospective [SentireAscoltare](https://www.sentireascoltare.com/recensioni/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/) compares this album’s agitation to more subdued songs. `postgrunge` is lower-confidence shorthand from 90s genre tagging; do not extrapolate a homogeneous grunge sound from the presence of two grungy riffs.
- **Scales**: `5/0/0/3/3/2/4/1`. Hardest/angriest and darkest phase 01 full-length; no source names synthesizers or electronic drums. Unpolished/noisy despite careful recording; variation to softer songs lowers not maximum danceability but leaves very low groove. Source: [Godano on Il vile in 2026](https://milano.repubblica.it/cronaca/2026/08/10/news/marlene_kuntz_brescia-425520530/amp/), [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/).
- **Production**: overdriven guitars substantiate `distortion`; [album credits](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/) explicitly identify digital editing, no tape model. Exclude conjectured guitar pedals/software. **Instruments** confined to sourced guitars/bass/drums.
- **Themes/influences**: violence, sexuality, discomfort, fractured narratives vs love/breakup according to [SentireAscoltare](https://www.sentireascoltare.com/recensioni/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/) and [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-il-vile/). Godano specifically cited **Sonic Youth + Soundgarden** in a [2026 interview](https://milano.repubblica.it/cronaca/2026/08/10/news/marlene_kuntz_brescia-425520530/amp/) as two touchstones for Il vile; no need to infer them from sound. `sounds_like` is not additional influence. **Disagreement**: heavy/metal elements are song textures, not an album-level *metal* identity. Recording heaviness does not equate to electronic production.

## Come di sdegno (1998) — `marlenekuntz-comedisdegno`

- **Why included**: Official [1998 six-track original release](https://marlenekuntz.com/come-di-sdegno/) and the [band’s own 2018 history](https://marlenekuntz.com/come-di-sdegno-per-la-prima-volta-in-vinile-in-occasione-dei-suoi-ventanni/) identify it as a **significant original mini-LP/EP**, not a hits compilation: two new remixes, the original Aurora and Donna L, plus a roughly half-hour improvisation/reading split into two tracks. The remixes do not make the *whole release* type `remix`; correct original type is `ep`. An original live track does not change the release's type to `live`. The 2000 CD reissue and 2018 vinyl anniversary pressing are *reissues*.
- **Producers vs remixers**: [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-come-di-sdegno/) names Marco L. Lega as original artistic producer; the [release credit transcription](https://readdork.com/album/marlene-kuntz-come-di-sdegno/pressing/2527690) identifies **Mauro Teho Teardo** and **Paolo Favati** as track remixers / reworkers only. They belong in evidence, **not** the producers column absent explicit producer credit. Avoid assuming remixes by those two establish a joint-billed EP in `coBilled.ts`.
- **Sound/instruments/production**: [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-come-di-sdegno/) explicitly names both electric guitars, bass, drums, *samplers* in Questo e altro; also live Donna L and a spontaneous 1997 rehearsal-session piece accompanied by a spoken reading. [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) corroborates electronic sampling on one remix and a lengthy studio improvisation. `sampling|remixing` and `concertrecording` for live Donna L are source specific, `liverecording` for improvised ensemble tape. **No exact sampler brand was found**. Treat 1997 **recording year** vs 1998 **release year** separately.
- **Genres/scales**: Source specifically labels noisy rock/noise-rock, but the remixed and experimental spoken material has an inconsistent sound. Do not label the EP wholesale `electronic`, `triphop` or `experimentalrock` by deducing genre from one technique; do not invent a genre from the adjective “sperimentale”. Values `3/1/1/3/5/2/2/1` reflect deliberate alternation between soft Aurora, loud Donna L, remixes and a long abstract improvisation. Tempo 2 is a *rough editorial overall* because of the unusually long jam; cautious confidence **medium**. [Band EP note](https://marlenekuntz.com/come-di-sdegno-per-la-prima-volta-in-vinile-in-occasione-dei-suoi-ventanni/); [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-come-di-sdegno/).
- **Influence field**: blank; no verified statement tied a *particular* external artist to the making of the EP. Similarity to Sonic Youth is editorial, not a sample, collaboration or influence assertion. Themes from rehearsal vocal recital, with limited relationship writing on Aurora; broad `introspection|identity|social|love` is less certain than the studio albums, thus row `medium`.

## Ho ucciso paranoia (1999) — `marlenekuntz-houccisoparanoia`

- **Original edition and dates**: [Official album page](https://marlenekuntz.com/ho-ucciso-paranoia/) **14 January 1999**, normal album **13 tracks**; an expanded two-CD format added *Spore* with **16 jam fragments**. [Band biography](https://marlenekuntz.com/biografia/) and [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) establish editions. One descriptor row for original 13-track album; **do not add Spore as an independent 1999 studio album**, nor double-count sessions in scales. The live album H.U.P. Live in Catharsis is excluded; it does not pass the landmark exception threshold for this task.
- **Producer nuance**: [Original credit reproduction](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/) and [CD sleeve transcription](https://readdork.com/album/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/pressing/1939885): **Marlene Kuntz + Marco L. Lega** produce most songs; **Marlene Kuntz + Gianni Maroccolo** produce tracks **Ineluttabile** and **Il naufragio** (tracks 7 and 9). Maroccolo also has executive production credit **overall**, but is admitted solely due to *two specifically credited artistic production roles*. Distinguish this from loosely worded metadata that makes him sole producer; do not credit Marco Lega on the two exceptional tracks just because he engineered/edited them.
- **Guitars/instruments and production**: [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/) identifies Godano and Tesio guitars, **Dan Solo bass**, **Bergia drums**; an acoustic arrangement is mentioned for the quieter Spora n.27, without instrument specificity: do **not** turn that into a credited acoustic guitar. [Original credit reproduction](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/) lists **digital editing** by Lega; [ImpattoSonoro](https://www.impattosonoro.it/2019/02/05/back-in-time/back-in-time-marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia-1999) and [Metallized](https://www.metallized.it/recensione.php?id=16139) corroborate distortion/fuzz. **Do not infer** drum machines, synth patches, effects processors, a sampler or particular recording software. A critic uses **electro-rock** for the very end of **L’odio migliore**; this is *one song texture* and is ranked last rather than defining the album.
- **Genres/scales**: `alternativerock|noiserock|indierock|postgrunge|rock|electrorock`. Qualified contemporary [Rockol](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/205/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia), [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/marlenekuntz/) and [Metallized](https://www.metallized.it/recensione.php?id=16139) agree on noisy rock plus melodically softened songs. `postgrunge` is a low-confidence [secondary album tag](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_ucciso_paranoia); check stronger critical support before promoting. No full-length `postrock` tagging based on a weak retail site's generic classification. Scores `3/1/1/3/3/3/3/1`: range from aggressive tracks to tender ballads and small abstract interludes; more crafted sonically, less consistently fast/intense; guitar foundation despite one electro-rock moment. Some reviewers consider it the mature pinnacle; other commentary sees the earliest pop turn. These can both be true and do not justify a forced bright mood score.
- **Lyrical/voice**: inward anguish, love poems and irony (e.g., Le putte) as per [Rockol](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/205/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia) and [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/). Audible contrasts described in sources support raspy, softer, forceful voice labels. [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/marlene-kuntz-ho-ucciso-paranoia/) compares **Lamento dello sbronzo** specifically to Sonic Youth, combined with artist's general acknowledged debt ([OndaRock interview](https://www.ondarock.it/interviste/marlenekuntz/)); album-specific `influenced_by` therefore retains Sonic Youth only. No other guest performing on an album is counted as a musical influence.

## Caveat: source and classification disagreements

The **three studio albums** in this batch plus the **one significant EP** have different evidence strengths. Several album-level tags such as grunge/post-grunge are used inconsistently among critics and contemporary catalogs; the hierarchy reflects well-supported noise/alternative music above the contentious categories. Other genre evidence rows explicitly state single-song scope. Genre `grunge` for Catartica is only weakly backed by secondary tag/meta-data and should receive a second reviewer check before being weighted strongly.

Occasionally historical profiles refer to **Gianni Maroccolo** as if he produced every early recording; contemporaneous/original producer/engineer/executive credits are more precise and supersede loosely worded secondary prose. No model numbers, DAW names, or unverified electronic gear were added. The 2026 interview about Il vile is retrospective, but Godano is directly naming the album's influences, hence usable.
