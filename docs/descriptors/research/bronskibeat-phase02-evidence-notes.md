# Bronski Beat — Fandome phase 02 evidence notes

**Research date:** 27 October 2026. **Only two new rows.** All assessments are `draft` and the eight 0–5 scales are editorial assessments, not data quoted from critics. `Out & About` has an especially serious **source-coverage risk**; do not treat its provisional genre sequence as a verified album analysis.

## Out & About — `bronskibeat-outabout` (2016 first public distribution; recorded 1987)

**Availability/year.** Independent's Steve Bronski obituary specifically says sessions for the unreleased 1987 LP appeared **online in 2016**, the most concrete journalistic statement found for first public access. Southbank Centre’s official heritage article instead says released **2017**. Numerous reference sites label it **1987**, which is the **recording date, not the original public release**. Until the actual original download or an archived dated Steve Bronski store listing is recovered, 2016 is the best-supported release year but should be revisited. Source rows give both claims. Do not backdate the release to 1987 simply because retrospective discographies group it there.

**What can actually be verified.** The record was tracked at **Berry Street Studios in London**, with **Brian Pugsley as engineer** (NOT producer). The few independently documented tracks include **'The Final Spin'** and **'Peace and Love'**, the latter featuring **Rose McDowall** (Strawberry Switchblade). A version of another associated song, **'European Boy'**, was recorded by the group **Splash**. These are archive-session claims, not proof of exact sequence or inclusion on 2016 digital download. A web biography says the release combined original tracks and **subsequent Steve Bronski remixes**. Do not characterize later remixes as songs recorded in 1987.

**Personnel and voice caution.** Original core musicians **Steve Bronski and Larry Steinbachek** used keyboards and electronic equipment during the group's mid-1980s work; the named instruments `synth|keyboards` are provisionally adopted from album-era/band evidence. No reliable complete Out & About credits, live engineer accounts specific to the album, verified primary-download tracklist or stable original master were located. **Do not assert Jimmy Somerville, John Foster, Rose McDowall or Jonathan Hellyer as a single album-wide lead singer** based on isolated collaborations; consequently `voice` is left blank. Do not promote engineer Pugsley to `producers` or invent drum machine models, DAWs or processing: `producers` and `production` blank. Somerville returned for a **1987 concert**, NOT necessarily these recordings.

**Genres and 0–5 scores.** Five provisional genres (`synthpop|hinrg|dancepop|disco|electronic`) are consistent with the band’s mid-1980s identity, instrument practices and the one disco-associated archived song; **none** is presented as an album-specific confirmed style. The genre-evidence table explicitly says **artist-era proxy** to prevent overstated certainty. Without reliable listening/reviews of the actual complete public album, all eight scales blank, `confidence=low`, sounds-like blank, full-album lyrical categories blank. `language=en` is based on known English-language related songs, still subject to confirmation against original tracklist. `landmark=0`, an archival, not defining, release. This deliberately favors unknown fields over fabrication.

**Import decision gate.** A strict evidence policy could defer importing this row until a real dated release artifact and complete recording credits become available. The CSV contains an explicitly provisional row because this phase had been planned with Out & About included; the import notes highlight this caveat so Claude can decide. A low-confidence row is preferable to unsupported fake certainty.

## The Age of Reason — `bronskibeat-theageofreason` (2017)

**Not a compilation.** Steve Bronski himself told Pennyblack Music / Classic Pop the group **recorded new backing tracks and Stephen Granville re-sang the songs** (instead of reuse of Jimmy Somerville’s original vocals). The 2017 album is a newly produced **studio re-recording**, plus three additional recordings rather than a reissue of original 1984 masters. AllMusic credits 2017, producer **Steve Bronski AND Ian Donaldson**, and Granville singer. Count as `type=studio`; do not duplicate the 1984 descriptor or replace it. The original commercial CD/stream contains **12 new recordings on disc 1** plus **13 bonus tracks/remix variants on disc 2**; exclude duplicated bonus remixes from core sound judgment. HMV release is dated 28 July 2017, while AllMusic metadata states 4 Aug 2017; year is unambiguous.

**Original song and cover nuances.** The first disc re-records classic early Bronski Beat songs like 'Why?', 'Smalltown Boy', 'Screaming', 'Love & Money', 'Heatwave', 'Junk' and medleys 'No More War / It Ain't Necessarily So'. The band recorded **'A Flower for Dandara'** as a new song; **'Stars' (a disco-era Sylvester/Patrick Cowley song cover) and 'I'll Be Gone'** are part of the CD's second-disc new-song/bonus-mix programme. **Sylvester/Patrick Cowley are cover subjects, not automatically first-person musical influences**, so `influenced_by` remains blank. Neither the synthpop label nor thematic identity should be mistaken for a claim that these are the 1984 audio masters.

**Genre/sound profile.** Both professional reviews describe mostly faithful reconstructions of early synth-pop with modern synth timbres and newly programmed rhythm tracks. `synthpop` first, plus `hinrg`, `dancepop`, `electronic`, `disco`, `clubdance`, broad `pop` with scope annotations in genre-evidence CSV. `hinrg` is directly attached to new track 'I'll Be Gone'; `clubdance` primarily reflects bonus mixes, therefore lower priority. *The Afterword* hears smoother modern instruments and a relaxed 'No More War' medley, whereas *Classic Pop* objects to harder beats, thin percussion and losing the raw tension of the 1984 performances; these disagreements support medium experimental score (2) and electronic (5) but **not mood 5**. Scales: energy 3, electronic 5, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 4. The political/personal texts contrast with the smoother programmed arrangements.

**Instruments and production (album specific).** AllMusic: **Steve Bronski** `keyboards, programming, producer, backing vocals`; **Ian Donaldson** `keyboards, programming, producer, mixing, backing vocals`; Stephen Granville `vocals`. Two independent critics discuss *modern synthesizers*; hence `keyboards|synth` and `programming|synthprogramming`. Reviews directly observe Granville's falsetto and vocal delivery; `falsetto` in production, `clear|powerful` are independent voice judgments. `drummachine`, sampled percussion, autotune, TR-808, digital recording and specific synth brand are **NOT documented** and are omitted. Rose McDowall appears as guest vocalist (not producer, not influence). Patrick Cowley appears in the songwriting credits for 'Stars'; the song is a cover, not a featured producer credit.

**Influences and sounds-like.** No album-era first-person named musical inspiration verified. `influenced_by` empty. Sounds-like Erasure/Soft Cell/Pet Shop Boys/Sylvester are **editorial sonic comparisons**, not asserted citations of influence; Sylvester is also a covered artist but that does not confer an influence attribute. Landmark 0 (1984 original remains the defining release); confidence high for personnel/album identity and main sonics, though specific sound values are Fandome editorial.
