## La mia razza (1990), `miamartini-lamiarazza`

- **Primary sources:** original Fonit Cetra TLPX 248 album track and session credits at [Hit Parade Italia](https://www.hitparadeitalia.it/disco_old/martinim_a.htm); songwriter Giangilberto Monti's [recollection and LP listing](https://www.giangilbertomonti.it/it/mia-martini-la-mia-razza/); [Radio Margherita](https://www.radiomargherita.com/mia-martini/) on repertoire styles; original [pressing listing](https://musicbase.net/la-mia-razza).
- **Genres:** `pop` album-wide per pressing listing; `songwriter` songs explicitly described as cantautorato. `ballad` covers the slower *La nevicata del '56* and *Un altro Atlantico*. `neapolitansong` is for songs by Enzo Gragnaniello, not the whole LP; `latin` is primarily *Chica chica bum* (1/11); `ethnicmusic` only *Danza pagana* (1/11). The latter two sit in the long tail and cannot outweigh the pop repertoire. Although *Chica chica bum* was also recorded by Carmen Miranda, **covers are not influences**. No `samba` is asserted from the original performer's nationality.
- **Scales:** energy **3**, a mix of driving rhythmic tracks and soft ballads; electronic **1**, electric band/keyboards documented but no machine model or dominant electronics; mood **3**, nostalgic/reflective compositions alongside warm and playful Latin rhythms; theatrical **3**, pronounced interpretive changes but not a concept/theatre album; experimental **2**, Neapolitan and ethnic/Latin textures within conventional song forms; polish **3**, mainstream studio sound with track-by-track arrangements but no justified claim of glossy pop production; tempo **3**, measured song range; groove **2**, dance-leaning passages are a minority of the 11 songs.
- **Instruments** are backed directly by original LP session credits, including Agostino Marangolo/Maurizio Ciaramella/Maurizio Dei Lazzaretti (drums), Maurizio Galli/Rino Zurzolo (bass), Massimo Fumanti/Gianni Guarracino/Enzo Gragnaniello/Dodi Battaglia (guitar), Centofanti/Vessicchio/Amoruso/Cherni/Abeni (keyboards), Abeni/Senesi (piano), Fumanti (bouzouki on *Io e la musica*), Ricuarte (panflute) and Sepe (quena) on *Cercando il sole*, Imparato/Sannimo (percussion). Rare `panflute`, `quena`, `bouzouki` are track-level but clearly documented, not genre guesses.
- **Production:** `choirvocals` is supported by credited background vocals on *Va a Marechiaro*, sung by Mia Martini, Gragnaniello and Guarracino. Do not add specific keyboard machines, `programming`, `stringarrangements`, or synth effects without independent credits. **Giovanni Sanjust** is the record's sole credited producer (sometimes called *Gianni Sanjust* in pressing listing). Alessandro Centofanti, Renato Serio, Enzo Gragnaniello and Peppe Vessicchio are **arrangers**, not producers; Mauro Pagani is a **composer** of title track, not a producer.
- **Voice:** left empty. This album's whole-record predominant vocal type was not reliably qualified in album-specific evidence; a reputation for a rasping/powerful voice alone cannot fill the cell.
- **Themes/language:** album lyrics document identity (title track), city/nostalgia, romance and family memories; `it|nap` because Gragnaniello's *Va a Marechiaro* and related material employ Neapolitan; predominant Italian first. `scene=cantautori italiani`. No direct artist/producer testimony about identifiable **musical** influences for these sessions, hence `influenced_by` blank. No critic's specific **sonic** comparison of the record as a whole, hence `sounds_like` blank.
- **Landmark 0:** Sanremo reception and critical praise for *La nevicata del '56* do not themselves establish that the *album* defined a sound, genre or career. **Confidence high** on repertoire, credits, genres and sound; rare genres track-scoped.

## Lacrime (1992), `miamartini-lacrime`

- **Primary sources:** original Fonit Cetra TLPX 306 credits and [track listing](https://www.hitparadeitalia.it/disco_old/martinim_a.htm); [DeBaser original album review](https://www.debaser.it/mia-martini/lacrime/recensione); [Recensiamo Musica](https://recensiamomusica.com/mia-martini-trentanni-di-lacrime/); original album arranger [Marco Falagiani's biography](https://marcofalagiani.it/biografia-falagiani/); [official digital label genre](https://www.qobuz.com/no-en/album/lacrime-mia-martini/wxbwkrqf2m8ic).
- **Genres:** `pop` confirmed by label. `songwriter` reflects the album's cantautore-written narrative songs, not that Mia wrote the songs. `ballad` especially prominent Sanremo number *Gli uomini non cambiano* (lead single). `neapolitansong` for Gragnaniello's *Scenne l'argiento* (1/8). `folk` from the review's explicit *folk singer* and Neapolitan roots; `ethnicmusic` from the critic's description of ethnically inflected *Il mio oriente* (1/8). Avoid generic `jazz`: review mentions her **previous** jazz collaborations, not this particular album. Do not label *Il mio oriente* `arabicmusic` merely from a title or Eastern-coloured choir.
- **Scales:** energy **2**, strong climaxes in ballads but generally restrained pacing; electronic **2**, computer programming and live instruments documented per original credits; mood **2**, heartbreak and societal criticism dominate, tempered by irony; theatrical **4**, dramatic Sanremo-scale climaxes and forceful storytelling; experimental **2**, unusual ethnic/folk touches on a mainly accessible album; polish **4**, multiple studios, orchestrated strings and programmed layers; tempo **2**, mainly slow/mid; groove **1**, the song repertoire is predominantly undanceable though folk/rhythmic episodes exist.
- **Instruments:** original LP original track-level credits specify Marco Falagiani/Maurizio Tirelli (keyboards and piano), Paolo Bianchi/Maurizio Dei Lazzaretti (drums), Carmelo Isgrò/Francesco Puglisi (bass), Paolo Carta (guitars), Matteo Campagnini (oboe), string orchestra of named violinists, violists and cellists. No synth model is sourced. `programming` is justified by explicitly credited computer programmers Massimo Barbieri, Goffredo Orlandi, Simone Papi and Maurizio Tirelli. `stringarrangements` is confirmed by composer/arranger Marco Falagiani's direct biography; `choirvocals` from the original chorus on *Dio c'è* and other tracks. Avoid `digitalrecording`, `protools` or drum machines: none directly documented by these accounts.
- **Producer credit dispute:** original record listing credits *Promovideo e Mia Martini per Voice Sound*. The company is **not entered as a personal artist string**, while Mia Martini **is** included as an explicitly credited producer. Independent chart database credits **Giancarlo Bigazzi** as producer of the lead single *Gli uomini non cambiano* on this album (and some other album tracks). He is included as a **track-level credited producer**; he is **not** inferred as sole producer of the entire LP. Marco Falagiani is an arranger and co-writer, **not** an album producer. Claude should preserve the company credit as original-label metadata and confirm how project handles institutional producers.
- **Voice:** `powerful` from reviews describing this album's grinta and vocal strength; not `operatic` or `clear` from generic reputation. Themes: heartbreak, social issues and gender critique (*Gli uomini non cambiano*, *Lacrime*, *Uomini farfalla*), storytelling (*Il fiume dei profumi*) and identity. Main `it`; Neapolitan on *Scenne l'argiento*.
- **Landmark 0:** successful Sanremo performance and last original-material record are facts but no independent source establishing a genre- or whole-career-defining effect. **Confidence high**. The critic admires the record but complains about the chorus on *Dio c'è*, a production-quality disagreement: do not inflate all polish to 5.

## La musica che mi gira intorno (1994), `miamartini-lamusicachemigiraintorno`

- **Primary sources:** 1994 RTI Music original credit transcription [Hit Parade Italia](https://www.hitparadeitalia.it/disco_old/martinim_a.htm); [Nico Donvito's album article](https://recensiamomusica.com/mia-martini-trentanni-dallultimo-album-la-musica-che-mi-gira-intorno/); [Bubi album sound review](https://www.debaser.it/mia-martini/la-musica-che-mi-gira-intorno/recensione); [AllMusic genre entry](https://www.allmusic.com/album/musica-che-mi-gira-intorno-mw0000765059); [1994 interview republished in 2024](https://www.telecorriere.it/mia-martini-lomaggio-del-poeta-e-musicologo-maurizio-gregorini-in-esclusiva-lultima-intervista-allartista/); participant [Lele Barlera recollection](https://www.lelebarlera.com/biografia_1.html).
- **Classification:** 12 newly recorded 1994 studio interpretations, NOT a compilation of existing masters: ten covers of other artists' songs, a newly recorded *Viva l'amore*, and a fresh recording of *Piccolo uomo*. Some services label it a `compilation` solely because it is a covers collection; Recensiamo Musica explicitly calls it the *last studio album*. Here use **`type=studio`**, with edition disagreement documented. The original RTI number varies across databases (Hit Parade reports RTI 1070-2, MusicBrainz reports 1066-2); year and release identity are firm, but don't force a doubtful physical catalogue number in the descriptor row. Exact date sources differ (October/December 1994); the row has **year 1994 only**.
- **Genres:** `pop` from melodic Italian vocal-pop identity, and AllMusic's Pop/Rock classification; `songwriter` because the entire album comprises new arrangements of Italian singer-songwriter repertory, directly called *musica d'autore* by Bubi; `poprock` for the recorded full electric/acoustic band and vigorous rhythmic arrangements, consistent with AllMusic Pop/Rock; `ballad` only on slower, expressive songs such as *Mimì sarà*, *I treni a vapore*, and *Hotel Supramonte*, as juxtaposed against dynamic tracks in the review. Covers preserve their **actual new arrangement**, not automatic assignment of genres from originals. Don't add `rock` merely because Vasco Rossi or Bennato composed a song. **All influences blank:** songwriting, admired authors, covers and musical guests are not musical influences by Fandome's definition. **Sounds-like blank:** not a single qualified critic explicitly compares this album's **sound** to another artist. Describing the songs' composers is not such a comparison.
- **Scales:** energy **3** due to energetic electric-band reinterpretations offset by slow songs; electronic **2** due to explicitly credited computer/keyboard programming alongside live band; mood **2** deeply reflective and painful yet with vigorous, affectionate songs; theatrical **4** sweeping expressive vocals without staged concept; experimental **1** new arrangements of familiar song forms, not sonic experimentation; polish **4** full professional multi-layered studio setup and several keyboard/programming credits; tempo **2** slow/mid overall with some uptempo songs; groove **2** distinct driving beats, yet not dance-focused.
- **Instruments:** original 1994 credits name Giorgio Cocilovo (acoustic/classical/electric guitars), Paolo Costa (bass), Lele Melotti (drums), Rosario Jermano (percussion), Fio Zanotti/Maurizio Bassi/Gaetano Leandro (keyboards), Bruno De Filippi (harmonica on *Diamante*), and occasional guitar guests Dodi Battaglia/Paolo Gianolio. `programming` is proven by Rillo, Giordano Mazzi, Gabriele Barlera and Gaetano Leandro. `choirvocals` is proven by Aida Cooper, Antonella Pepe, Mia Martini, Fio Zanotti and guest backing singers on *Viva l'amore*. Do not infer drum machine or sampling from computer programming. Contemporary interview says her previously recorded backing tapes were held on DAT which RTI acquired; this **does not establish** that the record was tracked to DAT, hence `datrecording` is left out.
- **Producer disagreement:** original Hit Parade discography lists **Mia Martini** under Production; a 2024 independently written Recensiamo Musica article calls **Nando Sepe** producer; Martini's own interview confirms her personal production involvement but describes Nando also as her **manager** who brokered the label move. Following the instruction to retain every *credited* record producer, both `Mia Martini|Nando Sepe` are included, **medium confidence**, with the qualification that Nando's formal sleeve credit remains unconfirmed. Fio Zanotti is an **arranger**, not promoted to producer; Lele Barlera is a production contributor/programmer, not a producer.
- **Themes/language/landmark:** records of love, loss, social commentary and personal memory, in Italian. Despite some critics treating it as an artistic testament, the evidence does not say it *changed* a style or defined the **whole career**, so `landmark=0`. The source's own value judgment about how affecting the recordings are does not qualify as landmark evidence. This is the **last original studio project**; no further phase is needed under the studio-only brief.
