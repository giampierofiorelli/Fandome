## labionda-fratellilabiondasrl — Fratelli La Bionda s.r.l. (1972)

Original LP Ricordi SMRL 6099, widely dated December 1972 (occasional discographies say 1973). Artist statement to Stefano Gilardino (Rolling Stone Italia, 2021) gives unusually clear evidence: a deliberately acoustic West Coast sound modeled on Crosby, Stills & Nash, with Bruno Lauzi handling lyrics. This supports **influenced_by=Crosby, Stills & Nash** and sounds_like editorial comparison; Bruno Lauzi is not an influence merely because he participated. A secondary release history credits Lauzi as *producer*, the only documented production attribution here; Natale Massara is the arranger/conductor, not a producer. Release catalog classifications also place the album under Pop, Folk and Country. `folkrock` is a qualified style bridge rather than a literally credited genre. A specialist retrospectively describes the early LPs as *progressive*; that justifies `prog` as a low-strength texture, not its first genre. This is **not** a disco record.

## labionda-ognivoltachetutenevai — Ogni volta che tu te ne vai (1975)

Original Ricordi SMRL **6171** (1975), not the 1974 digital single with the same title and not the distinct LPX 016 later pressing. A resale advertisement inconsistently calls it SMRL 6172, but Ricordi catalog indexes **6171**; do not propagate the typo. Original album has TEN tracks, Italian-language performances, and piano by **Nicky Hopkins**, according to the GDM archive, Rolling Stone interview and a specialist review. Carmelo describes the 1975 project as their **second** album and notes new exposure to Barry White, Philadelphia sound and Isaac Hayes. This is a documented historical listening context, but **not reliable proof of track-specific soul performances**. Hence no `soul` album token or names in `influenced_by` yet. Style interpretations: a specialist hears less edge than 1972 and movement toward rhythmic dance feel; one seller calls the album prog like Le Orme or adventurous Lucio Battisti, which is not a technical album-wide genre analysis. `prog` is therefore minority/low-strength. `softrock` is only a tentative texture. Do not add Nicky Hopkins as producer, as his documented role is piano. ## labionda-tuttovabene — Tutto va bene (1977)

The later LP has **Prisoner** and **Come Down Easy**, brings English-language repertoire, and drops some earlier Italian tracks. This is a release with material differences, so the phase preserves a **separate catalogue object** for discovery. ## labionda-labionda — La Bionda (1978)

First disco LP under their surname, Baby Records **LPX 24** (recorded Country Lane Studios, Munich). **1978 original LP 5 tracks** (some pressings reorder A-side; shorter single edits cannot replace album mixes). Carmelo explicitly recalls that they initially preferred the **slow** `There for Me` as a single; Michelangelo created the finishing riff on `One for You, One for Me`, which became the breakout disco hit. Hence *at least one ballad* coexists with dance tracks and is explicitly retained as `ballad`. The long 10-minute `Sandstorm` contributes a long-form structure rather than proof of techno or progressive electronic genres. Original sleeve-based metadata documents **Charly Ricanek** on ARP synthesizer and keys, **Martin Harrison** drums, **Gary Unwin** bass, electric/acoustic guitars, brass, Bavarian strings and male/female choir. Electronic 3 denotes hybrid live band plus synthesizer; not a fully electronic 5. `voice` is BLANK because male/female choir credits document backing participants, not the lead-voice type; do not misuse `choral` from backing-vocal credit alone. `Funk/Soul` is a broad database genre umbrella, NOT proof of one soul vocal composition, kept low-strength as texture. `eurodisco` is a qualified editorial mapping supported by the Munich production milieu; other sources label only `disco`/`europop`. `sounds_like` is editorial similarity, not an asserted artist influence. `influenced_by` left empty because historical Munich references to Giorgio Moroder/Donna Summer do not prove a specific influence on *this* album.

**Original five-track audit:**
  1. **There for Me** — Slow love ballad according to Carmelo; acoustic/electric piano and bass specifically credited. Important exception to disco.
  2. **One for You, One for Me** — Disco breakout single; 1978 release and production credits supported. Long LP 5:55–6:56 differs by pressing/edit.
  3. **Hey Woman** — On original LP; broadly disco classification. No evidence for track-specific other genre.
  4. **Sandstorm** — Extended 10:06 track with credited cymbal and ARP-enabled album personnel; avoid making unsupported ambient or space-disco claims.
  5. **Song for Smokey and the Bandit** — Original 3:55 album track; title alone does NOT justify country soundtrack or film-score genre.

**Scales:** energy=4 (long dance-side alongside an actual ballad), electronic=3 (live instruments + ARP synthesizer), mood=4 (upbeat disco contrasted with slow love song), theatrical=3 (choir/brass/strings), experimental=2 (one ten-minute extended track, otherwise conventional pop), polish=4 (large studio lineup), tempo=4 (long tracks are generally dance tempo yet includes slow song), groove=4 (dance core not unbroken nonstop album). Numbers are *document-informed editorial approximations*, not digital acoustic analysis. Avoid 5 for groove merely from the big single. `producers=Carmelo La Bionda` relies on explicit *Producer* credit on a 1978 LP; another pressing names both brothers as **executive producers** and 1978 singles show both as producers. Preserve disagreement instead of automatically promoting executive credit into album co-production.

## Cross-release notes, limits and abstentions

- The 1972/1975/1977 acoustic period is sonically distinct from late-1970s Eurodisco. It must not be backtagged Italo disco only because of the duo's later reputation.
- The duo recorded notable disco albums as **D.D. Sound**, which is a documented separate alias, not the same credited artist on the jackets. That discography should receive a later dedicated alias treatment (and possibly catalogue artist id decision). Do not merge its original records into the 1978 self-titled album.
- No sampled audio, waveform, or full authoritative lyric transcriptions were used. Some genres and theme columns are **provisional and weak** and are explicitly not described as verified song sonics. A blank scalar is a positive abstention, not missing work.
- Dates 1972/1975/1977/1978 and major catalogue identifiers are cross-checked, but 1977 as separate album vs variant needs human editorial choice.
