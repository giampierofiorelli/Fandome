# Evidence notes — Ennio Morricone / Phase 04

Checked: 26 October 2026. Method: describe the **first identifiable commercially released full soundtrack LP program**, not film-premiere dates or streaming copyright years. Four new rows only; never overwrite Phase 01–03 rows. Original release claims were verified independently from physical-release discographies and/or the publisher. Some specialised reviews discuss expanded CD editions: the exact original LP cue set constrains every instrumentation claim.

Fandome's eight numeric scales are *editorial* readings anchored to album-specific expert description, not measured acoustics. Empty values are genuine unknowns. `production` stays blank for all four: composing, conducting, orchestration or remastering are **not** evidence of original-session recording processes, equipment or LP record producer credits. `sounds_like` is an editorial analogy; `influenced_by` is blank unless a creator or expert links a particular album to a musical influence.

## 1. Metello (1970) — `morricone-metello`

**Edition / identity.** RCA Records Italy **KOLS 1009**, ten score tracks, originally issued 1970. Original track names, format and date: [SoundtrackCollector](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/6529/Metello) and [EIL original copy](https://eil.com/Shop/moreinfo.asp?catalogid=818466). The 2006 GDM 14-track remaster adds four previously unissued tracks. The 2024 Beat Records editorial account reproduced by [Soundohm](https://www.soundohm.com/product/metello) explicitly distinguishes original album stereo masters from additional session mono tapes.

**Genres**: `classical` applies to the classically inflected orchestral theme (Beat Records); `march` to two title-specific marches on original LP (*L’arrivo del Re*, *La Naja*). `soundtrack` is a format-only marker. Reviewer Jonathan Broxton identifies Renaissance-like harmony, but does not identify this album as a literal Renaissance-music genre; none is added.

**Instruments**: Beat Records specifically describes the **harp with organ** opening and piano/orchestra finale. [Broxton's score review](https://moviemusicuk.us/2021/02/13/ennio-morricone-reviews-part-x/) identifies guitar, woodwinds, solo violin (rolled up under `strings`), piano, solo trumpet, organ, saxophone, flute, oboe and military drums in the original cues. All these are tied to original-title tracks, not bonus compositions. The original LP credits one variant with *cembalo*, while Broxton calls a matching timbre **cimbalom**; this is instrumentally ambiguous. Neither `harpsichord` nor `cimbalom` is in the row pending definitive liner/session evidence. The cue name *Pianino Nella Strada* is NOT proof of an actual street piano; piano is independently attested in other original cues.

**Scales**: energy 2 and tempo 2 because the wistful opening and repeated strike laments dominate over short martial numbers. Electronic 0 rests on specifically acoustic/orchestral instrumentation documented by Beat and Broxton, **not** merely the year 1970. Mood 2 for wistful/solemn, theatrical 3 for expanded orchestral finale and regal march; experimental 1 for conventional themes with some timbral departures; groove 0 because the march is functional film rhythm rather than dance repertoire. `polish` unknown: later remaster quality cannot describe first LP's production.

**Voice/lyrics/language**: original LP is instrumental; political history belongs to film setting but does not imply sung political lyrics. Producer unverified: Bruno Nicolai is **conductor**, not record producer. `sounds_like` Nino Rota/Georges Delerue is editorial melodic-orchestral proximity, NOT an influence attribution. `landmark` 0: a strong but less defining entry within Morricone's huge catalogue. High confidence in year and cue-specific instrumentation.

## 2. La Califfa (1971) — `morricone-lacaliffa`

**Edition / identity.** 1970 is the Bevilacqua **film** and composition; original fourteen-track full LP is **CBS S 70090 (Italy, 1971)**. [BNF national-library LP record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb378062043), [MusicBrainz pressing](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/d1b16ae8-9dbf-3137-8849-6c700c3a3e6c) and [physical LP listing](https://eil.com/Shop/moreinfo.asp?catalogid=818423) agree. Original cues include *Sangue sull’asfalto*, *Le donne al fiume*, *La pace interiore*, *La Califfa* and *Prima e dopo l’amore*. The 2000/2004/modern expanded issues contain extra titles and alternative takes. In particular *Requiem per un operaio*, *La cena*, *Sotto la pioggia*, *Trittico per organo*, *Ricordo di un amico* are **not on the original 14-track LP** and may not establish original-album instruments or voice treatment; see [Beat Records](https://www.beatrecords.it/en/catalogo/2136/la-califfa).

**Genre evidence and disagreement.** AllMusic explicitly places this score under `classical` ([composition listing](https://www.allmusic.com/composition/la-califfa-mc0002471313)). MusicBrainz carries `easylistening`, `jazz`, and `avantgardejazz` tags, but these are community metadata, not independent expert listening. The lyrical themes plausibly support an easy-listening texture; the avant-garde jazz tag is particularly weak for the **original** program and must be reviewed, not elevated to main genre. Its final position in the row explicitly reduces recommendation weight. `soundtrack` is format only.

**Instruments and voice.** Broxton describes original-cue oboe melody, lush strings, piano undercurrent, subtle horns, plangent violas, organ in *La pace interiore*, tolling bells (`bells`), acoustic guitar, strummed mandolin and snare/percussion in *L’impatto*. `orchestra`, `strings`, `oboe`, `piano`, `organ`, `woodwinds`, `viola`, `acousticguitar`, `mandolin`, `percussion`, `brass`, `bells` all have original-track evidence. Distinct `synth` or machine unspecified: critic says 'electronic stabs' in *Sangue sull’asfalto* but identifies no model or instrument, so omit `synth`. Edda Dell'Orso contributes wordless soprano on *Le donne al fiume*; [Quartet Records](https://quartetrecords.com/product/la-monaca-di-monza-la-califfa/) confirms I Cantori Moderni participation. `soft|choral` reflect these unlexical vocalise passages; `instrumental` remains primary because most cues have no voice. Leave `language` and `lyrics` blank: film story (workers' conflict and forbidden love) cannot be projected as sung lyrics. Some sources attribute larger choir effect to bonus-only *Requiem*; choir is present in archival personnel but prominent original-LP choral treatment merits cautious review.

**Scales**: energy 2, tempo 2 and groove 0 reflect slow elegiac cues, versus more dramatic *Sangue sull’asfalto* / *L’impatto*. Mood 1 for tragedy and yearning, theatrical 4 for orchestral swells, experimental 3 for churning dissonance and atonal disruption, electronic 1 for the specifically mentioned *Sangue* electronic texture rather than genre-based guess. `polish` not documented for first-session technique. Confidence **medium** due to source-dependent jazz labels and voice-coverage uncertainty, though original album year is secure. `landmark` 1 for iconic theme, per specialist Broxton appraisal. Producers unknown: composer/film producer/label remaster engineer not eligible.

## 3. Sacco e Vanzetti (1971) — `morricone-saccoevanzetti`

**Edition / identity.** This is the original ten-track **1971 RCA soundtrack** (Italian **OLS 4**, American **LSP-4612**), not a Joan Baez hits compilation nor the two-track 7-inch. Originals and track lists: [SoundtrackCollector](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/5498/Sacco%2BE%2BVanzetti), [US first LP entry](https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/ennio-morricone-sacco-vanzetti-an-original-soundtrack-recording-lp-488916/) and [BNF French RCA 33rpm catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb380118484). Contemporary US coverage also dates the joint soundtrack to 1971, but the primary supporting sources here are the record itself and archival catalogues. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sacco-e-vanzetti-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0000095322) lists both Baez and Morricone as artists; the BNF original LP document also lists both. **Co-billed**: record `Ennio Morricone & Joan Baez`, taking care to check the literal credit of the specific regional cover in `coBilled.ts`.

**Artist roles:** Ennio Morricone composed the music; Joan Baez wrote English lyrics and sang *The Ballad of Sacco and Vanzetti* (three parts) and *Here's to You*. In a [2006 BFI interview](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/if-you-want-call-yourself-composer-you-follow-every-step-instrumentation-ennio-morricone-interviewed-2006?pubDate=20250516), Morricone describes sending Baez his music so she could add words and a vocal performance. Crucially this does **not** prove she served as a record producer. No producer credits for either name are included in the descriptor. Similar artists `Mikis Theodorakis|Judy Collins` are editorial analogies between political screen music and protest-folk singing, not documented influences.

**Genres:** AllMusic explicitly names **Contemporary Folk** for the album and describes music as folkier and more traditionally orchestral than Morricone's western albums. Thus `contemporaryfolk|folk|soundtrack`; the exact **NEW** token `contemporaryfolk` must be created, with `folk` parent in genreFamilies and validator. It would be false to label the entire LP folk: Morricone's orchestral instrumental cues share the album with Baez's folk songs. No `classical` token is asserted without a clear album-level genre label, although the score is orchestral.

**Instrumentation:** core `orchestra|strings` documented in professional AllMusic assessment and original cue descriptions; `oboe` appears in period performer credits in the soundtrack's history, but the specific player name needs original-sleeve corroboration if promoted to the performer graph. No claimed specific synthesizer or equipment: later uncredited IMDb mentions Walter Branchi, yet its allocation to the exact original LP tracks is insufficient. The album includes Baez's lead voice, sometimes with choral accompaniment (Cantori Moderni is linked to original song performances), plus standalone instrumental score. Clear / choral / instrumental therefore ordered by prominence. `language=en` pertains to sung Baez lyrics only; incidental film languages excluded. `lyrics=political|social|storytelling` describes injustice, martyrdom, advocacy, histories of two defendants. Cover art and later covers are not influences.

**Scales**: energy 2 for slow restrained ballads, electronic blank because early recording machines and a later suggested synthetic tone cannot securely be tied to original LP, mood 1 because funerary injustice and protest, theatrical 4 because orchestral drama and choral anthem, experimental 2 for unusual song–score combination, tempo 2, groove 1 since there is a measured pulse but this is not dance music. `polish` blank. Confidence high for music, credits and album date; tonal scales still interpretive. `landmark` 1, especially because *Here's to You* became a lasting Morricone–Baez anthem.

## 4. Giù la testa (1971) — `morricone-giulatesta`

**Edition / identity.** Cinevox itself lists the **1971 original LP MDF 33/50** (shown as MDF3350) with eleven tracks: [Cinevox master publisher catalogue](https://www.cinevoxrecord.it/it/catalogue/product/show/927). A [physical first pressing](https://www.45cat.com/vinyl/album/mdf3350) is dated **October 1971**. The 2000 CD in Broxton's review has a **repeated title cue** as an extra: the first eleven titles are on the original LP, not the later extra twelfth. This is distinct from the **1971 FILM** release but happens to share the same year.

**Genres:** [Broxton](https://moviemusicuk.us/2021/03/06/ennio-morricone-reviews-part-xi/) explicitly discusses spaghetti western idiom, `march` in *Marcia degli Accattoni* and a brief `lounge`-like main theme. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/giu-la-testa-mw0000411270) classifies the original under Classical and describes avant-garde quirks, justifying `classical|avantgarde` in lesser positions. `soundtrack` remains a format marker. Original has sentimental and dissonant music, not a continuous dance-groove record.

**Voice and instruments:** Broxton specifies the main score's strings, Alessandro Alessandroni's whistle, Edda dell'Orso's **wordless** soprano, and a separately repeated spoken/sung name *Sean* in the title, *Scherzi a parte*, and *Invenzione per John*. The repeated name is SUNG, not spoken. Since a proper name is not sufficient to establish song language or a lyrical theme, both `lyrics` and `language` stay blank. **Wordless vocalise is not a lyric**. Original-score instrumental sources: orchestra/strings, whistle, oboe (*Addio Messico*), woodwinds (*Mesa Verde*), guitar (*Messico e Irlanda*), percussion (*Marcia degli Accattoni*) from Broxton; `orchestra` also AllMusic. No inferred electronics or synthesizer model. `production` blank: shouted/whispered motif is composition/performance, not verified studio voice-processing. **Producer** blank because Morricone the composer and Alessandroni the whistler are not LP producers by default.

**Scales**: energy 3 because quiet elegy balances forceful revolution cues; electronic unknown: acoustic instrumentation described but no complete source audit excluding electronic insertions. Mood 2 for bittersweet nostalgic sections interrupted by deaths, theatrical 4 for soprano and martial climaxes, experimental 3 for odd vocal devices and dissonance, tempo 3 averaged across varied march and slow theme, groove 1 not dance-oriented. Polish unknown; soundtrack remaster is not original-production evidence. `sounds_like=Bruno Nicolai|Luis Bacalov` editorial western score timbres; not influenced_by. `landmark` 1 as key Leone–Morricone score.

## Cross-phase and source disagreements

- `Il gatto a nove code` was a 1971 **movie and 7-inch single**, **NOT a full LP in 1971**. [Publisher AMS/BTF](https://btf.it/prodotto/ennio-morricone-il-gatto-a-nove-code-soundtrack-lp/) explicitly says only a 45 was released at the film's premiere; complete soundtrack became public over twenty years later. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2bac44db-1840-469b-b93f-2adbcd25ee87/details) confirms original 1971 7-inch. A current streaming platform label `1971 original soundtrack album` uses original composition copyright, not evidence of an LP then. Defer the full-album descriptor until first issue and actual track program verified. Do NOT add a bogus `morricone-ilgattoanovecode` (1971) row.
- `La Califfa`: AllMusic's 1970 composition/date and 2000 52-minute edition conflict with physical 1971 first 14-track LP. **1971** wins for Fandome album date; later bonus tracks excluded. `avantgardejazz` tag is weak crowd metadata: importing user may remove after listening with verified specialist score-level review.
- `Metello`: Broxton's 14-track set was expanded in 2006; first LP 1970 had ten. `cembalo` on original track title and critic's `cimbalom` differ; both excluded rather than guessed.
- `Sacco e Vanzetti`: a true Joan Baez **artist credit** (not merely guest) is documented, unlike the usual co-performer issue; variants show both names, so Claude should update `coBilled.ts` with the original cover credit variant it verifies.
- Earlier Phase 03 has *L’uccello dalle piume di cristallo* (1971 original Italian LP), a DIFFERENT Dario Argento score from *Il gatto a nove code*. Do not redo it.
- No first-party verified original LP mastering machines; avoid invented `analogtape` and `remastering` tags. Avoid unconfirmed mixing engineers, film producers or conductors under `producers`.
