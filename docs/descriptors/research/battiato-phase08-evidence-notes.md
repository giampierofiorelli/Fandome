# Franco Battiato — Phase 08 evidence notes (26 October 2026)

## Source policy, rating method and file boundaries
- Phase 08 ONLY. Albums already described in Phases 01–07 are excluded; the GitHub `battiato.csv` public branch currently includes an earlier 20-row subset only through *Caffè de la Paix*. It does not prove that yet-undelivered Phase 06–07 files were imported.
- Scales use 0–5 anchors in Fandome guide. Values are **editorial judgments for this release**, grounded in album-specific listening accounts and credits; they are not numerical claims quoted from sources. Weakly documented tempo/groove for the heterogeneous compilation *Inneres Auge* are left empty.
- Any name in `sounds_like` is a sonic comparison, not a claimed historical influence. Cover authors are not entered in `influenced_by` merely because their songs were recorded.
- All source links are real publications found during this research; some original pages have anti-bot restrictions, but search previews and indexed references support the source metadata. Physical-booklet rechecking may refine personnel and exact language.

## Dieci stratagemmi (2004) — `battiato-diecistratagemmi`
**Release, identity and producer.** MusicBrainz lists the Italian Columbia CD as 1 October 2004. Original-disc transcription [d-original] gives Battiato as *producer and arranger*; Francesco Cattini is **executive producer**, and Angelo Privitera is pre-production assistant, neither is promoted into `producers`. The source's original styles are Electronic / Rock / Pop Rock / Experimental. Guests include Krisma (several tracks), Cristina Scabbia and Kumi C. Watanabe [d-rockol].

**Instruments.** Original CD credits [d-original] name Battiato on keyboards, guitar and piano, Guaitoli on piano and English Chamber Orchestra leadership, English Chamber Orchestra (tracks 1,4–7), other electric-band guitar/bass/drums. Period critic [d-sentire] explicitly hears *synth*, electric passages and strings. `guitar` intentionally avoids assuming which of the credited parts were electric vs acoustic. `strings` is orchestra subset, both documented. Do not add a named synth make: none is verified for this exact album.

**Production.** Pino Pischetola is specifically credited with *drum programming* [d-original] → `drumprogramming`. The orchestra and string-rich orchestration specifically noted by both the artist [d-official] and critic [d-sentire] → `stringarrangements`. Stockhausen's *Gesang der Jünglinge* is explicitly referenced in the introduction to track 8 [d-official]; this **does not verify a sampled master or any named machine**, so `sampling` stays out.

**Ratings.** Energy=3 and tempo=3 reflect variable, often mid-paced rock-pop and driving electronic pieces; electronic=3 reflects synthesizers and orchestra/band working together. Mood=2 reflects the critic's angry, uneasy political themes alongside moments of contemplation [d-ondarock]. Theatrical=3 for chorus/orchestra and guest dramatic delivery; experimental=3 for odd meters and unusual electronic/rock hybrids [d-rolling]. Polish=4 for polished orchestra/studio production; groove=2 for intermittent pulses, not sustained club music.

**Voice and themes.** The lead is predominantly controlled and clear; `spoken` derives from Sgalambro's credited recitation. Lyrical themes political/social (e.g. *Ermeneutica*) and spiritual/introspective. Italian with selected English, Japanese and German segments is noted in [d-rolling]; minor-language scope is not equal to full-album language dominance.

**Documented influence vs sonic likeness.** Battiato's official site explicitly links part of track 8 to Stockhausen: a documentary, track-specific compositional reference. `influenced_by=Karlheinz Stockhausen` is restricted to that verifiable connection, not a global claim that the album imitates Stockhausen. Mauro Roma's *David Sylvian* and *Subsonica* comparisons are **sounds_like**, not documented influence [d-ondarock]. Krisma are collaborators and not inserted as an influence.

**Critical disagreement.** OndaRock praises the integration and political urgency; SentireAscoltare assigns a moderate 6.8, and the Rolling Stone ranking is relative rather than a landmark designation. `landmark=0` because the 1981 breakthrough and earlier avant-garde records are stronger career-defining works; this does not mean the album was insignificant.

## Il vuoto (2007) — `battiato-ilvuoto`
**Release and producer.** The original Italian release is reported as **9 February 2007** in Rockol's archival anniversary [v-anniversary]; AllMusic's **6 March 2007** is a later listing/market date. Year 2007 is uncontested. AllMusic credits **Battiato** as producer, Angelo Privitera as production assistant, and Pino Pischetola in engineering/rhythm programming [v-allmusic]. Neither assistant is counted as a co-producer.

**Instruments.** Official biography [v-official] explicitly specifies piano, guitar, electronic layer and orchestra. AllMusic has Carlo Guaitoli piano/conducting, Battiato keyboards and synthesizer bass, Davide Oliveri drums, Luca Simoncini cello, Royal Philharmonic Orchestra, and further bass accompaniment. Rockol's artist interview confirms guest **MAB guitarists and voices** [v-interview], corroborated by the musicians themselves [v-mab]; `guitar` avoids assuming an uncredited acoustic/electric subtype.

**Production.** AllMusic's *Rhythm Programming* → `drumprogramming`; orchestral arrangement documented by official account → `stringarrangements`. Electronic =2 because critics explicitly stress a more restrained acoustic-led sound [v-official, v-rockol]; not a claim that the album is electronics-free. No specific drum machine, software or synth model verified.

**Genre evidence and disputes.** AllMusic: Italian Pop, Euro-Pop, Neo-Classical, umbrella Pop/Rock; OndaRock: songwriter, occasional world-music; Rockol: electronic plus rock and orchestra. `electropop` is narrower and album-specific but mainly describes electronic-pop tracks rather than every song. The album is largely melodic pop and piano ballads; world music is specifically a **late-track** descriptor. Do not confuse Tchaikovsky's melodic material with a whole-album classical genre.

**Ratings.** Energy=2, tempo=2 and groove=1 from mainly ballads with one or two more driving tracks; electronic=2 from underlaid minimal electronics plus live players. Mood=2 because the album presents disorientation and melancholy with consolation/joy [v-official]. Theatrical=2 due restrained vocals, orchestra, occasional dramatic crescendos. Experimental=2 compared with previous electronics-heavy albums; contemporary Rockol notes it is unusually direct. Polish=4 for metropolitan recording plus Royal Philharmonic.

**Lyrical content and language.** `The Game Is Over` contains sung English and Italian passages (licensed lyric metadata [v-english]); `Era l'inizio della primavera` sets/uses Tchaikovsky's material with original literary attribution [v-tschaik], justifying **track-specific** `influenced_by=Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky`. Do not list Tolstoy as music producer. Other themes: spiritual emptiness, consumer excess, nature, identity, simple life.

**Critical disagreement.** Rockol [v-rockol] calls the sound more consistent but insufficiently surprising; OndaRock [v-ondarock] values its lyrical/spiritual unity. Keep `landmark=0` despite commercial chart success.

## Fleurs 2 (2008) — `battiato-fleurs2`
**Original edition and type.** Mercury/Universal original CD 14 November 2008, barcode 00602517883819 [f-label]. This is a studio **songbook/cover album**, not a compilation of previously released masters. The unusual chronological trilogy goes *Fleurs* (1999), *Fleurs 3* (2002), *Fleurs 2* (2008) [f-lisola]. *Fleurs 2* includes the new duet *Tutto l'universo obbedisce all'amore* with Carmen Consoli and a substantial repertoire of covers [f-official].

**Producer precision.** Qobuz's label credits list **Franco Battiato, Producer**, **Francesco Cattini, ExecutiveProducer**; the contemporary specialist publication [f-lisola] likewise identifies artistic production as Battiato. Do **not** move Cattini into musical production.

**Instruments and guests.** Printed musician roster by [f-lisola]: Carlo Guaitoli piano, Royal Philharmonic Orchestra, Gabriele Comeglio saxophones, Giuseppe Pischetola Hammond organ, Emilio Soana trumpet, guitarists Davide Ferrario and Davide Tagliapietra, Essiet Essiet electric and double bass, Mylious Johnson drums, Pino Pischetola computerized rhythms. Acoustic chamber arrangement dominates although full band also participates: the source does **not** say all are heard on every track. Use generic `guitar`; actual acoustic/electric distinction unspecified there. `strings` grounded by reviewers' string-focused descriptions and orchestra.

**Production.** Piano and orchestra arranger treatment [f-allmusic, f-rockol] supports `stringarrangements`; 'ritmiche computerizzate' from musician roster supports `drumprogramming`. No exact sequencer/computer/hardware attribution. 'Sittin' on the Dock of the Bay' being a soul original does **not** justify a whole-album `soul` tag.

**Genre evidence dispute (important).** OndaRock classifies pop and describes a short **pop-noise-jazz** track [f-ondarock]. AllMusic calls the reworkings **chamber-music arrangements** and lists *Neo-Classical*; it also supplies seemingly ill-matched *Alternative/Indie Rock* and *Experimental Rock* database style tags. Per Fandome's **every-source-named genre** rule, those are retained **last and flagged as low-evidence provider metadata**; they should never outrank pop/chamber-pop. `chamberpop` is an analytic normalisation of the critic's precise description, NOT a direct genre label from the provider; re-check if importer requires exact source terms. `jazz` is limited to *La musica muore*, not the album as a whole.

**Ratings, voice, languages, themes.** Energy=1, tempo=1, groove=0 because mostly slow or restrained reinterpretations; electronic=1 from live instrumentation with computerized rhythms; mood=2, theatrical=2 for meditative lyrical tenderness and controlled vocal delivery; experimental=1 because critics widely describe familiar chamber treatments rather than radical reconstruction; polish=4 for top-level orchestral sessions. Songs in Italian, English and French. The five duet partners are **features**, not separate principal billing: Carmen Consoli, Antony Hegarty, Anne Ducros, Sepideh Raissadat, Juri Camisasca [f-label].

**Influences vs covers.** Songwriters Sergio Endrigo, Vangelis/Aphrodite's Child, Paul Simon, Otis Redding, Dalida, Juri Camisasca and Antony Hegarty are original repertoire authors/artists. Their direct **musical influence on Battiato** is not established by a cover credit alone, so `influenced_by` intentionally left empty. OndaRock and Rockol find the trilogy's final installment underwhelming; AllMusic acknowledges several moving moments and stronger duets [f-allmusic].

## Inneres Auge (2009) — `battiato-inneresauge`
**Identity, date and disputed type.** Label CD [i-label] is *Inneres Auge (Il tutto è più della somma delle sue parti)*, **13 November 2009**, Universal/Mercury, 10 tracks. The same song circulated as a single on **30 October 2009** (separate UPC); don't confuse these. Editors/critics call it both a studio album and a compilation of new/rare recordings: Rockol [i-rockol] describes four then-unreleased tracks, six prior compositions in new/reworked versions. AllMusic [i-allmusic] calls it partly rarities compilation, partly extended single. It is labelled `compilation` here to describe content and **retained** due distinct 2009 recordings/new songs, not treated as a greatest-hits reissue. Claude may decide `studio` better fits local catalog convention; do not treat old versions as original 2009 compositions.

**Previously issued track warning.** [i-labelbio] says three *new* original compositions plus De André's *Inverno* cover; other sources count four new-to-album tracks. `Tibet` had apparently circulated as digital *Fleurs 2* bonus before this CD. 'Previously unreleased' **varies by edition/platform**: preserve distinction in catalog notes. Four reworks of previous album songs + two earlier B-sides round out the 10.

**Producer and musicians.** L’Isola's detailed roster [i-lisola] identifies **Franco Battiato** as artistic producer; Guaitoli piano, Ferrario guitars/bass, Simoncini and Mazza violin, Comuzzi viola, Luca Simoncini cello, Emilio Soana trumpet, JoniaVoces choir and Pino 'Pinaxa' Pischetola computerised rhythms; Sgalambro voice. AllMusic credits also show Comuzzi viola [i-allmusic]. These are album-specific credits rather than guesses from 1980s production. `drumprogramming` represents computerized rhythms, `stringarrangements` source is review emphasis on string-led treatment and *Inverno* arrangement [i-allmusic]. No named drum-machine model is documented.

**Genre/rating uncertainty.** Qobuz gives Pop; AllMusic puts album under Pop/Rock; Rockol specifically documents electronic backing for the title track. The compilation's piano/strings-heavy tracks coexist with electronic political material, so electronic=2 and experimental=2 are moderate, while tempo and groove are **empty** (heterogeneous and no defensible album-wide value from current sources). Energy=2, theatrical=2, mood=1 for mostly melancholy, spiritual and angry songs; polish=4 supported by piano/chamber work and quality production [i-lisola]. `poprock` is low-priority database umbrella, not all-track rock. Art-pop is a plausible *interpretation* but omitted as an unsupported genre label.

**Language, politics, voices.** Italian mainstream, English *Tibet* and *No Time No Space*, Sicilian plus Latin in *'U cuntu* [i-rockol]; other languages require booklet corroboration. Battiato sung, Sgalambro spoken and JoniaVoces choir [i-lisola]. Protest/political corruption in *Inneres Auge*, spiritual introspection, plus heartbreak in De André's *Inverno*.

**Artist influences.** The actual De André cover is documented, but there is no album-specific explicit testimony that De André's composing shaped Battiato's own writing; no automatic inferred `influenced_by`. `sounds_like` De André references the reinterpreted song. AllMusic is skeptical of the compilation's cohesion, whereas L’Isola finds it emotionally unified. Album not a major discography-defining milestone (`landmark=0`).
