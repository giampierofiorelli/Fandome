# Robert Miles — Phase 01: evidence notes (research cutoff 2026-10-25)

## Method
All numerical scales are *editorial inferences* calibrated to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`, grounded in the album-specific sound descriptions below. They are not measurements or ratings from reviews. Genres are source-derived and each genre has an individually scoped evidence record. An artist-wide influence is **not** treated as proof that the album was influenced by that artist. Instrument categories describe timbres when sources explicitly say piano/synth-strings; they do not imply acoustic piano or live strings unless credited. No audio-source reanalysis was performed.

## Dreamland (1996) — `robertmiles-dreamland`
**Core sources:** AllMusic (Jose F. Promis), Synthtopia (2003 review), Miles artist Bandcamp, MusicBrainz credits, RA biography, Tokafi interview, and Impellizzeri's technical history. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/dreamland-mw0000183716) · [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2003/11/23/robert-miles-dreamland/) · [Bandcamp](https://robertmiles.bandcamp.com/album/dreamland) · [Technical history](https://decadancebook.wordpress.com/tag/robert-miles/page/2/).

**Genre hierarchy.** Specialist Synthtopia calls the *album* melodic trance, citing prominent piano motifs and synth bass; AllMusic lists electronic, house, progressive house and trance. Dream trance belongs to the context of his landmark Children-led style. Ambient/chillout applies to extended passages rather than every track; *new age* and *techno* are limited to track-specific descriptions in the review. This resists reducing the album to undifferentiated 1990s eurodance. (`dreamtrance`, `dreamhouse`, `progressivehouse`, `melodictrance`, `newage` require new tokens; `trance` already exists.)

**Instruments.** Synthtopia directly hears piano, synthetic strings/pads, synth bass, sequenced motifs and drums on *this album*. `piano` describes the **piano sound**, not proof of an acoustic grand piano. `synth` includes synthetic strings; `strings` is deliberately excluded since no acoustic string players are credited. `sampler` and technical machines are grounded in a specialised Italian account naming Kurzweil K2000, Akai S3200, Korg 01/W and Lexicon effects for *Children* and *Fable*. `sequencer` is substantiated by the review's description of sequenced ostinati. Do not infer Roland JD-800/Korg M1 from TV appearances; the specialist account disputes their studio role.

**Production.** `sampling` plus `kurzweilk2000|akais3200|korg01w` are documented by the Italian history, not by an artist-issued studio inventory. The Kurzweil-vs-Korg origin of the characteristic piano timbre is disputed by online recreations; do **not** present an exact patch assignment as established. Effects are discussed but generic Lexicon is *not* put in the token column because the model identity is unspecified. Producer Robert Miles is credited to the album. Robin Hancock is additionally credited on **One & One** alone; inclusion in the producer list applies to the commonly circulated North-American/expanded album edition, not the earliest European track list. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/f6b49bd1-703f-48a1-86c4-ee2a6a5f6313).

**Scales.** `energy 3` and `tempo 3` reflect driving but non-aggressive mid/fast dance passages; `electronic 4` predominantly synth/sample sounds; `mood 3` mixes yearning piano with uplift; `theatrical 2` largely instrumental/direct; `experimental 1` conventional repeated motifs and long builds; `polish 4` smooth tightly shaped studio arrangements; `groove 4` repetitive dance beats across majority. **Voice `instrumental|clear`** reflects primarily instrumental tracks with the vocal songs on some editions; `love` and language `en` depend most clearly on **One & One**, first added in 1996 and not on the first European pressing.

**Disagreement/uncertainty.** AllMusic praises the hypnotic continuous quality where others might perceive repetitiveness; Synthtopia calls it accessible/non-challenging. The album's first pressing / U.S. / Winter / remastered editions are not identical. `sounds_like` (Chicane/ATB) is an editorial likeness, **not** a historical influence or collaboration. `influenced_by` stays empty because artist-wide formative influences in RA do not establish per-album causality. Confidence high for overall style, medium for exact models and some late-edition vocal details.

## 23am (1997) — `robertmiles-23am`
**Core sources:** [AllMusic, Stephen Thomas Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/23-am-mw0000031845), [RA artist biography](https://ra.co/dj/robertmiles/biography), [Robert Miles interview](https://www.tokafi.com/15questions/interview-robert-miles/), [original CD credits](https://recordsale.de/de/kuenstler/robert-miles/albums/23-am), [MusicBrainz release group](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/0c0e4015-4239-38a6-816f-2a38884558aa).

**Genres.** AllMusic's metadata states electronic, club/dance, house, progressive house and trance, but its review says ambient is the **foundation**. Tokafi describes both spacious ambient and energetic electro. `ambient` leads; dance subgenres refer to portions of the album with singers, not its full duration. `electronica|chillout|pop` are described in the secondary genre index and included with genre-evidence strength medium. Do not miscategorise the album merely as a second *Dreamland*.

**Instruments and voice.** AllMusic credits **Denys Lable (electric guitar), Michel Gaucher (saxophone), Eric Giausserand (trumpet)**; both it and the period description note synthetic keyboard textures. The original marketing described added strings, but no identifiable live string players were confirmed in checked credits; `strings` therefore stays out. `synth`/`keyboards` come from album-specific review, **not assumed because it is trance**. Vocal tracks feature Kathy Sledge, Barbara Prunas and other voices; Emily Strode is credited as narrator. Main delivery is mostly instrumental, with some clear vocals and spoken narration. Exact vocalist Nancy Danino credit varies across accounts; avoid constructing a definitive single vocalist list.

**Production.** Miles alone is credited as **producer**, with others as recording engineers or writers. Album recorded/produced and mixed at Plus XXX Studios in Paris per sleeve reprint. Secondary account describes gathering audio samples during the *Dreamland* tour, supporting `sampling` cautiously; it does not verify particular hardware or reliably distinguish field recordings from sampled audio, so `fieldrecordings` is **left out**. Do not inherit instruments or devices from 1996 *Dreamland*.

**Themes and scales.** RA reproduces Miles's explanation of the 11 pieces depicting the human life cycle and desire to be free amid intrusive commercial expectations. AllMusic adds concern about environmental damage, supporting `social|introspection|identity`. `energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2` follow the ambient majority plus occasional dance tracks; `electronic 4` recurring synth/scapes; `mood 2` reflective tension; `theatrical 2` spoken and dramatic moments but less overt theatrics; `experimental 2` concept-album interludes beyond dance formats; `polish 4` clean orchestrated/synthetic studio finish.

**Disagreements.** AllMusic says ambient vignettes sometimes meander and vocal tracks feel pale; Tokafi and the artist present these changes as a deliberate departure from the commercial formula. RA biography contains an internally inconsistent claim of **November 1998** but MusicBrainz confirms **24 November 1997 (UK) / 1 December 1997 (Europe)** and AllMusic gives 1997. Use 1997. `sounds_like` is editorial and `influenced_by` remains blank.

## Organik (2001) — `robertmiles-organik`
**Core sources:** [AllMusic, Rick Anderson](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r532494), [Qobuz track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/organik-robert-miles/pey6q7eix3jga), [original 2001 CD credits](https://www.bibleinmylanguage.com/robert-miles-organik-s-alt-records-audio-cd-2001-saltcd001/), [Drowned in Sound, Sean Adams](https://dis11.herokuapp.com/releases/2613/reviews/1387-), [Tokafi first-person interview](https://www.tokafi.com/15questions/interview-robert-miles/), [RA biography](https://ra.co/dj/robertmiles/biography).

**Genres.** AllMusic explicitly calls the album dark and downtempo and identifies *Paths* as Indian-tinged trip-hop. Contemporary Drowned in Sound calls the album ambient/chill-out, describes a drum-and-bass **moment** in Paths and cites strings/synths/tabla. Original sleeve style identifies *breaks*; normalized here to `breakbeat` at **some_tracks**. RA contextualizes jazz players and India-derived musical traditions, flagged as jazz and world music *on select tracks/texture*. Limited remnants of trance are a minority. Phase9 identifies rock influence specifically on `Trance Shapes`.

**Instruments.** Original CD lists Robert Miles keyboards; Paul Falloon bass guitar; Bill Laswell fretless bass; Taylor Made acoustic/upright bass (Qobuz); Marque Gilmore and Trilok Gurtu drums, latter percussion; Nitin Sawhney electric and nylon guitar; Gianni Trevisan electric guitar; **Dhruba Ghosh sarangi**; and **London Session Orchestra strings** on tracks 1, 4, 6. Reviewer describes tabla. `doublebass` is Qobuz upright bass; `sarangi` and `tabla` require new instrument tokens. Their order reflects album prevalence, not the fame of a guest or the prominence of a single track. Do not assume all 12 tracks feature live orchestra or tabla.

**Production.** Miles is credited producer, engineer, keyboardist and programmer; Toni Economides *additional producer*, recorder and mixer, qualifying him for `producers` under the rule. The CD reproductions and Qobuz list track-level sample credits. `sampling` reflects sampled spoken NASA audio, original Smoke City snippets and Pianeta Terra extracts, while `fieldrecordings` reflects recordings specifically made in Tanzania and Ibiza (album liner context; secondary credits summary). Specific machines and DAW versions are not documented and are **not** filled. Acoustic/live *instrumentation* does not imply the whole album was `liverecording` in-studio; omit that token.

**Voice/language/lyrics.** *Paths*, featuring Nina Miranda, is identified by Phase9 as the sole lyrical track; a reviewer describes it as partly spoken. Other tracks are primarily instrumental. Therefore voice `instrumental|soft|spoken`; lyrical themes `introspection|identity` reflect Paths and artist statements; `en` only (do **not** infer French singing from the instrumental title Pour Te Parler). Foreign-language musical influences are not language codes. Source-reported artist-wide influences and appearance of Bill Laswell on the recording do not make him an eligible `influenced_by` entry under the user's rule: leave it empty.

**Scales.** `energy 2` dark rhythmic but not forceful; `electronic 3` programmed keys/sample processes balanced with a live band; `mood 1` dark/uneasy critical descriptors; `theatrical 2` cinematic spaciousness with few sung words; `experimental 3` structural and timbral cross-genre shifts; `polish 4` detailed studio work, orchestral recording; `tempo 2` downtempo majority; `groove 2` several rhythm-led passages but not consistently club-oriented.

**Critical disagreement.** AllMusic favours the unsettling textures but notes some improvisations do not match Release Me melodically; Drowned in Sound praises unusual instrumental collisions and rhythms while regarding the record as suitable for relaxed listening. Neither makes it an album of conventional trance anthems. Album release dates differ slightly between regional sources (AllMusic's May release vs UK 11 June 2001); both support year 2001.
