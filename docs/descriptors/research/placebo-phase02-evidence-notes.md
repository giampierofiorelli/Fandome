# Placebo — Phase 02: album-by-album evidence and editorial reasoning

Date cut-off: 27 October 2026. This file supports **only** the three Phase 02 CSV rows. Genre-evidence CSV provides one source URL and a scope per tagged genre. The numbers are calibrated editorial estimates against the Fandome scale, not measurements. A slash in a review is not a claim that a named synthesizer model was used.

## Sleeping with Ghosts (2003) — `placebo-sleepingwithghosts`

### Identity and production credits
- 2003 fourth original studio album, 12 tracks. MusicBrainz original-album CD credits name **Jim Abbiss** as producer on the songs and **Simon Barnicott** as engineer/mixer; mixing does not make the latter a producer. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/77ecdf3a-0a10-3253-b7c3-99ef2e3eca1d; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/mar/21/popandrock.artsfeatures4.
- **Instruments** `guitar|bass|drums`: the same original studio-release track credits name Brian Molko (guitar), Stefan Olsdal (bass) and Steve Hewitt (drums). `synth`: Molko explicitly talks about fusion with **vintage synthesizers** during this particular 2003 record. `harmonica` is documented for Simon Breed on *Protect Me From What I Want* (a minor instrument intentionally last). Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/77ecdf3a-0a10-3253-b7c3-99ef2e3eca1d; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **Production left EMPTY**: Molko recalls old-school analogue techniques, but does **not** unambiguously identify tape media, so `analogtape` would overstate the evidence. Nor does the source verify `synthprogramming`, `drumprogramming`, `tr808` or a particular vintage synth model. The analog approach is preserved as a sourced qualitative note. Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.

### Genres and disputes
- AllMusic: alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, pop punk, neo-glam; Shfl/Ned Raggett: alternative rock, art rock, post-punk revival, pop punk, rock. The Guardian specifically describes electronica grafted onto hard-rock beats. `electronica` is a texture and `hardrock` a limited beat/track-level element, *not* a wholesale genre for all twelve tracks. Exact tag-level citations in `genre_evidence.csv`.
- **Britpop omitted**: AllMusic’s narrative places Placebo in a Britpop-era social context, but Molko directly rejects Britpop membership; no distinct 2003 album-level Britpop tag verified. Context is distinct from genre. Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **Scales**: `energy=3`, `tempo=3` balance kinetic *The Bitter End*, *This Picture*, *Bulletproof Cupid* against *Special Needs* and *Centrefolds*; `electronic=3`, `experimental=3` based on Molko’s conscious electronics/analogue-synth blend; `mood=1` reflects regret and difficult memories; `polish=3`, `theatrical=3`, `groove=2` calibrated to professionally recorded rock with occasional electronica, not dance-first. Critics diverge: Adam Sweeting found the sound cold and gadgety, Molko considered it among their most sonically daring. Sources: https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/mar/21/popandrock.artsfeatures4; https://theshfl.com/album/Sleeping-With-Ghosts; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **Voice** powerful and soft: expressive, biting Molko vocals are emphasized in contemporaneous review alongside gentler songs; avoid an invented `falsetto` technique. **Lyrics** love, unreliable relationship memories and personal identity come directly from Molko’s interview. Sources: https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/mar/21/popandrock.artsfeatures4; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **`sounds_like`** Magazine/David Bowie from Guardian comparisons, Suede/Cure a tentative editorial timbral comparison; **`influenced_by` intentionally blank** because these comparisons do not establish specific composition-era influences.
- **`landmark=1`**: major shift toward vintage-synth rock acknowledged by the songwriter and reviewers, with high-confidence instrumentation and studio credits.

## Meds (2006) — `placebo-meds`

### Identity and production credits
- Original 13-song, 2006 studio album. **Dimitri Tikovoï** is the credited producer. Flood is credited for *mixing* on most tracks, Dave Bascombe and James Brown on others, not as co-producers. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/405cfcf8-0f5f-406e-84eb-ff8823d2579e; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/placebo/meds-album_frerfu.html; https://www.amoeba.com/ajax/album_credits.php?id=1780223.
- **Instruments**: `guitar|bass|drums|keyboards|synth|percussion` reflected in original-band performance credits (e.g. release-track metadata on Meds and contemporary review's explicit guitars and synths); **`violin|viola|cello|strings`** explicitly credited for *Space Monkey*, *Pierrot the Clown* and *Song to Say Goodbye* on the 2006 UK CD, with Fiona Brice the arranger. These are not full-album continuous orchestration. Sources: https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/placebo/meds-album_frerfu.html; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2006/mar/19/popandrock2; https://musicbrainz.org/release/405cfcf8-0f5f-406e-84eb-ff8823d2579e.
- **Production** `stringarrangements`: literal original edition credit. No `autotune`, `layeredvocals`, `drumprogramming` or `analogtape` tag: electronic sound on *Infra-Red* does not evidence a specific process, and the two guests are separate lead voices, not a vocal-stacking technique. Sources: https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/placebo/meds-album_frerfu.html; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.

### Genres and disagreements
- Aggregated album metadata calls *Meds* **alternative rock, post-punk revival, art rock**; contemporary original-CD records classify it as alternative rock. Indie-rock/neo-glam have narrower evidence from **the title-track single** on AllMusic; they are marked lower-support/selected-track context, not entire-LP tags. `poprock|rock` are broad categories from AllMusic and the original-CD listings. Avoid assigning `electroclash` just because Slant **compares the album to Peaches**, or `gothicrock` solely because a critic says there are 'goth guitars'. Sources: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/5307-placebo-meds.php; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/placebo/meds-album_frerfu.html; https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/placebo-meds/; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2006/mar/19/popandrock2.
- **Scales**: `energy=3` and `tempo=3` balance very driving *Infra-Red* with slow *Pierrot*, *Blind* and *In the Cold Light of Morning*; `electronic=2` reflects Molko's statement that *Infra-Red* is unusually electronic but the album remained band-focused; `mood=1` on psychological trauma and drug/relationship regret; `polish=3` for seasoned recording plus arranged strings; `theatrical=3` for expressive singing; `experimental=2` for relatively conventional composition; `groove=2` for a few stomping rhythms. Sources: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/; https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/placebo-meds/; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2006/mar/10/popandrock.shopping2.
- **Voice** `powerful|soft` is album-level variety; guest Alison Mosshart sings *Meds* and Michael Stipe sings *Broken Promise*, neither is a co-producer or influence. `lyrics`: Molko explicitly ties the record to psychological trauma, supported by Guardian's relationship/drugs readings. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/405cfcf8-0f5f-406e-84eb-ff8823d2579e; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2006/mar/10/popandrock.shopping2.
- **`sounds_like`** Suede/Cure/Muse editorial similarity, not a claim they directed the sessions. **`influenced_by` left blank**: no convincing original-session artist-named inspiration found. **`landmark=1`**: songwriter calls it a particularly accomplished, confessional fifth record; last with drummer Hewitt.

## Battle for the Sun (2009) — `placebo-battleforthesun`

### Identity, instrumentation and production
- Sixth full original studio album, first studio record featuring drummer **Steve Forrest**. Original credited as Placebo alone, despite guest musicians. Album principally recorded in Toronto at Metalworks; with additional studio overdubs in London. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/cdb4318d-e7ff-4a88-98a2-cf7da8d99bad; https://deploy.musik-sammler.de/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun-cd-dvd-365392/; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **Producers:** **David Bottrill** is credited throughout, with **Dimitri Tikovoï** additionally credited as co-producer on **track 13, *Kings of Medicine*, only**. Do not confuse **Alan Moulder** and **Dave Bascombe**, credited as mixers, or James Brown, the engineer, with producers. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/cdb4318d-e7ff-4a88-98a2-cf7da8d99bad; https://deploy.musik-sammler.de/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun-cd-dvd-365392/.
- **Instruments** `guitar|bass|drums|keyboards|synth|percussion` supported by band personnel (Molko/Olsdal guitars, Olsdal bass, Forrest drums/percussion), Bill Lloyd keyboard programming and reviews of analog/digital electronic textures. `strings|violin|viola|cello` supported by credited live string players, chiefly on four album songs; `brass|trumpet|trombone|saxophone|flute` supported by credited Toronto players and horn passages, mostly *For What It’s Worth*, *The Never-Ending Why*, *Kings of Medicine*. Sources: https://theshoprecords.com/en/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun; https://deploy.musik-sammler.de/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun-cd-dvd-365392/; https://musicbrainz.org/release/cdb4318d-e7ff-4a88-98a2-cf7da8d99bad; https://www.allmusic.com/album/battle-for-the-sun-mw0000815680.
- **Production** `stringarrangements` by Fiona Brice; `hornarrangements` by Peter Cardinali; `programming` for Bill Lloyd’s specifically credited keyboards; `distortion` AllMusic explicitly reports digital distortion across drum/keyboard/guitar arrangement. **Exact machines unknown**, so do not invent `protools`, `logic`, Moog or specific keyboard models. Sources: https://deploy.musik-sammler.de/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun-cd-dvd-365392/; https://musicbrainz.org/release/cdb4318d-e7ff-4a88-98a2-cf7da8d99bad; https://www.allmusic.com/album/battle-for-the-sun-mw0000815680.
- **Instrument conflict:** some transcriptions name Steve McDade on **piccolo trumpet**, others ambiguously label his part **piccolo** or **piccolo flute**. The reliable common denominator is **trumpet**; do **not** create a new `piccolo`/`piccolotrumpet` token until a booklet image resolves it. It is not a production machine. Sources: https://theshoprecords.com/en/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun; https://musicbrainz.org/release/cdb4318d-e7ff-4a88-98a2-cf7da8d99bad; https://deploy.musik-sammler.de/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun-cd-dvd-365392/.

### Genre evidence and critical divergence
- AllMusic: alternative indie rock, alternative pop/rock, neo-glam, pop punk and **Punk Revival**, plus **Britpop** (disputed in context). Because Fandome does not currently have an exact `punkrevival` token, register it as a new genre, **not** as post-punk revival or punk rock. NME specifically calls *Kings of Medicine* synthetic Britpop and *For What It's Worth* Dixie-rock; the latter requires new `dixierock` token. Guardian mentions a mariachi-flavoured close to *The Never-Ending Why*; `mariachi` is a **texture only**. These track-local tags go last, and must not dominate ranking. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/battle-for-the-sun-mw0000815680; https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-placebo-10534-318741; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/jun/07/placebo-battle-for-the-sun.
- **Britpop is contested**: Molko has rejected Britpop as the band's genre affiliation; NME's 2009 Britpop description is **one track**. Store it as track-specific at the end, not a core album genre. Source: https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-placebo-10534-318741; https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/.
- **Scales**: `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `theatrical=4` for sonically heavier, anthemic drum/guitar programme and prominent arrangements; `electronic=2` for synth/programmed keys within principally live rock; `mood=2` for increased optimism compared with *Meds* but persistent lyrical discontent; `experimental=2` for expanded instrumentation within familiar song forms; `polish=4` for large-studio multi-location sessions and muscular mix; `groove=2` because rhythmically firm yet rarely dance-first. Sources: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/; https://www.allmusic.com/album/battle-for-the-sun-mw0000815680; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/jun/07/placebo-battle-for-the-sun.
- **Lyrics/voice**: Molko describes the 2009 album as relatively optimistic after personal change, not uniformly sunny. Guardian heard harsh melodrama alongside affirmative attempts; NME criticised attempted originality and compared the band with Bowie, Suede, Muse and Primal Scream. `sounds_like` lists references as *comparisons only*. `influenced_by` remains blank: choosing Tool's producer does **not** make Tool an album influence; the shop's unspecified claim of PJ Harvey / My Bloody Valentine inspiration lacks a traceable musician quotation/qualified analysis. Sources: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/; https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-placebo-10534-318741; https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/jun/07/placebo-battle-for-the-sun; https://theshoprecords.com/en/release/placebo-battle-for-the-sun.
- **`landmark=1`**: the lineup break and heavier direction were explicitly identified by Molko as an identity reset for Placebo.

### Cross-phase exclusions
- Phase 01 songs do **not** recur here. *Sleeping with Ghosts & Covers* two-disc version includes separately recorded old covers and is NOT a second studio album; the user excludes reissues and live compilations. *MTV Unplugged* is a live album and deliberately not added, however Molko favours it. *Once More with Feeling* is a singles compilation and excluded. Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/rank-your-records-brian-molko-skeptically-rates-placebos-eight-lps/; original track-list and catalogue audit.
