**Scope:** original studio albums only. Source priority: official Dire Straits original album credits > MusicBrainz structured credits > specialist album reviews > album databases / contextual biographies. Direct interview evidence is kept separate from comparisons. ## Dire Straits (1978)

- **Scope/producers:** nine tracks, early original LP rather than the original Sultans single. Muff Winwood, produced; Rhett Davies, engineer; Basing Street Studios, London (official discography).
- **Genres:** bluesrock and rootsrock dominate. `rock`, `albumrock`, `rockandroll` are broader specialist indexing, not equally weighted styles. `softrock` from MusicBrainz tag; `countryrock` reflects country inflections discussed in AllMusic, less direct than the first four.
- **Scales:** energy 3 (intermittent drive rather than constant loudness), electronic 0 (traditional band instrumentation, not merely electric guitar), mood 2 (wistful observational stories), theatrical 1 (understated), experimental 1 (conventional songs and clean interplay), polish 3 (professional but airy), tempo 3 (midtempo with swing), groove 3 (tight small-band shuffles).
- **Voice:** Mark's spoken-adjacent rhythmic delivery and grainy low singing; `spoken` denotes delivery and must **not** imply wordless instrumental content.
- **Instruments:** official credits establish two guitars, bass, drums. The recording history identifies the resonator guitar on Water of Love / Wild West End and David's acoustic guitar; these are **specific-track** additions, not present continuously (secondary discography source; verify against original liner if available). No keyboard or synthesizer credited.
- **Production:** blank: lack of documented processing doesn't justify assigning analogtape, reverb or one-take. AllMusic reports lean production with little gratuitous overdubbing; this is not a verified technical session workflow.
- **Lyrics:** Sultans of Swing and Down to the Waterline support working-life narrative / observation; Water of Love and Wild West End support love and longing.
- **Comparison, not proven individual-album influence:** J.J. Cale, Bob Dylan, Clapton and the Shadows based on roots guitar and vocal narrative comparison. Direct general musician influence evidence sits in CLAUDE_IMPORT, not album `influenced_by`.

## Communiqué (1979)

- **Credits:** Wexler and Beckett are both producers; *B. Bear* keyboards = Beckett. Recorded December 1978 in Nassau and mixed January 1979 at Muscle Shoals. Do not describe this as recorded at Muscle Shoals.
- **Genres:** AllMusic hears J.J. Cale-like shuffles and bluesy playing. MusicBrainz independently identifies country rock, rock and pop; more general album-rock indexing also applies. `rootsrock` is a cautious tag for continuing the band's previous roots orientation, while `poprock` translates a broad MusicBrainz `pop` label in a clearly guitar-rock context; validate genre strength before expanding the ontology.
- **Scales:** energy 2 (largely relaxed), electronic 0 (no documented synthesizers), mood 2 (reflective), theatrical 1, experimental 1, polish 3 (Wexler/Beckett studio), tempo 3, groove 3 (shuffles and lightly syncopated guitar).
- **Instruments:** dual guitars, bass and drum kit from credits; Barry Beckett's keyboards are an additional confirmed instrument. Do not add Hammond organ or piano as a specific model without track-level confirmation.
- **Production:** empty because studio / location alone do not establish signal processing or analogtape token.
- **Lyrics:** Once Upon a Time in the West and Communiqué are satirical/social; Where Do You Think You're Going and Lady Writer supply intimacy and heartbreak.
- **Comparisons:** resemblance to the debut and to J.J. Cale are critics' claims; all in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`.

## Making Movies (1980)

- **Credits:** Jimmy Iovine and Mark Knopfler as producers, Shelly Yakus engineer, Roy Bittan keyboards, band vocals/guitar/bass/drums. David Knopfler left and is **not** on the official album performance credits. Mark is guitarist and singer; Bittan's E Street Band association is a comparison with Springsteen, not evidence of Springsteen producing or playing on this album.
- **Genres:** AllMusic directly calls it a jazzier variety of country-rock and folk-rock, describing a *new wave-tinged pop production*. The latter is tagged as `texture` (rank 8), not an assertion the record is new wave throughout. A secondary album overview calls it heartland rock; this is treated medium-strength (rank 1). Album rock and rock/roll in AllMusic styles.
- **Scales:** energy 4 (stronger full-band drive), electronic 0 (credited piano/keys do not prove synths), mood 3 (alternating exhilaration and melancholy), theatrical 3 (lengthier narrative episodes), experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3.
- **Instruments:** guitars, bass, drums, Bittan keyboards, piano-led openings; the resonator guitar in Romeo and Juliet is supported by a guitar-specific account only, and marked specific-track. If a primary gear source contradicts it, retain just `guitar` until resolved.
- **Production:** deliberately empty: new-wave *inflection* isn't evidence of particular electronic equipment or processing. No invented synth or sampled-drum model.
- **Lyrics:** Romeo and Juliet, Tunnel of Love and Skateaway are love / memory / human stories; Les Boys is theatrical social observation. The Carousel Waltz at the opening is a musical quote, excluded from `influenced_by`.
- **Comparisons:** Bruce Springsteen (heartland-rock scale and Bittan keyboard), Dylan (narrative voice), J.J. Cale (fingerpicked understatement) are editorial sonic links, not collaborators or sourced album-specific declared inspirations.

## Love over Gold (1982)

- **Credits:** Knopfler producer, Neil Dorfsman recording engineer; Hal Lindes guitar, Alan Clark keyboards, John Illsley bass, Pick Withers drums, Mainieri vibes/marimba, Ed Walsh synthesizer programming (official discography and MusicBrainz). No Guy Fletcher or Dire Straits co-producer credit on this 1982 LP.
- **Genres:** `prog` directly backed by AllMusic's progressive-rock description and 14-minute Telegraph Road; `artrock` from Album of the Year listing; `poprock` and `rock` from MusicBrainz. `bluesrock` is a secondary legacy sound discussed by AllMusic. `softrock` weak atmospheric tag from retrospective, and is intentionally last/low confidence in genre-evidence table.
- **Scales:** energy 3 (contrasts build to climaxes), electronic 1 (synth programming supplements live players), mood 2 (investigative and melancholy), theatrical 4 (long, dramatic suites), experimental 4 (extended forms and suspense), polish 5 (highly detailed arrangements), tempo 2 (long stretches deliberately slow), groove 2 (less dance-oriented than guitar shuffles).
- **Instruments:** official track-level list supports guitars, bass, drums, keys, Ed Walsh synths, Mainieri vibraphone/marimba. Official guitars note acoustic Ovation and resonator National among many guitar types; these are present on selected tracks only. The per-track MusicBrainz credits also locate Mainieri on two pieces, not uniformly across the album.
- **Production:** `synthprogramming` is directly credited. Reissues mention digital mastering or a digital recording note, but these do not reliably establish an original 1982 digital multitrack workflow. No `digitalrecording`, `digitaltape`, `analogtape` or specific synthesizer model added.
- **Lyrics:** Telegraph Road follows industrial development / decay; Private Investigations the disillusioned investigator; Industrial Disease satirical workplace ailments; title track vulnerability; It Never Rains romantic disappointment. `irony` applies especially to Industrial Disease.
- **Comparisons:** Pink Floyd for cinematic progressive dynamics, J.J. Cale for understated guitar and Alan Parsons Project for careful studio atmospheres are editorial comparisons only.

## Source strength and limitations

- `MusicBrainz` genre tags and `Album of the Year` genre labels are editorial/community classifications, medium evidence; the certified original credits and named specialist reviews are the primary evidence.
- `sounds_like` contains informed editorial similarity, **not artist-declared influence**. `influenced_by` stays empty in all four rows.
- Overall `confidence=high` signals dependable dates, musicians, producers, and several convergent style sources; individual genre labels with weak links are explicitly marked medium/low.
- Fandome reviewer should recheck any token supported only via a genre inference rather than explicit written classification before applying a strict no-inference import policy.
