# Bronski Beat — Fandome research notes, phase 01

Research cutoff: 27 October 2026. One row per ORIGINAL release, not per reissue, bonus disc or single. All rows remain `draft`. The eight numeric scales are original independent whole-album editorial estimates (not third-party ratings). Citations are recorded in the companion sources and genre-evidence CSVs. The artist spans different lead singers; vocal descriptions are album-specific.

## The Age of Consent (1984) — `bronskibeat-theageofconsent`

**Edition and identity.** Base the sound on the original 1984 ten-track British LP (Forbidden Fruit BITLP 1), ending in the medley *I Feel Love/Johnny Remember Me*. The better-known 1985 Marc Almond duet/extended single and archival anniversary mixes are distinct recordings and MUST NOT be silently substituted. Original vocal leads are by **Jimmy Somerville**, not John Foster/Jon Jon, Jonathan Hellyer or Stephen Granville. The LP producer credit belongs to **Mike Thorne**. [London Records; original LP tracklist; MusicBrainz]

**Genre selection.** AllMusic's direct album styles list Synth Pop, Hi-NRG, Dance-Pop, New Wave, Alternative Dance, Club/Dance. 'Disco' is independently supported by the period's Donna Summer/Moroder rhythmic model and the actual *I Feel Love* recording. Bronski Beat were an electronic band, though not a totally synthesized one. These styles are not collapsed into one unhelpful generic `pop` tag. Higher-priority electronic genres precede broader parent `pop`/`electronic`.

**Instruments / production.** Original LP personnel: Bronski and Larry Steinbachek keyboards and percussion; Pink Singers choir; horn players including Arno Hecht (clarinet/tenor sax), Crispin Cioe (sax), Paul Litteral (trumpet) and Bob Funk (trombone); Johnny Folarin congas; multiple credited cellists. Jimmy Somerville later explicitly affirmed that Bronski Beat combined real horn, real string and choir recordings with synthesizers. A contemporaneous 1986 Larry Steinbachek interview specifically says the **DX7 and Memorymoog were heavily used recording the debut**. This justifies `dx7|memorymoog`; avoids invented Fairlight, TR-808 or LinnDrum claims. Synthetic percussion, sequencing and the documented machine approach support `synthprogramming|drumprogramming`. Choir goes in `voice=...|choral`, not in the musical instruments field. No invented tape/console brands.

**Scales.** Energy 4 and groove 4 account for 'Why?', 'Smalltown Boy' and the medley, with mid-tempo narrative material keeping tempo at 4 not 5. Electronic 4 rather than 5 respects acoustic cello/horns/percussion. Mood 2 comes from political anger, exile, alienation and precarious love despite danceable surfaces. Theatrical 4 reflects Somerville's emphatic high-register dynamics; experimental 3 reflects queer protest/pop juxtaposition and Gershwin/medley treatments. Polish 4 reflects Mike Thorne's well-defined original studio sound; voice `powerful|clear|choral` plus `falsetto` production token deliberately separates vocal timbre from exact register.

**Documented influences.** Somerville's direct Quietus interview, in answer to what shaped early Bronski Beat, names dance music, Talking Heads, Simple Minds and Soft Cell; DJ Mag establishes Donna Summer/Giorgio Moroder's strong formative synth-disco role. These are the only album-level `influenced_by` names. A song **covered** (George Gershwin, Joe Meek composition, Donna Summer arrangement) does not **by itself** confer influence credit; Donna Summer/Moroder here stand on independent biographical testimony, not merely a cover. Sylvester, Yazoo and others are **comparisons** only in `sounds_like`. Direct group-level sources are listed in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`.

## Hundreds & Thousands (1985) — `bronskibeat-hundredsthousands`

**Why included / classification.** This is the group's significant original 1985 **remix album**, sometimes called a six-track remix EP by critics. Its actual September 1985 UK LP has exactly six tracks: 1984-album remixes of 'Heatwave', 'Why?', 'Smalltown Boy' and 'Junk' PLUS then non-LP 'Run from Love' and 'Hard Rain'. The 1985 CD adds B-sides and the Marc Almond collaboration medley; later reissues bring further mixes. Treat the LP, not the expanded edition, as the core release. Bronski Beat's label currently categorises it as an album and MusicBrainz calls it 'Album + Remix', so `type=remix` is better than either `studio` or `compilation`. Explicitly **do not duplicate** *The Age of Consent*'s row: this is separately extended dance production and two additional original songs. [Swedishcharts original LP; label Bandcamp; MusicBrainz; Second Disc]

**Genres and sound.** The rights-holder's release/AllMusic classify a hi-NRG, synth-pop, club/dance, dance-pop sound; longer instrumental breaks and bigger rhythm emphases justify groove 5, tempo 4. Mood 2 is driven by the unchanged protest/rejection words of 'Why?' and 'Smalltown Boy' as well as romantic songs; do not read all lyrics as happy simply because the mix is dance-oriented. Theatrical 4 reflects Somerville's soaring sustained vocal phrases. Experimental 3 captures the remixes' dramatic extensions rather than wholly experimental composition. Electronics 4 acknowledges the horn overdubs explicitly reported by Trouser Press.

**Instruments and producer.** The original LP sleeve establishes Mike Thorne as credited producer. **Harvey Goldberg** and **Dominick Maita** are mixer/remix names, NOT an additional row's `producers`; the latter spelling appears elsewhere as Maita/Meita and should be reviewed before creating a separate producer entity. Trouser Press notes newly added brass/horns; the 1985 LP credits record additional woodwinds/sax/trombone. Mark `remixing|hornarrangements`, and reuse synth/drum programming supported by the original tracks; do not invent a particular sequencer or drum-machine model for new remixes. The earliest LP contains no new lead singer; this is the last extended album statement with **Jimmy Somerville's recorded voice**.

**Influences.** Because this release repurposes prior musical recordings and does not add a new independently documented songwriting influence, `influenced_by` is empty; the group-wide documented inspirations in the import instructions remain applicable to the artist.

## Truthdare Doubledare (1986) — `bronskibeat-truthdaredoubledare`

**Lineup / producer / credits.** This is the first full Bronski Beat studio album with **John Foster (John Jon, credited Jøn)** rather than Jimmy Somerville. Original 1986 LP contains nine tracks; the commonly streamed later 11-track versions add 12-inch versions of singles and must not be treated as fresh album composition. **Adam Williams** is producer and bassist. **Gareth Jones** is a mixing engineer; **Tom Frederikse** is an engineer. Neither becomes `producers`. Credits name Steve Bronski (synth, piano, guitars, piano accordion, programming), Larry Steinbachek (keys, marimba, drums, sequencing and percussion), the **Kick Horns** (arrangements/performance), and players on saxophones, trombone, trumpet, flugelhorn, flute, guitar, bass and violin. [Original LP / MusicBrainz / AllMusic]

**Production equipment verified by contemporary firsthand reporting.** Larry Steinbachek's February 1986 *Sound On Sound* interview directly names their Yamaha **QX1** sequencer and **TX816** rack, Yamaha DX7, Memorymoog, LinnDrum and Roland TR-707 in band production; he explains building arrangements by programming and moving sessions into a recording studio. In the same period, *Electronics & Music Maker* corroborates the setup. The source is a **1986 band-production interview**, not a full per-track session log; the exact unit-to-track allocation remains unproven. QX1 and TX816 require new production tokens `yamahaqx1` and `yamahatx816`, identified in CLAUDE_IMPORT.md; `dx7|memorymoog|linndrum|tr707` already exist. Explicit acoustic piano, live drums, accordions and brass prevent an erroneous electronic=5. The new album is still electronically dominant, so electronic=4.

**Genre / scales.** Core tags are synth-pop, hi-NRG and dance-pop/club-dance (AllMusic). The original German LP also carries *Pop Rock* as a separate pressing style; retain lower-priority `poprock` alongside the exact AllMusic label `contemporarypoprock`. Energy 4/groove 5 reflect dance momentum in 'Hit That Perfect Beat', 'C'mon! C'mon!' and the title track. Mood 3 rather than 5 is due to 'Dr John' (AIDS), 'We Know How It Feels' and 'Punishment for Love' (social pressure), contrasted with playful dance tracks. The voice is clearer/less extreme than Somerville's and backing vocal ensembles are documented; `clear|powerful|choral` is intended. Experimental 3 reflects non-trivial instrumentation inside commercial structures. Polish 4 reflects well-defined 1980s studio arrangement.

**Similarity is not influence.** Dead or Alive, Erasure, Hazell Dean etc. are editorial comparison targets, not stated influences. No verifiable first-person album-specific named musical influences surfaced for this particular lineup, so `influenced_by` remains blank. Avoid recording direct band member/producer Adam Williams as an influence.

## Rainbow Nation (1995) — `bronskibeat-rainbownation`

**Lineup and edition.** Original ZYX 1995 studio album, **Jonathan Hellyer** lead vocals, rather than Somerville/Foster; **Steve Bronski** is the credited producer. **Ian Donaldson** credited keyboards/programming/sequencing; Steve Bronski keys, acoustic guitar, sequencing and backing vocals; **F. Serra** acoustic/electric guitar, programming and backing vocals; **Stefano Menato** saxophone; Hellyer vocals, finger snaps, sequencing. Record made at **Factory Sound Studios, Verona**, per AllMusic. There is no proof on the album credit list that either F. Serra or Donaldson is a co-producer: don't promote instrumental/programming credits to production credits.

**Edition / reinterpretations.** Original AllMusic tracklist consists of 'Hit That Perfect Beat', 'Slow Down', 'Kicking Up the Rain', 'Forever and a Day', Alicia Bridges cover 'I Love the Nightlife', 'Tell Me Your Name', a reworking of 'Why?', 'Taste Those Tears', 'Be Serious', 'Eastern Eyes', and 'No Difference'. Some country editions add 'Smalltown Boy '94 (Acoustic Radio Edit)' or use 'Rainbow Nation' as a later title rendering. Fandome must not mistake those cover/rework tracks for a *greatest-hits compilation*: the 1995 project is largely new studio recording and is a standalone studio album.

**Genre / evaluation.** AllMusic explicitly lists Club/Dance, Dance-Pop, Hi-NRG, New Wave, Synth Pop, Contemporary Pop/Rock. Its review describes Eurodisco sonics modelled on Giorgio Moroder, with churning sequencers and hard dance bass. This directly supports `eurodisco`; the Alicia Bridges cover provides track-level `disco`. AllMusic's generic Pop/Rock sits lower than the album's actual dance/electronic styles. Energy 4 / groove 5 / tempo 4 follows the thumping dance-driven material; mood 3 marks both celebration and lost-love/identity lyrics. Electronic 4 reflects guitar and sax. Theatrical 3 / experimental 2 / polish 4 describe a professionally streamlined 1990s sound rather than 1984's unusual stylistic rupture.

**Influence audit.** The AllMusic writer's 'Moroder-influenced' phrasing is **critical interpretation**, not a musician's or producer's direct testimony. **Do NOT set Giorgio Moroder in `influenced_by` for Rainbow Nation** solely for that sentence; keep the comparison in `sounds_like`. The 1995 personnel do not inherit a documented album-level artist influence from Jimmy Somerville, who is not the singer on this record. Empty `influenced_by` is therefore deliberate.

## Outstanding catalogue and research rules

- In the checked branch `src/data/catalog.ts`, Bronski Beat `bronskibeat` already lists only the 1984 and 1986 studio albums. No `bronskibeat.csv` exists in descriptors, and the artist was not found in `seed.ts`. Hence all four rows are new descriptors; **two catalogue records must be created** for this phase (1985 remix and 1995 studio).
- *Out & About* was **recorded in 1987, not formally issued at that time**. Sources differ on whether Steve Bronski put it online in 2016 or 2017; The Independent says online 2016, while Southbank Centre recalls 2017. Reserve `bronskibeat-outabout` for phase 02 **after** verifying a primary dated download listing; its eventual `year` should be the first verified public release, not automatically 1987. Do not fabricate a 1987 album release date.
- *The Age of Reason* (2017) is a **new studio re-recording** of much of *The Age of Consent*, with vocalist Stephen Granville and fresh material (not the Somerville master). It is suitable for phase 02 as a separate studio album, while its deluxe remix disc should not be used as the main album evidence.
- *The Age of Remix* (2018), *Forbidden Fruit – The Age of Consent Remixed* (2025) and anniversary archival packages (2024, 2026) remain excluded because they are later remix/reissues of these recordings and do not currently clear the 'important independent remix record' bar. Similarly, 1993 *The Album* appears in some metadata but needs edition-level checking rather than automatic admission as a fifth studio record.
