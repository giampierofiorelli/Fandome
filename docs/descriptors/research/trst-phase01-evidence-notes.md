# TR/ST — Phase 01: album-level evidence notes

**Research freeze: 25 October 2026.** Fandome artist ID `trst`, branch `descriptors`. No `docs/descriptors/trst.csv` existed when inspected. Four studio LPs selected chronologically, regardless of subsequent combined reissue. Scores are original editorial 0–5 judgments calibrated to the *guide's* anchors, not critic scores. All `influenced_by` are documented artist remarks, whereas `sounds_like` are comparisons, not proof of borrowing. `voice` maps descriptive vocals to the guide's limited allowed tokens; there is no `baritone` or `falsetto` token.

## `trst-trst` — TRST (2012)

**Original issue and personnel.** Fandome currently calls this catalogue entry *TR/ST* (artist styling), but the official original 2012 LP title is ***TRST***; update the display title without changing `trst-trst` [T12-PITCH, T12-QOBUZ]. Pitchfork dated its contemporary review 28 February 2012 [T12-PITCH], corroborating the February 2012 original issue; AllMusic's October issue date and Qobuz's November digital listing reflect other editions [T12-ALL, T12-QOBUZ]. Original 11 songs from *Shoom* through *Sulk*, rather than the Qobuz 13-track digital variant with *The Dazzle* and a remix. The Qobuz track-by-track producer credits establish **Robert Alfons and Maya Postepski** on the original recordings [T12-QOBUZ]. Damian Taylor is a mixer, not a producer; Emily Lazar and Heba Kadry mastering only.

**Genres:** Synth-pop, coldwave/darkwave, electronics, and electropop are central [T12-PITCH, T12-RA, T12-QOBUZ]. Producer Alfons calls the record pop [T12-MUSICOMH]. Resident Advisor lists experimental pop and notes *This Ready Flesh* as an **electroclash-like track**, while The Music names **industrial disco** for that track and a trance/dark-rave aesthetic; Soundvenue hears four-to-the-floor techno patterns [T12-RA, T12-MUSIC, T12-SOUNDVENUE]. `disco`, `electroclash`, `trance`, `rave`, `techno` are accordingly **limited scope**, not elevated to album dominance. `ebm` reflects the EBM vocabulary the artist discussed with Time Out Barcelona [T12-TIMEOUT] and machine-dance beats; more uncertain than synth-pop [T12-TIMEOUT]. `indieelectronic` is AllMusic metadata, not an alternative-rock band claim.

**Eight scales:** `energy 4` and `tempo 4` reflect forceful programmed beats on multiple tracks but recurrent drawn-out narcotic sections, below all-out extreme [T12-PITCH, T12-SOUNDVENUE]. `electronic 5`: synths and digital rhythms dominate; credits and critical accounts do not identify a meaningful acoustic arrangement share [T12-QOBUZ, T12-MUSIC]. `mood 1` from criticism describing claustrophobia/desolation, although *Sulk* has euphoric moments [T12-DAZED, T12-SOUNDVENUE]. `theatrical 3` for performative, shape-shifting vocal identities without a rock-opera scale [T12-PITCH]. `experimental 3` from pitched-vocal strangeness and unusual synth contours within pop forms [T12-RA, T12-PITCH]. `polish 4`: Pitchfork's glossy, room-filling production contrasts with a dark/degraded aesthetic; **lo-fi aesthetics do not mean the recording is technically raw** [T12-PITCH]. `groove 4`: recurrent club rhythms but alienated/listening music also [T12-MUSIC].

**Voice/lyrics:** `raspy|soft` codes Alfons' nasal/rough delivery and Postepski's sparse vocal contributions [T12-PITCH]; the tokens cannot capture his deep register. `sensuality|introspection|identity` is supported by the artist's control/isolation remarks and critics' sexual/withdrawn imagery [T12-DAZED, T12-SOUNDVENUE]. `language=en`. **No track-level speculative narrative.**

**Influenced by:** Alfons directly calls Ace of Base, Aphex Twin and Cocteau Twins formative in an album-era interview [T12-MUSICOMH]; the interview does not prove direct derivation of each track, so notes label these *artist-reported formative influences during the debut era*. Other names in `sounds_like` are Pitchfork and The Music critic comparisons and not converted to claimed influence [T12-PITCH, T12-MUSIC]. Pitchfork was positive about the darkness and vocals, Resident Advisor was more skeptical about revivalism [T12-RA]. Landmark `1`, confidence `high` (release, producers and central sound independently supported; peripheral genre evidence varies).

## `trst-joyland` — Joyland (2014)

**Original issue and production:** Original Arts & Crafts 11-track March 4 2014 release [J14-APPLE, J14-ALL]. **Robert Alfons alone** is credited producer; Maya Postepski's departure preceded this album and must not be carried into production credits [J14-ALL, J14-EXCLAIM]. Interview with Alfons clarifies that he recorded his own apparently female-sounding high-register vocals [J14-DUBLIN]; despite Apple's editorial description of effects, the artist says no digital voice treatment, so do not assert high-pitched singing is a separate female singer or necessarily pitch-shifted.

**Genres:** The central synthesis is `synthpop|darkwave|electronic|electropop`, supported by Apple, Pitchfork and artist interviews. The Skinny explicitly calls out `ebm` for *Icabod* and *Four Gut*, dark `house`, `techno`, early `rave`, `trance`, `eurodance` textures [J14-SKINNY]. `coldwave` is the Pitchfork critic's project-level nomenclature; it remains less specific to this album. `indieelectronic` is an AllMusic catalogue classification. `acidhouse` is **not** included merely because Alfons mentions acid house when explaining the *title*: a name association is not sufficient sonic evidence [J14-CULT]. `futurepop` is reported by secondary encyclopedic listings but lacked a comparably grounded independent source at this research cut; **not imported** pending corroboration.

**Eight scales:** `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4` because of dominant club momentum (e.g. *Geryon*, *Icabod*) tempered by *Slightly Floating* and patient transitions [J14-SKINNY, J14-PITCH]. `electronic=5` from synth and sequencer construction [J14-APPLE]. `mood=2` is still ominous but with more melodic lift than TRST [J14-EXEV, J14-EXCLAIM]. `theatrical=4` because Alfons plays dramatically different vocal characters and the album emphasizes widescreen dramatic hooks [J14-DUBLIN, J14-SKINNY]. `experimental=3` for volatile synth timbres and vocal shapes in comparatively direct songwriting [J14-PITCH]. `polish=4` from reviewers' explicit sleek production and huge studio sheen; stylistic grime does not mean poor recording [J14-SKINNY, J14-PITCH].

**Voice/words:** `raspy|soft|clear`: the first two model rough low register and hushed melodic intimacy; `clear` represents clean high register on several songs, **not a second vocalist** [J14-DUBLIN]. `sensuality|introspection|love|heartbreak|identity` from Alfons' statements that the songs are sensual/emotional and Vice's album interviews citing heartbreak [J14-VICE]. The words are often intentionally obscure, so exact lyric meanings can be uncertain [J14-DUBLIN]. English only.

**Influences vs comparisons:** Alfons directly describes **Kate Bush and Elizabeth Fraser/Cocteau Twins** as singing influences in a *Joyland* interview [J14-DUBLIN]; no generic influence claimed from Postepski or similar bands. Apple's list of Bauhaus, Sisters of Mercy, KMFDM, early Ministry belongs in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by` [J14-APPLE]. Some critics praise breadth [J14-SKINNY]; Pitchfork hears less development and weaker hooks [J14-PITCH]. Landmark `1`; high confidence.

## `trst-thedestroyer1` — The Destroyer – 1 (2019)

**Original version:** Original eight tracks, 19 April 2019 [D1-ARTIST, D1-QOBUZ]; album title from *current Fandome catalogue* is **The Destroyer – 1**. Streaming names 'Part One' or 'Vol. 1' are **aliases**, not separate albums. **Robert Alfons** produced throughout. **Maya Postepski** co-produced *Colossal, Unbleached, Grouch, Poorly Coward, Wake With* (five), while **Lars Stalfors** co-produced *Gone* and *Control Me* [D1-QOBUZ]. A contemporary Exclaim review says six Postepski tracks, conflicting with per-track label credits; favor those five listed in source. **Damian Taylor** is credited **additional production and mixing** of *Bicep*, not co-producer, so omitted from the `producers` field. **Lia Braswell** plays live drums on *Bicep*, not a co-producer.

**Genres:** Official Bandcamp tags synthpop, dark wave, electropop, dance, electronic and indie [D1-ARTIST]. AllMusic specifies `industrialdance|indieelectronic`; Exclaim identifies post-punk, dream pop and industrial strains [D1-ALL, D1-EXCLAIM]. *Grouch* is said to have a house groove by a specialist writer [D1-SYNTH], so `house` is **one-song/texture only**. `coldwave` appears as the comparison frame in signed reviews [D1-ATWOOD]. Contextual label genres do not imply all 8 tracks are strictly dance, post-punk, or indie rock.

**Eight scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` because *Bicep* and *Grouch* supply powerful club material, while *Gone* and *Control Me* deliberately slow and soften the record [D1-EXCLAIM]. `electronic=4` rather than 5 from synth dominance **with verified live drums** [D1-QOBUZ]. `mood=2` because grief, isolation and shame predominate, though critics note patches of warmth [D1-ATWOOD, D1-EXCLAIM]. `theatrical=3` for dramatic but more restrained vocal arrangements; `experimental=3` for shifts among sparse ballad and clanging dance music [D1-SLUG]; `polish=4` from precise layered production [D1-EXCLAIM].

**Voice/themes:** `raspy|soft|clear` approximates alternate growling/abrasive and unusually exposed melodic registers [D1-TREBLE, D1-EXCLAIM]. `introspection|identity|sensuality|heartbreak` draws from Alfons's accounts of trauma and alienation and critically discussed sexual identity and intimacy; the material is personal but lyrics are not a literal biography [D1-OUT, D1-ATWOOD]. English vocals. **Pet Shop Boys** in `influenced_by` is strictly anchored to *Grouch*, for which Alfons explicitly names the album *Very* as direct inspiration [D1-OUT]. The Soft Moon, Silent Servant etc. in `sounds_like` are comparisons by critics; no borrowed authorship asserted. Critics diverge on danceability, Exclaim saying *less* constant groove while other dance-focused writers emphasize energy; the scores follow album mix, not just *Bicep*. Landmark `1` as a major reunion/comeback; high confidence.

## `trst-thedestroyer2` — The Destroyer – 2 (2019)

**Original and credits:** Eight-track Nov 1 2019 official edition [D2-ARTIST]. All tracks produced by Robert Alfons. Bandcamp specifies Lars Stalfors co-produced *Enduring Chill, cor, Shame* and additional production on the final track, while Maya Postepski co-produced the closing **Slow Burn** only [D2-ARTIST]. The official prose erroneously calls this song *This Slow*, conflicting with the **actual 8-track original running order** and premiere coverage [D2-ARTIST, D2-FLAUNT]; preserve *Slow Burn* and flag it for second review. **Damian Taylor** additional production on *Iris, Darling, Destroyer, The Stain* but not formally credited as producer/co-producer, hence excluded from `producers`; Taylor also mixes these songs. **Lia Braswell** plays live drums on the same four, not producer.

**Genres:** Main `synthpop|darkwave|electronic|electropop`, specialist and official tags [D2-ARTIST, D2-ALL]. `industrialdance` on AllMusic is less expressive of this *quieter* half than Volume 1. Dais specifically notes `dreampop` and `ambient` approaches, substantiated by critical pieces [D2-DAIS, D2-POSTPUNK]. `indie` from label's Bandcamp tag is broad and uncertain; keep peripheral. `dance` belongs to intermittent *Iris* and other rhythm-heavy passages, not the entire record. A critic specifically hears Vangelis-like keys in the title song [D2-POSTPUNK] (sonic similarity, not attributed influence).

**Eight scales:** `energy 2`, `tempo 2`, `groove 2`: compared with Vol. 1, slower pace, ambient openings and piano-led ballads outweigh urgent club tracks [D2-VANCOUVER, D2-EXCLAIM]. `electronic 4`, **not 5**, because four songs credit live drums plus significant piano, while synths remain fundamental [D2-ARTIST]. `mood 1` for bleak loss and shame, even though tender moments provide counterpoints [D2-ALL]. `theatrical 3` and `experimental 3` for cavernous arrangement shifts and dramatic register without theatrical spectacle [D2-NORMAN]. `polish 4`: refined, reverberant production [D2-NORMAN]. Voice `soft|raspy|clear`: exposed tenderness foregrounded over previous distorted delivery, with some clearer singing [D2-VANCOUVER]. `introspection|heartbreak|identity`: supported by the critic's thematic account of shame and the aftermath of relationships [D2-EXCLAIM, D2-ALL]. English singing; instrumental *Shame* doesn't turn the whole album instrumental.

**Influence:** blank. No documented specific artist/record shaping the *whole* second volume was confirmed. `sounds_like` refers to critical similes (e.g. Nick Cave; Vangelis, Bauhaus/Depeche Mode comparisons) and is **not** an influence claim [D2-NORMAN, D2-POSTPUNK]. Some critics see Vol. 2 as the richer emotional half; The Electricity Club prefers Vol. 1 [D2-ELECTRIC]. Landmark `1` because essential conclusion to the artist's defining two-part work; high overall confidence.
