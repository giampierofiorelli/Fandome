# Lauryn Hill — Fandome Phase 01 evidence notes

One studio album only. Checked 29 October 2026 against GitHub `descriptors` branch. Source keys refer to `laurynhill_phase01_sources.csv` (14 evidence entries). All assessments are for the **original 1998 album**, not later anniversary reissues.

## `laurynhill-themiseducationoflaurynhill` — *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* (1998)

**Discography and ownership.** AllMusic dates the original release **25 August 1998**. Neither `catalog.ts` nor `seed.ts` has a Lauryn Hill solo entry or an existing album. The proposed artist ID `laurynhill` and album ID are new; Claude must insert both before running the validator. The Fugees are a different artist. *MTV Unplugged No. 2.0* (2002) is a LIVE record and therefore excluded despite consisting largely of new songs. (`allmusic_album`, `rollingstone_one_album`, `rollingstone_2021`)

**Genre method (14 documented tags):** R&B and hip-hop anchor the album. AllMusic explicitly names contemporary R&B, neo-soul and **alternative rap**; that exact genre is `alternativerap`, not the neighbouring `alternativehiphop`. Pitchfork documents classic soul and reggae as central sonic strands. Rolling Stone calls it hip-hop-soul. Gospel is supported by the *To Zion* singing, boom-bap by the rap numbers, doo-wop by the title of the single and the producers' described source, funk as a component, and dancehall by *Lost Ones*. These **last five do not represent the entire album**, hence they rank after the dominant styles. Pop is included only as the broad Pop/R&B designation used by Pitchfork. `genre_evidence.csv` has a separate source and scope per token. (`allmusic_album`, `pitchfork_2016`, `pitchfork_2018`, `xxl_newark`, `rollingstone_one_album`)

**Scales (0–5):** energy 3: hard-hitting spoken/rap sections coexist with slower R&B songs and tenderness. Electronic 2: notable programmed drums and samples, but ample verified live bass, guitar, electric piano, drums and horns. Mood 3: betrayal and heartbreak versus spiritual uplift and self-assertion. Theatrical 3: classroom interludes, dramatic declarations and expansive vocal turns, against intimate close-miked verses. Experimental 3: cross-genre collage and original structure, not avant-garde at all times. Polish 4: intricate, assured studio arrangements despite deliberate vinyl-warm/lo-fi textures. Tempo 3 and groove 4: midtempo hip-hop beats and syncopated reggae/R&B dominate despite slow ballads. These are editorial judgments grounded in multiple reviews, NOT measured album BPM or machine-specific inference. (`pitchfork_2016`, `washpost_1998`, `rollingstone_2021`, `qobuz_master`)

**Voice/lyrics:** Hill alternates rapping and strong, often softened soul singing; classroom spoken recordings appear as framing interludes. Themes include broken trust (*Ex-Factor*), motherhood and faith (*To Zion*), community ethics and gender (*Doo Wop*), remembered neighbourhood (*Every Ghetto, Every City*), personal identity and social criticism. Avoid assigning spiritual themes to every song. (`pitchfork_2016`, `pitchfork_2018`, `washpost_1998`)

**Instrument audit:** Qobuz original Sony/Columbia master and MusicBrainz track credits document live **bass** (Meredith/Barney/Fakhourie), **guitar** (Johari Newton, Earl Smith, Carlos Santana and others), **piano** (Tejumold Newton and others), **electric piano** / Rhodes and Wurlitzer (James Poyser), **organ**, **drummachine** / drum programming, actual **drums**, **percussion**, **synthesizer**, **synthbass**, **saxophone** (Dean Fraser), **trumpet/trombone**, **strings** (Indigo Quartet), DJ **turntables**, **harp** (Grace Paradise), **harpsichord** (Poyser, Superstar), **celesta** (Doo Wop), **flute** (credited ensemble) and **acousticguitar** (Carlos Santana guest on To Zion per contemporaneous Washington Post review). These are verified album instruments, NOT instruments inferred from the R&B label. The number of tokens does not imply every instrument is heard on every track. (`qobuz_master`, `musicbrainz_credit`, `washpost_1998`)

**Production:** `drumprogramming` and `programming` appear explicitly in Qobuz/MusicBrainz personnel; *Lost Ones* credits Vada Nobles, *To Zion* credits Ché Guevara as co-producer. `sampling` appears in *Superstar* (The Doors), *Ex-Factor* (Wu-Tang), etc. The Washington Post describes Hill layering vocals (`layeredvocals`) and Pitchfork characterises the vinyl-like warm `lofi` textures. **Do not assume** analog tape recording, a particular sampler model, Auto-Tune or that all drums are sampled. Hip-hop genre labels do not establish equipment. (`qobuz_master`, `musicbrainz_credit`, `washpost_1998`, `pitchfork_2016`)

**Producer roles and historical dispute:** Qobuz marks Lauryn Hill producer for the principal tracks, Vada Nobles **Producer** for *Lost Ones*, Ché Guevara **Co-Producer** for *To Zion* and *Every Ghetto, Every City*, and Don Harris **Producer** for the unlisted *Tell Him* coda. Therefore those four appear under `producers`, not just people listed under programming/writing/engineering. The 1998 New Ark contributors Vada Nobles, Johari Newton, Tejumold Newton and Rasheem Pugh disputed aspects of writing/production credits in a lawsuit later settled, and published accounts disagree on allocation. We preserve the released source's **explicit** credit roles, explain the challenge, and do not claim a legal finding establishing authorship or assign blanket producer credits to all New Ark members. (`qobuz_master`, `xxl_newark`, `la_times_1998`, `rollingstone_2021`)

**Influence discipline:** *TIDAL*'s Michael A. Gonzales recalls Hill personally saying she found old soul records by **Curtis Mayfield, The Temptations and Roberta Flack**, and that these inspired her **when making the album**. This is testimony passed through the reporter, not an audio recording of Hill: included in `influenced_by` with **medium-strength** sourced caveat. Hill's direct Academy interview names **Stevie Wonder (Songs in the Key of Life), Shep and the Limelites, Gladys Knight and the Pips, Marvin Gaye (What's Going On)** as the music of her formative years. Those are separately recorded under ARTIST INFLUENCES in the import document, not silently asserted as proven album-level. Pitchfork's **Stevie Wonder/Joni Mitchell comparison** and comparison of *Ex-Factor* to **Aretha Franklin** are critic analogies and belong only in `sounds_like`. Carlos Santana, Mary J. Blige and D'Angelo are guests; Fugees are Hill's former band; sampled Doors and Wu-Tang-Clan tracks are NOT influence claims. (`tidal_2018`, `academy_interview`, `pitchfork_2016`, `qobuz_master`)

**Critical context / dissent:** Pitchfork gives an extremely positive long-term appraisal, celebrating the fusion and the personal writing. AllMusic calls it a major stylistic and lyrical debut; The Washington Post notes reliance on live instrumentation rather than intensive sampling. The later credit dispute is material evidence on **authorship**, not a reason to remove Hill's credited production or to change sonic ratings. `confidence=high` is for the whole row's well-established musical features, but see the medium-grade influence attribution and contested production specifics above. (`pitchfork_2016`, `allmusic_album`, `washpost_1998`, `la_times_1998`)
