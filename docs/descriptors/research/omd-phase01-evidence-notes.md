# OMD — Fandome phase 01: research and judgment notes

Research date: 28 October 2026. Source URLs and precise claim summaries are in `omd_phase01_sources.csv`. The genre-by-genre provenance is in `omd_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`. Numbers are original editorial judgments using the Fandome guide (0–5); they are not grades from the publications.

## omd-orchestralmanoeuvresinthedark — *Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark* (1980)

- **Genres:** AllMusic lists synth-pop, new wave and post-punk (https://www.allmusic.com/album/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-mw0000202497); the album source compilation documents electropop (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestral_Manoeuvres_in_the_Dark_(album)). Album of the Year tags minimal synth and coldwave (https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/9199-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark.php); these are secondary database tags and should be reassessed if the owner prefers stricter criticism-only genres. No generic rock tag.
- **Instruments:** the contemporary album personnel list indicates bass, keyboards/synths, saxophone (Martin Cooper), guitar (Dave Fairbairn), percussion/drums and rhythm programming (https://progrography.com/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/review-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-1980/amp/). Andy and Paul describe the Korg Micro-Preset, Korg MS-20, Roland synth and reel-to-reel 'Winston' as the heart of their early recording (https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-debut-album/); the Liverpool museum describes this early hardware (https://confidentials.com/liverpool/omds-cutting-edge-gear-becomes-museum-piece). Drum-machine sound is included as a source alongside acoustic rhythm parts; do **not** treat the named tape recorder itself as a drum machine.
- **Production:** `drumprogramming` follows the original album credits; `analogtape` is supported by Winston's reel-to-reel backing and the Gramophone Suite recording account. No Martin Hannett in the producers column: he remixed/re-recorded early single variants, not the original full debut LP. Chester Valentino is Paul Collister's production alias and *is* part of the credited album production (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11793-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-darkorganisationarchitecture-morality/; https://progrography.com/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/review-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-1980/amp/; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-debut-album/). Mike Howlett produced a later re-recording of 'Messages', not the original album master; album version distinction matters.
- **Scores:** energy 3 and tempo 3 reflect choppy, driving early singles against calmer songs. Electronic 4 reflects synth/drum-machine dominance alongside audible bass/guitar and percussion. Mood 2 reflects 'The Messerschmitt Twins' and melancholy synth songs versus cheerful 'Electricity'. Theatrical 2 and experimental 3 reflect sparse intimate presentation mixed with electronics and krautrock-inflected structure. Polish 1 is grounded by the band describing their own early DIY 'garage' studio sound; groove 2 reflects rhythmic pop, not primarily club music (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11793-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-darkorganisationarchitecture-morality/; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-debut-album/). The vocal type was left empty because the sources describe expressiveness, not a consistent allowed voice label.
- **Lyrics:** storytelling, introspection and love are conservative summaries of named first-album songs. The sources don't demonstrate a single cohesive social or political concept for the entire album; no `political` default added based on OMD's later records.
- **sounds_like vs influenced_by:** Kraftwerk, Neu! and Sparks are directly mentioned as comparisons in music press (https://www.allmusic.com/album/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-mw0000202497; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-debut-album/); a later writer hears Gary Numan sonics on the debut (https://hauntologynow.substack.com/p/orchestral-manoeuvers-in-the-dark). *Kraftwerk* alone appears as this album's documented musical influence because McCluskey directly connects the debut single 'Electricity' to 'Radio-Activity' (https://www.buzzmag.co.uk/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-interview/). Broader influences belong in CLAUDE_IMPORT.md, not assumed album by album. No sample/cover is used as influence evidence.
- **Confidence:** high for foundational instrumentation, genre and credit evidence; individual coldwave/minimal synth labels are weaker than the album's core genres.

## omd-organisation — *Organisation* (1980)

- **Genres:** synth-pop/new wave/post-punk (AllMusic), electronic and experimental (original UK pressing genre metadata). The *gothic* description from reviews identifies mood rather than independently establishing the unrelated genre `gothicrock` (https://www.allmusic.com/album/organisation-mw0000198493; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/organisation-album_fhvnnp.html; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/).
- **Instruments:** the original UK LP names synthesizer, bass, electric organ, treated piano, conventional/electric piano, drum rhythm programming, acoustic/electronic percussion and Malcolm Holmes on drums (https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/organisation-album_fhvnnp.html). The drum-machine pulse on 'Enola Gay' is specifically documented by the performers (https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/jan/07/orchestral-manoeuvres-dark-enola-gay). No sax or guitar inferred merely from band membership.
- **Production:** Mike Howlett and the group are explicit original album producers; this is a more professionally made record than its predecessor. `drumprogramming` is explicitly in sleeve credits. `heavyreverb` is confined to the featured single 'Enola Gay': Paul Humphreys credits the instrumental grandeur substantially to studio reverb (https://www.theguardian.com/music/2013/jan/07/orchestral-manoeuvres-dark-enola-gay); it is **not** inferred to dominate every song. No sequencer brand added without evidence (https://www.omd-messages.co.uk/organisation-interview-andy-mccluskey/).
- **Scores:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2 and mood 1 reflect the darker, slower, funereal LP with the notably bouncy *Enola Gay* as an exception; electronic 4 and polish 3 follow the machine-driven core and Howlett studio direction; theatrical 3 follows dramatic lead vocals; experimental 3 accounts for industrial sound effects and atmospheric songs. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11793-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-darkorganisationarchitecture-morality/; https://www.omd-messages.co.uk/organisation-interview-andy-mccluskey/; https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/.
- **Lyrics:** political/social anchors 'Enola Gay' and industrial 'Stanlow'; inward melancholy on the remaining songs. `heartbreak` is a modest tail classification for relationship songs, not the record's defining subject.
- **sounds_like vs influenced_by:** McCluskey **explicitly** calls *Organisation* Joy Division-influenced (https://www.scotsman.com/whats-on/arts-and-entertainment/orchestral-leap-in-the-dark-2480880), supporting `influenced_by=Joy Division`. Pitchfork invokes Joy Division and Echo & the Bunnymen for tonal resemblances. Kraftwerk relates to the electronic vocabulary, but there is no separate Organisation-specific Kraftwerk influence statement, so it stays in `sounds_like` only (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11793-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-darkorganisationarchitecture-morality/).
- **Version note:** distinguish the October 1980 original 9-song LP from 2003 remaster bonuses and original free live 7-inch. Those recordings are excluded from album-wide descriptors (https://www.omd-messages.co.uk/organisation-interview-andy-mccluskey/).

## omd-dazzleships — *Dazzle Ships* (1983)

- **Genres:** specialist critics call it synth-pop, new wave, musique concrète and electronic sound collage; original album summaries name avant-pop and experimental, with art-pop in album genre databases (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11363-dazzle-ships/; https://www.allmusic.com/album/dazzle-ships-mw0000652994; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzle_Ships_(album); https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/9202-orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-dazzle-ships.php). Do not use generic `rock` just because an outlet places it in Pop/Rock.
- **Instruments:** Andy/Paul/Martin/Malcolm are credited with synths, keyboards, guitar, bass, drums and percussion. The album's early digital sampler is the E-mu Emulator, alongside Mellotron and analogue synths (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzle_Ships_(album); https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890). Do not replace `emulator` with `emulatorii`, or import later Fairlight hardware into 1983.
- **Production:** `sampling` for shortwave radio, speaking clocks, public announcements and found-sound snippets; `emulator` confirmed by Andy/Paul in 1984; `tapeeffects` for taped/spliced audio collage (not an assertion of any named delay rack); `layeredvocals` for distinctive arranged robotic/synthetic-voice layers described by Pitchfork. The treated speech is not proof of a literal hardware vocoder (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11363-dazzle-ships/; https://www.allmusic.com/album/dazzle-ships-mw0000652994; https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890).
- **Scores:** experimental 5 and electronic 5 reflect radical audio collage, cut-up interludes and machine textures. Mood 1 for cold-war anxiety, theatrical 3 for stark, sometimes dramatic song presentation; energy and tempo 2, groove 1 for mostly atmospheric fragments rather than continuous dance tracks; polish 3 for controlled studio assemblage rather than a later glossy pop mix (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11363-dazzle-ships/; https://www.electronicsound.co.uk/features/long-reads/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-razzle-dazzle/).
- **Lyrics:** political/social Cold War, technological and dehumanisation concerns, with introspective song breaks. `storytelling` captures fragments/miniatures; do not claim that every track is political: several are instrumental or documentary excerpts.
- **sounds_like:** Kraftwerk is named a clear sonic counterpart by critics; AllMusic explicitly compares the disjointed electronics and mood with Radiohead's *Kid A* (https://www.allmusic.com/album/dazzle-ships-mw0000652994). **Not** asserting influence of Radiohead on the 1983 recording (which would be anachronistic).
- **influenced_by:** Kraftwerk's conceptual and sonic example is repeatedly acknowledged by McCluskey; musique concrète is a *technique/movement*, not the name of a musical artist for this column. No unverified Stockhausen personal influence inserted (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11363-dazzle-ships/; https://www.theartsdesk.com/new-music/theartsdesk-qa-musician-andy-mccluskey-orchestral-manoeuvres-dark).
- **Reception dispute:** commercially and critically rejected in 1983; strongly re-evaluated in 2008 and later. This is a defining artist landmark **despite** its original reception (https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11363-dazzle-ships/; https://www.electronicsound.co.uk/features/long-reads/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark-razzle-dazzle/).

## omd-junkculture — *Junk Culture* (1984)

- **Genres:** AllMusic describes synth-pop/new-wave/pop at album level, reggae groove on the title track. OMD described electropop instrumentation, and their Montserrat experience introduced calypso/soca and reggae to the album, notably 'Locomotion'. Therefore the track-specific `reggae|calypso|soca` labels are in the *long tail*, not replacing synth-pop. `soca` is a new genre token proposed, explicitly documented by the band's 1984 and 2015 accounts (https://www.allmusic.com/album/junk-culture-mw0000191164; https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890; https://pennyblackmusic.co.uk/Home/Details?id=22842; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-junk-culture-album/).
- **Instruments:** original album credits establish synths, bass, piano, drums, percussion, tenor sax, trumpet, trombone and Martin Cooper's marimba (= `mallets`). Contemporary/retrospective hardware sources confirm LinnDrum, E-mu Emulator, Fairlight CMI, Prophet-5 and Roland Jupiter-8, plus celeste. 'Locomotion' features a steel-drum rhythm, but the sources do not prove the steel drums were physically played rather than constructed/programmed; the `steeldrums` token is for the **audible rhythm**, not an assertion of a particular session instrumentalist (https://www.onamrecords.com/artists/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/discography/united-states/a-m-records/sp-5027/junk-culture; https://pennyblackmusic.co.uk/Home/DetailsMobile?id=22858; https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890; https://www.omd.me.uk/index.php/omd-albums-and-singles/omd-singles/omd-70s80s-singles/133-locomotion).
- **Production:** fairlight and emulator sampling, LinnDrum plus acoustic snare rolls, sampled bassline on Locomotion, and arrangements for brass all documented by Andy/Paul and original credits. `jupiter8` and `prophet5` are named on the album's musician instrument inventory. No `emulatorii` token: sources just state Emulator (https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890; https://pennyblackmusic.co.uk/Home/DetailsMobile?id=22858).
- **Production credit caveat:** artist members, Brian Tench and Stephen Hague appear in source credit lists as producers. Tony Visconti came in for specific **horn arrangements** (especially 'Locomotion' and 'All Wrapped Up') but did *not* produce the whole record, so he is omitted from `producers` and represented by `hornarrangements` (https://www.onamrecords.com/artists/orchestral-manoeuvres-in-the-dark/discography/united-states/a-m-records/sp-5027/junk-culture; https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890; https://www.omd.me.uk/index.php/omd-albums-and-singles/omd-singles/omd-70s80s-singles/133-locomotion). More precise track-by-track production allocations should be retained when available.
- **Scores:** mood 4/energy 3/tempo 3/groove 3 for a generally upbeat, more rhythmic LP that still holds slower melancholic songs; electronic 4 because Fairlight sampling and drum machine are joined by live horns, marimba and other physical playing; polish 4 for more layered commercial studio construction; experimental 2 compared with the deliberately fragmented 1983 record (https://www.allmusic.com/album/junk-culture-mw0000191164; https://www.muzines.benhall.co.uk/articles/junk-culture/7890; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/omd-junk-culture-album/).
- **Lyrics:** love, introspection, and social observation of pop/consumer culture in song lyrics; `irony` cautiously limited to witty science-themed numbers such as 'Tesla Girls'.
- **sounds_like/influenced_by:** AllMusic directly compares 'Tesla Girls' to Sparks as an audible resemblance. Artist testimony supports soca **as a style** and Arrow as a source of interest in Caribbean music, but the album-specific chain to Arrow's *Soca Savage* is not established firmly enough to insert a precise artist-album influence into `influenced_by`. The general statement is described in CLAUDE_IMPORT; no producer or collaborator mistaken for influence (https://www.allmusic.com/album/junk-culture-mw0000191164; https://pennyblackmusic.co.uk/Home/Details?id=22842).

## Cross-album integrity

- Do not duplicate `omd-architecturemorality`: already present in `docs/descriptors/omd.csv` in repository branch `descriptors`. This ZIP contains only the four new rows.
- Every album here is **studio**, not a compilation, live show or reissue. Source material referring to 2003/2008 deluxe reissues is used for critical retrospectives, not as additional albums.
- The 2026 OMD official discography contains 14 studio albums through *Bauhaus Staircase* (2023). *Crush*, *The Pacific Age*, *Sugar Tax* and *Liberator* follow in phase 02.
- Fandome's `scene` and subjective similarity fields are editorial judgments grounded in sources; these are descriptive labels, not formal historical scenes. `voice` is blank rather than assume a type not established across full albums.
