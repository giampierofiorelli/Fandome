# Phase 01 — Tame Impala: field-level evidence and caveats

Research against the GitHub descriptor-guide scale anchors. Numeric values are independent editorial evaluations based on documented features of the **specific record**, not on artist stereotypes. `sources.csv` contains URLs, while `genre_evidence.csv` contains the per-token/album citation and scope. An empty `influenced_by` is intentional, not an implied negative conclusion.

## `tameimpala-tameimpala` — Tame Impala EP (2008)

- **Existence, type, status and importance**: The EP is explicitly listed as a 2008 release in [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/tame-impala-ep--mw0002259254), [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/tame-impala-ep/1445020668) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/08a252a8-2daf-4990-bf5d-4601c40757c6). [AllMusic's biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tame-impala-mn0002014409/biography) records the EP's Australian breakthrough. `landmark=1` refers to this formative commercial and artistic breakthrough, not a claim it was bigger than the LPs.
- **Edition disagreement**: AllMusic displays a four-track issue; Apple and MusicBrainz provide six tracks, with “Skeleton Tiger” and “Wander” added. The 2008 Modular six-track EP is the primary reference. The separate early 2008 self-titled Hole in the Sky/H.I.T.S. 003 material is **not** counted as another album.
- **Genres**: AllMusic gives `neopsychedelia`, `indierock` and `alternativeindierock`; Apple lists `rock` and details 1960s/1970s psych-rock touchstones. Parker's [LA Times interview](https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/pop-hiss/story/2010-06-29/tame-impalas-kevin-parker-discusses-his-bands-success-origins-and-impressions-of-california) explicitly links the EP to Blue Cheer-style heavy 1970s music. The broad `psychedelicrock` label appears directly in [AOTY's EP genre tags](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/8751-tame-impala-tame-impala.php), which also names `acidrock` and `heavypsych` as lesser facets.
- **Scales**: `energy=4` because of the EP's forceful staccato riffing and the critic's description of stomping songs; `electronic=1` because the evidence is for amplified guitars rather than keyboards or electronic programming; `mood=3` distinguishes swaggering riffs from relatively mellow/spacey pieces; `theatrical=2` refers to unshowy vocals; `experimental=2` reflects era-revival structures plus guitar effects; `polish=1` is based on home-demo genesis in AllMusic's band history; `tempo=3` and `groove=3` reflect midpaced riffing and the specifically groovy “Half Full Glass of Wine”. These are editorial whole-EP judgements, `confidence=medium` because the available primary technical credits are sparse.
- **Voice/instruments**: Apple's editorial describes Parker's smooth vocals, guitar effects and a bass/drum rhythm section. [AllMusic's biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tame-impala-mn0002014409/biography) says early home recordings were mainly Parker's work, so don't read the Apple “trio” blurb as proof that each person recorded a specific part.
- **Production**: `lofi` and `overdubbing` reference the home-recording genesis, `distortion` refers to the explicitly crunchy/affected guitars, with no exact pedal or multitrack model asserted. `producers=Kevin Parker` is explicitly stated in [Qobuz's six individual track label credits](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/tame-impala-tame-impala/0060251783918). The MusicBrainz release page omits a producer relationship; this is absence of data, not proof against Qobuz's credits.
- **Lyrics and comparisons**: The `lyrics` field is intentionally **empty**: album-specific reviews and reliable lyric interpretations are not detailed enough to certify themes at the required standard. The LA interview directly ties Parker's style to Blue Cheer, so that entry is in `influenced_by`; other 1960s bands in Apple's description are **similarities**, not proven album influences.

## `tameimpala-innerspeaker` — Innerspeaker (2010)

- **Genres**: [Pitchfork's original review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/14279-innerspeaker/) focuses on psychedelic guitar rock, with a specifically named “paisley pop” current. [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/list/49-stereogums-top-50-albums-of-2010/) adds neo-psychedelia, psychedelic pop and Garage Rock Revival. `paisleypop` (texture) and `garagerockrevival` (some songs) therefore rank last. They require new accepted tokens.
- **Scales**: `energy=3` for sustained full-band propulsion without continuous high intensity; `electronic=1` because [Parker expressly says he owned no synths](https://guitar.com/features/interviews/tame-impala-kevin-parker-10-years-innerspeaker/) and shaped electric-guitar sounds to mimic them; `mood=3` for both sunlit and solitary passages; `theatrical=2` for understated singing; `experimental=3` for strange octave/EQ guitar processing within rock songs; `polish=3` because [the Quietus interview](https://thequietus.com/interviews/tame-impala-interview-innerspeaker/) describes greater care than the EP and professional outside mixing; `tempo=3`, `groove=3` across midpaced grooves and slower expansive cuts.
- **Instrument credit**: electric guitar is primary (Parker's Fender Stratocaster); drums and bass are credited on [Amoeba's album entry](https://www.amoeba.com/innerspeaker-cd-tame-impala/albums/821195/), along with `handclaps` (Tansie Bennetts). No synthesizer token. Some tracks feature separate Jay Watson/Dom Simper contributions; Parker is the main studio performer.
- **Production evidence**: Parker describes layered guitar/overdubbed recordings, guitar octave changes, studio presets, and distortion/EQ manipulation in [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/features/interviews/tame-impala-kevin-parker-10-years-innerspeaker/). `heavyreverb` reflects audible studio effect wash in the [Quietus interview](https://thequietus.com/interviews/tame-impala-interview-innerspeaker/) and [2011 Rocksucker Parker interview](https://rocksucker.co.uk/2011/06/interview-tame-impala.html) (the latter explicitly explains guitar reverb-before-distortion trick). No specific hardware recorder model is encoded: recollections mention an eight-track while a later Sound on Sound account mentions a Boss BR-1600. Recording years circa 2009 for 2010 release.
- **Producer/mixer discrepancy**: [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/da41f10f-89e9-3a51-808a-877ee1109157) and [Amoeba](https://www.amoeba.com/innerspeaker-cd-tame-impala/albums/821195/) agree `Kevin Parker` produced, Tim Holmes engineered, Dave Fridmann mixed. Fridmann is **not** a co-producer. Parker reinforces this division of labour in the Quietus interview.
- **Voice, lyrics, influences**: subdued airy lead vocal from reviewers; isolation and romantic estrangement in songs “Solitude Is Bliss” and “It Is Not Meant to Be” justify `introspection|love`. [LA Times Parker interview](https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/pop-hiss/story/2010-06-29/tame-impalas-kevin-parker-discusses-his-bands-success-origins-and-impressions-of-california) connects his melancholy, dreamlike wall-of-sound pursuit to My Bloody Valentine, a stronger basis for `influenced_by` than the critic's Beatles and Verve sonic comparisons. `landmark=1` for debut LP impact/long-standing reception.

## `tameimpala-lonerism` — Lonerism (2012)

- **Genres**: [AOTY's album entry](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/3879-tame-impala-lonerism.php) explicitly lists `neopsychedelia`, `psychedelicrock`, `psychedelicpop`, `spacerockrevival`, `heavypsych`; the last two are secondary texture/track-specific labels and not yet on the Fandome list. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/lonerism-mw0002401709) adds `indierock` and `alternativeindierock`. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/17131-lonerism/) documents the interaction of psychedelic rock and electronic production; electronics as technique do not alone prove an electronic-genre label.
- **Scales**: `energy=3` because many tracks are rolling/propulsive but there are quiet conclusions; `electronic=3` strongly documented shift to synths, drum machines and computer-recorded sound in [Parker's Stereogum interview](https://stereogum.com/1115931/progress-report-tame-impala/interviews/progress-report); `mood=1` for loneliness, alienation, anxiety and lovelorn themes despite brightly coloured tunes ([Guardian review](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/oct/04/tame-impala-lonerism-review)); `theatrical=3` for expansive arrangements but restrained lead; `experimental=4` for disruptive, distorted, electronically processed arrangements; `polish=3` for a home-recording sound deliberately retaining grain under polished mixing; `tempo=3`, `groove=3` for a mix of rolling jams and song-led psychedelia.
- **Instruments**: [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/1115931/progress-report-tame-impala/interviews/progress-report) has Parker reporting synths, guitars, drum machines and samples. [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/lonerism-tame-impala-in-the-studio) documents analogue synthesizers and live drums. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/be3e3f45-2963-46b2-807b-21c2e947eeee) credits Jay Watson's keyboards and piano, and [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/oct/04/tame-impala-lonerism-review) identifies organ sounds. No sampled break is misrepresented as a hardware `sampler` credit.
- **Production**: `juno106` and new `sequentialproone` come from [Parker's AudioTechnology account](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/lonerism-tame-impala-in-the-studio); `ableton` is explicitly named in the [Louder Than War interview](https://louderthanwar.com/tame-impala-big-interview-with-australian-tripped-out-groove-band/). `sampling` from Stereogum, `overdubbing` from Parker's studio recording descriptions, and `distortion|sonicmanipulation` from [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/oct/04/tame-impala-lonerism-review). Avoid overclaiming tape hardware or a particular mastering chain.
- **Producers and disagreement**: Parker is the [credited producer](https://musicbrainz.org/release/be3e3f45-2963-46b2-807b-21c2e947eeee), Fridmann the *mixer*. [The Guardian's shorter 2012 review](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/oct/07/tame-impala-lonerism-review) loosely calls it a Fridmann-produced follow-up, conflicting with detailed credits; prefer album credits and Parker's technical accounts.
- **Lyrics and voice**: reviewer documents isolation, panic, disconnection and love, which justify `introspection|identity|love|heartbreak`; vocal type is gently sung rather than operatic or overtly theatrical. Although Parker characterised the album as “Britney Spears fronting the Flaming Lips” ([Under the Radar](https://www.undertheradarmag.com/interviews/tame_impala/)), that is a self-descriptive *sonic metaphor*, not confirmation the songwriting sampled or drew direct compositional influence from those artists. `influenced_by` is left **empty**; `sounds_like` are editorial comparisons.

## `tameimpala-currents` — Currents (2015)

- **Genre breadth and weight**: Principal `psychedelicpop|neopsychedelia|synthpop` is [listed for Currents](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/32344-tame-impala-currents.php); [Observer review](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/19/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker-hearbreak-album) describes disco/funk and **electropop**, not a misspelling of synthpop. [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/20578-currents/) specifically discusses pop, soul and R&B. AOTY additionally lists dream pop, alternative R&B, indietronica, chillwave, and residual psychedelic rock. [NME Parker interview](https://www.nme.com/features/tame-impala-interview-inside-the-mind-of-a-psych-pop-shaman-358) names `discorock` on “Let It Happen”, `yachtrock` on “Past Life” and `dancepop` on “The Less I Know the Better”: these **belong last** because they name particular songs, not the entire album.
- **Scales**: `energy=3` for mostly rolling/medium-level pop with explosive moments; `electronic=4` for synth/processed/electronic drum timbres dominating much of the record; `mood=2` as glossy, seemingly light music accompanies heartbreak and re-identification; `theatrical=3` for big layered hooks counterposed with close vocals; `experimental=4` for “Let It Happen” stutter, radical structure and “Past Life” robotic spoken voice ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/16/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker)); `polish=4` for high-fidelity, self-mixed, carefully textured sound; `tempo=3` spans relaxed slow jams and uptempo cuts; `groove=4` reflects Parker's self-described dance-music turn ([NME retrospective](https://www.nme.com/features/tame-impala-currents-fifth-anniversary-interview-2020-rihanna-2706819)).
- **Instruments**: [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/tame-impalas-current-nostalgia) records real drums, bass, samplers and drum-machine hybrids, Juno and JV1080 synth keyboards, and DrumTraks; [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/tame-impala-kevin-parker) has Parker state the “The Less I Know the Better” riff was recorded using **Roland GR-55 guitar synth**, rather than an ordinary tracked bass guitar. `guitarsynth` and `synthbass` reflect documented instrument families; `organ` is present in “New Person, Same Old Mistakes” according to [Observer](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/19/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker-hearbreak-album). The bass and organ **emulations in the GR-55 passage** are not claimed as separately recorded instruments.
- **Production**: `layeredvocals` and `vocalfilter` are documented in the [Guardian review](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/16/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker); `falsetto` from the [Observer](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/19/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker-hearbreak-album). `sampling|drumprogramming|ableton|juno106|rolandjv1080|sequentialdrumtraks` derive from [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/tame-impalas-current-nostalgia). `rolandgr55` from Parker directly in Guitar World. `sonicmanipulation` covers stutter/spliced and reverberant sound treatments but **not** literal glitch/sampling provenance where unspecified. Do not promote a reviewer's “what sounded like Auto-Tune” guess into a verified hardware/software credit.
- **Album producer**: Kevin Parker alone is credited as producer, engineer and mixer in the [MusicBrainz release credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/724e590d-47b4-49db-9536-02108215b24c); [NME](https://www.nme.com/features/tame-impala-currents-fifth-anniversary-interview-2020-rihanna-2706819) reports it was his first independently mixed Tame Impala album. Do not add Fridmann, Mark Ronson or other song collaborators to `producers`.
- **Lyrical themes**: Guardian/Observer and NME repeatedly describe separation, romantic hurt, personal transformation and lingering memories; hence `heartbreak|introspection|identity|love|nostalgia`. The groove mood is distinguished from lyrics mood (`mood=2` instead of euphoric).
- **Album-specific influence**: Parker says [hearing the Bee Gees inspired his shift](https://www.nme.com/news/music/tame-impala-45-1222411), so `influenced_by=Bee Gees`. Not every Daft Punk/Prince comparison is causal. `landmark=1` for documented stylistic reinvention, widespread acclaim and status in [Pitchfork's 2010s list](https://pitchfork.com/features/lists-and-guides/the-200-best-albums-of-the-2010s/).
- **Critical disagreement**: Most reviewers celebrate its pop pivot, while [Observer](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/jul/19/tame-impala-currents-review-kevin-parker-hearbreak-album) criticizes excessive sonic uniformity/polish. Those perspectives are preserved in `polish=4` and `experimental=4`, not flattened to uncomplicated dance-pop.

## Across-phase restraint

- Album-level production tokens are sourced to these albums, not Kevin Parker's equipment photographed years later.
- `sounds_like` is editorial and intentionally separate from sourced `influenced_by`.
- Descriptors describe original releases, not later remixes or 10th-anniversary bonus tracks.
- All four `status=draft` because no independent second-person review has occurred.
