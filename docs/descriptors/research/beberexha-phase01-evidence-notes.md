# Bebe Rexha — Phase 01 evidence notes

**Basis:** first original editions, not 2024 joined EP reissue. Album-specific claims use the exact linked sources in `beberexha_phase01_sources.csv`. Scales are editorial judgements anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`; they are not measured quantities. Artist is **absent** from the current `catalog.ts`, so `beberexha` is a proposed ID requiring creation and verification by Claude.

## I Don't Wanna Grow Up (2015) — `beberexha-idontwannagrowup`

- **Genres:** Qobuz classifies the five-track EP pop. PopCrush explicitly calls **Pray** electro-pop and describes gospel-like call-and-response within the song. The Associated Press calls **I Can't Stop Drinking About You** EDM-flavored. Thus `gospel` is marked *texture*, not whole-record gospel; no unsupported rock or hip-hop label was extrapolated from her later output.
- **Energy 4, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 4:** AP hears passionate, forceful delivery and anthemic highs; PopCrush details pounding beats and emotional distress, with title track softer. Together these justify intensity and a heartbroken rather than euphoric center. **Tempo 3, groove 3:** varied balance, not an uninterrupted dance EP. **Experimental 1, polish 4:** concise commercial pop structures and controlled production. These scales remain judgements, not assertions by the critics.
- **Voice `powerful|raspy|soft`:** strong, sometimes cutting delivery against a soft title track. **Production `falsetto|drumprogramming`:** AP directly identifies falsetto-heavy title; Qobuz credits Jon Levine as Drum Programmer on I'm Gonna Show You Crazy. The register tag `falsetto` is legitimate even though no hardware is identified.
- **Instruments `keyboards|guitar|bass`:** Qobuz credits Jon Levine with all three on I'm Gonna Show You Crazy and guitar player Kenji on Pray. It credits Jason Evigan with generic instrumentation on Sweet Beginnings, insufficient to assert a particular synthesizer or drum machine.
- **Producers:** title track Sir Nolan as producer with Nasri and Adam Messinger co-producers; Sweet Beginnings Jason Evigan; I'm Gonna Show You Crazy Jon Levine; Pray Monsters & Strangerz. Qobuz lists Rexha and The Monsters & Strangerz as *additional producers* only on I Can't Stop Drinking About You, and they are not counted on the strength of that credit alone. Nasri and Adam Messinger are included specifically as co-producers. The 2015 interview supports growth/realism themes, not named album musical influences.
- **`influenced_by` empty:** the general influences documented in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` cannot automatically be applied to this EP. **`landmark=1`:** debut release and first solo artistic statement; historical importance, not a sales claim.

## All Your Fault, Pt. 1 (2017) — `beberexha-allyourfaultpt1`

- **Genres:** AllMusic identifies dance-pop, EDM and pop, plus a *trap* turn specifically for Bad Bitch. Trap has limited track scope. Apple describes dance grooves and relationship narratives; no tropical-house tag is assigned simply because AllMusic calls I Got You *tropical-tinged*.
- **Energy 4 / electronic 4 / mood 2 / theatrical 3 / experimental 1 / polish 5 / tempo 4 / groove 4:** propulsive singles and studio-produced club pop sit beside sad or guarded relationship writing. Conventional hit-writing, polished programmed surfaces, varying dynamics across six songs.
- **Voice `powerful|raspy|clear`:** headline singer's distinctive cutting leads, with clear hooks. G-Eazy and Ty Dolla $ign contribute guest verses: **do not** assign `rap` to the *lead voice* solely on that basis.
- **Instruments `keyboards|violin|viola|cello`:** Qobuz's Gateway Drug personnel (The Invisible Men, Alex Oriet, Maths Time Joy, Sarah Button, Katherine Chibah, Louise Dearsley, Reid Chibah) establish keyboard and bowed strings. `I Got You` and `F.F.F.` list Captain Cuts as `All Instruments`, but no named machines, therefore not translated to synth or drum machine.
- **Production `programming`:** Captain Cuts, The Invisible Men and Maths Time Joy explicitly credited as programmers on individual tracks. No model such as TR-808, software or Auto-Tune is claimed without direct proof.
- **Producer roster:** The Wiild, Scott Robinson (co-producer on Atmosphere), Captain Cuts, Gladius, The Invisible Men, Salt Wives, Stargate. **Not** Mitch Allan (`Additional Producer` on Atmosphere), Maths Time Joy (`Additional Producer` on Gateway Drug) nor mixing-only staff. Qobuz credits may distinguish an individual and collective with different roles; collective duplicates are not double-counted.
- **Sonic resemblance** to Cher Lloyd and Rihanna is supported by ABC critic comparison, not a claim of influence. `influenced_by` intentionally empty.

## All Your Fault, Pt. 2 (2017) — `beberexha-allyourfaultpt2`

- **Release date/edition:** original August 11 2017 six-song EP, corroborated by Apple Music and MusicBrainz. Later combined issue `All Your Fault, Pts. 1–2` is excluded; country labels on chart sites sometimes group the same songs under different categories.
- **Genres:** Apple identifies pop broadly, rap collaborators, a dancehall **Comfortable** and country-pop **Meant to Be**. The electropop, contemporary R&B, dance-pop and pop-rap tokens are additionally named in Album of the Year's user-maintained release genre tags. They are transparent *lower-strength* metadata, not independent academic critical consensus; genre evidence file marks exact scopes. `hiphop` is localized to collaboration material rather than every track.
- **Electronic 4, energy 4, tempo 4, groove 4, mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 1, polish 4:** Qobuz's Rob Wacey calls out bright synth leads and pulsing electro beats, but the country-pop closer prevents treating this as wholly electronic dance music. Positive melodic framing is counterweighted by varied narratives.
- **Instruments `synth|drums|keyboards|guitar|bass|percussion`:** Qobuz credits Jason Gill drums, synth, bass, percussion on (Not) The One; Frank Dukes guitar/keys on Comfortable; David Garcia drums, guitar and keyboards on Meant to Be. No specific machine models or real/sampled drum status are asserted.
- **Production `programming`:** Jason Gill and David Garcia named as programmers. Distinguish this from a specific TR-808 device, which is not documented.
- **Producers:** Murda Beatz, Stargate, Joel Little, Jonas Jeberg, Jason Gill, Frank Dukes, **Willshire**. Apple Music's song credit confirms Willshire as *Producer* on Meant to Be, while one Qobuz alternate edition (0093624910770) says *Additional Producer*. Apple and the primary Qobuz release (0093624910763) both list producer; discrepancy warrants attention when importing. David Garcia is credited as writer, instrumentalist and engineer, *not* producer in Apple/Qobuz sources. Joey Moi is engineering/vocal production only and is excluded.
- **No co-billed album:** Florida Georgia Line, Lil Wayne, Kranium, Gucci Mane and 2 Chainz are guests on named tracks, not co-primary artists for the EP. `influenced_by` empty: the Whitney Houston allusion in the title The Way I Are and the Whitney interpolation do **not** establish musical influence.

## Expectations (2018) — `beberexha-expectations`

- **Genres:** Metacritic and AllMusic designate pop, dance-pop and pop/rock. Entertainment Voice and AllMusic describe R&B/hip-hop components, with the Guardian naming specific tropical-house (`I Got You`) and Latin (`Shining Star`) songs; The Bulletin explicitly calls Meant to Be *country-pop*. **Order deliberately weights pop over these limited-scope colors.** The source's slash-separated *pop/rock* is treated as `poprock` in the genre CSV, with the artist's own reference to guitar-led pop-rock giving more exact support.
- **Energy 3, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 4, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 3:** both electronic rhythmic singles and acoustic/piano ballads belong to the *original 14-track* album. Anxiety, romance and uncertain public image dominate lyrical tone. The Guardian hears formulaic genre-hopping while NME sees coherent personality: disagreement is preserved and constrains experimental score.
- **Voice `powerful|raspy|soft`:** the Guardian reports strained bluesy belting; Entertainment Voice describes gentle breathy singing on Steady and falsetto on Pillow. No `rap` voice on account of guests Quavo or Tory Lanez.
- **Instruments `guitar|keyboards|drums|piano|bass|violin|percussion`:** Qobuz/Warner song credits list Couros Sheibani and D Mile guitars on Shining Star; David Garcia guitars, keys and drums on Meant to Be; Charlie Bisharat violin and Jake Hawkes piano on Grace; The Stereotypes' track Pillow credits Jeremy Reeves bass, Ray Romulus drums, Jonathan Yip percussion, Ray Charles McCullough II piano. No specific synth *model* or software demonstrated. An editorial review describes synth bass on Steady; `synthbass` was omitted because the official musician credit does not name a specific player/instrument for that track and broader album cues already cover the electronically generated sound.
- **Production `programming|autotune|belting|falsetto`:** Qobuz officially credits programmer roles across many tracks. The Guardian directly documents Auto-Tune on Self Control and Mine and Rexha's belting. Entertainment Voice calls out Pillow falsetto; only that performance registers the technique, not every track. The artist's mention of '808s' is not documentation of a genuine Roland TR-808 drum machine; `tr808` is intentionally absent.
- **Producers:** Jason Evigan, Jussifer, Ali P, Dre Pinckney, Robot Scott, David Garcia, Captain Cuts, Andrew Wells, German, The Monsters & Strangerz, Hit-Boy, Louis Bell, The Stereotypes, Willshire. Warner/Qobuz explicitly names these as producers on album tracks; exclude Gian Stone, Devon Corey, Aaron Z. as 'additional producer' only, Joey Moi as vocal producer/engineer, Quavo as featured artist, Justin Tranter as writer, Mitch McCarthy as mixer, etc. Qobuz also lists Ali P / Ali Prawl as variants of names in the Shining Star credits; Ali P is specifically credited as producer.
- **`influenced_by=No Doubt` only:** Rexha's NME interview explicitly links *this album's* guitar material to No Doubt. Her broader taste in 1990s music is not generalized to the other EPs. `sounds_like` is a separate editorial resemblance list backed by AllMusic's peer comparisons. **`landmark=1`:** first album proper, central artistic statement.
- **Edition overlap:** I Got You originally appeared on All Your Fault Pt. 1; Meant to Be on Pt. 2. Keeping Expectations is required as a real first studio album; do not treat its reused singles as new tracks or create an album from a later combined compilation.
