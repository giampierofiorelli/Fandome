### taylorswift-folklore (Folklore, 2020)

Sources:
- Pitchfork, Jill Mapes, 27 Jul 2020, 8.0. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-folklore/
- The Guardian, Laura Snapes, 24 Jul 2020, 5 stars. https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/jul/24/taylor-swift-folklore-review-bombastic-pop-makes-way-for-emotional-acuity
- Variety, Chris Willman. https://variety.com/2020/music/reviews/taylor-swift-folklore-album-review-1234715385/
- NME, Hannah Mylrea ("an extraordinary indie-folk makeover"). https://www.nme.com/reviews/taylor-swift-folklore-album-review-2713965
- Irish Times. https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/music/taylor-swift-folklore-review-a-triumph-of-wistful-escapist-melancholy-1.4312625
- Full liner-note credits, reproduced by The Fader. https://thefader.com/2020/07/24/taylor-swift-folklore-credits-nationl-bon-iver-jack-antonoff
- Grammy.com, 2021 Album of the Year credits. https://www.grammy.com/awards/categories/album-of-the-year/2021/
- Rolling Stone, Best Albums of 2020, #1. https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-albums-2020-1096814/

Cells:
- **genres**
  - indiefolk: NME headline "indie-folk makeover"; Variety "indie-electro-folk/chamber-pop balladeer".
  - folk, alternativerock, indie: NME "Swift dives headfirst into the world of folk, alternative rock and indie". Pitchfork also calls it Swift's "'indie' album".
  - chamberpop: Variety.
  - dreampop: Irish Times "folksy dream-pop".
  - folkrock: NME, on Betty, "this new folk-rock sound".
  - electronica: Guardian "fractured, glitchy electronica".
  - country: NME, Betty has "moments of country". One track only, so it goes last.
- **energy 1, groove 1, tempo 2**: Variety "wholly banger-free", "mellow drum programming"; Variety also says "highly subdued".
- **electronic 2**: the base is acoustic and orchestral (piano, guitars, strings), with drum programming, OP-1 and synths on most Dessner tracks (credits; Guardian "glitchy electronica").
- **mood 1**: Irish Times "wistful, escapist and melancholy"; Guardian on loss of innocence.
- **theatrical 2**: RS 2020 list "stark, elegant intimacy". Guardian: This Is Me Trying grows into "racked orchestral grandeur" (one song only).
- **experimental 2**: Guardian calls it "her most experimental"; Pitchfork calls it "mainstream 'indie' homage than innovation". The two disagree, so it sits in the middle.
- **polish 3**: Pitchfork says the production is "more minimal than she typically goes for"; NME says "The glossy production ... is cast aside".
- **voice soft|clear**: Guardian on her voice "weatherworn yet at peace" and her "vocal directness".
- **instruments**: the Fader credits list piano, acoustic and electric guitar, synth, synth bass, Mellotron, drums, percussion, bass, keyboards, B3 organ, violin, viola, cello (orchestration), saxophone, trombone, trumpet, pedal steel and lap steel, slide guitar, accordion, flute, clarinet, harmonica, harpsichord, vibraphone (mallets) and Wurlitzer (electric piano). NME mentions "twinkling music box instrumentals".
- **production**
  - drumprogramming and op1: credits ("Drum Programming", "OP1").
  - stringarrangements: "Orchestration by Bryce Dessner / Rob Moose".
  - layeredvocals: NME "encased in layered vocals".
  - fieldrecordings: "peace", "Field Recording" in the credits.
  - programming: Antonoff's tracks, "Programming".
- **lyrics**: Pitchfork "works of fiction and autofiction", the teen heartbreak trilogy. Guardian on nostalgia, loss of innocence and self-conception (identity). Variety calls it "most overtly contemplative" (introspection).
- **producers**: Grammy credits "Joe Alwyn, Jack Antonoff, Aaron Dessner & Taylor Swift, producers". Swift's own credit is dropped. Dessner is first: Pitchfork says production is "mostly by Dessner".
- **sounds_like**
  - Lana Del Rey and Florence + the Machine: Pitchfork, "sonically situates her closest to Lana and chamber-pop belter Florence Welch".
  - Sufjan Stevens: Pitchfork, "Sufjan Stevens if he killed his more ambitious tendencies"; Guardian, "More than one song evokes ... Sufjan Stevens circa Carrie and Lowell".
  - Mazzy Star, Imogen Heap and The Cranberries [6/16]: Pitchfork, "The tracks with Antonoff ... lean into the Mazzy Star swoon ..., Swift's ongoing fascination with Imogen Heap, and a twinge of the Cranberries". Antonoff produced 6 of the 16 tracks per the credits: my tears ricochet, mirrorball, august, this is me trying, illicit affairs and betty.
  - Bob Dylan [1/16]: Irish Times, "Betty (with its Bob Dylan-esque instrumentals)".
  - Excluded:
    - The National: Dessner's band.
    - Big Red Machine: Dessner and Vernon.
    - Bon Iver: a guest.
    - Mary Chapin Carpenter: a lyric-only comparison (NME).
- **influenced_by**: empty. I found no statement by Swift or her producers naming an outside influence on this album.
- **landmark 1**: Rolling Stone 2020 list, "may go down in history as the definitive quarantine album"; Pitchfork, "will forever be known as Taylor Swift's 'indie' album".
- **Reception 5/5/3**: importance 5, because it remade her career (Grammy AOTY, RS #1 of 2020, Pitchfork's "forever" line). Acclaim 5. Mainstream 3: an artist-led record with an indie producer.

Cells:
- **genres**

  - ballad: Exclaim "the majority of the tracks are soft ballads".
  - teenpop: Stereogum "landmark release in the teen-pop continuum".
  - alternativerock: Pitchfork "alt-rock squall à la the Kills".
  - grunge: Pitchfork "grunge guitar and distortion" on brutal.
  - poprock: Stereogum "hard-edged '90s pop-rock".
  - powerballad: Pitchfork, drivers license is "a piano-driven power ballad".
  - bedroompop: Stereogum, "a professional Top 40 tint on the bedroom-pop".
  - folkpop: Inquirer "delicate folk-pop on ... Happier and ... Deja Vu".
- **energy 2, tempo 2, groove 1**: mostly ballads (Exclaim), with three loud cuts.
- **electronic 1**: guitars, piano and strings throughout. No source stresses electronics.
- **mood 1**: Pitchfork "melancholy and mischief"; a breakup album.
- **theatrical 3**: NPR "underscored with operatic flair"; Stereogum "melodramatic".
- **experimental 1**: classic pop structures.
- **polish 3**: Pitchfork, "Universal left her and Nigro largely to their own devices".
- **voice soft|powerful|spoken**: Pitchfork, "gossamer falsetto and the emotive belt", "a wry sprechstimme on brutal", "flute-like lilt".
- **instruments**:
  - piano: Exclaim "piano-driven".
  - acousticguitar: Stereogum "finger-plucked acoustic guitar".
  - electricguitar: Stereogum "jagged power-chord guitar riff".
  - bass: Exclaim "bubbling bass".
  - drums: Exclaim "punchy drums".
  - strings: Pitchfork "lugubrious strings".
- **production**:
  - belting and falsetto: Pitchfork.
  - layeredvocals: Stereogum "a choir of Rodrigos"; NPR "harmonizing with herself".
  - distortion: Pitchfork.
- **lyrics**: heartbreak (Pitchfork). Insecurity and self-image (introspection, identity). Social media and influencer culture (NPR, on "jealousy, jealousy"). Wisecracking (irony, Pitchfork).
- **producers**: Grammy credits "Alexander 23, Daniel Nigro & Olivia Rodrigo, producers". Rodrigo is dropped. Stereogum: Nigro "co-wrote and produced every song".
- **sounds_like**
  - Lorde and Billie Eilish: Stereogum, "irons out the quirks from Lorde's conversational tone and Billie Eilish's jazzy swooning slow jams" (album-wide). RS also says "like Lorde covering a Dookie B side".
  - Taylor Swift: Inquirer, "The most obvious influence is Taylor Swift"; RS says Traitor is "the long-lost cousin of My Tears Ricochet".
  - Paramore [1/11]: Pitchfork and Stereogum (good 4 u). See the uncertainty note below.
  - Kelly Clarkson [1/11]: Stereogum.
  - Green Day (Dookie) [1/11]: RS.
  - Ingrid Michaelson [1/11] and The Kills [1/11]: Pitchfork.
  - Phoebe Bridgers [1/11]: Stereogum, drivers license "began like a trembling Phoebe Bridgers track".
- **influenced_by**
  - Alanis Morissette (Jagged Little Pill): Rodrigo herself, "It's reminiscent of sort of the albums like Jagged Little Pill that I love" (American Songwriter).
  - Taylor Swift: Rodrigo, "She's such a brilliant storyteller, and that's just so inspiring to me" (same source). Pitchfork: "She's said that the shouty bridge in Swift's 'Cruel Summer' directly inspired her own in 'deja vu'".
- **Uncertain**: Paramore (Hayley Williams, Josh Farro) and Swift were later credited as songwriters (interpolation credits on good 4 u, 1 step forward and deja vu). I treated those as quotation credits, not collaboration, and kept them. The owner may prefer to drop Paramore from sounds_like.
- **landmark 1**: Stereogum "a landmark release in the teen-pop continuum ... a generational touchstone".
- **Reception 5/4/3**: importance 5, the debut that made her. Acclaim 4: Pitchfork 7.0, Grammy Best Pop Vocal Album, positive NPR/RS/Stereogum. Mainstream 3: artist-written, a single producer, left alone by the label (Pitchfork).
