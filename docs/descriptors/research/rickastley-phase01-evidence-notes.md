# Rick Astley — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes

Research basis: original-era release credits where available, MusicBrainz per-track relationships, professional album reviews, and a direct artist interview. Numeric scores and `sounds_like` are independent editorial judgments, not review ratings. Each `influenced_by` field is deliberately blank because the available direct interview documents broad vocal influences, not album-specific writing or production influences.

## Whenever You Need Somebody (1987)

- **Genres and scoring.** AllMusic lists dance-pop, adult contemporary, club/dance and soft rock. The emphatic PWL rhythmic programming justifies energy 4, electronic 4, groove 5 and polish 5; calmer ballads reduce no single-track rating to an album-wide genre claim. Mood 4 reflects a predominantly buoyant, romantic presentation despite heartbreak songs.
- **Instrumentation.** Original record and track relationships credit Matt Aitken on guitars and keyboards, Mike Stock on keyboards, Gary Barnacle on saxophone, and Linn 9000 and Fairlight programming in recordings. No instrument token is inferred solely from PWL's reputation.
- **Production.** Production is split: Stock Aitken Waterman produce tracks 1–4, 6, 10; Phil Harding and Ian Curnow produce tracks 5 and 7; Daize Washbourn produces tracks 8 and 9. Linn 9000 and Fairlight are explicit in MusicBrainz credits; programmed percussion and layered backing voices are recorded in detailed credits. The Fairlight is credited by programmer Ian Curnow, not listed as an additional producer without evidence.
- **Sounds-like** references to contemporaneous PWL releases are editorial; not documented musical influences. `influenced_by` blank.
- **Sources.** AllMusic album review; MusicBrainz original 1987 release; RCA vinyl credit transcription; official BMG recordings metadata.

## Hold Me in Your Arms (1988)

- **Genres and scoring.** AllMusic explicitly lists dance-pop and adult contemporary. `soulpop` is an editorial secondary classification, reflecting Astley's own-written ballads; `rnb` is a low-weight track-specific classification related to the Motown cover, not an album-wide claim. More midtempo and acoustic elements than debut: electronic 3, tempo 3, groove 4.
- **Instrumentation.** MusicBrainz documents Fairlight and other programming by Ian Curnow, guitars by Robert Ahwai and Roddy Matthews, saxophone by Phil Todd, and drums/keyboards by Rick Astley and Daize Washbourn on specified tracks.
- **Production.** MusicBrainz original-edition annotation differentiates: tracks 2, 3, 5 and 6 by Stock Aitken Waterman; track 1 co-produced by Rick Astley, Phil Harding and Ian Curnow; tracks 4 and 7–9 by Rick Astley and Daize Washbourn; track 10 by Harding and Curnow. Do not conflate producers with additional engineers. The mix of producers is reflected in the CSV, without implying every producer worked on all songs.
- **Cover caution.** Ain't Too Proud to Beg is a recording of a song associated with Motown. The covered performer is NOT a demonstrated musical influence and is not entered in `influenced_by`.

## Free (1991)

- **Genres and scoring.** Alex Henderson (AllMusic) reports a deliberate move from dance-focused SAW productions to adult-contemporary pop/R&B, with some rock-influenced material. The gentler approach gives energy 2, groove 2, electronic 2. `softrock` and `poprock` are secondary editorial interpretations; the latter is supported by the critic's explicit description of Is This Really Love.
- **Instrumentation.** AllMusic, MusicBrainz and session credit transcriptions identify Dave West on synthesizers, drum programming, piano, organ and Rhodes; acoustic drums by Vinnie Colaiuta and Per Lindvall; double bass by Niels-Henning Ørsted Pedersen; Elton John on piano; Anne Dudley's string arrangements; Jerry Hey's horn arrangements; saxophone, brass and guitars. The Andraé Crouch Choir appears on Cry for Help, supporting voice `choral` but not claiming the album is wholly gospel.
- **Production.** Gary Stevenson and Rick Astley are both officially credited producers; Rob Fisher is a contributor, co-writer and pianist, NOT a credited producer. Techniques limited to documented drum programming, overdubs and string/horn arrangements.
- **Influence caution.** Michael McDonald features as a backing vocalist and contributed a song: a guest does NOT establish `influenced_by`. He stays only in `sounds_like` as sonic comparison.

## Body & Soul (1993)

- **Genres and scoring.** AllMusic lists adult contemporary and dance-pop. Its category Pop/Rock supports pop as the broader family. The original RCA credits show extensive synths, drum programming, Moog bass and live musicians; `electronic` is secondary texture rather than an assertion that this is a pure electronic album. `rnb` and `soulpop` are cautious secondary editorial interpretations, not explicit AllMusic tags. Mood 2 captures reflective/sentimental tracks and the romantic lyric focus.
- **Instrumentation.** Original RCA CD credit transcription and AllMusic list Dave West's drum programming and keyboards, Richard Cottle's electric piano/synths, Mark Brzezicki on drums, Ian Thomas on percussion, Nigel Hitchcock on saxophone, guitar and acoustic/classical guitar contributions and credited Moog bass. The album includes both programmed and live drums.
- **Production.** Rick Astley and Gary Stevenson are credited producers, with Tom Lord-Alge credited for mixing only. `moog` is omitted from the production field because credits identify Moog bass as instrumentation, without a specific synth model or album-wide production method.
- **Style caution.** Lisa Stansfield appears as a songwriter, not a documented album influence or producer. No album-specific influences are asserted.

## Disagreements and source boundaries

- AllMusic's Pop/Rock category is broader than a specific pop-rock style: use `pop` when reviewing the actual pop arrangements, not to infer `rock` across all four albums.
- MusicBrainz track relationships are often inherited onto remasters and include inconsistent romanizations or duplicates; the vintage original-edition producer annotation is prioritized for *Hold Me in Your Arms*.
- `sounds_like` is explicitly an editorial resemblance without claiming artist testimony.
- Confidence `high` applies to album identity, core genre and performer/producer credits, not to every secondary editorial genre. All four rows remain `draft`.
