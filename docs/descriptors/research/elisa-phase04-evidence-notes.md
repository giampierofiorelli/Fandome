# Fandome / Elisa / Phase 04 — evidence and field decisions

Research date: 2026-10-24. Source of truth: docs/album-descriptors-guide.md, GitHub blob `e5b62ea899e699c1e77ba22b4f44137a4109c7d1`.
Scope: **only two original studio-album descriptors**, with their precise Fandome catalog IDs. No previous-phase rows. All ratings are editorial and remain **draft**. No field has an artificial cap on listed values; the guide still prescribes a 15-word maximum for `notes` and numeric scales 0–5.

## 1. Diari aperti (2018) | `elisa-diariaperti`

**Edition**: Universal confirms the 26 October 2018 album had eleven Italian-language tracks [D01]. The November 2019 *Diari aperti (Segreti svelati)* combines the original record with later EP material and new duets [D07]. The original is used for every judgement.

**Genre evidence**:
- `pop`: Italian mainstream pop and singer-songwriter craft are central [D03–D05].
- `songwriter`: diaries, childhood memories, parenthood, close voice and acoustic/piano-led compositions [D03–D06].
- `itpop`: a **specific** Calcutta-linked single, not the album's dominant architecture [D04]. Canonical token absent from current genre set; add to site/validator if retaining.
- `reggae`/`electronic`: **specific** track *L'estate è già fuori*, characterized by Rockol as reggae with electronic touches [D03]. **Do not** let an isolated arrangement inflate overall electronic score.
- `calypsopop`: Rolling Stone describes the **same track** as calypso pop [D04]. This is a critical vocabulary difference rather than evidence that the entire album is calypso. Token absent from current set; add before validation.
- `punk`: **intentionally excluded**. Rolling Stone's title uses punk as a rhetorical characterization of lyrical boldness; the review explicitly places the music in Italian pop, not a punk-rock genre [D04].

**Numeric rationales** (0–5 subjective on source-backed album sound):
- `energy=2`, `tempo=2`: Rockol describes many whispered and soft arrangements, not the propulsion of 2016's *On* [D03]. Some brisk songs do not determine the median.
- `electronic=1`: guitar, piano, brass, ukulele/banjo/mandolin and strings dominate original recordings; scattered programming and one electronically tinged reggae cut [D02–D04].
- `mood=2`: reflective vulnerability/fragility dominates despite bright exceptions (*L'estate è già fuori*) [D03–D06].
- `theatrical=2`: conversational intimacy and De Gregori duet; climaxes exist but are not sustained stage drama [D03,D05].
- `experimental=1`: mainly accessible pop structures. Rolling Stone sees some daring ideas but judges much of the record conventional, while All Music Italia warmly welcomes its songwriting [D04–D05].
- `polish=4`: elaborate but unobtrusive studio work, arranged strings, brass and acoustic instrumentation by multiple credited specialists [D02–D03].
- `groove=1`: reflective, low-danceability overall; one reggae/calypso-pop accent [D03–D04].

**Voice**: `soft|clear|powerful` (the critics emphasize muted/whispered delivery, clear song interpretations and occasional strong melodic peaks; sequence reflects the album, not Elisa's maximum vocal capacity) [D03,D05].
**Lyrics**: `introspection|love|identity|nostalgia|storytelling`. Direct diary material; childhood, relationships, raising children and personal retrospection [D01,D03,D06].
**Scene**: Italian introspective songwriter pop; a descriptive scene phrase, not a newly invented genre.
**Producers**: album-level `Elisa|Taketo Gohara|Andrea Rigonat`, with `Katoo` credited as producer on *Tua per sempre* in original MusicBrainz record [D02]. Included because field has no producer-count limit and track-specific credits qualify. Do not confuse engineer, mixer, arranger, songwriter or De Gregori as producer. No MACE credit: Rolling Stone specifically explains that the artist discussed a MACE arrangement **not present** in this album [D04].
**sounds_like**: `Max Gazzè|Riccardo Sinigallia|Mango|Jason Mraz`, comparative sonic/editorial references only to **specific** pieces [D04–D05]. They are not purported formative influences of the album. `influenced_by` deliberately blank because no supported causal album influence was established.
**landmark=1**: editorial assessment of a distinctive Italian-language diary/concept pivot supported by multiple contemporary reviews and ongoing prominence [D01,D03,D04].
**confidence=high** for sonic and thematic assessment from three expert reviews + originals, notwithstanding moderate local genre uncertainty.

**Dissent**: Rockol and All Music Italia praise intimacy; Rolling Stone considers large portions overly conservative and some standout rhythmic arrangements refreshing [D03–D05]. This is a disagreement about artistic achievement, **not** conflicting evidence of a predominantly rock album.

## 2. Ritorno al futuro/Back to the Future (2022) | `elisa-ritornoalfuturobacktothefuture`

**Edition**: original 18 February 2022 Island/Universal two-disc CD has **25 tracks** (12 Italian and 13 English) [R01,R03]. Some digital/bonus versions contain **27** including *On Me* and *Shout* [R03,R08], and some sources mix these versions in their discussions. All numeric assessments are oriented to the official original 25-track CD; genre attributions to bonus-only tracks are excluded.

**Genres**: OndaRock categorizes the **album** `electro-pop` and `indie-rock` [R03]. Specialist critics also describe multiple `rnb`, `soul`, `rock` and `electronic` pieces on both discs [R04–R06]. `house` (*Seta*, *Palla al centro*), `gospel` (*Show's Rollin*), and `hiprock` (*Fuckin' Believers*) are **song-level** accents only [R04–R05]. The generic hip-hop label here captures **isolated** urban/hip-hop textures, not a predominant rap record [R04–R06]. `hiprock` is a specialist's actual hyphenated descriptor for one track, normalized to letters-only; add to site's registry and validator if accepted. No automatic genre scores from guest artists. `indierock` already exists in current site set.

**Numeric rationales**:
- `energy=3`: energetic summer/urban sequences and powerful vocals counter slower introspective English songs [R03–R06].
- `electronic=3`: explicitly combines programmed house/urban beats, synthesizer/Moog layers, guitar, piano, bass and live drumming [R01,R03,R07]; not fully electronic.
- `mood=3`: sunshine and joy in Italian tracks, intimate/darker strands in English [R03,R06].
- `theatrical=3`: dramatic vocals and fuller arrangements contrast with understated soul/indie parts [R03–R06].
- `experimental=3`: broad production and collaborations were a change from the previous diary-pop project, but retain accessible structures [R03–R07].
- `polish=5`: heavily produced multilayered contemporary studio record with many named specialists and orchestrated performance [R01–R04].
- `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: uneven by intent across 25 songs, club/house cuts and mid-tempo soul balanced by slower ballads [R03–R06].

**Voice**: `powerful|clear|soft` based on substantial album-level powerful belting and soul phrasing contrasted with gentler performances [R03–R06]. Rap collaborators are not assumed to alter the **lead voice type of Elisa**.
**Lyrics**: `love|social|identity|introspection` as personally articulated by Elisa regarding love, environment, sustainability, social expectations and outward gaze [R07–R08]. Avoid assigning `political` just because the artist mentions environmental protection.
**Languages**: Italian `it` and English `en` (12/13 original CD) [R01,R03]. Treat as a single double album, not two separate unrelated Fandome albums.
**Producers**: `Elisa|Andrea Rigonat|Sixpm|DRD|MACE|Venerus|Don Joe|Marz|Zef|Michelangelo|Shablo|Stevie Aiello`: 12 documented producer or **additional producer** credits from the label [R01] and MusicBrainz [R02]. `DRD` is Dardust's production name and `Sixpm` is Andrea Ferrara's production name: do not duplicate by listing aliases twice. `Michelangelo`, `Marz`, `Zef` are additional production on selected tracks, not implied overall controlling producers. No assumption that featuring Jovanotti, Rkomi or Elodie makes them producers.
**sounds_like**: sonic/artistic comparisons named by album specialist reviewers: `The Weeknd|Alicia Keys|Mary J. Blige|Florence + The Machine|Depeche Mode|Alanis Morissette|James Blake`. They are **heterogeneous track-level parallels**, not evidence the 25 songs sound uniform [R04–R06].
**influenced_by**: `The Weeknd` is described by Rockol as a strong stylistic reference for this specific project; *My Mission* is described by Rockol as a deliberate Depeche Mode homage [R04]. No other critics' sonic analogies are automatically promoted to documented causal influences.
**landmark=1**: editorial defining entry for its all-in, bilingual, multi-production pivot [R03–R08].
**confidence=high** for title/credits, album-wide sonic breadth, multilingual makeup. Local song-only genre tokens less certain and marked above, and scale numbers remain editorial.

**Dissent**: Rockol welcomes the collaborative plurality and restlessness [R04]; OndaRock admires its wider stylistic reach [R03]; SentireAscoltare [R05] and Newsic [R06] question whether the long double album achieves coherence and sustained emotional connection. Confidence is not a critic consensus score.
