## 1. `mina-minacantalucio` — *Minacantalucio* (1975)

**Identity and historical status.** Official Mina catalog C01 lists ten consecutive songs by Lucio Battisti (music) and Mogol (lyrics); C02 is the original independent PDU PLD L 6036 release. Some early packages bundle it with *La Mina*, but this is its own 10-track studio re-recording project, not a greatest-hits compilation of Mina singles. Album follows the 1973–74 phases; it is not the 1994 retrospective anthology *Mazzini canta Battisti*. Landmark=1 for conceptually unified reinterpretation of an iconic Italian composer; no chart-driven ranking is claimed.

**Genres / arrangement.** `pop`, `musicaleggera`, `italianpop` are broad classifications (C04). `blues` is secondary discography category (C04). `poprock` is cautious, **low-scope** genre based on the source songs' stylistic roots (C01, C05); Gabriel Yared arranged the album for orchestra, and the covers need not duplicate Battisti's arrangements. Claude may remove weak pop-rock if no version-level evidence survives. Genre ordering weighs actual vocal orchestral/pop identity above blues or rock association.

**Scale reasoning.** energy=2 and tempo=2: extensive reflective ballads (*Emozioni*, *I giardini di marzo*) with some livelier rock/pop songs rather than relentless forward propulsion (C01,C03). electronic=0: Yared's orchestrated acoustic studio treatment; neither synthesizer-forward production nor electronic genre supported (C01,C03). mood=2: nostalgic and bittersweet repertoire balances softer reverie against brighter cuts. theatrical=3: full vocal inflection and larger arrangements in *I giardini di marzo*, but not dramatic theatre throughout. experimental=2: reinterprets established compositions via Yared rather than avant-garde gestures. polish=4: arranged and conducted studio orchestration; production engineering fine-grain unknown. groove=2: a few rhythmic songs within a ballad-centered sequence. Most scale estimates have medium support; high *row* confidence comes from original documentation and a coherent single-composer concept, **not** evidence of every scale.

**Voices / lyrics / language.** `powerful|clear` singer led, no need for choral designation. Love/heartbreak/introspection/nostalgia themes reflect the explicitly named compositions, not verbatim analysis of every lyric. `it` per ten original Italian-language Battisti–Mogol covers. `sounds_like` (Mia Martini, Ornella Vanoni, Battisti) is editorial comparison, not proven artistic influence. `influenced_by=Lucio Battisti` reflects a **directly documented project built entirely on his songbook**, not an unverified studio influence. Source C01 is sufficient for this narrowly defined conceptual link. Producer **blank**: C01/C03 explicitly credit Gabriel Yared only with *arrangements and conducting*, not album producer. Do not transfer him to producer column.

## 2. `mina-lamina` — *La Mina* (1975)

**Identity.** Separate 1975 PDU PLD L 6037 album, ten original LP recordings L01/L02/L06; packaged in a shared edition with *Minacantalucio* but independently indexed. All songs performed in Italian, including French and American covers translated into Italian (L01). `landmark=1` for definitive *L'importante è finire* single and contemporary mature repertoire; one standout song does not define the album's overall sonic features.

**Genres.** Pop/Italian pop dominate; `chanson` and `ballad` are **explicit original issue style tags** L02. `musicaleggera` is the broad national style. `bossanova` and `funk` specifically derive **only from the Pino Presti arrangement of L'importante è finire** L07, which is an AI-assisted secondary summary: label LOW, strictly track-level, and avoid promoting either to dominant album genres without corroboration. Most remaining LP is arranged ballad-pop, adaptations, torch performance (L01,L02).

**Scales.** energy=2, tempo=2, groove=2 reflect substantial slow ballads and extended *Tu no* alongside isolated groove figures. electronic=1 is a conservative mix of predominantly live/arranged orchestration and reported electronic keyboard/drum details on **one** song; this detail comes from weak L07, so confidence medium. mood=2 for emotive relationship conflict across track list. theatrical=3 due to ballad interpretations and restrained dramatic accents. experimental=1 because conventional song arrangements predominate. polish=4 supported by professional PDU and rotating experienced arrangers, not digital techniques. `voice=powerful|clear|soft` multi-register performance rather than three lead singers. `lyrics=sensuality` weighted among other themes due to known Malgioglio song L03. `sounds_like` is editorial.

**Producer credits.** Pino Presti orchestrates and arranged four tracks, Gabriel Yared two, Toto Torquati two, Shel Shapiro and Riccardi remaining L01; this **does not automatically establish producer roles**. Leave `producers` empty pending verified first-press production credits. Some descriptions credit Presti with producing *L'importante è finire* specifically, but no definitive album-wide sleeve production credit is shown; distinguish from production of the LP.

## 3. `mina-singolare` — *Singolare* (1976)

**Identity.** 1976 original standalone 10-track PDU PLD L 6067 album (S01, S03). Issued paired with the next album as a double set, but conceptually the **solo-lead side** versus Plurale's overlaid chorus textures. Do not create a fifth combined album row. `landmark=1` because the coordinated Singolare/Plurale studio concept is artistically distinctive; critical discussion S04 is independent but subjective.

**Genres.** Album Pop (S03); Italian popular song; soft rock and pop rock from S04/S06 album assessments; ballad from MusicBrainz original release group S03. Note these are **not** subgenres assigned to all ten tracks. `Sognando`, `Colpa mia`, `Nuda`, `Io camminerò` and Brazilian-origin *Pra dizer adeus* cover demonstrate varied repertoire and a mix of acoustic, band and soft rock arrangements (S01).

**Scales.** energy=3 / tempo=3: more rock/pop drive than *La Mina*, balanced by slower ballads (S04). electronic=0: guitars and studio orchestra predominate, with no credible synth-led evidence (S01,S05). mood=2: darker/melancholic songs with lighter moments, explicitly noted by critic S04. theatrical=3: intense lead vocal and arrangement climaxes, still a solo style versus Plurale. experimental=1: traditional arrangements; originality is conceptual pairing not track-level innovation. polish=4: 1976 La Basilica recordings and multiple arranged studio sessions S05. groove=2: vocal ballad/pop record with some rhythmic pieces.

**Voice/thematics.** `powerful|clear`, Italian lead throughout (even Brazilian and other non-Italian originals are in Italian translations). Lyric categories love, heartbreak, introspection, sensuality are album-level abstractions with variable track coverage; not claims of identical themes on every song. `sounds_like` editorial only. Producer blank: S01/S05 provide **arranger** roles for Pino Presti, Gianni Ferrio, Tony Mimms, Roberto Soffici, Simonluca and Massimo Salerno, but **no uniform producer credit**. Do not mistake the mix / studio staff for producers.

## 4. `mina-plurale` — *Plurale* (1976)

**Identity and primary evidence.** Original standalone PDU PLD L 6068 contains **11 tracks**, 34:56 P03, distinct from *Singolare*; reissues and combined double sets are **not extra rows**. The artist's own sleeve reproduction P01 states: all choral parts were recorded via **successive overdubs of Mina's one voice**, allowing groups from trio up to **16 voices**; Ferrio arranged all voices and instruments. In firsthand interview P02, Massimiliano Pani confirms a 16-track recorder and identifies Gianni Ferrio's production work, making `producers=Gianni Ferrio` grounded, independently of instrumental arranger credit. On official page P01, **Alba Ferrio = assistant to production**, **Nuccio Rinaldis = engineer**; neither belongs in producer column. Musicbase P05 misleadingly elevates Alba Ferrio to producer; treat as secondary metadata conflict and honor primary record and Pani. `landmark=1`: unusual overdub architecture and documented prize history P06. This is **studio choral**, not a multi-person chorus or live album.

**Genres.** `jazz|vocaljazz|standards` reflect standard material with vocal harmonies, notably *Moonlight Serenade*, *Pennsylvania 6-5000*, *Mood Indigo*. `bigband|swing` are only applicable to the Glenn Miller-associated repertoire and Ferrio's orchestra; `pop` from Qobuz and issue label, Italian `musicaleggera` broad. `blues` explicitly one song *Scettico Blues*. `folk` explicitly one traditional Alpini song *Il testamento del capitano*. `traditionalpop` editorial umbrella for popular standards, not a separate documented era or source. Scope is all recorded in genre CSV, no cap on number of genres.

**Scales.** energy=2: orchestra and lively swing intermittently, not sustained high-intensity rhythmic drive; electronic=0: natural orchestra and overdubbed *human voice*, multitrack recording does not mean electronic instruments. mood=3: mixed wistful standards and exuberant stylized novelty/spoken sketches. theatrical=4: 16-voice self-choir is grander than a typical pop record, while nuanced swing ballads prevent 5. experimental=4: radical studio vocal stacking, but recognizable songs and conventional harmony; production is complex rather than avant-garde for its own sake. polish=4: careful 16-track construction in professional La Basilica sessions. tempo=3 due to swing material and restrained pieces; groove=3 because swing sections are danceable but not modern dance production. `voice=choral|clear|powerful` vocal multiplicity made by a single performer; all three belong to singer identity, not extra credited choir. Lyrics `love|nostalgia|storytelling` vary by repertoire.

**Language caution.** Primary source P01 lists track titles in English, Italian and Spanish, and *Michelle* includes French lyrics. Set `en|it|es|fr`, but note Piedmontese performance may be present in secondary P07 and **should not be treated as fully verified official language without listening/transcript**. The guide expects ISO language codes; if confirmed, Piedmontese would be `pms`, not `it` or `fr`. Do not infer language from cover song title alone. `sounds_like` editorial parallels with close-harmony groups, jazz vocalists and big-band arrangements; not `influenced_by`: playing *Mood Indigo* does not establish a stated Duke Ellington influence on Mina's conception. No documented `influenced_by` for this album in reliable sources.
