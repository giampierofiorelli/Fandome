# Lana Del Rey — Phase 01 evidence notes

Scope: original releases through 2014 only, researched 26 October 2026. This is **four incremental rows**, not cumulative. 2010 debut kept even though pulled from shops. `Paradise` is an independent 8-track EP; `Born to Die: The Paradise Edition` reuses earlier tracks and is not imported. All scale numbers are editorial readings of cited album-specific sonic descriptions, **not objective measurements**.

## lanadelrey-lanadelray — *Lana Del Ray* (2010)

- **Release / credit:** Originally released digitally by 5 Points in January 2010, later withdrawn. Original printed name is *Lana Del Ray* (spelled Ray) and the project is also called *Lana Del Ray A.K.A. Lizzy Grant*. The row's `artist` intentionally matches Fandome canonical `Lana Del Rey`, while title and slug keep original spelling. Producer **David Kahne** is identified independently by the musician (Pitchfork 2011) and original-track MusicBrainz credits. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/features/rising/8657-lana-del-rey/); [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/every-lana-del-rey-album-ranked/); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/77e8378d-7b0c-47e5-ba38-c73ffee6027e).
- **Genres and strengths:** Album of the Year labels `indiepop`, `songwriter`, `chamberpop`, `artpop`, `indierock`, `indiesurf`: these are **aggregate metadata, not six independent expert descriptions**. Pitchfork uses *dusty Americana* for the record. Slant calls “Gramma” trip-hop-infused and “Raise Me Up” guitar-driven; therefore `triphop` and the surf/rock margins must not dominate the algorithm. `altpop` is the wider stylistic umbrella. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/71520-lana-del-rey-lana-del-ray-aka-lizzy-grant.php); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/features/rising/8657-lana-del-rey/); [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/every-lana-del-rey-album-ranked/).
- **Instruments and production:** Slant explicitly names a guitar-driven track. Record only `guitar`; its precise electric/acoustic kind is not established. `production` is **empty**, because Kahne's producer credit does not prove any particular equipment, tape technique, DAW, vocal treatment or studio effect. No 808, samples or drum machine inferred from the trip-hop tag. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/every-lana-del-rey-album-ranked/).
- **Scales and themes:** Energy 2, electronic 2, mood 1, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 2, groove 2. Slant contrasts bouncier glossy cuts with slower, gloomy Americana; mood is dark, but novelty/club passages occur. Lyrics: love, heartbreak, narrative American fantasies, sensuality and self-presentation. `raspy` is secondary; breathy / intimate voice predominates. Reduced `confidence=medium`: no access to original 5 Points full session sheets. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/every-lana-del-rey-album-ranked/); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/features/rising/8657-lana-del-rey/).
- **Influence guardrail:** `sounds_like` names are comparative suggestions; no evidence that those exact artists drove **this** 2010 album, so `influenced_by` is empty. The album's historical importance as precursor does not automatically make it a landmark relative to later defining albums.

## lanadelrey-borntodie — *Born to Die* (2012)

- **Genres and order:** The GRAMMY feature explicitly names baroque pop and describes orchestral string writing integrated with hip-hop; Pitchfork and Guardian name trip-hop. AOTY groups alt-pop, chamber pop, trip-hop, art pop and baroque pop. The broad indie-pop and orchestral-pop tags are less distinctive, placed after the core. `sadcore` is a critic/artist-era “Hollywood sadcore” descriptor (new token) rather than a longstanding standard genre; tag with uncertainty. R&B is specifically “Off to the Races”, placed last. [GRAMMY](https://www.grammy.com/news/lana-del-rey-born-die-album-record-year-anniversary/); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/16223-lana-del-rey/); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/3061-lana-del-rey-born-to-die.php); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/jan/29/lana-del-rey-born-die-review).
- **Instruments:** Album sources name sweeping **strings** and orchestral arrangements, **piano** and guitar arpeggios. FADER calls its orchestral sounds MIDI strings; the tone *does not establish a specific analogue synthesizer model or orchestral recording technique*. Therefore list the audible verified families `strings|piano|guitar` and avoid guessed branded machines. [GRAMMY](https://www.grammy.com/news/lana-del-rey-born-die-album-record-year-anniversary/); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/jan/29/lana-del-rey-born-die-review); [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2019/08/30/lana-del-rey-guide-best-tracks-list-norman-rockwell-2019).
- **Production and producer boundary:** `stringarrangements` documented in label metadata (Larry Gold, Steve Tirpak, Dan Heath); `drumprogramming` by credited Patrik Berger and Chris Braide; `sampling` refers to the FADER's explicit vocal samples (not proof of sampling hardware). Qobuz names Emile Haynie lead producer and other genuine producer/co-producers in track-level credit. Exclude Jeff Bhasker on “National Anthem”, where he is credited **only additional producer**, while retaining him for “Diet Mountain Dew” (`CoProducer`); likewise exclude Justin Parker's additional production on the title cut, but retain him for “Radio” and “Carmen”. Exclude Dan Heath where his only role is string arranger, and lyricists with no producer credit. [Qobuz credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/born-to-die-lana-del-rey/0060252787091); [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2019/08/30/lana-del-rey-guide-best-tracks-list-norman-rockwell-2019).
- **Influences:** In a 2011 interview discussing this sound, Lana explicitly evokes **Thomas Newman's *American Beauty* score** and early **Bruce Springsteen**. Both are musical touchstones, so the artists go into `influenced_by` (not literary/filmic imagery), albeit soundtrack-first for Newman. [Interview](https://www.justjared.com/2011/12/05/lana-del-rey-kurt-cobain/).
- **Voice, scales, language:** Breathiness and subdued singing dominate (`soft|clear`); certain narrative lines verge on spoken/rhythmically recited speech (`spoken` subordinate). Energy 3 / electronic 3 / mood 1 / theatrical 4 / experimental 2 / polish 5 / tempo 2 / groove 3: lush, pulsing yet mostly slow-to-moderate and gloomy. Lyrics examine romance, money, fatalism, sensuality and stylised memory. Core album is English with a brief **French spoken verse by guest Lenha Labelle** on “Carmen”;  `fr` is minor and language ordering keeps `en` first. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/16223-lana-del-rey/); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/jan/29/lana-del-rey-born-die-review); [GRAMMY](https://www.grammy.com/news/lana-del-rey-born-die-album-record-year-anniversary/).
- **Critical disagreement:** Pitchfork initially disliked the presentation; GRAMMY's anniversary feature views the identical orchestral/hip-hop fusion as historically innovative. `landmark=1` concerns identity and lasting reach, not a consensus high review score. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/16223-lana-del-rey/); [GRAMMY](https://www.grammy.com/news/lana-del-rey-born-die-album-record-year-anniversary/).

## lanadelrey-paradise — *Paradise* (2012)

- **Identity:** Distinct 8-song original EP of November 2012: “Ride”, “American”, “Cola”, “Body Electric”, “Blue Velvet”, “Gods & Monsters”, “Yayo”, “Bel Air”. “Blue Velvet” is a new performance of an existing song, not a compilation of old Lana recordings; “Yayo” is a **new recording** of an earlier composition. The *Paradise Edition* appends this EP to *Born to Die*: do **not** create a separate descriptor for that reissue. [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/paradise-lana-del-rey/0060253718149); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/paradise-ep/lana-del-rey); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/paradise-mr0004230772).
- **Genres:** AOTY lists alt-pop, chamber pop, art pop and dream pop. The EP's liner-note-derived genre description identifies baroque pop and trip-hop. Metacritic / AllMusic add broad Adult Alternative Pop/Rock and Alternative/Indie Rock taxonomies, deliberately trailing after more informative genres. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/4854-lana-del-rey-paradise.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/paradise-mr0004230772); [release/liner-note account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(Lana_Del_Rey_EP)).
- **Instruments:** Liner-note personnel explicitly name strings/violins, piano, electric guitar, generic guitar, organ, Mellotron, synthesizer, drums, bass guitar, glockenspiel, brass and other minor instruments. Main first; *brass* and *glockenspiel* are occasional tracks (Cola), and Mellotron occurs on Body Electric. Do not transform a string-orchestra credit into an assumption about every song. [Liner personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(Lana_Del_Rey_EP)); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/paradise-mr0004230772).
- **Production and names:** Official distributor track credits name **Rick Rubin** for “Ride”; **Rick Nowels** for “American”, “Cola”, “Body Electric”; **Dan Heath** for “Body Electric”, “Yayo”, “Bel Air”; **Emile Haynie** for “American” (co), “Blue Velvet”, “Gods & Monsters”, “Yayo”; **Tim Larcombe** for “Gods & Monsters”; **DK** co-production for “Cola”. Credited **string arrangements** and **drum programming** justify those production tokens; musicians and string arrangers acting in that capacity alone are not producers. [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/paradise-lana-del-rey/0060253718149); [Liner personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(Lana_Del_Rey_EP)).
- **Scores / voices:** Slow orchestral songs / theatrical diction, particularly “Ride” and “Bel Air”: energy 2, electronic 2, mood 1, theatrical 5, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 1, groove 1. Soft passages and powerful climaxes explain `soft|clear|powerful`; romantic obsession, mythology, sexuality and spiritual imagery explain lyrics. This episode becomes a pivotal standalone Lana release (`landmark=1`), even as its 2012 contemporary reception remained divided. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/paradise-mr0004230772); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/paradise-ep/lana-del-rey).
- **Influences:** References to Elvis or Marilyn Monroe **inside lyrics** are cultural references, not documented musical shaping; “Blue Velvet” is a **cover**, not licence to list Bobby Vinton or other prior interpreters as sources of influence. Thus `influenced_by` intentionally blank.

## lanadelrey-ultraviolence — *Ultraviolence* (2014)

- **Genres and critical disagreements:** NME explicitly calls it desert rock; AOTY lists psychedelic rock, dream pop, neo-psychedelia, art pop, chamber pop; AllMusic classifies adult alternative pop/rock, alternative indie rock and contemporary singer-songwriter. AudioTechnology describes **trip-hop influences** as a residual texture, lower weight than psychedelic guitars. `desertrock` is a *new* token. `slowcore` is corroborated by album-level discographic labelling and slow-motion criticism but remains lower confidence than psychedelic rock. [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-lana-del-rey-15401-308249); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/15950-lana-del-rey-ultraviolence.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ultraviolence-mw0002677059); [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-robert-orton-on-lana-del-reys-ultraviolence).
- **Instruments:** Credits name electric and acoustic guitars, **pedal-steel guitar** (`steelguitar`, generic family), drums, electric and upright bass, Mellotron, piano, synthesizer, handclaps, saxophone, percussion and **Omnichord** (already in repository instrument labels). Strings appear on the orchestral “Old Money” track only and go near end; this is not the principal orchestrated pop of *Born to Die*. `omnichord` is documented for “Shades of Cool”. [MusicBrainz release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0a53ce48-aebc-49ce-9098-f4e2b476e951); [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ultraviolence-mw0002677059).
- **Live-production specificity:** Interview with Lana describes seven instrumentalists in one room and **single takes**; Metacritic describes the live-band sessions. These justify `liverecording|onetake`, **not** `concertrecording` (this is a studio LP). Mixer Robert Orton gives firsthand account of heavy reverb and core electric-guitar textures. MusicBrainz trackwise data specifically credits drummer programming on “Ultraviolence” / “Fucked My Way Up to the Top” and string arranging on “Old Money”; thus `drumprogramming|stringarrangements` represent some songs, not the entire LP. No DAW, microphone brand, drum machine or exact pedal-steel model asserted. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/12/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-album); [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-robert-orton-on-lana-del-reys-ultraviolence); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0a53ce48-aebc-49ce-9098-f4e2b476e951).
- **Producer / edition audit:** The **11-track standard album** documents Dan Auerbach (majority), Lana Del Rey, Blake Stranathan and Lee Foster (“Pretty When You Cry”), Greg Kurstin (“Money Power Glory”), and Daniel Heath (“Old Money”). On deluxe variants Paul Epworth is producer on bonus “Black Beauty”, and Rick Nowels producer on “Guns and Roses” but **vocal producer** on several others. Qobuz's summary calls Nowels a producer on “Sad Girl”, whereas detailed official personnel credits describe him as vocal producer for track 6. To avoid incorrect attribution as main album producer, the draft deliberately omits Nowels pending conflict resolution, notes the conflicting credit, and excludes bonus-only Paul Epworth. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0a53ce48-aebc-49ce-9098-f4e2b476e951); [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/ultraviolence-lana-del-rey/0060253786568); [Album personnel](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ultraviolence-mw0002677059).
- **Musical influences, not collaborators:** In her KROQ interview Del Rey says **The Eagles** and **The Beach Boys** directly inspired the 1970s West Coast fusion she pursued with Auerbach. In an Aftonbladet interview she explicitly connects **Nina Simone** and **Billie Holiday** to her minor-key blue-note vocal writing on the album. NME describes “West Coast” as **Chris Isaak-indebted**, valid as a critic-described musical debt; it is narrower to one lead single. Do **not** use Dan Auerbach (the actual producer), Lou Reed (would-be collaborator), The Crystals (a lyrical citation), or Jessie Mae Robinson (a cover's songwriter) as invented influences. [KROQ interview](https://www.antimusic.com/news/14/June/13Lana_Del_Rey_Explains_Ultraviolence_and_West_Coast_Inspirations.shtml); [Aftonbladet](https://www.aftonbladet.se/nojesbladet/musik/rockbjornen/a/1krOJJ/i-dont-want-to-glamourize-death); [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-lana-del-rey-15401-308249); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/jun/12/lana-del-rey-ultraviolence-album).
- **Numbers:** Energy 2 / electronic 1 / mood 0 / theatrical 4 / experimental 3 / polish 3 / tempo 1 / groove 1. Recording made with a live band, a far less glossy electronic bed; long slow songs, layered reverb, overt tragic melodrama. Lyrics: addiction and unstable love, loneliness, self-image, identity, irony (“Money Power Glory”), nostalgia; the primary vocals range from hushed to strong climaxes. Critics differ on whether unbroken languor makes this cohesive or monotonous. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/19449-lana-del-rey-ultraviolence/); [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/mix-masters-robert-orton-on-lana-del-reys-ultraviolence); [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-lana-del-rey-15401-308249).

## Safeguards

1. Original `Lana Del Ray` under different spelling; no unlicensed leaks (2006 *Sirens*, leaked demo compilations). *Kill Kill* 2008 EP and *Lana Del Rey* 2012 EP recycle songs included in full original studio albums, so no separate rows: duplicate sonic coverage would harm album-to-album comparison.
2. Correct `Paradise` as an independent EP, not *Born to Die: The Paradise Edition* duplicated studio record. No arbitrary live albums or compilation records.
3. Produced-by labels distinguish actual production from arrangements, songwriting, vocal production, executive production, and additional production. Where deluxe/standard credits differ, the standard album controls.
4. Only included cited 2014 album-specific music influences; general artist influence is separately disclosed in CLAUDE_IMPORT.
5. Scoring confidence: 2010 medium (sparse detailed credits), other rows high for overall dominant character, but individual genre tags from metadata and the 2010 equipment remain limited. Some genres are specifically labeled track / texture in `genre_evidence.csv`.
6. Neither *Stove* nor its proposed companion has been released as of 26 October 2026; do not add future titles or provisional dates. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/story/lana-del-rey-new-album/); [release status Oct 21](https://www.konserterinorge.no/artister/lana-del-rey/).
