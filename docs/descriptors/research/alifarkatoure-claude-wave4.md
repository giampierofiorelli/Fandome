## Reception reasoning

- **Aman Iman 4/4/2**: Christgau says it is the one Tinariwen album to own, and Pitchfork gave it 8.1. It was a crossover bid on an indie world label.
- **Afrique Victime 4/4/2**: Pitchfork 8.4 Best New Music; #7 on PAM's 2021 list and #5 on Afrocritik's; Songlines was mixed. It came out on Matador, an indie label.
- **Savane 4/5/1**: five reviews I opened call it a classic or a masterpiece, and Pitchfork calls it his defining posthumous statement. It is a traditional roots record.
- **The Mandé Variations 3/4/0**: strong reviews (Pitchfork 8.3, Guardian, Observer, Independent), with BBC mixed. It is solo instrumental kora.
- **Mogoya 4/4/2**: NPR calls it a "landmark release" and it is on PAM's decade list. It deliberately aims at younger, club audiences on a boutique label.
- **Metascores**: search snippets give Savane 94 and Mogoya 86. Metacritic returned 403, so these are not cited.

## Legacy

No source I opened says that any of these five albums influenced later music. Pitchfork's Savane review says "It seems that modern Saharan bands like Tinariwen and Etran Finitawa learned much from Touré", but that is about the artist, not this album (and Tinariwen predate Savane). `legacy.csv` therefore has only its header.

## Scenes (src/data/scenes.ts)

```ts
tinariwen: 'ML tmh 1970 afro,rock', mdoumoctar: 'NE tmh 2000 afro,rock', alifarkatoure: 'ML son 1960 afro,folk', toumanidiabate: 'ML bm 1980 afro,classical', oumousangare: 'ML bm 1980 afro,folk',
```

`blues` is not in the scenes.test.ts base list, so the desert-blues acts get `afro` plus `rock` or `folk`.

- **Tinariwen**: country ML; sung in Tamasheq (Pitchfork "sung in Tamashek"); formed 1979 (canon file "1979–"). The rock base follows Pitchfork's "more fully embraces rock texture".
- **Mdou Moctar**: country NE; Tamasheq (Pitchfork "sung primarily in Tamasheq"); active since 2008 (canon file "2008–"). The rock base follows Pitchfork's "psychedelic rock".
- **Ali Farka Touré**: country ML; active since the 1960s (canon file; his first MusicBrainz album is from 1976).
  - Language `son` (Songhai) is **uncertain**. Pitchfork calls the Sonrai "his own" people and says he "spoke seven regional languages". For Savane the Guardian says "mostly in Malian dialects such as Peul". No source I opened names his main sung language over the whole career. The alternative is `ff` (Fula/Peul).
- **Toumani Diabaté**: country ML; active since 1987 (canon file). He is mostly instrumental.
  - Language `bm` is **uncertain**: it stands for the Mande griot repertoire (Pitchfork: "Mali's Manding people"). No source names a sung language.
  - The classical base follows the Observer's "African classical music".
- **Oumou Sangaré**: country ML; Bambara (LOBF "sung in French-accented Bambara", Christgau "the Bambara lyrics"); debut 1989 (NPR "her 1989 debut, Moussolou").

## Artist-level influences (own words)

- **Mdou Moctar**
  - Boston Globe, 9 Sep 2021: “It’s true with time I came to discover many Western artists ... I love what Hendrix does, and I support his legend and his memory. The same for Eddie Van Halen — I love his guitar-tapping style.” https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/09/09/arts/saharan-guitarist-mdou-moctar-desert-storm-psychedelic-rock/
  - Abdallah Ag Oumbadougou is his primary influence according to Pitchfork ("He attributes this style to his primary influence, the Nigerien guitarist Abdallah Ag Oumbadougou"). That is reported speech, not a direct quote. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/mdou-moctar-afrique-victime/
  - The Skinny (paraphrase, not a quote): "a particular passion for the legendary Nigerien Abdallah Oumbadougou and his contemporaries in Tinariwen". https://www.theskinny.co.uk/music/interviews/mdou-moctar-interview-afrique-victime
  - Album-level influences (Van Halen, Black Flag, Black Uhuru, Black Sabbath, ZZ Top) are in rows.csv.
- **Tinariwen, Ali Farka Touré, Toumani Diabaté, Oumou Sangaré**: I found no influence stated in their own words. BBC (Jon Lusk) writes that Tinariwen "make no secret of their 'Western' influences – Hendrix, Robert Plant", but that is the critic's report.

## Discography sources and choices

- **Tinariwen**: https://musicbrainz.org/artist/5c98fc12-be83-4246-ae5b-2184192913b9
  - Left out: the 1993 Ténéré cassette and Kel Tinariwen (2022), a reissue of an early cassette, both because they are early local releases; and the live and remix releases.
  - Amassakoul: MusicBrainz says 2003, the Songlines 50 list says 2004 (international release). I used 2003, the original.
  - Emmaar: MusicBrainz says 2013, but Songlines' every-album feature gives "(V2 Records, 2014)" and Christgau gives Anti- 2014, so I used 2014.
  - Idrache (2024) is an album of archive recordings from the 1990s, issued as a new album. Hoggar (13 Mar 2026) is in MusicBrainz.
- **Mdou Moctar**: https://musicbrainz.org/artist/d85defaf-b82e-48be-beb5-2672099e392e
  - Left out: Akounak Tedalat Taha Tazoughai (soundtrack), the eight 2020 mixtapes, Afrique Refait (remixes) and the live sets.
- **Ali Farka Touré**: https://musicbrainz.org/artist/9be2a5ac-8201-489b-b5f6-91f958bf9060
  - Left out: the 1976–87 Sonodisc LPs, which are mostly titled Ali Farka Touré and would clash with the 1988 World Circuit album; Radio Mali (1996, archive radio sessions); Red & Green (compilation); and Ni Foli and the Sahel EP, whose status is unclear.
  - Voyageur (2023) is a posthumous album of unreleased recordings.
  - Talking Timbuktu (with Ry Cooder), In the Heart of the Moon and Ali and Toumani are collaborations; the last two appear in both rooms.
- **Toumani Diabaté**: https://musicbrainz.org/artist/b96ba7e7-d882-409a-9825-ed3d6dbb437d
  - Left out: New Ancient Strings (1999, with Ballaké Sissoko), because MusicBrainz tags it Live. It is often treated as a studio album: add it if confirmed.
  - Also left out: Boulevard de l'Indépendance (Symmetric Orchestra), because it is not under his name in MusicBrainz and I found no source for its year; Kôrôlén (with the London Symphony Orchestra, recorded in concert); and compilations.
- **Oumou Sangaré**: https://musicbrainz.org/artist/2013f3af-51a3-404d-9afc-91b3f277ea4e
  - Moussolou: NPR says "her 1989 debut", while MusicBrainz has 1991 (the World Circuit edition). I used 1989.
  - Left out: Bi Furu, Denw and Laban (local cassettes), compilations, the live album and the remix releases.
