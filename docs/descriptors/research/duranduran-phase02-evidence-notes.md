# Duran Duran — phase 02 evidence notes

## Method

This is an album-level editorial descriptor, not a collection of tags inherited from each original song or from reissues. Source URLs and their precise roles are in `duranduran_phase02_sources.csv`; a separate line for **every genre** is in `duranduran_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`. 0–5 scores are consistent editorial inferences. Comparisons in `sounds_like` identify audible similarities **only**, not evidence of an artist influencing Duran Duran. No verified album-specific statement justified `influenced_by`, so blanks are intentional.

## Big Thing (1988) — `duranduran-bigthing`

- **Genres and dynamics:** Album of the Year further tags the record pop-rock, sophisti-pop, synth-funk and big-music textures; Rhino and the label's 2010 commentary connect the hard-edged *I Don't Want Your Love* and *All She Wants Is* to emerging house, while the original LP's second half has spacious, quieter *Land*, *Palomino*, *The Edge of America* and *Do You Believe in Shame?*. AllMusic adds dance-pop, synth-pop, new wave and alternative/indie rock. This supports `electronic=4`, but not a mechanically high overall energy: `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=4`, `mood=2`. `experimental=4` reflects the unusual division into dense grooves and sparse, shadowy material; `polish=4` reflects the studio layering with retained rough edges. `theatrical=3` avoids exaggerating the restrained side.
- **Instruments:** Original-pressing credits and Sessiondays identify John Taylor bass, Nick Rhodes/ Jonathan Elias keyboards and synths, Steve Ferrone drums, Cuccurullo/Chester Kamen guitar, Marc Chantereau percussion, Patrick Bourgoin/Stan Harrison saxophones, Mac Gollehon trumpet and Glenn Ferris trombone. Their appearance on **some tracks** suffices for non-leading tags. No assumption that a horn section plays on every song.
- **Production:** Sherman Foote is explicitly credited with programming, hence that sole technique tag. Other synthesis and overdubs are audible/texturally described but not promoted into unsupported method-specific tokens. No specific TR-808, Fairlight or other model is verified on *Big Thing*. `Drug` was remixed for the original LP, a fact to preserve as an **edition caveat** rather than claim the whole album is remixed.
- **Lyrics:** Relations, sensuality and loss coexist with wider alienation; *Do You Believe in Shame?* and *The Edge of America* motivate the sober topics rather than treating *All She Wants Is* as the only lyrical register.
- **Comparisons, not influences:** LA Times hears Kraftwerk and early Brian Eno in technological textures and Joni Mitchell's then-contemporary atmospheric writing in *Land*. No band/producer statement affirming these as direct influences on the album was located.
- **Disagreement:** Mike DeGagne at AllMusic disliked disjointed structures; *Classic Pop* and Rhino identify the quiet second half as precisely its artistic strength. The balance warrants mid-high `experimental` without a universal quality judgment.

## Liberty (1990) — `duranduran-liberty`

- **Genres and dynamics:** Album of the Year separately names alternative dance, dance-pop and sophisti-pop; AllMusic explicitly hears disco, Motown, Philly soul and new wave, alongside guitar rock; the original LP is styled synth-pop and Herb Music lists pop/alternative rock. These are separate **texture**/track-level markers, not assertions of a full Motown or Philly soul production. `energy=3`, `electronic=3`, `groove=3`, `experimental=2`, `polish=4` capture the glossy dance-rock and softer *Serious* / *My Antarctica* episodes. `mood=3`, `theatrical=3`, `tempo=3` describe its mixed register.
- **Instrumentation:** Sterling Campbell drums, John Taylor bass, Cuccurullo guitars, Rhodes and John Jones keyboards, John Jones programming, Luis Jardim percussion, Stan Harrison saxophone and Roddy Lorimer trumpet appear in explicit album credits. No unverified named synthesizer/processor models.
- **Production:** Sleeve reproduced in source says digitally recorded and mixed in London, crediting Chris Kimsey production in association with the group. Hence `digitalrecording` and `programming`. Most accounts praise the crisp arrangement of *Serious* but criticize the overstuffed rockers; descriptions vary between slick and stripped-back. The `polish` scale refers to surface recording, not album quality.
- **Sounds-like:** The Smiths is **only** an editorial nod to a reviewer describing *Serious*'s guitar in Johnny Marr terms; INXS is a lower-confidence comparative dance-rock fit. Neither band is entered in `influenced_by`.

## Duran Duran (The Wedding Album) (1993) — `duranduran-durandurantheweddingalbum`

- **Identity:** The actual cover reads *Duran Duran*, as did the 1981 debut. Fandome's parenthetical title and computed album ID disambiguate the 1993 LP. Preserve that existing ID.
- **Genres and scope:** Core is 1990s pop-rock and dance-pop; AllMusic names dance-rock, new wave, synth pop. AOTY adds soft rock, alternative dance, downtempo and trip hop. Classic Pop identifies a bossa nova element on one track, *Breath After Breath*, and Prince-styled funk in *U.M.F.*; the band describes a rave stomp in *Drowning Man*. `bossanova` and `triphop` are **small local contributions** only. Directly described rock, R&B and dance idioms underlie higher-level tags. `energy=3`, `electronic=3`, `groove=3`, `tempo=3`, `mood=3` express variety from *Ordinary World* to *Drowning Man*. High clarity/reworked arrangements merit `polish=5`, not a claim of emotional cheerfulness.
- **Instruments and recording:** Warren Cuccurullo is credited both acoustic and electric guitars; Taylor bass; Rhodes keyboards; John Jones keyboards, programmed parts, bass on *Come Undone*, and playing drums; Ferrone and Colaiuta live drums; Bosco percussion. Rhodes explicitly remembers many drum machines. Le Bon confirms first direct-to-hard-drive recording. The Second Disc reports a drum-break loop from *Ashley’s Roachclip* on *Come Undone*. `sampling` is **track-specific**. Session credits explicitly mention vocal samples on *Drowning Man*, adding distinct support.
- **Vocals/language:** Simon Le Bon sings English; Milton Nascimento performs portions of *Breath After Breath* **in Portuguese**. This is attested by Duran Duran's own Q&A and 30th-anniversary sleeve notes (rather than one contemporaneous misidentification as Spanish). The overall voice stays in the allowed lead-types rather than calling Nascimento's guest contribution a separate lead register.
- **Themes:** *Ordinary World* deals with a friend's loss; *Too Much Information* critiques media saturation; *Come Undone* is intimate; *Sin of the City* is social storytelling. The variety warrants `introspection|social|sensuality|heartbreak` in addition to love.
- **Sounds-like:** U.M.F. / Prince and *None of the Above* / the Rolling Stones are source-backed critical sonic comparisons. Sade is an editorial match for the softer atmospheric groove, not an established influence. No album-specific musical person-artist influence claims promoted into `influenced_by`.
- **Disagreement:** Some genre databases apply `triphop` to the LP broadly, whereas documentary discussion focuses on a few breaks and slower textures. Scope restricts overclaiming. Production credit belongs to Duran Duran and John Jones, not David Richards, who mixed tracks.

## Thank You (1995) — `duranduran-thankyou`

- **Album type:** Eleven covers plus one band composition, *Drive By*. This is a Duran Duran *studio album*; it is not a compilation of earlier Duran Duran recordings. Do **not** import songwriter or covered performer as producers, collaborators or `influenced_by` merely because their song was covered.
- **Genres and arrangements:** Album of the Year lists alternative rock and downtempo (more prominent on slow covers); AllMusic styles include adult alternative pop-rock, synth-pop and dance-rock. A contemporary LA Times critic observed *White Lines* and the Sly/Temptations cuts rebuilt into guitar-driven group arrangements with strong rhythm; the first of those retains live rap by Melle Mel. `hiphop` therefore denotes *White Lines* and the reinterpretation of *911 Is a Joke*, **not** a homogeneous rap album. More meditative ballads include *Perfect Day*, *Lay Lady Lay*, *Crystal Ship*, *Thank You*. `energy=3`, `electronic=2`, `groove=3`, `polish=4`, `experimental=3` reflect that wide and sometimes awkward treatment of disparate source material.
- **Instrument provenance:** Original liner-credit transcriptions identify band guitars/bass/keyboards plus drummers Ferrone, Roger Taylor, Tony Thompson, Terry Bozzio, Anthony J. Resta and Abe Laboriel Jr.; Lee Oskar harmonica on two tracks; Jonathan Elias **Moog on Crystal Ship**; violin, viola, cello ensemble. `moog` is an exact named production model. Strings are present as instruments, but there is no inferred `stringarrangements` technique without a named arranger.
- **Production/personnel:** Consistently credited album producers are Duran Duran and John Jones. Secondary credits sometimes list Anthony J. Resta and Bob St John as (additional) producers, engineers or mixers; original sleeve distinguishes **additional production**. The CSV excludes them. Arrangement/recording of covers is new studio work, not remastering of original artists' recordings.
- **Voices/themes:** Le Bon remains central; Melle Mel raps and backing vocalists appear in credits. Lyrics range from drug criticism and political frustration (*White Lines*, *911 Is a Joke*, *Ball of Confusion*) to love and longing. These are **performed lyrical themes** even where someone else wrote the original songs.
- **Sounds-like:** A contemporaneous comparison to Beck (*911 Is a Joke*) and Rolling Stone's parallels to Gary Glitter (*Success*) and Chris de Burgh (the title song) are **track-only judgments**. The artists covered should not automatically populate either influences or soundalikes.
- **Disagreement:** Critical consensus was harsh (LA Times, AllMusic, Classic Pop), while Lou Reed publicly praised their cover of *Perfect Day* (Rhino). The genre and acoustic data record what the music does; they do not replicate reviewers' value judgments.
