# Cameo — Phase 03: source-backed evidence notes

Research date **28 October 2026**. Fandome editorial descriptors are the writer's own judgments about **whole albums**, unless a genre is identified as a song-level tail. References for each album are in the included CSV, with permanent source links. Scale values follow Fandome anchors; they are qualitative inferences and not acoustic measurements.

## 1. *Style* (1983) — `cameo-style`

**Source anchor:** Alex Henderson, *Style*, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/style-mw0000841067); original release overview (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/style); original LP personnel (https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/style); secondary liner-note transcription (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_(Cameo_album)).

- Main sound: funk and R&B with much greater emphasis on keyboards/synthesizers and less on live horns, reflecting downsizing from the older Cameo lineup. AllMusic discusses the 1983 shift to synth-funk and electro-funk and identifies "Aphrodisiac", "Cameo's Dance", "Slow Movin'" and the title track as examples. Thus `funk|rnb|synthfunk|electrofunk` dominates; `ballad` is the tail for the remake of "Can't Help Falling in Love".
- Original line-up: Larry Blackmon vocals/bass/drums/percussion, Tomi Jenkins vocals, Charlie Singleton guitar/bass/keyboards/synth, Nathan Leftenant trumpet, Kevin Kendrick keys. Liner-note transcription names **Prophet-5, Roland Jupiter-8, Fender Rhodes and Minimoog** for Kendrick; support for *exact models* is weaker than support for the presence of synthesizers in general.
- Production: credited producer Larry Blackmon; electronics described as a sound-source choice, not automatically evidence for drum programming/sampling. `prophet5|jupiter8|minimoog` are documented instrument models in production vocabulary, with qualified confidence. Some pages loosely mention electronic drums, but these LP personnel listings do **not** establish a particular model or track; no drum machine or Simmons token was assigned.
- Scale [energy 4, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 4, groove 4]: repeated punchy funk songs support danceability and medium-high energy; acoustic rhythm section and horns remain. Relatively accessible arrangements; studio production without demonstrated high-gloss digital processing.
- Lyrics: title and lead songs support image/identity, dance and attraction; love appears in the Elvis-standard cover. `landmark=0`: worthwhile period transition but usually not singled out as signature album by the specialist critic. Voice left empty because vocal timbre evidence across the album is insufficient.

## 2. *She's Strange* (1984) — `cameo-shesstrange`

**Source anchor:** Alex Henderson, *She's Strange*, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/shes-strange-mw0000649948); liner-note credits (https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/shes-strange); Apple label-derived music credits (https://music.apple.com/us/song/1445362155); Soul and Funk Music original LP (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/shes_strange).

- AllMusic notes that the title track and "Talkin' Out the Side of Your Neck" add **rap** to the funk as a response to contemporary **hip-hop**. These are major tracks, hence `hiphop|rap` placed after the core `funk|rnb|contemporaryrnb|synthfunk`, not represented as whole-album hip-hop. The critic identifies "Tribute to Bob Marley" as **reggae** (one song), "Lé Ve Toi!" as **funk with rock** (one song; no new inferred mixed-genre token), and "Hangin' Downtown" as smooth, jazzy **quiet storm** (one song).
- Personnel names: Blackmon drums, bass, vocals, percussion; Singleton guitar, synth and keyboards; Kendrick additional synth; other bass, trumpet, trombone and alto sax. The horns are documented on the record although less central than on the 1970s albums. `hornarrangements` is explicit in sleeve and label metadata, not assumed. Tom Race and Steve Jerome are engineers/mixers, not album producers.
- Scale [4,3,3,2,3,3,3,4]: dance rhythm and rapped tracks support energy/groove 4; reggae, jazzy ballad and funk-rock track increase stylistic variety/experimental 3. Full album remains band+keys, not fully programmed.
- Voice: `rap` because professional critic explicitly describes substantial rapping on its prominent songs; this does **not** claim that all seven tracks use it. Lyrical domains: sensual/romantic descriptions, topical criticism in "Talkin' Out...", tribute and social concern. Tribute is **not** proof of Bob Marley as direct compositional influence for album.
- `landmark=1`: first number-one R&B album in the cited original LP chart listing and recognized key hit. This is an album-level editorial significance judgment, not a critical vote count.

## 3. *Single Life* (1985) — `cameo-singlelife`

**Source anchor:** Alex Henderson, *Single Life*, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/single-life-mw0000189389); original LP musicians (https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/cameo-single-life-lp-528746/), original credit listings (https://www.lovevinylrecords.com/products/cameo-single-life-vinyl-lp-used-near-mint-nm-or-m-near-mint-nm-or-m).

- Most of the LP is synthesizer/electric-bass-led synth-funk, characterized as much more electronic than earlier horn-based albums by AllMusic. Principal three-person member lineup is Blackmon, Tomi Jenkins and Nathan Leftenant; several guest players provide bass, guitars, **guest** horns, keyboards. The presence of horns in album credits does not make them core full-time members. `synthfunk|funk|rnb|contemporaryrnb|electrofunk` first.
- **Specific programming:** LP liner transcriptions name **Sammy Merendino** for drum programming; confirmed by two independent credit transcriptions. Do not infer drum hardware brand. Kevin Kendrick, Eric Rehl keyboards and synthesizers; Roy Leftenant timbales; Melvin Wells alto sax, Keith O'Quinn trombone, John Gatchell trumpet. `hornarrangements` credited to Larry Blackmon and Nathan Leftenant. Original producer Blackmon alone; engineer/mixer Dave Ogrin not a co-producer.
- The specialist critic calls "Little Boys – Dangerous Toys" a political reggae song and "Urban Warrior" a rap number about worldwide partying/peace. The latter is **track-scoped** `rap` and supplies allowed vocal token `rap` but does not mean the whole album is rapped. "A Good-Bye" is a rock ballad, noted as a `ballad` tail rather than general `rock`. Quiet storm style explicitly listed by AllMusic.
- Scale [4,4,3,2,3,4,4,4]: high synthesizer dependence and documented rhythm programming support electronic 4/polish 4; hard dance songs contrasted with a ballad. Mood mixed because romantic longing and Cold War anti-nuclear themes offset celebrations.
- Lyrics: party, romance, breakup, politics and social idealism. `landmark=0`: highly regarded studio album, while AllMusic explicitly considers *Word Up!* more essential; assigning 1 here would be defensible but would obscure the more distinctive record.

## 4. *Word Up!* (1986) — `cameo-wordup`

**Source anchor:** Alex Henderson, *Word Up!*, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/word-up%21-mw0000193157); Ed Hogan, "Word Up" song review (https://www.allmusic.com/song/word-up-mt0062415883); original production/credits in MusicBrainz (https://musicbrainz.org/release/0a121859-a98c-4eed-bf59-500d07a73137) and sleeve-derived personnel (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/word_up/credits).

- Funk defines the record, with programmed 1980s rhythms, funky bass, keyboards and synthesizers, characteristic clipped vocal rhythmic delivery in the title song. AllMusic specifically praises "Word Up!", "Candy", "Back and Forth", and "Fast, Fierce & Funny" as high-energy funk. Its genre styles also include contemporary R&B, soul and quiet storm: quieter moments are not the same as whole-album ballad format.
- Album engineering evidence unusually strong: **Sammy Merendino drum programming**, **Peter Scherer Synclavier programming**, horn arrangements Larry Blackmon/Cameo, plus studio keyboards, brass/woodwinds, bass, guitars, percussion and Blackmon acoustic drum performances. The Synclavier credit is album-specific; not every keyboard sound is necessarily Synclavier. No specific TR drum machine model verified. Cameo, Bernard Wright, Uptown Horns and other guests contribute but are not external producers.
- **Voice treatment is song-specific:** Ed Hogan (AllMusic) says Larry Blackmon sang the title hook through a small transistor-radio speaker to filter high frequencies. `vocalfilter` production token is defensible on the signature single, **not universal across album**. The critic also mentions the distinctive snare sound, but doesn't specify the effect chain, so no `noisegating` or sampler model added.
- Scale [5,4,4,3,2,4,4,5]: dance-focused major tracks and polished, highly rhythmic electronic arrangements, balanced by some organic bass, guitars and horn section. `electronic` not 5 because heavily hybrid. `mood` exuberant 4. `experimental` 2 refers to conventional structures despite identifiable sonic details.
- Lyrics: party, attraction, identity/playfulness and relationships. `landmark=1` with cross-format breakthrough and sustained critical recognition; `status=draft` since second-review validation is outstanding. No inferred artist `influenced_by` claims or unverified direct `sounds_like` names.

## Common guardrails

- `producers` is principal producer Larry Blackmon on all four albums. Composer, engineer, mixer, guest singer and programmer credits stay separate.
- A generic influence statement comparing the *band* to P-Funk does not meet Fandome's test for a documented influence on *this album*. `influenced_by` intentionally empty; so is `sounds_like` without a justified direct sonic comparison.
- Recording dates/pressing dates conflict in several editions. Each CSV `year` is the original studio album's year, verified against physical LP and professional music metadata, **not later remaster years**.
- No live or compilation material enters the ZIP. The full 17-LP original studio album list is for auditing only, **not** 17 proposed descriptor rows.
