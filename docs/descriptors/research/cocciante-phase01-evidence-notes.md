# Cocciante — fase 01, evidence and scoring notes

**Scope and audit.** Fandome artist ID is `cocciante` (NOT `riccardococciante`). Checked `docs/descriptors/cocciante.csv` and `docs/descriptors/riccardococciante.csv` in GitHub branch `descriptors`: both 404 on 2026-10-30. `src/data/catalog.ts` contains only *Concerto per Margherita* (1976) and *Cervo a primavera* (1980). No seed record. All four 1972–1975 LPs are new and need catalogue entries. Recheck immediately before import because these conditions may change. Historical title artist on original *Mu* and *Poesia* LPs is **Richard Cocciante** but the existing global Fandome identity is **Riccardo Cocciante**; do not create a duplicate artist.

## 1972 — Mu (`cocciante-mu`)

**Identity and edition:** first RCA studio LP DPSL 10549; original 1972 *Mu*, not Sony Legacy’s catalogue page displaying **1990** (CD release date). Nine indexed tracks incl. instrumental introduction organized into four narrative sections. Sony Legacy, original Sony/Qobuz 1972 phonograms, ItalianProg, AllMusic and Rolling Stone corroborate.

**Genres:** ranked `prog` (Rolling Stone), `rockopera` (Sony Legacy), `symphonicprog` (ItalianProg), `songwriter` (Planet Mellotron identifies this strand beneath prog arrangements). All are album-level descriptions. Note some reviewers, such as Rockol, caution that it is only *partially* progressive in the music, so avoid presenting hard-rock intensity or electronic dominance. This is a distinctive, singular progressive LP in a largely pop career, which Rolling Stone includes among its notable 1972 albums: `landmark=1` with caveat *niche defining*, not a global commercial breakthrough.

**Actual credited instruments:** Sony Legacy cites Moog, harpsichord, Mellotron, sitar, Indian harp, multiple guitars, flutes, keyboardists and drums. AllMusic’s named credits confirm Cocciante piano/Moog/harpsichord, Rustichelli synth/Mellotron, Giammarco flute/Mellotron, Vandroogenbroeck wind instruments, Derek Wilson drums; 1972 sleeve transcription adds Michael Brill bass, Douglas Meakin acoustic guitar, Dave Sumner electric guitar/sitar, Miky Fraser Fender electric piano/organ, David Agnew percussion, Carol Muriel ocarina, Joel mbira/recorders and choir. Both `santoor` and `flexatone` also appear on the sleeve transcription but are **deliberately not in the rows** until Claude corroborates and adds these optional new tokens. Mellotron strings on *Uomo* and *Era mattino sul mondo* are documented by Planet Mellotron; the `strings` token refers to sampled Mellotron strings, NOT a credited live orchestra. The Indian-harp performer is recorded as Carol Muriel in some credits, with disputed specific harp type: mapped to existing `harp`.

**Production:** `moog|choirvocals` only; concrete Moog and backing singers are named. Italo Greco also arranged; **Paolo Dossena** is the sole producer, independently confirmed by Sony original recording metadata on *Ora che io sono luce* and *Uomo*. Do not credit all writers as producers, or guess tape format. Cocciante tells Rolling Stone it was recorded over **15 nights**, but that does not mean recorded live in one take.

**Eight scales (0–5):** `energy 3` for shifts between impassioned sections and contemplative passages; `electronic 3` for central Moog/Mellotron amid live players; `mood 2` for apocalypse, fear and solemnity tempered by optimism; `theatrical 4` for rock-opera narrative and ambitious form; `experimental 4` for mythical suite construction and atypical timbres; `polish 3` for richly arranged, briskly recorded RCA studio production; `tempo 3` for mixed slow expanses and rock segments; `groove 2` for predominantly narrative rather than dance rhythm. Album vocals `raspy|powerful` align with direct Cocciante retrospective (singing half rock/half melodic) and archival commentary on raspy dramatic vocal release. No claim of opera-trained singing.

**Sounds like vs influence:** The Italian Song critic explicitly calls the album stylistically derivative of **King Crimson’s In the Court of the Crimson King** and **New Trolls’ Concerto Grosso**. These are retrospective critical SONIC references. The criticised artist did not assert either as a musical influence; `influenced_by` stays EMPTY. Joel Vandroogenbroeck/Brainticket, musicians on the LP, also are NOT to be counted as influences. Specific-record comparisons are added in album parentheses without track fractions because critic compares the whole album.

**Lyrics:** ancient continent, environmental decline, doomed civilization, ambition, violence, redemption and nature from Sony Legacy and Rockol. `spiritual|storytelling|social|political|identity|introspection` summarizes these themes; there are biblical references but not proof of Cocciante’s personal religious beliefs.

## 1973 — Poesia (`cocciante-poesia`)

**Identity:** original RCA DPSL 10578, second LP (as Richard). Official Spotify and Apple original-copyright listings contain **10 songs**, including **Noi** and **Soli** as separate tracks; some secondary lists incorrectly compress them into **Noi soli** and report nine songs. The original issue was 1973 (some reissues say 1998 on AllMusic, and album storefronts disagree on exact day/month); only original YEAR retained. Sony/Qobuz songs document **Paolo Dossena** as producer and arranger with Giorgio Loviscek as engineer (engineer is NOT producer).

**Genres:** `pop` is Apple/Sony original label genre. `songwriter` is Rockol’s description of his shift from *Mu* to conventional authored romantic songs; `ballad` is an editorial track-level term for the delicate guitar-driven love songs described by laCOOLtura, not a formal entire-disc source classification; `italianpop` is AllMusic’s specific Poesia reissue style (same original recording). This is four grounded but partly overlapping categories; no prog token carried forward just because Mu was progressive.

**Instruments:** laCOOLtura independently describes actual guitar in *Poesia*, keyboards and live drums in *Soli*, and background harmonies in *Lei* / *Asciuga i tuoi pensieri al sole*. Cocciante says *Poesia* was the only tune he composed on guitar rather than piano. Cocciante’s original-album photographed studio session with bassist Mario Scotti independently corroborates bass. Piano is a core element of his performance/composition from contemporaneous accounts; if Claude requires more precise album playback credit, keep piano provisional (see confidence and guide). No presumed Moog from prior album.

**Production:** `choirvocals` (specific backing choruses). Do not imply studio choir on all ten tracks. No strings or explicit microphones/recording-process claims.

**Eight scales:** `energy 2` mostly restrained love songs, with *Soli* more rhythmic; `electronic 1` amplified keyboards but no documented synth/drum machine; `mood 2` longing and vulnerability; `theatrical 3` occasionally expansive vocal phrasing but producer requested a less unorthodox delivery; `experimental 1` conventional song cycle after Mu; `polish 3` regular RCA production, distinct from raw lo-fi; `tempo 2` mostly moderate/slower with *Soli* as the exception; `groove 1` introspective, not dance-oriented. `voice` blank: a source reports Dossena asked him to sing more normally than his preferred style; we do not force the lifetime raspy label onto this LP without album-wide evidence.

**Landmark 0:** the title song became a classic, but the album was commercially weaker and Cocciante later explicitly said he disliked it apart from title track. No other musicians in influenced_by. A Patty Pravo recording of *Poesia* is NOT an influence, nor evidence of similar sounding arrangements.

## 1974 — Anima (`cocciante-anima`)

**Identity:** RCA TPL 1-1060, 8 songs, not a 1976 Richard Cocciante English-language edition or reissue. This was the original career breakthrough, with *Bella senz’anima* and *Quando finisce un amore*. Cocciante himself describes the album as crucial in his 2024 anniversary interview; therefore `landmark=1`.

**Genres:** `pop` and `songwriter` align with historic Italian singer-songwriter and AllMusic biography; `orchestralpop` is an editorial album-level descriptor specifically grounded in the 8 orchestrated arrangements split 4+4 by Pisano and Morricone; `ballad` for *Quando finisce un amore* and solo-piano intimacy of *L’odore del pane*, not blanket album claim; `poprock` is the original Sony/Qobuz record’s broad Pop/Rock category and dynamic, forceful vocal arrangements (NOT unqualified pure rock).

**Producer-credit discrepancy IMPORTANT:** Michele Neri’s *Ar.Disc.* notes Cocciante, **Paolo Amerigo Cassella** and **Marco Luberti** as co-writers, with Cassella and Luberti album producers; **Ennio Morricone** arranged *Qui*, *Quando finisce un amore*, *Lucia*, *Puoi chiamarmi col mio nome*; **Franco Pisano** arranged the other four. HOWEVER RCA/Sony original phonogram credits reproduced by Qobuz explicitly add `Producer` to **Franco Pisano** on *Il mio modo di vivere*, *L’odore del pane*, and *Se io fossi*, while Ar.Disc. labels his role arranger only. To obey **all named producers credited**, row contains `Marco Luberti|Paolo Amerigo Cassella|Franco Pisano`, but Claude must note Pisano is **track-level contested**, not definitively album-wide. **Morricone is not a credited producer**, nor is Giulio Spelta (recording engineer). The Morricone first-person interview independently confirms he arranged half of the record. Strict album-only primary-release producer interpretation may omit Pisano after checking actual 1974 sleeve, but preserve the source conflict.

**Instrumentation:** Piano is directly supported on *L’odore del pane*; original half-LP Morricone and Pisano orchestrations, along with rhythmic/bass-drum parts from Morricone’s direct testimony, ground `orchestra|strings|drums` in the broad sense. `strings` and `stringarrangements` are explicitly corroborated by Cocciante's first-person 2024 **Corriere del Veneto** interview praising Morricone's unusual arrangements for **archi**; the exact players are not established. Do NOT recycle specific *2024 anniversary touring band* members (Golino et al.) as 1974 players. No fabricated electric guitar or synthesizer.

**Production:** `stringarrangements` reflects original composed orchestral backing; `belting` is large dramatic projection explicitly described for *Bella senz’anima*. The code intentionally uses instrument `strings` AND production `stringarrangements` for separate audible source and arranged recording characteristic. Do not assume multi-tracking or reverb.

**Eight scales:** `energy 3` for explosive ballads against piano lull; `electronic 1` primarily acoustic piano and orchestra, slight studio-electric rhythmic fabric; `mood 1` intense breakup, bitterness and disillusion; `theatrical 5` sung confrontations and high-volume climbs; `experimental 2` complex divided arranging but accessible forms; `polish 4` exact Morricone/Pisano arrangements and RCA polish; `tempo 2` ballad-heavy; `groove 1` contemplative, not dance-led. `powerful|raspy` supported by on-record review of *Bella senz’anima* and singer’s established rasp; avoid `operatic`, which is a classical training descriptor.

**Influences:** all empty. Morricone/Pisano are direct arrangers, not artists Cocciante named as influences on Anima. Any later covers by Mia Martini, Marco Borsato and others are irrelevant to influenced_by.

## 1975 — L’alba (`cocciante-lalba`)

**Identity:** 4th LP, original RCA TPL 1-1167; first issue 1975. Some metadata say April 11, others June; avoid invented exact release date. The ten songs share a disappointed protagonist’s desire to escape, but are **independent songs**, not a formally continuous concept LP (Il Mondo che C’è). Rockol calls it less commercially successful than Anima; reviews recognize its distinctive angry/reflective voice. `landmark=1` on the strength of multiple 2020/2025/2026 retrospectives discussing it as an essential representative of his mid-70s sound, not because of original sales numbers; Claude may lower if its strict landmark threshold requires a single generational industry shift.

**Genres:** `pop` and `songwriter` grounded in contemporary and retrospective descriptions; `ballad` on *Smania*, *La morte di una rosa* and *Era già tutto previsto* (laCOOLtura). `pianorock` is specifically for **title track only**, Rockol describes piano pulse growing from intimate slow verse to rock-edged aggressive crescendo, placed 4th because track-specific. `musicaleggera` is editorial for Italian melodic 1970s mainstream LP described in Recensiamo Musica; distinct from *Mu* rock opera. Any suggestion the entire LP is hard rock/prog is unsupported.

**Producer:** original Sony/RCA metadata gives **Marco Luberti** throughout; Sergio Rendine and Lucas Sideras are arrangement credits. Vangelis.online claims 5 tracks arranged by Sideras and refers to Niko Papathanassiou as a **producer**, but Sony reissue credits identify Niko as **Project Coordinator**, not Producer. Do NOT add Niko to producer field without original sleeve explicitly confirming 'producer' rather than coordinator. Vangelis’s 1976 *Concerto per Margherita* work should NOT be projected backward onto L’alba. Other named mix engineers Rodolfo Grappa, Maurizio Montanesi and Paolo Venditti are NOT producers. All sources agree this is a Cocciante/Luberti album.

**Instrumentation:** only `piano` included because Rockol describes the title track’s piano pulse and Recensiamo Musica describes repeated piano in *E lei sopra di me*; laCOOLtura notes piano in *Smania* and *La morte di una rosa*. There are clearly fuller instrumental arrangements on some 1975 tracks but without attributable original sleeve player credits, **do not add generic drums, bass, strings or synth** from later albums. `production=belting` reflects the specifically reviewed high-register crescendos of *L’alba* and *E lei sopra di me*, not assumed vocal effects.

**Eight scales:** `energy 3` slow sections with intense eruptive chorus; `electronic 1` original studio piano-led popular music (electric sound not actually pinned down: cautious 1); `mood 1` longing, alienation and escape; `theatrical 4` crescendos and emotional staging; `experimental 2` quasi-concept thematic unity and dual Italian/Greek arranging circles; `polish 3` professional pop with arranger credits, but no evidence of lavish high-gloss studio tech; `tempo 2` slow/moderate; `groove 1` not rhythm-driven. Voice `powerful` from reviewer of 1975 tracks, rather than baseless `raspy` for every song.

**Sounds like/influence:** No explicit credible music-critic artist comparison on this album was found, so both cells are empty. Covers by Andrea Bocelli and Ornella Vanoni on other recordings do NOT qualify. Niko’s brother Vangelis is not an influence simply by association.

**Forward catalogue warning:** the original 2026 new studio album *Ho vent’anni con te* is outside this phase and should be researched in later chronological phases, not incorrectly excluded based on pre-2026 discography lists. The 1981 Q Concert is a live collaboration with Rino Gaetano/New Perigeo and not eligible in these studio batches.
