## rota-ladolcevita (1960)
- genres: Variety (Nick Vivarelli, 20 Oct 2022, on the CAM Sugar reissue), via https://ca.news.yahoo.com/nino-rota-la-dolce-vita-152410218.html: "blends jazz, rock 'n' roll, Latin rhythms and circus music". Quartet QR300 (https://cdn.soundohm.com/product/la-dolce-vita-expanded-2c): "melancholic orchestral music, jazz, light pop, rollicking rock, circus fanfares, dances", "Babel of genre". I mapped "rollicking rock" to rockandroll (a 1960 rock idiom; Variety says rock 'n' roll), and "light pop" to pop. Movie Music UK (Craig Lysy, Fathers of Film Music part 15, https://moviemusicuk.us/2016/05/01/nino-rota-fathers-of-film-music-part-15/): "circus themes, lounge music, and Italian café interludes". Main Titles (Joep de Bruijn, 2022, https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/la-dolce-vita/): "orchestral, lounge, jazz, pop, rock, circus music, dance, classical".
- instruments: Rough Trade/Lysy: "Using organ, piano, electric guitar, accordion, clarinets, trumpets, vibes, and, at times, a full orchestra" (https://shop.roughtrade.com/products/la-dolce-vita-original-motion-picture-soundtrack). Main Titles: "a gloomy electric organ", "a gently plucked harp" (Trevi Fountain cue). Vibes are mapped to mallets.
- electronic 1: electric organ and electric guitar inside an acoustic orchestra. mood 3: "melancholic orchestral" next to the upbeat material (Lysy: the music fits Marcello's optimism; the "carefree dance" ending). experimental 2: an eclectic "Babel of genre", but the forms are classic. groove 3: dances, rock 'n' roll, Latin rhythms. tempo 3: mixed.
- voice instrumental: the original LP is instrumental. The vocal version by Katyna Ranieri is described as "previously unreleased" on the CAM Sugar 2022 set.
- sounds_like / influenced_by: none. Lysy says the theme "alludes to the Kurt Weill song 'Ballad of Mack the Knife'" and interpolates Respighi's Pines of Rome. A blog quotes Fellini using Moritat during the shooting (https://musicaficionado.blog/2022/10/28/1960-film-scores-european-films/). These are quotations or interpolations, so under the rules they are not links.
- landmark 1: Lysy places it with 8½ among "two scores most critics place in his top ten". It also had two Grammy nominations. The landmark is borderline: please check.
- Reception: grammy.com (https://www.grammy.com/artists/nino-rota/12263) lists the 1962 nominations for Best Sound Track Album and Best Instrumental Theme. On the reissues: soundtrackcorner (CAM Sugar CD) and Quartet's 300th release.
- Not opened: the Fondazione Cini book page (Viviani, Sources of the Creative Process) returned 403. Forced Exposure returned an empty page.

## rota-8 (8½, 1963)
- genres: Quartet QR330 (https://cdn.soundohm.com/product/8-2-cd): "carnivalesque, vigorous quality", "swing, galop, waltzes and dance music", "dramatic, nostalgic and even mysterious sounds". Doxy reissue (https://www.soundohm.com/product/8-1-2-o-s-t): "a music hall-like melange of jazz, classical, lounge, and circus music", "operatic interludes, cabaret swing, polka romps, and organ combo ditties". Lysy (MMUK, link above): "a silly circus-like march"; "Jazz, Foxtrot, Swing … a jazzy interpolation of Wagner's Ride of the Valkyries". Joseph Crisalli's blog (https://stalkingthebelleepoque.blogspot.com/2010/10/song-of-week-theme-from-otto-e-mezzo-by.html): "marches, jazz, classical music, lounge music and melodies of the circus". "Music hall-like" is a comparison, not a genre label, so I did not add musichall.
- instruments: only organ ("organ combo ditties", Doxy). The MusicWeb flute remark concerns a concert suite by William Ross, not the album, so it is not used.
- theatrical 4: circus fanfares and operatic pastiche (Irish Times, Tara Brady: the score adds to the sense of "an extravagant piece of choreography"). experimental 2: Taste of Cinema says the score "fits the avant-garde nature of the film", but the forms are classic dances. mood 3: "comedic feel" (Taste of Cinema) next to "nostalgic, mysterious" (Quartet).
- sounds_like: not used. Crisalli says one version of the theme is a cabaret tune "in the style of Edith Piaf" and another is "Sousa-esque", but that covers a few cues, I could not get the 1963 LP track count, and the source is a blog.
- Reception: Lysy's top ten. A Nastro d'Argento 1964 for Best Score is reported only by Wikipedia, so it is not cited. Taste of Cinema's top 10 Rota scores: https://tasteofcinema.com/2016/the-10-best-film-scores-of-nino-rota/2

## rota-ilgattopardo (1963)
- genres: Movie Music UK (Craig Lysy, 25 Jan 2021, https://moviemusicuk.us/2021/01/25/il-gattopardo-nino-rota/), on the Quartet QR-327 album: Verdi's "Valzer Brillante", "a wondrous, resplendent waltz"; a mazurka, a polka, a quadrille and a galop, each named; the village band plays Verdi's Traviata chorus; "This supremely romantic score". Lysy (Fathers part 15): "grand romanticism and elegant sweep", "traditional Sicilian dances", "Rota's most unabashed romantic score". Taste of Cinema: "The overall melody mirrors that of an epic opera". MusicWeb (Ian Lace, https://www.musicweb-international.com/film/2003/Jun03/nino_rota.html): the theme comes from Rota's symphony Sinfonia sopra una canzone d'amore. That symphonic base, plus the Verdi waltz, is the reason for the classical genre.
- instruments (MMUK): "forlorn horns", "warm French horns, to solo oboe, to strings", the Love Theme for strings, and bugle cues ("bugle militare"). Bugle is a new instrument token.
- mood 1: "melancholia" (Lysy) and "wistful, nostalgic, and full of yearning" (MMUK). theatrical 4: "grand", "epic opera". groove 2: ballroom dances, but a dramatic score overall. experimental 0.
- influenced_by: none. Rota reused his own Sinfonia, which is self-reference.
- Reception: MMUK calls it "one of the finest scores of the Silver age" and "an essential film score".

## bacalov-ilvangelosecondomatteo (1964)
- content and genres: Senses of Cinema (Pasquale Iannone, 2007, https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2007/cteq/vangelo-secondo-matteo/): music "ranging from Bach's 'St. Matthew's Passion' … to pieces by Mozart and Prokofiev to popular Russian songs, Congolese spirituals and … Odetta's spine-tingling version of 'Sometimes I Feel Like a Motherless Child'". Cinemateca Portuguesa sheet (Rui Brito, 2022, https://www.cinemateca.pt/CinematecaSite/media/Documentos/2022-04-06_IL-VANGELO-SECONDO-MATTEO_1.pdf): "canções espirituais negros", the Gloria of the Missa Luba, Alexander Nevsky, Webern, the Russian revolutionary hymn. Blog review (https://moonmusick.blogspot.com/2009/11/red-moon-musick-other-arts.html): "self-consciously eclectic … from Bach to Prokofiev to folk music", "the haunting spiritual". cinemaitaliano.info (https://www.cinemaitaliano.info/news/24817/la-musica-de-il-vangelo-secondo-matteo-all.html): Bacalov was the "autore delle musiche originali, nonché arrangiatore e direttore delle altre musiche".
- genres: sacredmusic covers the masses and the Passion. spiritual is a new token, named by both Iannone and the Cinemateca. folk comes from the blog and the Russian popular songs.
- mood 1: Martellozzo (JRFM 2019, https://austria-forum.org/web-books/en/jrfm01de2019isds/000096) says the Bach BWV 1042 ground bass "marks a progressive darkening of mood"; the blog says "haunting". experimental 2: "decidedly singular" (Iannone) and the eclectic mix. Tempo, groove and theatrical are empty: there is not enough evidence.
- instruments: violin (Bach violin concerto BWV 1042, track list) and orchestra. language: la (Missa Luba Gloria), de (Bach Passion), en (Odetta), ru (Russian songs). I could not confirm which of the pieces are on the original 1964 LP versus the 1996 RCA CD, hence confidence low.
- landmark 0: no source calls it defining for Bacalov.
- Reception: Oscar nomination for Best Scoring of Music, Adaptation or Treatment, 1967 (MMUK obituary, https://moviemusicuk.us/2017/11/15/luis-enriquez-bacalov-1933-2017/). AllMusic returned 403.

## piccioni-lemanisullacitta (1963)
- genres and mood: Chris' Soundtrack Corner (GDM/Legend 4209, https://www.soundtrackcorner.de/from-three-movies-of-francesco-rosi-le-mani-sulla-citta-p4955.htm): "a recurring and quite macabre theme of tension, that explodes with the powerful action of the bass together with the entire orchestra"; "jazz-lounge music for the more picturesque moments". Dagored LP (https://soundohm.com/product/le-mani-sulla-citta): "A racy and doomy soundtrack filled with incredibly dark jazz tunes". Joco Records: "a masterful blend of jazz and dramatic, moody themes", "distinctively dark, sophisticated mood".
- instruments: orchestra only. The source says "the bass" without saying whether it is a double bass or a bass guitar, so I left it out.
- energy 3: "explodes", "powerful". mood 1: macabre, doomy, dark. Tempo and groove are empty.
- The original 1963 release was an RCA 45 (PME 30-494), per soundtrackcorner. The complete score came out first on CD from GDM and first on vinyl in 2016 (Dagored, 500 copies).
- Reception: Francesco Rosi (https://pieropiccioni.com/memorials.php): "Ennio Morricone has written that this is one of the most beautiful soundtracks ever written." Davinotti user review: the score is not very distinctive.
