## burnaboy-africangiant (African Giant, 2019)
- genres: Pitchfork (Sheldon Pearce, 2019-08-01) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/burna-boy-african-giant/ "Afro-fusion—blending pop, American hip-hop and R&B, Jamaican dancehall, and hard UK rap with Nigerian music". Clash https://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/burna-boy-african-giant "The relaxed Afrobeat vibe"; "a subtle dancehall theme which threads through them". NME (Kyann-Sian Williams) https://www.nme.com/reviews/burna-boy-african-giant-review-2533270 "fuses R&B and Afro-beats (usually called Afro-swing)" (Gum Body); "dancehall and reggae for 'Omo'". Pulse (Motolani Alake) https://www.pulse.ng/story/album-review-pulse-does-a-breakdown-of-burna-boys-african-giant-2024081321490330121 "a mixture of Fela's afrobeat melodies, afrobeats, vibes, reggae and dancehall"; 'Different' "on reggae fusion"; Gum Body "R&B fusion/synth pop". OkayAfrica readers' list https://www.okayafrica.com/the-100-best-african-albums-according-to-our-readers/1404354 "Afrobeat, fuji, reggae, R&B and rap". notJustOk https://notjustok.com/news/burna-boy-sunny-ade-and-fela-kuti-in-rolling-stones-500-greatest-albums/ "dancehall, R&B, and hip-hop". "hard UK rap" mapped to ukhiphop.
- scales: groove 4 (Pitchfork "never sacrifices its bewitching groove", "splendid hour of jams"); energy 3 / mood 3 (Clash "relaxed", "upbeat and infectious", "summer friendly" vs political songs); polish 4 (Pulse "the production on African Giant is simply exceptional"; Pitchfork "more robust in sound"); experimental 2 (genre blending, accessible pop); electronic 3 (programmed beats per MusicBrainz programming credits, guitars and sax).
- voice: Pitchfork "drifts into a refined falsetto" (Secret); NME "rapping on 'Show and Tell'".
- instruments: NME "guitar-assisted instrumental" (Collateral Damage); Pulse "The electronic guitar riffs"; Pitchfork "washed-out guitars"; MusicBrainz saxophone (Venna, Anybody) https://musicbrainz.org/release/8f2e3dff-fb71-43e3-88a9-a42d5463bbde
- production: programming (MusicBrainz credits); sampling (Pitchfork: mother's words "sampled from the BET speech"); falsetto (Pitchfork); lofi (Pulse "the lo-fi production on title track", one track).
- lyrics: Pitchfork "both personal and political", "dressing down corrupt officials"; Pulse "topics of love and gratitude", "activism and pan-Africanism", 'On The Low' "a song about sex".
- language: Pitchfork "here he sings primarily in Pidgin, Yoruba, and Igbo" (English used too).
- producers: MusicBrainz per-track credits. P2J is credited only for programming on Anybody (no producer credit) and Tourist as additional producer: both left out.
- sounds_like: Pan African Music 2010s list (Wale Owoade) https://pan-african-music.com/en/80-best-albums-2010s/ "incorporating Fela-esque adlibs"; Pulse "The electronic guitar riffs seem like Burna Boy's equivalent of Fela's saxophone".
- influenced_by: empty at album level (Fela statement is artist-level, see artists.md).
- landmark 1: Zikoko https://www.zikoko.com/pop/the-10-best-nigerian-albums-of-the-2010s/ "career-best LP ... already feels like a decade-defining classic"; Pulse "one career-defining album".

- lyrics: Pitchfork "heartbreak, grief, anxiety, politics, sex, and love"; Whiskey on pollution (social/political); Glory gratitude (spiritual).
- language: Pulse "richly blends English and Pidgin"; "Vanilla" "Delivered in pidgin"; OkayAfrica Ladysmith interlude "sung in isiZulu"; Pulse (Alake) "J Balvin sing-raps in Spanish".
- producers: MusicBrainz 19-track edition (incl. Toni-Ann Singh). Executive producers (Bose and Damini Ogulu, Discogs) dropped.

## davido-timeless (Timeless, 2023)
- genres: OkayAfrica https://www.okayafrica.com/davido-timeless-afrobeats-nigerian-music/ "songs that lean into dancehall, reggaeton, and highlife, the driving force for a significant portion of this album is amapiano"; "the konto groove of 'PRECISION'"; E Pain Me "hearkens to the pon-pon sound of the mid-2010s". Clash https://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/davido-timeless/ "afrobeats kingpin"; The Native https://thenativemag.com/featured/review-davidos-timeless/ "Afropop", "The neo-Highlife flourish on 'NA MONEY'", "Amapiano-coloured log drums"; Afrocritik https://www.afrocritik.com/?p=9937 "Dancehall influences", "basic Afroswing drums" (U Juju), "simple percussive Afropop drums"; Native 1-listen https://thenativemag.com/davido-timeless-1listen-review "R&B-tinged slapper", "afro-bashment knock" (mapped to dancehall).

## 2baba-face2face (Face 2 Face, 2004)
- genres: OkayAfrica https://www.okayafrica.com/african-music-albums-turning-20/ "With fine splashes of R&B, hip-hop, reggae and multiple genres in-between"; News Digest https://newsdigest.ng/?p=136946 "blended elements of hip-hop, the R&B influences ... and the emerging Afrobeats sound"; The Upper Ent https://theupperent.com/?p=10688 "mid-tempo R&B tinged 'Right Here'".
- scales: most left empty (no source describes energy/production in detail). mood 4: Pulse https://www.pulse.ng/entertainment/music/face-2-face-by-2face-idibia-14-years-after-id8384675.html "a very soulful, vibrant and conscious album"; groove 3: notJustOk https://notjustok.com/article/2face-idibias-albums-ranked-from-worst-to-best/6/ "the party and crowd moving Keep On Rocking" + love ballads.
- voice: Upper Ent "a distinctive tenor", "smooth talker" (clear); Pulse "2face spitting a few rap bars" (Nfana Ibaga).
- lyrics: Pulse "moods falling somewhere in between love, reality, fun and gratitutude", "socially reflective in songs like U no holy pass".
- language: en (lyrics quoted in English by Pulse) | pcm (Pidgin titles/lyrics, "U No Holy Pass"); weak, judgment.
- producers: Discogs https://www.discogs.com/release/33012624-2face-Idibia-Face-2-Face "Producer - OJB Jezreel"; "Written-By, Composed By, Arranged By, Producer - 2face Idibia". (Pulse says "production solely handled by the late OJB Jezreel"; disagreement noted.) Mark Drummond (remixes, mastering) and executive producer Kenny Ogungbe dropped.
- landmark 1: Pulse "2baba's golden album"; OkayAfrica "set the convention for modern Nigerian music"; News Digest "the landmark 'Face2Face'".
- Upper Ent compares the debut to Michael Jackson's Off the Wall as a career launch, not sound: not used.

## Artist-level influences (own words)

- **Burna Boy**, interviewed in The FADER (2015-11-25), https://www.thefader.com/2015/11/25/burna-boy-interview:
  - "Music-wise obviously number one is Fela Kuti. My granddad used to be his manager."
  - On his dad's records: "Super Cat, Ninja Man, Buju Banton. On his side, I heard a lot of reggae and dancehall."
  - "this girl I was trying to get with gave me a Joe CD for my birthday, which introduced me to R&B."
  - "Everything I know I pretty much learned from Fela."
  - Influences: Fela Kuti, Super Cat, Ninja Man, Buju Banton, Joe.
- **Wizkid**, quoted by TheNewsGuru, https://thenewsguru.ng/entertainment/not-trying-live-like-fela:
  - "Fela happens to be a very big inspiration to my music, Bob Marley as well."
  - The Source (https://thesource.com/?p=404554) adds, in the outlet's words, that he listened to King Sunny Ade and Fela as a child. That is not a direct quote.
  - Influences: Fela Kuti, Bob Marley.
- **Davido**, quoted by notJustOk (2025-12-07), https://notjustok.com/?p=509504:
  - "When I was younger, I listened to more of Hip Hop, so like 50 Cent, Nelly, Ludacris. Also R&B, Usher."
  - Influences: 50 Cent, Nelly, Ludacris, Usher.
- **2Baba**, interviewed by Afropop Worldwide (2017-10-26), https://www.afropop.org/articles/2baba-talks-the-future-is-bright-for-nigerian-music:
  - "I gravitated towards reggae music more. Bob Marley was my favorite, you know, and I guess that's the basis of most of my music, reggae."
  - "Growing up a little bit further, Majek Fashek came about and he became my favorite."
  - Local musicians he looked up to: "Sonny Okosun, Chris Okotie, Bongos Ikwue, Fela Anikulapo Kuti".
  - Influences: Bob Marley, Majek Fashek, Fela Kuti, Sonny Okosun.
