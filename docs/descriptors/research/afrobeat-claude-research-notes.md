# Afrobeat: evidence notes

## Why these three
- **Fela Kuti, Zombie (1976)**: the most-cited Afrobeat album: first Nigerian album in the Grammy Hall of Fame (2025), 1001 Albums, Pitchfork's 70s top 100 (#90), Treble 70s #19, AllMusic "most popular and impacting record"; it provoked the 1977 army raid on Kalakuta. AOTY/RYM user charts (search summaries only, both 403) also put it first among 1970s Afrobeat.
- **Tony Allen, Black Voices (1999)**: Allen is the genre's co-creator (Fela: "without Tony Allen, there would be no Afrobeat", en.wikipedia). NME calls Black Voices "one of Allen's most important works"; Songlines says it was received as a new direction for Afrobeat. Alternatives considered: his 1970s Africa 70 LPs (Jealousy, Progress) have little critical coverage I could read; Songlines' Essential 10 picks Film of Life (2014), which has less historical weight.
- **Femi Kuti, Shoki Shoki (1998)**: the record that relaunched Afrobeat after Fela's death (1997): Afropop calls it his "international breakout", Songlines lists it in Afrobeat's Essential 10. Critics were mixed at the time (3/5 from AllMusic and Rolling Stone, Christgau "dud", EW A-), hence acclaim 3. Antibalas (US) left out: the owner's discovery rule avoids US acts; Ebo Taylor (Ghana, 1977) was the other candidate but is closer to highlife/Afro-funk and thinly documented.

## Zombie
- **Year 1976** (en.wikipedia, MusicBrainz, AP, Graphic, Grammy coverage); Discogs dates the Coconut LP 1977 and the UK Creole issue is 1977. Kept 1976.
- **Drummer conflict**: every sleeve credit on Discogs (Nigerian Coconut, UK Creole, 2001 MCA) names **Ladi Alabi** (Leader, Drums); NME, Bandcamp and AP credit Tony Allen's drumming on "Zombie". Not resolved; doesn't change any column (no `influenced_by` used). Flag before writing album notes about Allen.
- **Instruments** straight from the sleeve: tenor/alto/baritone sax, two trumpets (Tunde Williams solo), three guitars (first, tenor, third), bass, three congas, sticks/claves, maracas, Fela on piano. Music of Africa mentions no organ; Fela usually played organ live, but the sleeve says piano, so `organ` left out.
- **genres**: afrobeat (all sources); jazz and funk (AP: blends West African rhythms with jazz and funk; Discogs genre Jazz; MusicBrainz jazz); `afropop` only from a 1-vote MusicBrainz tag: weakest, last. Discogs' "Folk, World, & Country" is a shelving genre, not used.
- **Scales**: tempo 4 / energy 4 from "brisk", "frenetic", quick-march; groove 5 (danceable per all reviews); theatrical 4: drill-sergeant commands, mock parade, call-and-response chorus; polish 2: 1970s Lagos two-track LP; electronic 1: electric guitars/bass, no synths.
- **voice** spoken (barked commands; cool, dispassionate delivery) | choral (eight credited female singers).
- **language** pcm: en.wikipedia: most of Fela's songs are in Nigerian Pidgin.
- `sounds_like`: editorial (Allen and Femi carry the same Africa 70 template; James Brown per AP's funk link).

## Black Voices
- Two versions exist: the 1999 Doctor L mix (the album as released, described here) and the 2010/2011 *Black Voices Revisited* with the raw mix. Songlines and Record Collector prefer the raw mix and find the 1999 trip-hop/dub production dated; Exclaim (1999) and NME praise the original release. That disagreement is why confidence is medium.
- **producers** Doctor L (= Loïc Dury, credited under both names on Discogs) | Da Link (track 3). Eric Trosset "produced for release" for Comet (label role, not listed).
- **production**: Exclaim: drums recorded, edited and looped, programmed drum sounds added → `drumloop|drumprogramming`; "vocoded riffs" → `vocoder` (also in instruments); Songlines: remixed in trip-hop style with DJ effects → `remixing|djeffects`; dub effects (Bandcamp, Record Collector) → `dubeffects`.
- **instruments**: Discogs credits: drums/percussion (Allen), bass (Cesar Anot), guitar (Sébastien Martel; Bandcamp "waves of electric guitar"), keyboards, Wurlitzer (`electricpiano`), clavinet (Fixi). Record Collector calls the *original* mix brass-heavy and Songlines notes a trumpet solo by Nicolas Giraud on the raw version, but brass isn't credited on the 1999 CD: brass left out.
- **voice** soft|spoken from Exclaim's "half-sung, half-whispered"; P-Funk guests Clip Payne and Gary Mudbone Cooper sing lead on tracks 2, 3, 6 (Discogs); Record Collector's track mapping differs.
- **language** en: English titles and P-Funk singers; Allen's "Asiko" / "Ariya" titles are Yoruba words, but no source says he sings in Yoruba, so `yo` left out. **lyrics** left empty: no source describes the themes.
- **genres**: afrobeat (all), dub (Exclaim, Songlines, Record Collector), electronica (NME, Record Collector), triphop (Songlines, for the Doctor L mix), futurejazz (Discogs), funk/jazz/highlife (NME), electronic (Discogs, MusicBrainz).

## Shoki Shoki
- **producer** Sodi (Sodi Marciszewer, also produced Fela records). Mark Saunders mixed and did additional programming (mixer, not producer).
- **genres**: afrobeat; funk (Discogs Funk/Soul, PEI "James Brown-style funk"); acid jazz, hip-hop, soul (EW via Wikipedia); neo-soul (The Naija Way); big-band jazz (PEI review) → `bigband|jazz`; downtempo, electronic (Discogs).
- **instruments**: Discogs credits: alto/tenor/baritone sax, trumpet, trombone, two drummers, congas, rhythm guitar, keyboards; bass isn't credited (probably a guest among the uncredited-role "Musician [Guest]" names), so left out. **production** `programming` (Sodi, Saunders, Neskovic, Yansos credited for programming); `hornarrangements` (Femi "Arranged By").
- **voice** left empty: no source describes Femi's singing on this record.
- **lyrics** political/social (A.V. Club: AIDS, poverty, corruption; anti-government per Naija Way), identity (Blackman Know Yourself, Black identity), sensuality ("Beng Beng Beng", banned for sexual content).
- **influenced_by** Fela Kuti: A.V. Club describes the album reworking Fela's rave-ups and Rolling Stone (via Wikipedia) says Femi treats Afrobeat's basic formula as sacred; plus Femi's own statement (UK Jazz News). Medium.
- **sounds_like** The Jungle Brothers: A.V. Club comparison for "Blackman Know Yourself" ("during the group's Pass The Peas peak"; no album named, so no brackets). James Brown: PEI review.
- Correction to a search summary: the "not overly catchy" line in that PEI paper is about Kid Koala, not Femi.
- Dates: Nigeria/France 1998 (Barclay), international/US 1999–2000 (MCA). Kept 1998.
