# Mia Martini — Phase 01 evidence notes

Only albums 1971–1974. Four new rows. Treat scales as project judgments informed by whole-album evidence rather than a quantitative measurement. Values not established by a qualified album-specific source are left empty.

## Oltre la collina (1971) — `miamartini-oltrelacollina`

**Scope.** Original RCA PSL 10516, twelve tracks (November 1971); Hit Parade and the 2022 Rockol retrospective verify original track list and date. Do not merge singles under the previous stage name Mimì Bertè or expanded remasters. `landmark 1`: Rockol and OndaRock identify both the major thematic programme that persisted in her career and an exceptionally early, musically forward-looking Italian concept LP. This is more precise than mere critical acclaim. Independently, DROGA hears progressive traits over multiple songs.

**Genres.** `prog` first because DROGA describes progressive construction on multiple album numbers and Rockol calls the key single *Padre davvero* prog-rock. `pop` second from the original-release Apple Music pop categorisation. `soul` occurs specifically in *Testamento* (DROGA) and does **not** imply every song is soul. `songwriter` is the concept-driven Italian song-author tradition: OndaRock describes the lyrical arc by Baglioni, Coggio and De Sanctis, with first-person narratives from Martini. Because this is a *singer-interpreter* album, this last style is a medium-confidence genre rather than a claim that Martini herself composed everything. No unsupported `psychedelic` token; genres are not inferred from the year.

**Eight scales (0–5).** `energy 3`: forceful prog/electric passages versus ballads and quiet, reflective songs. `electronic 1`: electrified guitar, organic orchestra, no documented synthesizer or drum machines. `mood 1`: sustained anguish, estrangement, death and family conflict, despite a lighter cover track. `theatrical 4`: strong projected vocal, sharp protest and dramatic concept arrangements, below overt rock opera. `experimental 4`: unusual songwriting, difficult themes, early concept sequencing and progressive rock, not fully avant-garde. `polish 2`: professional RCA studio and orchestral musicians, but relatively unglossy edgy vocal/guitar foreground; no engineer evidence for lo-fi. `tempo 3`: strong and moderate tempo pieces with slower narrative episodes. `groove 2`: some rhythmic propulsion; not dance-led. These are interpretive album-wide scores.

**Instruments.** `electricguitar` specifically supported by DROGA descriptions of the first tracks; `guitar` is broad because the reviews do not classify all other guitar timbres by acoustic/electric model; `orchestra` supported by original RCA original record conductor credits (Tony Mimms most cuts, Piero Pintucci on *Padre davvero*, Ruggero Cini on *Amore... amore... un corno!*). Do NOT add a horn, organ, exact synthesizer or percussion simply because orchestras imply instrumental sections. Original RCA credits reproduced by Hit Parade Italia. **Production:** `choirvocals` from *I Cantori Moderni di Alessandroni* on *Padre davvero* and *Amore... amore... un corno!* and Four Kents on *The Lion Sleeps Tonight*. No overdubbing claims without an engineer source. `voice powerful` from the album-specific DROGA/critic descriptions of aggressive, soaring delivery. Spoken actors on the closing track do not make the entire LP spoken-word.

**Producer dispute.** Original release and digital label credits **Alberigo Crocetta** (the collector discography has a misspelling *Allberigo*). OndaRock calls the album a Baglioni–Coggio production; the label explicitly calls Coggio production coordinator and Rockol describes Baglioni as songwriter. Keep Crocetta only under the strict producer-credit rule. **Lyrics:** `social|identity|spiritual|heartbreak|introspection|storytelling|political` (intergenerational conflict, spirituality and suicide etc.); priority social/identity based on censored singles. `language it|en` because *The Lion Sleeps Tonight* retains its English text (unlike most cover adaptations). **sounds_like and influenced_by** blank; no comparisons from critics of the LP's sound at the artist level, and covers are not influences. Citations in the sources CSV.

## Nel mondo, una cosa (1972) — `miamartini-nelmondounacosa`

**Scope.** Ricordi SMRL 6101, 12 Italian-language songs, original 5 October 1972. Original 1972 sleeve track/performer/arranger reproductions (Hit Parade Italia; Passionemusicaitalia) and BMG Apple/Qobuz metadata. `landmark 1`: independent OndaRock history identifies the two signature singles and her decisive early commercial and interpretive breakthrough, with the album cementing this identity. Alternative 0 is defensible only if Claude applies a stricter threshold than a career-changing breakthrough.

**Genres.** `pop` album-wide per Apple Music, `soul` the widely discussed *Donna sola* performance (album hit), `blues` specifically the *Madre* reworking and blues colouring of *Donna sola* (OndaRock and Hit Parade), `gospel` from a documented gospel chorus on *Donna sola* (lead single); `waltz` only the *Valsinha* rendition (1/12), low-weight tail. The fact she **covers** John Lennon, Elton John, Gary Wright or Chico Buarque does not justify `influenced_by`. Musical style of a covered composition may remain in the arrangement but only the actually documented named genre is added here. No speculative `mpb`, `folk` or `softrock` just from a covered author.

**Eight scales.** `energy 2`: predominantly slow-midtempo love songs, sparse driving tracks. `electronic 1`: piano, organ and acoustic guitars, conventional orchestral setting, not synth-programmed. `mood 2`: romantic hurt and difficult themes dominate over occasional uplifting choruses. `theatrical 3`: emphatic vocal and sweeping arrangement but generally direct interpretations. `experimental 2`: gospel/soul/blues/waltz incorporations into conventional Italian pop; more accessible than 1971. `polish 3`: coordinated Ricordi pop and orchestral sessions, clean studio rather than raw live. `tempo 2`: ballads and waltz with a few more forceful tracks. `groove 1`: mainly listening-oriented, not dance-led. These are original album averages, not the 2025 remaster.

**Instruments.** Original record explicitly lists **Dario Baldan Bembo piano and organ**, **La Bionda brothers plus Maurizio and Salvatore Fabrizio acoustic guitars** (not automatically `electricguitar`). `orchestra` directly documented in Natale Massara and Baldan Bembo conductor/arrangement credit. `choirvocals` is warranted specifically by gospel choir on *Donna sola*. Do not infer model of organ, exact brass or drum sound from the generic orchestral setting. **Producer** Crocetta: Rockol composer interview 1973 explicitly says Sanjust succeeded him as producer from the third record. The 1972 original LP discography leaves its production line blank, and earlier 1972 single producer credits differ; if original *full LP* credit later confirms Sanjust as co-producer, add only with citation. **voice powerful|soft** from album-specific Hit Parade criticism of formidable soul performance and contrasting tenderness. No album-specific `sounds_like` artist comparisons found, no session artist testimony for influenced_by.

## Il giorno dopo (1973) — `miamartini-ilgiornodopo`

**Scope.** Original Ricordi SMRL 6114, twelve Italian-language tracks, first released May 1973. The OndaRock review discusses the **2023 remaster** but all core observations concern original 1973 tracks; excluded bonus instrumental and French/Spanish *Minuetto* versions. `landmark 1`: OndaRock explicitly says this album cemented her place in Italian audiences' hearts; independently singles *Minuetto* and *Bolero* were career-defining. A high quality review is not itself basis for landmark; the stated career impact is.

**Genres.** `pop` and `songwriter` are explicitly listed by OndaRock (original LP discussed even though review page is filed under a 2023 remaster). `ballad` from multiple acoustic/piano ballads; the 1973 album opens with a quiet acoustic song and climaxes in the constructed pop ballad *Minuetto*. `musicaleggera` is Rockol’s music-leggera-era characterization of the original LP and broad Italian mainstream popular-music context; this last genre is secondary and has an element of judgement. `Minuetto` does not equal the traditional minuet dance genre; the title alone does not justify adding a `minuet` token. Only four sufficiently well-grounded categories, rather than invented long-tail genres.

**Eight scales.** `energy 2`: quiet ballads and controlled dramatic buildups. `electronic 1`: ordinary 1973 piano, guitar, orchestral instrumentation, no machines mentioned. `mood 1`: despair, abandonment, addiction, fear and painful dependency. `theatrical 4`: strongly expressive lead and piano/choir-led crescendos, notably *Minuetto*. `experimental 2`: unusually complex three-section *Minuetto*, otherwise familiar pop song forms. `polish 3`: professionally orchestrated Ricordi studio work. `tempo 2`: slow/midtempo songwriting is dominant. `groove 1`: little dance emphasis despite track title *La discoteca* (no genre deduction from that title).

**Instruments.** `piano` explicitly from *Minuetto*, *Bolero*; `guitar|acousticguitar` specifically supported by OndaRock's *Ma quale amore* and *Dove il cielo va a finire*; `keyboards` supported in original participants Dario Baldan Bembo, but model unspecified; `orchestra` original 1973 conductor track credits from Hit Parade/Ricordi and Qobuz. `choirvocals` directly heard/critic-described in *Minuetto* finale, no unverified string or horn production token. Original LP producer **Giovanni Sanjust** from Rockol's direct interview with the composer (and OndaRock critic); Massara and Gianfranco Lombardi are conductors only. **voice powerful|clear** from OndaRock's album-specific soaring yet clean/transparent performance descriptions. **Lyrics** focus `love|heartbreak|introspection|social|storytelling`, especially *La malattia* social taboo. Covers of Elton John and Serrat excluded from `influenced_by`; no critic comparison with their own styles documented for this performance.

## È proprio come vivere (1974) — `miamartini-epropriocomevivere`

**Scope.** Original Ricordi SMRL 6126, ten tracks, May 1974; original 1974 LP came in two sleeves with different insert detail, not two albums. Reissue covers differ. `Agapimu` sung in Modern Greek and otherwise Italian songs, so `language it|el`; its Greek lyric is preserved independently of post-1974 versions. *En Español* 1974 is a separate *compilation of rerecorded versions*, excluded by current compilation rule.

**Genres.** `pop` from Apple original LP label, `ballad` from 1974 pressing catalogue metadata (original LP tagged Pop/Ballad), `musicaleggera` broad national pop song classification (OndaRock context), `chanson` only for *Domani*, which OndaRock specifically calls Aznavour-like: the French song-comparison is one of 10 songs, never the dominant genre. No whole-album rock claims.

**Eight scales.** `energy 2`: predominance of lyrical ballads; occasional stronger crescendos. `electronic 1`: documented conventional guitar/keyboards/live rhythm and orchestra, no sourced synthesizer. `mood 2`: introspective and romantic songs with some uplift, still largely plaintive. `theatrical 3`: full expressive vocals and orchestration but not the explicit drama of 1973. `experimental 2`: complex song structures in *Inno*, a Greek sung song, and a moderately varied arrangement palette, otherwise conventional melodic pop. `polish 3`: professional well-produced arrangements and pop session, no evidence for extreme studio gloss. `tempo 2`: slow-midtempo numbers. `groove 1`: little club/dance feel. Treat these as editorial averages.

**Instruments.** `guitar|keyboards|bass|drums|orchestra`: original second issue says personnel were credited; secondary reproductions name players (Dario Baldan Bembo on keyboards, Gigi Cappellotto bass, Andy Surdi drums, Maurizio/Salvatore Fabrizio and others guitar) but the opened original record listing on Hit Parade only confirms the *existence* of a later pressing credit list. Claude should reconcile the full **second pressing** against these transcribed names and remove anything disputed. `choirvocals` based on named Expo 80 group credited for backing vocals in documented later-press transcriptions, medium certainty. The Qobuz reissue lists conductor Natale Massara but does not verify session model or studio hardware. Do not infer specific instruments solely from modern orchestration. **producer Giovanni Sanjust** from album history and continuity from 1973; some individual single credits include Alberigo Crocetta, but not a verified original 1974 LP co-producer credit. An odd 2025 remaster per-track *Dick Halligan producer* metadata must not be read backward as a 1974 original LP credit.

**voice** left blank: sources evaluate her phrasing generally but do not unambiguously designate the main 1974 vocal timbre using the guide's voice tokens. **sounds_like** `Charles Aznavour [1/10]` because the independently reviewed track *Domani* is called Aznavour-like. Critic sonic resemblance is *not* `influenced_by`; there is no session-specific influence interview. **Lyrics** `love|heartbreak|introspection|identity|nostalgia`: most songs revolve around emotional and personal reflection; Greek *Agapimu* expresses romantic yearning. `landmark=0`: review calls album enjoyable and commercially successful but has not been shown to independently *define* a new sound or career era, so no inflation.

## Album-wide source disputes and omissions

- Songs **covered** are not direct influences; songwriter credits are not automatically producer credits. Independent reviewer style comparisons are `sounds_like`, not influences. Retrospective artist influence interview names in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` never migrate into the album's `influenced_by` without album-specific evidence.
- No Wikipedia URL is in the sources CSV. Some original pressing details are mirrored by secondary bibliographies; the weakest uncertain details are marked in this notes file, not asserted as certain.
- Record-review references to 2023/2025 *remastering* concern subsequent products, not original 1970s album recording methods. The production column contains no `remastering` token.
- 1974 genre `chanson` and 1973 `musicaleggera` have lower evidence strength; Claude should remove a token rather than force the wrong genre if these stylistic reads cannot be corroborated.
