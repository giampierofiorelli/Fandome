# Harry Styles — Phase 01 | evidence notes

Snapshot: 27 October 2026. Original studio albums only. Album-level scales are **editorial estimates**, with guide anchors, from cited album-specific evidence. They do not claim scientific measurements. A source key in brackets matches the `source` column of the genre-evidence CSV and the URL table in the sources CSV.

## harrystyles-harrystyles — Harry Styles (2017)

**Style / genre weighting.** Primary pop-rock, soft-rock and rock follow the period review and critic genre classification. Britpop is strongest around “Sign of the Times”; hard rock applies to “Only Angel” and “Kiwi”; folk-pop to the intimate acoustic sections; alt-country is explicitly a *few tracks* in Pitchfork. Psychedelia is a secondary texture, not the main category. [pf2017, g2017, n2019, a2017]

**Scales.** Energy **3** because there is hard-rock drive but also slow guitar songs. Electronic **1** because reviewers and credits describe predominantly played guitars, drums, bass and piano, with some studio programming. Mood **2** for the extensive regret, longing and loneliness despite swaggering rock sections. Theatrical **3** for the scale of “Sign of the Times” against restrained closers. Experimental **1** for conventional classic-rock forms. Polish **4** because a major-label team shapes a detailed, clean record; tempo **2**, groove **2** given ballad-heavy pacing and some rock stomps. [pf2017, g2017, mb2017]

**Vocals.** Clear lead tone, occasional gravelly intensity, intimate soft singing on “From the Dining Table”, and the powerful finale of “Sign of the Times”. The falsetto value has direct support in Pitchfork; backing-vocal credits do not automatically justify a vocal-processing effect. [pf2017, d2017]

**Instruments / production.** Album release credits document electric and acoustic guitars, drums, bass, piano, keyboards, organ, strings (including violin/viola/cello), and handclaps. Production `programming` comes from explicit Tyler Johnson programming credit, not an inference that programmed beats dominate. No specific drum machine or analog-tape format is asserted. A 2020 artist interview describes an early wish to record to tape which he later abandoned as a stylistic fixation; this does **not** establish a verified tape-based recording chain for the album. [mb2017, d2017, nr2020]

**Producers.** Jeff Bhasker was executive producer and has underlying producer/co-producer credits; Alex Salibian, Tyler Johnson and Kid Harpoon have production credits on tracks. Thus all four are included, not engineer-only staff. Note that the label’s phrase “additional production” by itself would not meet the user’s producer threshold; MusicBrainz track credits resolve it. [s2017, mb2017]

**Influence vs resemblance.** Stones, Beatles and Fleetwood Mac are independently named by Styles among the formative artists whose references shaped his musical vocabulary. David Bowie is described by NME as inspiration for “Sign of the Times.” Elton John, Wolfmother and Coldplay are critic comparisons, not asserted album-making influences. [nr2020, n2019, pf2017]

**Critical disagreement.** Pitchfork praises the singing and classic-rock texture while criticising thin lyrical characterization; The Guardian likewise sees accomplished homages but doubts how thoroughly he escapes derivative gestures. `landmark=1` recognizes his defining solo debut, not universal acclaim. [pf2017, g2017]

## harrystyles-fineline — Fine Line (2019)

**Genre weighting.** Pop-rock and psychedelic pop are central. Power pop and Laurel Canyon folk-rock are expressly identified in Pitchfork; NME hears indie-pop on “Golden”, soul on “Lights Up”, and funk on rhythmic songs. AllMusic’s “Americana” is limited to “Canyon Moon”. Genres placed late have restricted track coverage. [pf2019, n2019, am2019]

**Scales.** Energy **3** (highly rhythmic early tracks contrasted with reflective ballads); electronic **2** (substantial guitars, piano and percussion, with synth textures rather than electronic dominance); mood **3** (bright surfaces / sad breakup material); theatrical **3** (expansive arrangements but intimate delivery); experimental **2** (psychedelic details within familiar pop songwriting); polish **4**; tempo **3** and groove **3** reflecting a mix of movement-oriented and slower numbers. [pf2019, n2019]

**Voice / lyrics.** Soft intimate passages, clearly articulated pop singing, rising choruses; regret, breakup and sensual longing are discussed in contemporary reviews and interviews. Layered harmonies on “Golden” justify `layeredvocals`, not a claim about specific vocal plugins. [pf2019, n2019]

**Instrumentation.** The credited players use acoustic and electric guitars, live drums, bass, keyboards, piano, synths and Moog bass, organ, Mellotron, horns, celeste/glockenspiel in scattered tracks, strings and cello, percussion, and a dulcimer made by the instrument maker associated with Joni Mitchell. MusicBrainz identifies hammered dulcimer on “Canyon Moon.” These are album-wide lists of documented appearances, not claims that every instrument is on every track. [mb2019, q2019, mw2019]

**Production.** Credited drum programming on individual cuts and background-harmony layering; instrumental Moog credits justify machine family `moog` without asserting a specific model. String arrangements follow published album credits. **No analogtape:** Styles explicitly describes abandoning the notion of recording entirely to tape. [mb2019, q2019, nr2020]

**Producers.** Kid Harpoon and Tyler Johnson are the principal producers; Jeff Bhasker, Sammy Witte and Greg Kurstin have producer/co-producer credits on selected tracks. An engineer credit alone would not qualify Witte, but the recording/track credits indicate actual producer work. [mb2019, q2019, rw2019]

**Influences.** Styles describes his earlier formative listening (Stones, Beatles, Fleetwood Mac, etc.), and Music Week profiles this project's listening diet as McCartney and Fleetwood Mac. His pursuit of a Joni Mitchell-type dulcimer supports her as a specific sonic inspiration. Don't confuse the critic's Coldplay/Bon Iver comparisons with sourced intent; those remain in `sounds_like`. [mw2019, nr2020, pf2019, am2019]

**Critical disagreement.** Pitchfork admires arrangements but finds the lyrics generic; NME hears a stronger, more coherent artist identity. Both justify strong confidence in *sound*, not unanimous praise. [pf2019, n2019]

## harrystyles-harryshouse — Harry’s House (2022)

**Genre weighting.** Contemporary pop first; the album repeatedly uses synth-led 1980s stylings, funk and disco, with new-wave and soul colors. The Guardian names soft rock alongside synth-pop; Slant also explicitly identifies synth-pop. Yacht rock describes “Cinema” in the Guardian, folk the acoustic “Matilda” / “Boyfriends”, R&B appears in “Daylight”. City-pop is explicitly named by a second-tier review as an influence, so placed **last** with texture scope and a caveat; see new token below. [pf2022, gr2022, n2022, g2022, sl2022, gg2022, pa2022]

**Scales.** Energy **3** and tempo **3** because many songs move with springy rhythms while ballads remain; electronic **3** from near-constant synth layers plus playing by a conventional band; mood **4** for predominant warmth and optimism; theatrical **2** for domestic/intimate presentation; experimental **2** for playful synth/funk arrangements in otherwise polished pop; polish **5** for dense precise production and groove **4** for the album’s regular disco/funk pulse. These are *whole-album* judgments. [n2022, pf2022, g2022, gr2022]

**Instrumentation.** The full Pitchfork credit sheet documents synthesizers, electric/acoustic guitars, piano, bass and synth bass, drums and drum programming, trumpets, Mellotron, Rhodes/Wurlitzer electric piano, cello, saxophone, guitar synthesizer, clavinet, pump organ, glockenspiel, percussion and tambourine. Even unusual credits are track-specific: sax and cello occur on “Matilda”; talkbox and guitar synth on “Cinema”; pump organ and clavinet on “Satellite.” `drummachine` is stated explicitly across Qobuz’s label-supplied credits for many tracks (including 'As It Was'), independently of credited drum programming. [pc2022, q2022]

**Production.** `sampling` is specifically from the Brothers Johnson’s “Ain’t We Funkin’ Now” used on “Daydreaming” (not an artist influence). `talkbox` is Kid Harpoon on “Cinema”; `minimoog` is the Moog Model D credited on “Love of My Life” (identification supported by corresponding MusicBrainz Model D credit). Drum programming, generic programming and vocal layering are attested in liner-note credits and contemporary criticism. `falsetto` is explicitly identified in the Recording Academy analysis. No software brand is invented. [pc2022, gr2022, pf2022, q2022]

**Producers.** Kid Harpoon and Tyler Johnson produce the album's majority, with Sammy Witte **co-producing “Cinema”**; other guest writers, performers, mixing engineers and sample-rights holders are excluded. [pc2022]

**Influences.** Styles told Rolling Stone that Haruomi Hosono’s 1973 *Hosono House* inspired the album. Hence `Haruomi Hosono (Hosono House)` is an album-specific documented influence. Joni Mitchell's similarly named song “Harry's House/Centerpiece” is discussed in the NME review; a shared title is not proof of a musical influence. The Brothers Johnson sample is expressly not entered in `influenced_by`. [r2022, n2022, pc2022]

**Critical disagreement.** NME hears a confident intimate album and particularly inventive “Satellite”; Pitchfork sees more charm than lyrical insight; Guardian hears meticulously built pop amid familiar retro cues. These interpretations explain `experimental=2` rather than a high experimental score. [n2022, pf2022, g2022]

## Research limits and production precision

- Verified tokens are deliberately not extrapolated from genre. No unverified synthesizer model, tape medium, effect plugin or specific DAW is assigned.
- `drummachine` for *Harry’s House* is independently attested by Qobuz's track-level instrument credits. This does not identify a specific make/model; none is assigned.
- `citypop` is a proposal based on a lower-authority album review, not a universally agreed central classification. It is a **new genre token** and should be added before validation if retained.
- A prominent credited instrument appearing on only a few tracks still belongs in the instruments list but does not determine the genre ordering or electronic scale.
