# Enya — Phase 01 research / evidence notes

Scope: **Enya** (1987), **Watermark** (1988), **Shepherd Moons** (1991), **The Memory of Trees** (1995). Sources are keyed by their titles and URLs in `enya_phase01_sources.csv`. 0–5 values are editorial assessments of each **entire album**, not numeric claims from the publications. `instrumental` is not used in voice simply because some tracks lack singing.

## Enya (1987) — enya-enya

- **Identity and date:** first pressed in the UK by BBC in 1987 as *Enya*, under catalogue REB 605 (45cat). MusicBrainz groups some 1986 production/copyright dates as 1986 releases. This phase takes the commercial original-album year **1987**, with the 1992 *The Celts* reissue mapped to the same row (Rhino), not counted again. The 1992 programme lengthens/replaces 'Portrait'.
- **Genres:** AllMusic assigns Celtic New Age, Celtic, Contemporary Celtic, Contemporary Instrumental, ethnic-fusion descriptors. `newage` is the market/critical category, not Enya's preferred self-description. `ambient` and `etherealwave` here refer to album-specific AOTY style tags rather than an identical sound throughout; there are discrete folk/dance moments and an electric-guitar part. `celticfolk` is a published album style tag with weaker support than AllMusic's new-age categorisation.
- **Instruments:** transcribed original credits specifically state Enya on piano, Juno-60, Yamaha DX7, Emulator II sampler and Kurzweil; Arty McGlynn plays **electric guitar** on 'I Want Tomorrow'; Liam O'Flynn plays **uilleann pipes** on 'The Sun in the Stream'; Patrick Halling plays **violin** on 'To Go Beyond II' (Enya One and EnyaBlues). `synth`, `sampler`, `keyboards` map to the separately named devices. Emulator II is a sampler, but this does **not** prove sampled vocal choirs.
- **Production:** use `juno60|dx7|emulatorii` precisely; `layeredvocals` is consistent with the debut's multi-voice arrangement, but do **not** transfer the specific 32-track Mitsubishi setup of *Watermark* to this record. Produced by Nicky Ryan; Bruce Talbot is **executive producer**, Nigel Reid engineer, neither is a producer column credit.
- **Voice and lyrics:** Irish `ga`, English `en`, Welsh `cy`; the original is largely instrumental or wordless/chant-based. Historical/mythological themes and imagery explain `storytelling|spiritual`, supported by the BBC historical series and lyrics; no invented song-by-song autobiography.
- **Scales:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 0 reflect short ambient themes with some rhythmic processional passages. Electronic 3 reflects substantial synth/sampler alongside human voices and isolated acoustic guests. Polish 3 recognises polished scoring while distinguishing it from later large-budget studio production. Theatrical 3 follows multi-part ceremonial settings; experimental 2 marks soundtrack construction but retains melodic forms.

## Watermark (1988) — enya-watermark

- **Genre balance:** AllMusic and Apple Music position this at Celtic/new-age crossover, while AOTY lists ethereal-wave, ambient pop and Celtic folk descriptors. The most famous single, 'Orinoco Flow', is atypically brisk; it cannot make the entire album dance-pop. Album genre order weights slow pieces more heavily. `modernclassical` derives from MusicBrainz tagging and especially instrumental piano writing, a texture-level classification.
- **Instruments:** Nicky Ryan's March 1989 **Sound On Sound** interview specifically credits Enya keyboard performances (not sequenced); a rented **Roland D-50** provided the pizzicato patch for 'Orinoco Flow'. Original guests are Chris Hughes percussion/hand drums/roto-toms, Neil Buckley clarinet, Davy Spillane uilleann pipes and low whistle. Later retrospective MusicRadar describes a 30-piece live string section/timpani on 'Orinoco Flow'. Piano/keyboards backed by Apple Music album editorial.
- **Production technique:** **hundreds of separate sung parts** captured and bounced across **two Mitsubishi 32-track digital tape machines**, mixed on separate final tracks; **no sequencer used** and no sampled vocal parts on this album (Sound On Sound). `layeredvocals|digitaltape|heavyreverb|rolandd50` are therefore justified, and `sampling`/`sequencer` must **not** be inserted. `juno60` appears in album liner-discussions (Enya One), but D-50 is explicitly track-specific. MusicRadar describes Lexicon reverb; the specific Lexicon model isn't in the column because no new plugin is warranted. The analogue-tape token would be *wrong* here.
- **Producers:** the 1988 MusicBrainz release specifically distinguishes `producer: Nicky Ryan` and `co-producer: Enya, Ross Cullum`. All three are therefore included. Roma Ryan is lyricist, Rob Dickins executive producer; neither should be included. Sound On Sound spells the engineer 'Ross Collum', other label credits 'Ross Cullum'; normalise to **Ross Cullum**, note spelling discrepancy.
- **Languages and themes:** `en|ga|la`; track 'Cursum Perficio' is Latin, 'Na Laetha Geal M’Óige' Irish. `nostalgia` and `introspection` follow reflective material, `storytelling` the lyrical voyage and narrative setting. `mood` 3 reflects light and melancholy on one album; `tempo` 1 is driven by producer's report of unusually slow rubato songs, despite two uptempo songs. Electronic 3, groove 1, theatrical 3, polish 5, experimental 2 are source-informed editorial judgements.
- **Critical disagreement and influences:** Apple's appraisal of voice/arrangement is favorable; Robert Christgau sharply criticizes perceived sentimentality and lyrics. Genre label 'new age' is resisted by Enya, but is widely applied by critics. The Los Angeles Times 1996 identifies Irish tradition, Phil Spector and The Beach Boys in the group's overall sound; without a clear **album-specific** attribution, **leave `influenced_by` blank** rather than automatically distributing those names across the discography.

## Shepherd Moons (1991) — enya-shepherdmoons

- **Genres:** AOTY independently lists new age, ambient pop, Celtic new age, ethereal wave and neoclassical new age; AllMusic emphasizes Celtic New Age, Celtic Pop and Contemporary Celtic. Old traditional tunes and instrumentals keep `celticfolk`, while piano/keyboard orchestration merits neoclassical texture but not a claim of a classical performance recording.
- **Instruments:** booklet (Enya One) and detailed MusicBrainz/Qobuz credits document Enya's keyboards/voice; **Roy Jewitt clarinet** on 'Angeles', **Steve Sidwell cornet** on 'Evacuee', **Andy Duncan percussion** on 'Book of Days' and **Liam O'Flynn uilleann pipes** on 'Smaointe...'. The producer's contribution on 'Ebudæ' is percussion only, not a second producer. `cornet` and `uilleannpipes` require instrument-token import. `piano|synth` is supported at album level by keyboard/synth work, not claims of one particular machine model. Leave named hardware tokens empty.
- **Production:** Nicky Ryan is the sole producer; Gregg Jackman mixes/engineers on indicated titles, while Rob Dickins is executive producer. `layeredvocals` describes the arrangement and documented multitracked singing but there is no album-specific proof of the *Watermark* tape-machine model or a sampled choir. Avoid attributing identical machine chain to all releases.
- **Language and edition differences:** initial 1991 `Book of Days` is in Irish; some later issues replace it with English for the film *Far and Away*. `en|ga|la` valid for the album as a whole; 'Afer Ventus' is Latin. Use the album's original version for musical scoring.
- **Musical scales:** melancholic reflective tone and slow mostly non-dance music support energy 2, mood 2, tempo 1, groove 1. 'Caribbean Blue' is waltz time, **not** a dance-floor dance genre. Theatrical 3 and polish 5 reflect vast vocal space and polished textures; experimental 1 because the follow-up stays relatively close to *Watermark*.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic (Ned Raggett) calls it a very close successor yet values its refinements; some listeners consider this lack of change a drawback. `sounds_like` is editorial, *not* documented direction of influence.

## The Memory of Trees (1995) — enya-thememoryoftrees

- **Genres:** AllMusic places the record in Celtic/new-age contemporary pop; MusicBrainz tags `ambient`, `electronic`, `modernclassical` and AOTY tags `ambientpop`, `celticfolk`, `etherealwave`, `neoclassicalnewage`. Include both specific and umbrella terms with weaker scope labels for texture-only descriptors. The singer's disagreement with the term *new age* remains relevant.
- **Instruments:** Apple Music and original-release Qobuz metadata name Enya **piano, violin, cello, percussion, synthesizer, keyboards and vocals**. No specific model is recorded in these credits, so **no D-50 or Juno-60 token** should be copied over. No guest musicians in the original credit data.
- **Production:** Nicky Ryan alone is credited producer, recording engineer and mixer. Multi-tracked vocal and synthesizer arrangements are described by the period Los Angeles Times review, and a contemporary review reproduced at EnyaBlues describes echo-rich production. Hence `layeredvocals|heavyreverb`. Rob Dickins is executive producer only.
- **Lyrics:** 1995 El País artist interview confirms English, Irish, Latin and Spanish. 'La Soñadora' is Spanish, 'Pax Deorum' Latin, 'Athair ar Neamh' Irish; myths and sacred trees support spiritual/storytelling. `identity` is weakly supported by the self-referential dreamer theme and should be reassessed if needing strictly album-wide lyric subjects.
- **Scales:** the album's mostly quiet, slow, heavily processed and polished tracks warrant energy 2, tempo 1, groove 1, electronic 3, polish 5. Mood 2 because reflective/sombre passages outweigh the bright 'Anywhere Is'. Theatrical 3 reflects ritual-like choral atmosphere, experimental 1 because arrangements reprise previously established methods.
- **Critical disagreement:** Rick Anderson (AllMusic) is severely critical of perceived formulaic repetition. Don Heckman (Los Angeles Times, 2 December 1995) praises coherent atmosphere and careful studio construction. Both interpretations are recorded; no agreement about *quality* is implied by `confidence=high`, which refers to **fact/sound evidence quality** only. The later Los Angeles Times interview quotes Enya and Nicky Ryan on general influences from Irish tradition, Phil Spector and the Beach Boys; this is not direct album-level attribution, so `influenced_by` remains empty.

## Inclusion decisions and missing data

- **The Frog Prince (1985):** significant early film commission but soundtrack is mostly other performers/arrangers; only two cuts are actually sung by Enya (MusicBrainz release and Enya One film overview). Not one of the artist's defining musical records; excluded under the user rule for non-landmark soundtracks. Review separately if editorial policy later broadens soundtrack coverage.
- **The Celts (1992):** the same debut album, reissue with altered 'Portrait' (Rhino), not an independent fourth/fifth studio album.
- **No new album after 2015 was verified as released as of October 25, 2026** on the checked AllMusic/MusicBrainz/Apple catalogues; future releases should be verified at their time.
- **No explicit sources for album-specific influences** beyond general artist/producer formative preferences; `influenced_by` deliberately blank for all four rows.
- **New tokens** must be installed in genreFamilies.ts, `scenes.ts`, instruments.ts, production.ts and the validator's genre whitelist before running strict validation. Blank means unknown; low-reliability claims are noted rather than treated as definitive. All four rows are `draft`.
