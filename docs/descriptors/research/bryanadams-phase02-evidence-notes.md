# Bryan Adams — Phase 02 | Evidence and editorial decisions

**Research date:** 28 October 2026. 27-field descriptor rows are new to this phase and describe **original studio album editions**, not bonus tracks, live reissues or old single remixes. Scales, `sounds_like`, and the short `notes` are editorial judgments. `influenced_by` remains blank unless a contributor actually documents a musical influence on that particular album.

## Into the Fire (1987) — `bryanadams-intothefire`

- **Credits and date:** Original ten-track release. The A&M discography credits **Bryan Adams and Bob Clearmountain** as the two producers, not Jim Vallance (co-writer, keyboard player, percussionist). Recording personnel include Adams and Keith Scott on guitars, Mickey Curry on drums, Dave Taylor on bass, Dave Pickell on piano, organists, and multiple keyboardists. The individual title-track session credits from Vallance confirm that he performed **sequencer** and percussion on that finished recording.
- **Genres:** Album of the Year classifies this release Pop Rock, AOR, Hard Rock and Heartland Rock. These categories overlap: mainstream guitar melodies, commercial polish and historical storytelling describe different dimensions. Generic Rock is grounded in original instrumentation and release classification; no genre is inferred solely from Adams's public persona.
- **Lyrics:** Vallance states that humanitarian projects and the Amnesty tour changed their writing goals. His *Remembrance Day* came from First World War family research; *Native Son* addresses dispossession of Indigenous Americans. He says only *Victim of Love* and *Hearts on Fire* were conventional love songs. Accordingly `social|political|storytelling` take priority over `love|heartbreak`.
- **Electronic / production distinction:** Vallance explicitly reports using **Emulator II sampling** and a Linn drum machine in the **songwriting process**. This does *not* prove those exact machines appear on the released master. Thus production is blank rather than asserting `emulatorii`, `sampling` or `linndrum` for the record. The **sequencer instrument**, in contrast, has a titled released-track performance credit; `electronic=2` is editorial.
- **Mood / disagreements:** Darker, sometimes socially concerned lyrics justify mood 2, though the rhythm section remains assertive. Vallance's rueful recollection and Christgau's hostile review are perspectives on the writing, not contradictory production facts. Likewise, his named fellow Amnesty performers **Sting, Peter Gabriel and U2** were a touring context: their music was not explicitly specified as a sonic influence on this album, so `influenced_by` stays blank.

## Waking Up the Neighbours (1991) — `bryanadams-wakinguptheneighbours`

- **Credits / original edition:** Bryan Adams and **Robert John “Mutt” Lange** are credited producers. The original issue has 15 tracks. Jim Vallance supplied some writing but is not an album producer; Bob Clearmountain mixed rather than produced. Sources include A&M original issue, MusicBrainz per-track personnel and Adams's own preserved booklet.
- **Recording method:** Original booklet explicitly states recording and mixing on a **Sony PCM-3348 digital tape machine**, so `digitaltape|digitalrecording` are strong album-wide tokens. Do not confuse a tape multitrack with an arbitrary workstation or sampler. Sound on Sound also quotes Adams discussing his recording sessions and Lange's iterative writing/recording method.
- **Genre hierarchy:** AOTY supports Pop Rock, AOR and Hard Rock. Los Angeles Times describes classic-rock choruses, and Rolling Stone explicitly distinguishes punchy rockers and sentimental ballads. `arenarock` is backed by the Washington Post's explicit account of several full-tilt tracks, rather than being inferred from overall production or extended to the ballads. No Def Leppard “influence” is invented from Lange's separate producer résumé: sonic proximity belongs in `sounds_like`.
- **Instruments:** Scott and Adams guitar; Curry drums/percussion; Taylor/Klein bass; Bill Payne piano/Hammond; other keyboardists and organists. A specific guitar pickup, pedal, drum-machine brand or synth is not proven for the full issued album. `electronic=2` is a scale judgment, not a claim of synth-dominated arrangements.
- **Mood / contrasting reviews:** Mostly enthusiastic or romantic lyrics, expansive choruses and theatrical delivery, with some insecurity and the topical *Don't Drop That Bomb on Me*. The 1991 critics differ on the value of classic-rock recycling, not the credited digital recording.

## 18 til I Die (1996) — `bryanadams-18tilidie`

- **Credits / production:** **Bryan Adams** and **Mutt Lange** produced the original. Official liner text credits engineering and **programming** to Olle Romo. Adams tells Sound on Sound that the mobile studio's **Sony 3348 48-track digital recorder** captured these sessions in Jamaica and France. These facts justify `programming|digitaltape|digitalrecording`. Only the appropriate original album recordings are described.
- **Instruments:** A&M's pressing and MusicBrainz document guitars, electric bass, real drums, piano and keyboards, percussion, Bryan Adams's **harmonica**, Romo's **castanets**, and **Paco de Lucía's Spanish guitar** on *Have You Ever Really Loved a Woman?* Adams's handclaps are also credited; Michael Kamen provided piano and a string arrangement on relevant tracks. No generic synthesizer model is attributed to Romo's unspecific electronic programming.
- **Genres:** AOTY indexes Pop Rock, Soft Rock and AOR; original release classifications include Rock. `flamenco` is deliberately *last*: Stereogum describes the castanets and flamenco guitar on the Don Juan DeMarco soundtrack ballad, included as track 13 on the US 1996 album. It is NOT an album-wide flamenco LP. The 1995 soundtrack origin does not transform the 1996 album into a soundtrack.
- **Lyrical and mood variation:** Playful bravado and sexual double entendre coexist with lifelong romance, mature reassurance and an anthem about resisting adulthood. Contemporary Los Angeles Times criticism found the cheekiness immature; this evaluative disagreement is preserved but does not erase the genre contrast between rockers and ballads.
- **Edition trap:** Some 1996/1997 territory-specific CDs substitute *I Finally Found Someone* and other songs or alter the number/order. Use the **original 13-track US 1996 issue** for the baseline row, and do not import extra musicians or technical claims from 1997 remixes or bonus songs.

## On a Day Like Today (1998) — `bryanadams-onadayliketoday`

- **Credits / editions:** Official booklet confirms a **four-producer album**: Bryan Adams, Phil Thornalley, Bob Rock and Phil Western, in different combinations on different tracks. Do not reduce the record to only Adams and Bob Rock, and do not interpret Bob Clearmountain's mixing credit as a fifth producing credit. The base album is the first 13 tracks; official digital page also mixes in numerous additional tracks, and some 1998 regional versions carry *Lie to Me*.
- **Genre evidence:** Album of the Year explicitly assigns Pop Rock and Alternative Rock; MusicBrainz has Rock and Soft Rock. These overlapping categories are carried separately, with `alternativerock` signalling limited late-1990s guitar/arrangement directions rather than treating every track as alt-rock. The four-category minimum is thus reached without fabricating a trip-hop or electronic genre.
- **Instrumentation and production:** Official original credits: Adams plays guitar, bass, **Mellotron** and **Wurlitzer electric piano**; Scott guitar and six-string bass; Curry drums; Danny Cummings percussion; Phil Western/Robbie Buchanan keyboards; Hammond organ, acoustic piano; **Vancouver Orchestra** strings. Phil Western, Mike Gillies and Olle Romo are individually credited for programming. Track-specific orchestral/string arrangements by Dave Munday and Phil Thornalley support `stringarrangements`. Unknown drum machines and samplers are not invented from the programming credit.
- **Voice / mood:** The lead remains raspy/clear/powerful. **Melanie C** provides an actual duet on *When You're Gone*, documented on the booklet, but no separate unrecognised vocal type is introduced. Romantic uncertainty and mature reflection sit beside brisk sing-along songs. Reported critical assessments vary sharply; source genre classifications are not evidence for an inflated `experimental` score.
- **Soundalikes vs. influences:** Matchbox Twenty, Goo Goo Dolls, Bon Jovi and Richard Marx are editorial timbral/arrangement comparisons. No statement by Adams or the producers documents these as the artists shaping the recording, so `influenced_by` is blank.

## Validation decisions shared by all four rows

- `status=draft` (independent review pending); all source evidence is linked in CSV. No repo mutation.
- Instruments and techniques reflect this album's actual session or issue credits. Scales are editorial, not measured features. `sounds_like` is a similarity signal, not documented causality.
- Only album-specific musical influence statements can populate `influenced_by`; major producers and guest performers are excluded by design.
- Beware music-site “Album of the Year” date variations, regional LP track lists and 1991 album title spelling. Original album year is stable; variants are mentioned rather than silently merged.
