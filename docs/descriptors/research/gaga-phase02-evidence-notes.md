# Lady Gaga — Phase 02 evidence notes (research cutoff 2026-10-24)

## Scope and conventions
Four **new** descriptor rows only: *Cheek to Cheek* (2014), *Joanne* (2016), *A Star Is Born* (2018), *Chromatica* (2020). Prior Phase 01 rows (*The Fame*, *The Fame Monster*, *Born This Way*, *ARTPOP*) are not repeated. Scores are editorial calibration against `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`, **not measured audio features**. Genre evidence file distinguishes whole-album identities from named track subsets, context, or critics' references. The genre list is exhaustive for defensible album- or track-level terms **attested by cited sources**, not a claim that every track belongs to every listed genre. Sources are keyed in `gaga_phase02_sources.csv`. Values not sustained by source evidence are blank rather than inferred from Gaga's general aesthetic. `sounds_like` is independently reasoned sonic similarity rather than proof of influence.

### Cheek to Cheek (2014) — `gaga-cheektocheek`
Original collaborative studio album credited to **Tony Bennett and Lady Gaga**, but entered in Gaga's Fandome room. The original programme is American Songbook vocal jazz and swing, recorded with real piano, brass, double bass and drums; no electronic beats. Apple Music names *Vocal Jazz* [C_APPLE], AOTY names *Big Band* [C_AOTY], and Metacritic uses *Traditional Pop/Vocal Pop* [C_MET]. This `pop` means historical standards-oriented pop, **not** modern dance-pop. Both exuberant duets and pensive ballads matter; standards are repertoire, not included as a stand-alone musical genre. Gospel, contemporary pop and EDM are not justified. `swing` is substantial, not every ballad [C_GUARD].

- Scales: `energy=2` (many relaxed standards offset by brisk swings); `electronic=0` (acoustic jazz ensemble); `mood=4` (overall buoyant/humorous with wistful solos); `theatrical=3` (expressive theatrical duet interplay, never arena-scale production); `experimental=1` (faithful interpretations, limited rearrangement); `polish=4` (clear professional band/vocal production); `tempo=3` (mix of brisk swing and ballads); `groove=2` (rhythmic swing, not dancefloor-electronic) [C_GUARD,C_QOBUZ,C_PBS]. These are judgments, not source-provided numerical measurements.
- `voice=clear|powerful|soft`: Bennett's clarity and Gaga's full-bodied jazz singing, hushed solo ballads; `lyrics=love|heartbreak|irony`: repertoire-specific romantic and witty texts, not Gaga-authored new themes. `language=en` even when title/references sound European [C_GAGA,C_GUARD].
- **Producers:** Dae Bennett is explicitly credited on original recording tracks by Qobuz and Apple [C_QOBUZ,C_AMOEBA]. Gretchen Anderson has a producer credit in AOTY release metadata [C_AOTY]; **this credit is not corroborated by Apple/Qobuz's track listings and requires liner-note audit**. Do not confuse Danny Bennett's *executive producer* role with track production. If a strict liner-only import is required, omit Gretchen pending resolution. Confidence is high for the sound and album, not for the disputed additional producer.
- Documented formative models: Gaga specifically describes her admiration for **Ella Fitzgerald**, listening to **Billie Holiday** and **Frank Sinatra** in interviews directly about her jazz album [C_HARP,C_VOGUE]. Tony Bennett is excluded from `influenced_by` because he is the other primary album artist. `sounds_like` includes Ella's swing, Sinatra's standards, Diana Krall and Michael Bublé as **editorial sonic comparisons**, not evidence of direct borrowing.
- Critic disagreements: The Guardian praises Gaga's sincerity and warmth; Erlewine's review on Qobuz is more ambivalent about the set's showiness [C_GUARD,C_QOBUZ]. Neither view warrants a change from jazz to pop dominance. A clear career-defining jazz pivot justifies `landmark=1` [C_GAGA,C_PBS].

### Joanne (2016) — `gaga-joanne`
Original **11-track standard edition** is used. The deluxe edition adds 3 songs, including the RedOne-produced *Angel Down (Work Tape)*, so RedOne must **not** be assigned to the base 11-track row. Gaga's official site explicitly credits Lady Gaga, Mark Ronson, BloodPop, Kevin Parker, Emile Haynie, Jeff Bhasker and Josh Homme as **producers** (not simply songwriters) [J_GAGA]. Production has conventional drums, electric/acoustic guitar, piano and synth touches, and remains recognisably pop [J_APPLE,J_AM].

- Scales: `energy=3` (Diamond Heart/Perfect Illusion against Joanne/Million Reasons); `electronic=2` (mostly band instrumentation, yet pop programming remains); `mood=2` (family loss, disillusionment, serious topics, with upbeat exceptions); `theatrical=2` (more intimate framing, retained belted choruses); `experimental=2` (genre mixture within recognisable pop composition); `polish=4` (tight Mark Ronson/BloodPop studio sound); `tempo=3` (mixed midtempo); `groove=2` (several danceable tracks but ballads and folk material dominate) [J_GAGA,J_PF,J_AM,J_GUARD].
- `voice=powerful|raspy|soft|clear` distinguishes a strong lead from vulnerable quieter performances. Lyric values: `introspection|love|heartbreak|identity|social|storytelling|nostalgia`. *Angel Down* addresses racial violence; *Joanne* concerns her aunt; *Million Reasons* concerns relationship uncertainty; *Diamond Heart* is autobiographical [J_PF,J_NME]. **Social** is a meaningful track theme, not whole-album political concept.
- Disagreement is substantive. Pitchfork and The Guardian foreground country/folk and doubts about the roots pivot [J_PF,J_GUARD]. AllMusic stresses continuity with traditional dance-pop and disco even when dressed in Americana [J_AM]. Both country/folk and dance-pop appear, with explicit per-genre scope. AOTY independently names country-pop and piano rock [J_AOTY]. `alt` is a secondary broad classification only [J_PLUG].
- `influenced_by` remains blank: a listed collaborator (Josh Homme, Kevin Parker, Beck, Florence Welch or Father John Misty) is **not** automatically a cited outside influence. Influential family members are not music-artist influences. `sounds_like` is qualified editorial resemblance; no artist is claimed to have inspired this album causally.

### A Star Is Born (2018) — `gaga-astarisborn`
The **2018 original 34-track film soundtrack** is treated as one work credited jointly to Gaga and Bradley Cooper. The standard sequence has 19 songs **plus 15 dialogue tracks** [S_UMG]. Do not score a special dialogue-free playlist as an album without recording its edition. Sound changes across Jackson Maine's bluesy/country-rock performances, the two leads' acoustic/piano duets, Ally's contemporary pop songs, and the closing torch-song grief. Thus the mixture is deliberate narrative sequencing, and **genre ordering does not imply a uniform sound** [S_PF,S_AM].

- Scales: `energy=3` (loud rock bookends many quiet ballads); `electronic=1` (band and piano dominate; pop sections use synths); `mood=2` (love turns to grief, with high moments); `theatrical=4` (cinematic emotional peaks, big belting, live stage); `experimental=1` (traditional forms and mainstream pop); `polish=4` (professionally produced, performance-forward); `tempo=2` (many slow ballads); `groove=2` (pop/dance cuts are minority) [S_PF,S_AM,S_APPLE,S_UMG].
- `voice=powerful|raspy|soft|clear|spoken` captures Lady Gaga's climactic belt, Cooper's rough singing, softer duets, and the considerable **spoken film-dialogue** component. Sung songs are mostly English; Gaga's **La Vie en Rose** is sung in French, so `language=en|fr` with French a one-track exception [S_UMG]. `lyrics=love|heartbreak|storytelling|introspection|identity|nostalgia` follows the film narrative. The *storytelling* score covers the whole song and dialogue sequence, not a claim every track recounts a linear episode.
- **Producer roll:** Gaga, Cooper, Lukas Nelson, Benjamin Rice, Dave Cobb, Mark Nilan Jr., Nick Monson, Paul 'DJWS' Blair and Brian Newman are explicitly credited as *track producers* by label track listing [S_UMG,S_QOBUZ]. **Mark Ronson wrote `Shallow` but is NOT credited as its producer**; similarly Jason Isbell and Hillary Lindsey are authors only where shown. No unverified songwriters or recording engineers promoted to producer.
- Genres `bluesrock`, `countryrock`, `americana`, `electropop`, `synthpop`, `disco` are documented in different sections; `disco` comes from *Why Did You Do That?*, not from the whole film [S_PF,S_AM,S_TIME]. Qualified source [S_BAZ] documents that the country-rock onscreen character's approach draws from the **Neil Young** milieu; that is an explicit **qualified critic description**, not a direct Gaga influence. `sounds_like` is editorial, and Eddie Vedder is mentioned as a **character** inspiration in [S_APPLE], **not** asserted as a whole-album production influence.
- Critical split: Pitchfork admires Gaga and major ballads but views Cooper's blues rock and some fictional mainstream pop as uneven [S_PF]; AllMusic values the standalone musical arc yet finds dialogue interruptions damaging [S_AM]. This is a key reason to limit groove and avoid claiming a single rock/pop identity.

### Chromatica (2020) — `g-chromatica`
The **original 16-track 2020 release** is used; this includes three short orchestral transitions (*Chromatica I–III*). Gaga and BloodPop served as executive producers, **but individual tracks also credit** BURNS, Axwell, Klahr, Tchami, Max Martin (co-producer), Morgan Kibby, Madeon, Skrillex, Liohn and Rami Yacoub (additional producer). Original Universal Music producer list distinguishes actual credited production from songwriters (e.g. Ryan Tedder on *Sine From Above*), guest singers and mixers [H_UMG,H_PFCRED]. The CSV preserves all credited producers/co-/additional producers.

- Scales: `energy=4` (high sustained momentum, with brief orchestral rests); `electronic=5` (songs principally built from electronics, with three orchestral exceptions); `mood=3` (joyous dance sonics and dark first-person lyrics balance); `theatrical=4` (grand pop and orchestral framing); `experimental=3` (house revival plus structure, jarring track codas, but familiar song shapes); `polish=5` (maximal clean dance-pop); `tempo=4` (fast four-on-the-floor dominates); `groove=5` (made for club dancing) [H_PF,H_GUARD,H_UMG].
- Lead voice `powerful|raspy|clear|soft`: Gaga's full-throated phrases, rough timbral shifts, sometimes intimate/processed vocals. Themes `introspection|identity|love|heartbreak|social|sensuality`: trauma and mental health, selfhood, protection and love, autonomy (*Free Woman*), romantic/fantasy longing. The track *911* is about Gaga's medication, not an album of party-only lyrics [H_PF,H_GUARD,H_SLANT].
- Genres: the foundation is **dance-pop and 1990s house** [H_PF,H_GUARD]; Soundvenue's Danish review explicitly describes **eurodance** across much of the album [H_DK], Slant calls *Free Woman* **eurohouse** [H_SLANT]; NME identifies synth-pop/disco in *Rain on Me* and electro-pop in *Stupid Love* [H_NME]; one *Sine From Above* section shifts into **drum-and-bass** [H_PF]. Three orchestral interludes are a **texture/sequence feature**, not justification for classifying the whole record as classical. Guardian's 'industrial synths' is **texture** rather than a full industrial-music classification [H_GUARD]. No automatic K-pop genre inference from BLACKPINK's presence.
- Causal influence `Daft Punk` relies only on **NME explicitly describing `911` as Daft Punk-inspired** [H_NME], hence **track-scoped**, not an artist statement of the entire album. Robin S., Martha Wash, Madonna, Cathy Dennis, Dua Lipa are sonic-era comparisons in Slant [H_SLANT]; these are `sounds_like`, not causal influences.
- Critical disagreement: Pitchfork regards it as an ambitious personal/club crossover with imperfect sequencing [H_PF], whereas Slant calls its concept and 1990s house idiom insufficiently developed [H_SLANT]. The difference is evaluation of execution; both concur on 1990s house-pop, artificial polish and dance orientation. `landmark=1` records its significant artist-discography return to dance-pop after the jazz/roots/film projects, not a universal critical consensus.
