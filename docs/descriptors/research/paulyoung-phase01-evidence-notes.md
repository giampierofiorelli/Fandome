# Paul Young — phase 01: album-level evidence and editorial decisions
Research checked: 29 October 2026. Branch `descriptors` read-only. The four 27-column descriptor rows are new; no previously described albums repeated.

## No Parlez (1983) — `paulyoung-noparlez`
- Core identity: 1983 album-level blue-eyed soul, soul, pop and new wave. John Earls' 2025 *Classic Pop* oral-history feature explicitly adds Bowiesque art-pop, and AllMusic places it within Contemporary Pop/Rock. Art-pop is placed after the central descriptors because it is a strand, not the record's sole identity.
- Bass and electronics: Pino Palladino's fretless bass and Chapman Stick underlie the soul interpretations. The original credits identify Ian Kewley's Oberheim OB-X and E-mu Emulator, Matt Irving's Roland MC-4, Mark Pinder's Simmons drums/TR-808, vocoder, acoustic piano, marimba, trombone and congas; there is no evidence that all devices occur on every track. Those devices therefore describe the LP palette.
- Voice: expressive baritone, both rasp and tenderness; backing vocalists are an important production element but not the lead voice token `choral`. Rough scales: energy 3, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 2, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3 (comparative editorial scoring anchored to the guide, not numerical claims in sources).
- Themes: covers address love, loss and social issues (e.g. *Love of the Common People*). The cover of Joy Division's *Love Will Tear Us Apart* does **not** make Joy Division a confirmed `influenced_by` artist. Nor do Marvin Gaye, Booker T. & the M.G.'s and Jack Lee become album influences merely because Young covers their songs.
- Caveat: original 1983 LP, original CD and anniversary editions have different song edits/mixes. Classify the original album; do not import extra remixes/live material as new studio albums.
- Sources: https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/paul-young-no-parlez-interview/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Parlez ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/no-parlez-mw0000651012 ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/ca080308-d7c2-4a29-b1a1-f2d9a73f1776

## The Secret of Association (1985) — `paulyoung-thesecretofassociation`
- Musical style: pop-rock and blue-eyed soul (album metadata); adult contemporary, Contemporary Pop/Rock and new wave (AllMusic); Young's voice remains soul-derived. Emphasise a smoother, grander, multi-layered production than the debut.
- Personnel from original record: Laurie Latham sole credited producer; Ian Kewley plays DX7, OB-Xa, Fairlight CMI and E-mu Emulator alongside piano/organ; Mark Pinder plays Simmons drums, LinnDrum and TR-808; Pino Palladino plays bass/Chapman Stick; guitar, electric sitar, pedal steel, trombone, sax, violin and harmonica are documented. Some instruments are song-specific (electric sitar on *Everytime You Go Away*, violin on *I Was in Chains*), not generalised to every song.
- Vocals: smoky/raspy but able to belt, with a developed backing vocal choir. Widened palette and extremely high studio polish (5); electronic 3 rather than 5 because acoustic drums, guitars and bass remain central. Groove 3 reflects a mix of ballads and funkier material.
- Themes: intimate relationships and breakups plus anti-war/social commentary. Christgau discusses two anti-war songs. Do not treat cover sources Hall & Oates or Tom Waits as musical influences automatically.
- Source caveat: a private collector transcription calls drummer “Mike Pinder”; primary-style personnel lists **Mark Pinder**, so the names in the notes follow these. The model names are supported by the personnel lists, but no hardware model should be assigned to an individual song without track-specific documentation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_Association ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-secret-of-association-mw0000192541 ; https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/the-secret-of-association-paul-young/0884977754469 ; https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?id=4149

## Between Two Fires (1986) — `paulyoung-betweentwofires`
- Musical style: album-level pop-rock/soul, with AllMusic Contemporary Pop/Rock and New Wave classifications. Album is more songwriter-led: Young and Ian Kewley contribute most of the songs. Some tracks lean more toward polished adult pop; do not infer synth models, exact recording medium or gated reverbs because the album credits do not verify specific devices.
- Original credits: **Hugh Padgham, Paul Young and Ian Kewley** as producers. Palladino is credited with *production assistance*, not as full producer. Pino plays bass; David Palmer drums and does drum programming; Steve Boltz plays guitar/sitar; Ian Kewley organ/keyboards, Matt Irving keyboards, Danny Cummings percussion.
- Voice: expressive raspy soul phrasing but less concentrated soul material than 1983–85, an observation supported by the AllMusic biography. Scales are editorial: polish 5, electronic 2, groove 2, experimental 1.
- Lyrical themes: relationships and introspection combined with political/social material (*War Games*, *Prisoner of Conscience*). Rolling Stone's Laura Fissinger heard the LP as a delayed reveal of personal and social connection; AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine criticised the weak material and overly slick production. Preserve this disagreement instead of reporting universal acclaim.
- Edition caveat: *Steps to Go* is a bonus track on selected CD editions, not part of the ten-track core vinyl LP.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Between_Two_Fires_(album) ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/between-two-fires-mw0000192441 ; https://musicbase.net/between-two-fires ; https://www.allmusic.com/artist/paul-young-mn0000034382/biography

## Other Voices (1990) — `paulyoung-othervoices`
- Album-specific styles: pop-rock (album metadata); soul and synth-pop appear in the original European CD discography styles. A specialist retrospective describes it as blue-eyed soul; electronic elements are song/production-team-specific, so synth-pop is in the long tail rather than the primary genre. Overall arrangements remain acoustic/electric band driven, despite some programming.
- Producer credits: **Warne Livesey**, **Nile Rodgers**, **Martin Page**, **Pete Wingfield**, **Peter Wolf**. The record credits Paul Young as **executive producer** only, which is excluded from the producers column. The liner notes can render Warne as 'Wayne' in some transcriptions: keep Warne Livesey.
- Personnel include Pino Palladino, David Gilmour (guitar on two cuts), Chaka Khan (guest duet), Steve Winwood (Hammond), Stevie Wonder (harmonica), Anne Dudley (strings), Vinnie Colaiuta and Manu Katché (drums), plus keyboards, saxophone and flugelhorn. Guests are neither album influences nor extra co-billed principal artists.
- Textures: strings plus Hammond, acoustic piano, rhythm-section recordings and track-by-track programming/overdubs. `stringarrangements` is supported by credited string arrangements; `synthprogramming` by Warne Livesey's credit, not by speculation about particular machines. No drum-machine hardware models are assigned.
- Lyrics: cover selections focus on yearning and affection (*Oh Girl*, *Calling You*, *Softly Whispering I Love You*). Some original work co-written with Martin Page. The Chi-Lites, Free and Bobby Womack are covered; do not add them automatically as influences.
- Edition caveat: 2012 two-CD reissue adds remixes and a live track; original album contains ten 1990 studio tracks. Low-to-moderate agreement in contemporary reviews (AllMusic ~3/5, *Select* 1/5, *Smash Hits* 6/10); do not present it as universally praised.
- Sources: https://sessiondays.com/2023/04/25/1990-paul-young-other-voices/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Other_Voices_(Paul_Young_album) ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/other-voices-mw0000315291 ; https://musicbase.net/other-voices-0rzjesp

## Influence methodology
Paul Young personally named Free/Paul Rodgers, James Taylor, Stevie Wonder, The Who, Small Faces, the Faces, Patto, Dire Straits, Robert Palmer, the Stranglers, Jr. Walker, Joe Tex, Otis Redding, Sam Cooke, Wilson Pickett, Garnet Mimms, Chuck Jackson, Johnnie Taylor, Talking Heads and Soft Cell as influences across his career. His detailed chronology spans several bands and his solo transition. Because the sources do not explicitly assign those names to the making of a **particular one of these four albums**, `influenced_by` is conservatively blank in the CSV; the verified *artist-wide* list is preserved in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` instead. Editorial `sounds_like` associations are subjective comparisons, not historical influences.
Source: https://www.rediscoverthe80s.com/2019/08/interview-with-paul-young.html (interview originally 2011, reprinted 2019).
