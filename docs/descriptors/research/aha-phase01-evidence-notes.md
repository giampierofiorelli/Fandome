# a-ha — Phase 01: album-level evidence and cautions

Research date: 27 October 2026. Scope is *original studio album configurations*. Sources file distinguishes direct primary testimony, credited recordings, and critical styles. All numeric fields are researcher judgments under the Fandome 0–5 anchors, not sourced numerical facts. `sounds_like` is editorial resemblance, **not** a claim of influence. `status=draft` until independent editorial review.

## Hunting High and Low (1985)

- **Core sound:** AllMusic's Synth Pop, New Wave, Dance-Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock make the debut synth-led but with driving guitar and overt pop songwriting. The record as a whole remains electronical (5/5) despite guitar and the *one-track* oboe credit.
- **Personnel:** MusicBrainz original-recording credits place Pål Waaktaar on guitar and drum-machine programming, Magne Furuholmen on keyboards and bass programming, and Claire Jarvis on oboe for **Living a Boy's Adventure Tale**. `synthbass` interprets the documented programmed bass line; there is no unsupported claim of a dedicated session bassist.
- **Specific equipment (not album-wide):** Alan Tarney's interview, quoted by mu:zines from Sound On Sound, ties **Take On Me's final Alan Tarney version** to Roland Juno-60, PPG Wave, Yamaha DX7, LinnDrum, and UMI sequencing. `juno60|ppgwave|dx7|linndrum|umi2b` refer to *Take On Me*, not a proven feature of the other nine tracks. Pål programmed the LinnDrum, while UMI drove synths. A Yamaha/PPG model beyond stated family is not specified.
- **Producers:** Tony Mansfield most tracks; Alan Tarney **Take On Me** and **The Sun Always Shines on T.V.**; John Ratcliff **Love Is Reason**, credited together with a-ha. John Ratcliff's numerous *remixer* credits are not generalized as production credits.
- **Influence exception:** a-ha's own pre-debut listening to Soft Cell and their appeal to its synthetic coldness (Guardian 2009) makes Soft Cell plausible for *early a-ha specifically*. Morten Harket explicitly says that the ascending octave melody in *Take On Me* referenced Richard Strauss's *Also sprach Zarathustra* (Guardian 2015). Strauss is an actual documented compositional prompt, **not** a critic's sound-alike. No attempt is made to attach a modern studio album to a nineteenth-century classical work.
- **Lyrics and scales:** album combines romantic pursuit and loss, anxious internal narrative and expansively theatrical vocals. `mood=3` averages brighter singles and darker album tracks; `groove=4` reflects danceable singles, not each ballad.
- **Caveat:** Official site documents worldwide 28 Oct 1985 issue, while some databases report earlier territory releases. Year is uncontroversial. 1984/early-1985 unsuccessful versions of *Take On Me* are **not** the LP production.

## Scoundrel Days (1986)

- **Core sound:** a-ha's own official retrospective states a darker, demo-like character. `mood=1`, `experimental=3` relative to the debut, guitar-led and dynamic arrangements across *The Swing of Things* and *Manhattan Skyline*.
- **Instrumentation:** Qobuz original-master credit metadata confirms Magne keyboards and bass/drum programming, Paul guitars and drum programming, Michael Sturgis acoustic drum kit on *The Swing of Things*, *I've Been Losing You*, *Soft Rains of April*, Leif Karsten Johansen bass guitar on *I've Been Losing You*, and Graham Prescott strings on the title track (MusicBrainz lists Graham Preskett, a name variant). Øystein Jevanord drums on *Cry Wolf* appear in original-issue personnel. No precise synthesizer model sourced for these sessions, so none assigned.
- **Producers:** Alan Tarney on 1,2,4–8; Magne Furuholmen (credited also as Mags) and Pål Waaktaar on 3,9,10. Not to be confused with engineer Gerry Kitchingham or mixer John Hudson.
- **Genres:** AllMusic specifically describes Synth Pop, New Wave and Dance-Rock. The discography source describes Electronic/Pop at the album level. Broad Pop/Rock belongs last; AllMusic's isolated **Swamp Pop** tag is rejected as an unsupported outlier when compared with the documented recording and dominant styles. No new `swamppop` genre token added on this evidence alone.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Morten's clear, high-reaching dramatic delivery accompanies alienation, heartbreak, and anxiety. Critical 1986  production is immaculate, but arrangements retain a harder guitar-and-drum edge than debut.
- **Caveat:** Use only 1986 original ten tracks. Deluxe 2010 demos, mixes and live recordings do not change original album description.

## Stay on These Roads (1988)

- **Production:** official liner notes credit **Alan Tarney alone** as producer, Gerry Kitchingham as engineer and John Hudson as mixer. Qobuz shows repeated track-level Magne keyboard, drum and bass programming and Paul guitar/string contributions, so `drumprogramming|programming` is evidenced. Avoid speculative synth models: tertiary internet lists conflict/are insufficient to tie exact machines securely to the original production.
- **Genres:** Synth Pop and Punk/New Wave from AllMusic; New Wave and Electronic explicitly named on a contemporaneous 1988 European pressing; Pop from original cassette/Qobuz; Pop/Rock as broad taxonomy. Some upbeat songs are dance-oriented, but **do not add dance-pop as an album-wide category without a source that names it**.
- **Instruments:** `strings` reflects Paul Waaktaar's credit as recorded by Qobuz, and not necessarily a separately hired string orchestra. `synthbass` interprets Magne's explicit bass programming. No extra percussionists or brass are presumed merely from later touring line-ups.
- **Themes:** elegiac title ballad vs the brighter *Touchy!* and *You Are the One*. `energy=3`, `groove=3`, `polish=5`, `experimental=1` describe the LP average.
- **Caveat:** *The Living Daylights* first appeared as a Bond song in 1987 but belongs on the 1988 album. This does **not** turn the 1988 LP into a soundtrack. John Barry is a **co-writer**, not a credited album producer or a demonstrated a-ha influence.

## East of the Sun, West of the Moon (1990)

- **Change in recording method:** a-ha's official account explicitly contrasts **full live band studio takes** (Per Hillestad drums; Jørun Bøgeberg bass) with the preceding machines. `liverecording` means musicians playing together **in a studio**, never a live concert album. Piano and acoustic guitar are documented in the official history and original album's audio/credit context.
- **Additional specific credits:** Qobuz original 1990 masters: Phil Todd saxophone on *I Call Your Name*; Martin Ditcham tambourine on *I Call Your Name* and *Slender Frame*, percussion on *Rolling Thunder*; Magne harmonica on *Rolling Thunder*; Pål piano on *(Seemingly) Nonstop July*. Some keyboards/synth remain but much less pervasive; hence `electronic=2`. Morten leads most tracks; **Magne Furuholmen sings lead on *The Way We Talk*** (official).
- **Producers:** Chris/Christopher Neil on most tracks; **Ian Stanley** on *Early Morning*, *East of the Sun*, *Waiting for Her*, *The Way We Talk* and *(Seemingly) Nonstop July*. Do not list recording/mix engineer Nick Davis or *Waiting for Her* arranger David Bedford as producers.
- **Genres:** Pop/Rock and Pop from Qobuz, Contemporary Pop/Rock and residual Synth Pop from AllMusic. The latter is de-emphasized because of new organic band sound, as a-ha and Norway's National Library both note. No 'folk' or 'blues' style is implied by a one-off acoustic or harmonica part.
- **Cover caveat:** the opening song *Crying in the Rain* covers a song known from the Everly Brothers; **cover performance ≠ influence**. Do not put the Everly Brothers, Carole King or Howard Greenfield in `influenced_by`; songwriting/cover credits are distinct.
- **Date caveat:** October 1990 undisputed. Sources vary on **22 vs 27 October**. The export only needs year 1990 and does not silently settle the day.

## Confidence, attribution, and listening

`confidence=high` because official album liners, original-period label metadata and named critical sources converge. These values remain original editorial assessments and should be second-reviewed. The genre lists have no arbitrary cap and are ordered by prevalence; ambiguous umbrella categories are intentionally secondary. `sounds_like` contains editorial sonic comparisons only, with no false historical relationships.
