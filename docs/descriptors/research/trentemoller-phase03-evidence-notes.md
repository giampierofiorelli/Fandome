# Trentemøller — Phase 03: evidence notes

Research cut-off: **24 October 2026**. These are **two new rows only**: `trentemoller-memoria`, `trentemoller-dreamweaver`. Music included: original 14-track *Memoria* (11 February 2022), original 10-track *Dreamweaver* (13 September 2024). Phase 01 and Phase 02 are intentionally absent. Both rows are drafts; 0–5 scores are calibrated editorial synthesis, not measured loudness/BPM or acoustic-feature extraction.

## Memoria (2022) — `trentemoller-memoria`

**Scope, attribution and personnel.** Sixth studio album, 14 tracks and ~72 minutes [M_BC, M_AM, M_Q]. Qobuz gives Trentemøller the sole **producer** credit on each track; Lisbet Fritze is principally singer/guitarist, Silas Tinglef an instrumental contributor, not co-producers [M_Q, M_AM]. Official announcement and 2024 interviews confirm Anders wrote the songs' lyrics and vocal melodies himself for the first time, with Lisbet singing them [M_OFF, M_RDX]. Qobuz also credits the artist singing on **Dead or Alive**; do not misreport every vocal track as Fritze or add her to `producers` [M_Q]. One record, not a soundtrack, despite reviewers' cinematic analogies.

**Whole-album context and critical tension.** Bandcamp explicitly tags **dream pop, indie rock and shoegaze** [M_BC]. Soundvenue hears the joined-up **electronica–dream pop–shoegaze** approach, with post-punk and krautrock peaks [M_SV]. Echoes, Magnetic and Echoes and Dust document the balance between slow floating instrumentals and delicate vocal songs, not an album of straight dance music [M_ECHO, M_MAG, M_ED]. The genre evidence table distinguishes `whole album`, `substantial`, `some tracks`, and `one track`. `noiserock`, `krautrock`, and `punk` are driven particularly by **Dead or Alive** / **When The Sun Explodes**, *not* a claim that all 14 songs share these genres [M_BC, M_SV, M_NMR]. `indiedance` directly reflects Magnetic's *indie dance* term, but is a **low-strength, limited-scope** genre attribution and should be reviewed [M_MAG, M_YEAR].

**Calibrated 0–5 scores.**
- `energy=3`: slow, soft passages are numerous, but sustained motorik/noise-rock surges mean not a low-intensity ambient set [M_BC, M_SV, M_ECHO].
- `electronic=3`: programmed synth textures, keys and processing across the record, with real electric guitar, bass, drums and vocals; below a synth-dominant Kraftwerk 5 [M_Q, M_UT, M_SV].
- `mood=1`: recurring dark, mournful and twilight material; some warmth/hope prevents a zero [M_SV, M_NMR, M_BC].
- `theatrical=3`: very cinematic crescendos and long dynamic arcs, while singers remain intimate and restrained [M_META, M_MAG, M_ECHO].
- `experimental=3`: varied song-to-instrumental, electronic-to-organic transitions and extended form; critics also find familiar territory and repetition [M_UT, M_META, M_WIM].
- `polish=4`: articulated spacious layering and careful track production, not intentionally lo-fi or Max-Martin pop gloss [M_SV, M_ECHO, M_Q].
- `tempo=2`: the typical experience is slower/midtempo, with deliberately isolated fast/motorik peaks [M_ECHO, M_BC, M_MAG].
- `groove=2`: palpable rhythms on In The Gloaming and When The Sun Explodes; nearly half instrumental, often non-club [M_YEAR, M_MAG, M_ECHO].

**Non-numeric fields.** `voice=soft|instrumental` reflects Lisbet's hushed vocals and extensive instrumental tracks; the harsher lead on Dead Or Alive is a track exception, not evidence for global `raspy` [M_SV, M_Q, M_ECHO]. `lyrics=introspection|love|heartbreak` reflects impermanence of relationships/mortality and separation; avoid reading album title *Memoria* as proof of **nostalgia** in all lyrics [M_BC, M_OFF, M_YEAR]. `language=en` follows song texts and English sung tracks; no evidence for second language [M_BC]. `sounds_like` is editorial **sonic resemblance**, e.g. Undertoner compares M83 + Slowdive and Echoes hears guitar-effect kinship with Ulrich Schnauss [M_UT, M_ECHO]. `influenced_by` is distinct: the artist explicitly names Cocteau Twins, Slowdive, Ride, Suicide in *retrospective Memoria-specific discussion*, and My Bloody Valentine in a near-release interview [M_RDX, M_WARP]. Do not turn every critic comparison into a causal influence. `landmark=1` proposed because it inaugurates his full lyric/melody authorship and is substantial in the 2022 year-end evaluation [M_OFF, M_YEAR]. `confidence=high` concerns album core, while individual genre lines retain uncertainty.

**Disagreements/caveats.** Soundvenue celebrates the blend of cinematic instrumentals with voices [M_SV]; Undertoner finds some passages formulaic/repetitive [M_UT], Written in Music finds moments of pathos and excess [M_WIM], while Metacritic aggregates broadly positive published reviews [M_META]. No evaluation is flattened into a universal critical consensus. The collector's track labels vary between editorial metadata and the original Bandcamp listing (e.g. *The Rise* / *Rise*, *A Summer’s Empty Room* / *Summer’s Empty Room*). Those do **not** create additional albums. Critics' descriptions of a film score are not documentary soundtrack credits.

## Dreamweaver (2024) — `trentemoller-dreamweaver`

**Scope and producer.** Seventh studio album; 10 songs from In My Room, issued 13 September 2024 [D_OFF,D_BC]. Qobuz credits **Anders Trentemøller** as composer, producer and mixer across all 10 songs; **Heba Kadry is mastering engineer**, not producer. The singers and instrumentalists, including **DíSA / Disa Jakobs**, guitarist **Silas Tinglef**, bassist **Jacob Haubjerg**, violinist **Maria Jagd** and double bassist **Ida Duelund**, are not automatically co-producers [D_Q]. Anders confirms the Copenhagen-studio production personally [D_808]. He supplied most of the lyrics/melodies; DíSA specifically wrote lyrics to **Dreamweavers** [D_Q, D_KIY, D_808]. The *album* is singular *Dreamweaver*; track three is the plural *Dreamweavers*. Do not create a second album from the title-track single.

**Album identity and genre scope.** Official album text moves from acoustic/space-folk opener to mid-album guitar-noise climaxes to ambient/slowcore end [D_OFF]. Bandcamp tags **dream pop**, **indie rock**, **shoegaze** [D_BC]; the label biography identifies **darkwave**, psychedelic/electronic dream-pop and noise rock [D_BIO]; OndaRock hears darkwave and electropop in *Dreamweavers* but considers the album mostly song-led [D_OD]. Qobuz adds synthpop on selected songs and points to acoustic elements [D_QOBREV]. Genres that occur in one or two songs are intentionally low in ordering (`electropop`, `noiserock`, `spacerock`, `folk`, `slowcore`). The term `motorik` is a beat pattern here, not a new genre token. `psychedelicrock` is a **contextual song/artist influence** from his interview and official psychedelic framing, not a claim the album is a standard psych-rock set [D_MET,D_BIO].

**Calibrated 0–5 scores.**
- `energy=3`: broadly restrained with strong spikes on I Give My Tears, Behind My Eyes and In A Storm [D_OFF, D_OOR].
- `electronic=3`: guitars and vocals central, but Qobuz lists synths, programming, electric rhythm and keyboards on almost every track; the artist calls electronic proportions smaller than on his earlier records [D_Q,D_QMAG].
- `mood=1`: wintry melancholy; some hopeful lyrical warmth but rarely bright celebration [D_OFF,D_OD,D_OOR].
- `theatrical=3`: sweeping, panoramic production counterbalanced by intimate acoustic openers [D_OOR,D_MET].
- `experimental=3`: creative pedals, evolving arrangements, noisy climaxes; accessible song structures and some critics deem style familiar [D_QMAG,D_OFF,D_OD].
- `polish=4`: detailed authored soundscape and hi-res licensed production with careful mixing/mastering credits [D_Q,D_OOR].
- `tempo=2`: a largely ruminative slow-midtempo song cycle, punctuated by insistent rock pulses [D_OFF,D_OD].
- `groove=1`: dance-floor drive is peripheral; chiefly song, ambience and guitar swells [D_QMAG,D_OFF,D_OD].

**Voice/lyrics/influences.** Qobuz documents DíSA on **8 of 10 tracks** (everything except *In A Storm* and *Closure*); `soft|instrumental` therefore describes ethereal vocals and two instrumentals, not an instrumental album [D_Q]. OndaRock says **one instrumental** and Qobuz's own prose says **three instrumentals** even while its track credits show **two**; this is an explicit **source disagreement**: favour track-level credits, but hold precise counts for listening or liner verification [D_OD,D_Q]. `lyrics=introspection|love|heartbreak`: the artist and label discuss intimacy, emotional memory, fleeting bonds, and personal inner states; no album-wide political or spiritual claim [D_SC,D_808,D_OFF]. `language=en`: English vocal texts, supported by artist's account of composing in English [D_KIY,D_Q]. `sounds_like` is based on specific qualified comparisons of The Cure, Joy Division, New Order, Julee Cruise, Badalamenti, Mazzy Star, Cocteau Twins, This Mortal Coil, Dead Can Dance [D_OD,D_QOBREV,D_GA,D_NP,D_SV]. `influenced_by` relies on direct 2024 artist statements about Nick Drake for opening acoustic arrangement, Mazzy Star for a spacey 1970s folk/psych texture, The Cure and Smiths as musical DNA, Velvet Underground and My Bloody Valentine influence, plus album-specific Cure/Joy Division markers from a qualified critic [D_MET,D_808,D_OD]. **Not** all collaborations equal influences. `landmark=1` is an editorial classification for an identifiable new chapter of a single main vocalist and studio-centered guitar/drone songcraft [D_OFF,D_808,D_QMAG].

**Conflicting criticism and edition notes.** OOR praises giant screen-width mix + delicate space, and noisy contrast [D_OOR]. OndaRock likes songwriting but says the album is less surprising and often uniform [D_OD]; Nieuweplaat also reports a sense of sameness [D_NP]. GAFFA's review is paywalled; only its visible Mazzy Star comparison is used [D_GA]. No remixes or bonus editions included. Single *Dreamweavers* is not a separate album/EP for this phase. Release metadata alternates capitalization `DíSA`, `Disa`, `Dísa`, `Disa Jakobs`; keep canonical singer reference in notes, never list her as an album producer.

## Release eligibility and future batch

As of the fixed research cut-off, the artist has two additional studio albums after Phase 02: these 2022/2024 entries. The **self-titled 2026 album has not been released**: In My Room schedules **13 November 2026** [D_2026], while available songs are pre-release singles. Do not import an unreleased album or assign its final album-wide descriptor prematurely. No substantial standalone remix or collaboration album from 2022–2024 met the landmark/documented-collaboration threshold; the 2022 DíSA/Raveonettes singles and 2024 preview singles are not duplicated [D_OFF,D_BC]. Phase 04 should be created **only after the self-titled album is actually released**, with review and track-credit verification, and should contain only the subsequently released new album (or other important new releases), never repeat this phase.
