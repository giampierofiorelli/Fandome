# Dua Lipa — Phase 01: evidence, scoring and disagreements

## Scope and principles

This incremental batch covers the **original editions** of four qualifying records. Fandome artist ID: `dualipa`. The three existing IDs are preserved exactly. No `docs/descriptors/dualipa.csv` was present on the inspected `descriptors` branch. We use the calibrated integer 0–5 guide for **whole-album editorial judgments**, not numerical scores obtained from reviewers. A genre documented on one track can appear in the multivalue genre field, but the accompanying genre-evidence file marks its limited scope. Production credits are not interchangeable with composition, mixing, DJ-mixing or remixing. `sounds_like` is editorial and `influenced_by` is documentary. The notes column alone has a 15-word limit.

## 1. Dua Lipa (2017) — `dualipa-dualipa`

**Edition:** 2 June 2017, original twelve-song sequence; do not evaluate the later 17-song deluxe or 2018 Complete Edition [D_AP,D_AM]. The record combines punchy electropop and dance-pop with some tropical-house/EDM influence in hits such as Hotter Than Hell and New Rules; its acoustic and piano-led passages, especially Thinking 'Bout You and Homesick, keep it from being an uninterrupted club album [D_GU,D_NME,D_AP]. AllMusic tags Left-Field Pop, Pop and Dance-Pop; the unusual style-tag wording is retained without inflating experimentalism [D_AM]. NME identifies a dancehall accent on Hotter Than Hell, not necessarily on the whole tracklist [D_NME].

**Scores:** `energy=3` because driving early singles coexist with softer closing material; `electronic=4` because keyboards and programmed drums predominate despite acoustic/piano exceptions; `mood=3` balances summer confidence with heartbreak and unease; `theatrical=2` direct, personal vocal delivery; `experimental=2` mild eclecticism but mostly conventional pop structures; `polish=4` clear commercial studio recording; `tempo=3` mixed tempos; `groove=3` danceable hooks, uneven club continuity [D_AM,D_NME,D_GU,D_AP].

**Voice:** `raspy|powerful|clear|soft`. NME explicitly foregrounds a smoky rasp and impressive projection, while gentler singing matters in ballads [D_NME,D_GU]. **Lyrics:** love, heartbreak, self-worth/identity, attraction and introspection, all documented in song examples and Lipa's autobiographical account [D_AP,D_VO]. **Language:** English. **Producers:** Koz/Stephen Kozmeniuk, Axident, Miguel, Digital Farm Animals, Jack Tarrant, Greg Wells, GRADES, Ian Kirkpatrick, James Flannigan, Bill Rahko and Larzz Principato have original-track producer/co-producer credits [D_Q,D_MB]. No songwriter/mixer inferred to be a producer. **Influences:** P!nk, Nelly Furtado [D_VO], plus Destiny's Child, Missy Elliott, Christina Aguilera [D_PA], specifically stated by Lipa when discussing her early musical identity; these are documented formative influences, not sample credits. **sounds_like:** Sia and Adele reflect critics' vocal analogies; Tove Lo, Rihanna and Zara Larsson are editorial pop-adjacency candidates, not asserted borrowings [D_AM,D_NME]. **Landmark=1:** defining debut and New Rules breakthrough.

## 2. Future Nostalgia (2020) — `dualipa-futurenostalgia`

**Edition:** 27 March 2020, original eleven tracks, without Moonlight Edition additions or subsequent remixes [F_AM,F_MB,F_Q]. Critics consistently identify disco, dance-pop and pop-funk as album-wide rather than single-only features [F_PF,F_GU,F_AM]. Electronic drums and synths are mixed with bass guitar, strings, percussion and voices; do not rate it completely electronic [F_MB,F_Q]. **Genre nuance:** `electropop` references Lipa's formative sonic direction [F_GQ], while `synthpop` refers to audible retro synthesizer textures [F_OB,F_IN]. `powerpop` is a period color in the Guardian retrospective, not the record's main label [F_GE].

**Scores:** `energy=4` driving but not extreme; `electronic=4` sequenced tracks with live-sounding rhythm/string details; `mood=4` mostly exuberant despite romantic friction and the sober closer; `theatrical=3` oversized choruses plus camp spoken emphasis; `experimental=2` creative recombination using largely conventional pop forms; `polish=5` meticulous bright multi-producer record; `tempo=4` sustained dance tempos with a closing exception; `groove=5` unusually strong dancefloor consistency [F_PF,F_GU,F_OB,F_AM].

**Voice:** `powerful|clear|spoken|soft|choral`: strong sung leads, some half-spoken phrases, softer lines, choir on Boys Will Be Boys [F_GU,F_OB]. **Lyrics:** love, heartbreak, physical attraction, celebration and identity/self-belief; `social` is included for the specific commentary on girls' safety in Boys Will Be Boys, not an album-wide political platform [F_GU,F_GE]. **Producers:** 11-song track-level credits identify Jeff Bhasker, Ian Kirkpatrick, Stuart Price, TMS, Jason Evigan, Koz, SG Lewis, The Monsters & Strangerz, Andrew Watt, Lindgren, Take a Daytrip, Gian Stone; **Lorna Blackwood and Lauren D'Elia** have producer labels with vocal-production scope, and **Rupert Christie** an additional-production role. Their names are included transparently and require Claude to confirm whether vocal/additional producer credits are in-scope for the final model [F_MB,F_Q]. Explicit exclusions: songs' writers, featured artists, instrumentalists, and mixers where not credited as producers. **Influences:** Jamiroquai, Moloko, Prince and Blondie are named by Dua as inspirations for this specific record [F_IN]; Kylie and Madonna are sustained comparisons by professional critics [F_PF,F_AM]. Other `sounds_like` candidates are resemblance judgments, not influence claims. **Landmark=1**, a career-defining studio record with multi-source critical recognition.

## 3. Club Future Nostalgia (2020) — `dualipa-clubfuturenostalgia`

**Edition/identity:** 28 August 2020 DJ mix co-curated by Dua Lipa and The Blessed Madonna, not the September unmixed 17-track compilation, parent album or an original studio LP [C_TQ,C_GR,C_MB]. It contains new versions of familiar songs, alternate/previously unreleased songs, guests, samples, and brief DJ-mixed connections [C_GR,C_NYI,C_Q]. Piano house, Baltimore club, Detroit/Chicago house, disco, 1980s soul and trap/house elements are well attested but often only on *some tracks* [C_PF,C_NME]. `baltimoreclub` is a genuinely new schema token. **Critical disagreement:** NME embraces the eclectic mix; Pitchfork finds some transitions and abbreviated remixes frustrating and hears inconsistency despite strong separate contributions [C_NME,C_PF].

**Scores:** `energy=4` propulsive but alternating dark or lighter passages; `electronic=5` predominantly club-oriented digital/electronic arrangements; `mood=4` euphoric with edgier Moodymann turns; `theatrical=3` guest vocals, spoken inserts and DJ performance; `experimental=3` bold sample collage and house styles but familiar hooks; `polish=4` technically prepared yet editors' choices divide critics; `tempo=4` rapid club flow; `groove=5` set deliberately structured for dancefloor listening [C_PF,C_NME,C_NY,C_NYI].

**Voice:** clear/powerful/soft Dua lead plus spoken host inserts and Missy Elliott guest rap; `rap` is *not* a claim that Dua primarily raps [C_GR,C_NYI]. **Lyrics:** largely inherited love, heartbreak, confidence and sensuality; `party` describes prominent remix setting; `social` limited to the Boys Will Be Boys reinterpretation [C_NME,C_GR]. **Language:** `en|ko`, provided the original DJ mix includes the bilingual BLACKPINK appearance in Kiss and Make Up; confirm Korean presence in whichever version Claude ultimately imports [C_Q].

**Important producer-role dispute:** Qobuz and MusicBrainz distinguish original-song producer, additional producer, separate remixer, and DJ-mixer. Dork aggregates many under a broad Producer header, including people credited as remixers elsewhere [C_Q,C_MB,C_DK]. **This row is conservatively limited to names with album-production credits in the aggregated list that also occur as producer/co-producer of songs reused in the mix, plus The Blessed Madonna whose creative producer function is documented by her interview and Dork.** The separate remixers Joe Goddard, Jayda G, Zach Witness, Gen Hoshino, Midland, Masters at Work, Horse Meat Disco, Mr Fingers, Paul Woolford, Yaeji, Mark Ronson and Moodymann are **not** automatically inserted as producers merely for remixing. Their work remains directly evidenced in sources and notes. Claude must reconcile producer labels against individual master recording credits; therefore `confidence=medium` [C_DK,C_MB,C_Q,C_NYI]. **Influences:** curator independently explains how Madonna's remix history, Deee-Lite, Stevie Nicks, Jamiroquai, and Missy Elliott informed the specific project [C_NYI]. **Landmark=0:** major documented remix initiative, but not a principal studio-album milestone.

## 4. Radical Optimism (2024) — `dualipa-radicaloptimism`

**Edition:** 3 May 2024 eleven-track original, not the extended mixes or regional reissues [R_OC,R_MB,R_Q]. Major producers **Kevin Parker, Danny L Harle, Andrew Wyatt and Ian Kirkpatrick** have producer-labelled original track credits; Caroline Ailin and Tobias Jesso Jr. are prominent writers, and Caroline Ailin and Cameron Gower Poole have specified vocal-production duties but are not silently added as principal track producers [R_MB,R_Q]. Synths, programmed drums, guitars, bass, piano and percussion all feature [R_Q,R_SL].

**Genre disagreement:** Slant describes *neo-psychedelia* and live playing, but Guardian/Pitchfork contest the idea that psychedelia or Britpop audibly dominates. `neopsychedelia` is included with **disputed/texture** scope and modest genre order, not treated as a new primary pop category [R_SL,R_GU,R_PF]. The singer explicitly reports considering psychedelia, trip-hop and Britpop, which documents musical research/inspiration but not necessarily those genres performed in the record [R_OC,R_GI]. Euro-pop and French-touch/house notes refer mainly to Illusion; Latin color to Maria [R_PF,R_SL].

**Scores:** `energy=3` softer or mid-tempo average versus the earlier disco record; `electronic=4` electronic production coexists with fuller live instrumentation; `mood=4` warm resilient, tinged with breakup sadness; `theatrical=2` more casual and contained melodic approach; `experimental=2` small sound-design deviations within simple pop songs; `polish=4` rich technically precise but some disagreement over excess density; `tempo=3` varied and less relentless; `groove=4` many danceable songs without nonstop club purpose [R_AM,R_SL,R_GU,R_OB,R_PF].

**Voice:** strong/clear with quieter softer stretches [R_SL,R_AM]. **Lyrics:** love, painful breakups, self-worth and accepting former partners, plus sensual invitation [R_SL,R_GI]. **Documented influences:** *Currents* by Tame Impala was expressly cited by Dua as an aspiration [R_AP]; press and critics also report Lipa's stated inspiration from Primal Scream, Massive Attack, Oasis and Blur, even though critics say the album seldom resembles them [R_OC,R_PF,R_GU]. **sounds_like:** Tame Impala, ABBA, Caroline Polachek and the Avalanches arise from critics' sonic parallels; Jessie Ware is editorial adjacent dance-pop rather than proven influence. **Landmark=1:** third original studio-album milestone, although the critical reception was mixed rather than uniformly enthusiastic.

## New tokens and import cautions

`baltimoreclub` (source C_PF): propose parents `house|electronic|dance`; `neopsychedelia` (R_SL, with strong critical disagreement R_GU/R_PF): propose `pop|experimental`. Add these to `scenes.ts` and `genreFamilies.ts` and the validator whitelist, choosing parent edges that actually fit Fandome's ontology.
