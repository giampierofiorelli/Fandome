# Fandome — Whitney Houston Phase 03: evidence, reasoning and limitations

Research date: **25 October 2026**. Phase covers only original releases **2003, 2009, 2012**. Values 0–5 are editorial whole-album assessments of record-specific sources; no audio fingerprinting or unverified equipment attribution. Per-genre citation and scope appear separately in `whitney_phase03_genre_evidence.csv`. Music producers are credited music makers, not writers alone, executive producers, managers, film producers or a film score composer.

## `whitney-onewishtheholidayalbum` — One Wish: The Holiday Album (2003)

**Release and type.** The estate's [official discography](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/one-wish-holiday-album/) dates the original to November 18, 2003 and lists **11 cuts**. It is a studio seasonal album, *not* a 2020/2022 lyric-video reissue. The last two songs, *Who Would Imagine a King* and *Joy to the World*, are reused **1996** recordings from *The Preacher’s Wife*, not fresh 2003 studio sessions; a track version/credit distinction is essential for similarity recommendations. *Little Drummer Boy* features Bobbi Kristina Brown, not a separately co-billed album.

**Genres.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/one-wish-the-holiday-album-r666808) categorizes it under R&B, Christmas and Adult Contemporary and describes a synth-forward, often minimal backing; [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/31418-whitney-houston-one-wish-the-holiday-album.php) also explicitly lists Pop Soul; [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/whitney-houston-one-wish-the-holiday-album/) identifies gospel-infused 1996 encore, [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/dec/05/popandrock.shopping3) describes *O Come O Come Emmanuel* as cocktail doo-wop. Put `gospel` and `doowop` toward the end because they are song-/texture-limited, **not** blanket album styles. `christmas` is an existing music genre token and is relevant in addition to the `studio` release type.

**Instrument and recording evidence.** Label-supplied [Qobuz original credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/one-wish-the-holiday-album-whitney-houston/0828765099622): Troy Taylor records **drums, bass, keyboards** on *The First Noël* and *The Christmas Song*; Mervyn Warren contributes **piano, keyboards** on the ballads and produces most seasonal songs, and Scott Frankfurt is credited for **drum programming**; title track has **keyboards (Barry Eastmond), live drums, electric/baritone guitar, bass, percussion and strings** (violins, violas, cellos). AllMusic unambiguously names **synth** and **drum machine** texture. Output `keyboards|synth|drummachine|piano|bass|drums|strings|electricguitar|guitar|percussion`; generic Qobuz “guitar” is not reinterpreted as acoustic/electric without a specific credit. There is **no demonstrated make/model** of synthesizer, DAW or named drum machine and no defensible automatic `vocalchops`/`layeredvocals` claim. `production` therefore remains **blank**; a synth is `instruments`, not a proprietary production token.

**Musical producers, unlike executives.** Qobuz/song credits: **Mervyn Warren**, **Troy Taylor**, **Gordon Chambers**, **Barry J. Eastmond** and **Whitney Houston** (specifically on the two reused 1996 compositions). The [title song official credits](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/one-wish-for-christmas/) explicitly name Chambers and Eastmond. Ivy Skoff is production coordinator, not credited as a music producer; the late-Clive-Davis label oversight is not a musical producer credit.

**Scales.** `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=1` reflect mid-slow seasonal standards and sparse rhythm rather than a dance record. `electronic=2` synthesizers/programmed drums coexist with live piano, drums, bass and string orchestra. `mood=4` peace/fellowship/seasonal warmth; `theatrical=4` gospel climaxes, projected vocal lines; `experimental=0` familiar arrangements; `polish=4` studio cleanliness. `voice=powerful|clear|soft|choral` balances solo lead, intimate phrases and guest gospel/choral passages. `lyrics=spiritual|love|nostalgia|social` includes the original title track's explicitly documented peace/unity rather than politics. `landmark=0` (a discrete seasonal release, not a defining creative step); `confidence=high` because credits and consistent critical descriptions survive.

**Disagreement.** [Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/one-wish-the-holiday-album-r666808) finds harmless polished Christmas arrangements but an overly safe choice; [Cinquemani](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/whitney-houston-one-wish-the-holiday-album/) particularly likes Warren's arrangements while disputing vocal consistency; [Sullivan](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/dec/05/popandrock.shopping3) regards performances as disengaged but singles out an a cappella moment and the doo-wop texture. These aesthetic differences do **not** contradict the credited instruments or 2003 release.

## `whitney-ilooktoyou` — I Look to You (2009)

**Release.** Official [estate original listing](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/i-look-you/) gives 31 Aug 2009 (US), **11 tracks**, Whitney's final new full studio album, no later remix editions folded in. Label-supplied [Qobuz track metadata](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/i-look-to-you-whitney-houston/vr87surfl14ab) and [liner transcription](https://sessiondays.com/2025/08/08/2009-whitney-houston-i-look-to-you/) underpin production role choices.

**Genres, weight and disagreement.** The album is mostly contemporary R&B/pop soul, with dance and more traditional adult-contemporary performances: [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/whitney-houston-i-look-to-you/) specifically documents **disco** on *Million Dollar Bill*, a **house** version of *A Song for You*, **reggae** on *I Got You*. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/i-look-to-you-mw0000821474) identifies neo-disco beside modern soul; mapped to existing `nudisco`, low in list, because only part of repertoire. [Alexis Petridis](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/oct/15/whitney-houston-cd-review) calls its dominant voice pop-soul with **eurodisco** touches. `dancepop` relates principally to those track-specific dance crossovers; `reggae` is **only texture**. No `electropop`/`synthpop` by inference.

**Documented instruments.** [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/i-look-to-you-whitney-houston/vr87surfl14ab) explicitly attributes **guitar** to Brent Stray (*Call You Tonight*), Tony Love (*I Got You*) and Donnie Lyle (*Salute*); **keyboards** to David Foster (*I Didn't Know My Own Strength*); **synth** to Tricky Stewart (*I Look to You*) and Jochem van der Saag (*I Didn't Know My Own Strength*). [Original estate title-track personnel](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/track/i-look-to-you/) describe Emanuel Kiriakou playing “all instruments” apart from Tricky's synth pad and the Voices of Praise choir; this phrase is **not** evidence for every possible instrument. The reviewer explicitly describes a **rubbery bass line** and electronic programmed rhythm in *Million Dollar Bill*, and a piano-melody-led ballad; `bass|piano` are texture/instrument evidence at the album level, not model-specific studio session claims. There is no unequivocal drum-machine credit, so **`drummachine` is left out** despite programmer personnel. `production` blank: `programmer` is a personnel credit but does not identify sampling, specific machine, vocal filter or software.

**Musical producers.** Song-level credits include Alicia Keys and Swizz Beatz (Million Dollar Bill); Danja, Fernando Garibay, Harvey Mason Jr. (Nothin' But Love); StarGate/Johntá Austin (Call You Tonight); Emanuel Kiriakou, Tricky Stewart and Harvey Mason Jr. (title track); Akon/Claude Kelly/Giorgio Tuinfort (Like I Never Left / I Got You); David Foster (I Didn't Know My Own Strength); Eric Hudson (Worth It); R. Kelly (Salute). Producer/arranger/songwriter are distinguished in each Qobuz entry; **do not append Clive Davis, Whitney Houston, Larry Jackson** merely because the estate page's general “Producers” credit includes them: [Whitney explicitly thanks Jackson](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/i-look-you/) for *finding songs and connecting producers*, an A&R/curatorial rather than unambiguous track-level music-producer role. Vocal production by Harvey Mason Jr. is directly stated, not conflated with a named `production` technique.

**Scales and lyrics.** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` balance club-friendly mid/upbeat songs and reflective anthems; `electronic=3` synth and programming versus traditional rhythm/guitar and ballads; `mood=3` resilience and pain coexist with celebration; `theatrical=3` vocal style more internalized than 1992, though with big refrains; `experimental=1` house reworking of a pop standard amid safe formats; `polish=5` contemporary top-tier studio treatment. `voice=raspy|powerful|soft|clear|choral`, led by a markedly **rougher/huskier** mature register attested by [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/whitney-houston-i-look-to-you/), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/i-look-to-you-mw0000821474) and [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/oct/15/whitney-houston-cd-review/). `lyrics=introspection|identity|love|heartbreak|spiritual` from recovery and prayer themes (title track) vs pop romance; `landmark=1` final studio album and notable reinvention. Critic scores at [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/music/i-look-to-you/whitney-houston/critic-reviews) show mixed-to-positive reception, while [Kitty Empire](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/oct/18/whitney-houston-look-to-you) criticizes anonymous healing platitudes; don't convert reception into mechanical “low polish.” `sounds_like` are sonic *editorial* analogies; these artists are **not documented influences**, so `influenced_by` stays empty.


## `whitney-sparkleoriginalmotionpicturesoundtrack` — Sparkle: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack (2012)

### Identity, artist scope, and provenance

[Official estate release](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack/) establishes a July 31, 2012 US soundtrack with **11 tracks**. This album is generally billed as **Various Artists**, although it appears on Whitney’s estate discography and she sings only **two tracks**: solo gospel *His Eye Is on the Sparrow* and finale duet *Celebrate* with Jordin Sparks. Fandome’s `artist=Whitney Houston` is a curatorial catalogue placement, **not** a claim that all 11 songs feature Whitney; mark this prominently in catalog metadata if possible. There is **no equal co-billed album artist**: Jordin’s duet credit is at track level. The estate [confirms](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/news/whitney-houston-jordin-sparks-celebrate-was-released-as-a-single-in-2012/) that *Celebrate* was Whitney's **last song recorded before her death**, making `landmark=1` meaningful despite her limited track count. Do not equate soundtrack release year (2012) with the entire original musical repertoire or with the song’s recording day. Album relates to the 2012 remake of *Sparkle* (1976 original).

### Styles, disagreements, and influence

[Andy Kellman, AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0002390300) explicitly files the soundtrack as **Retro-Soul** and **Adult Contemporary R&B**, a highly supported sonic centre combining faithful new recordings of Curtis Mayfield’s original *Sparkle* songs and later R&B productions. Thus `retrosoul|rnb|soul|contemporaryrnb` take priority, with `pop|adultcontemporary` as secondary crossover tags. `gospel` is present on Houston’s *His Eye Is on the Sparrow* and Jordin’s *One Wing*, and `nudisco` is **only** AllMusic’s explicitly identified *Celebrate* rhythm; neither is a majority-album style. `retrosoul` is a **new allowed genre token to register**, mapped directly from AllMusic’s Retro-Soul tag; it must be added to `scenes.ts`, `genreFamilies.ts` and the validator before full validation. `soundtrack` belongs in `type`, not in `genres`.

*Documented influenced_by: Curtis Mayfield.* [Kellman](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0002390300) identifies four faithful versions of his songs from the original 1976 soundtrack, supplying direct demonstrable repertoire lineage, not an inferred sound-alike. Keep him `influenced_by`, never mistake for a 2012 album producer. The `sounds_like` analogies of Aretha Franklin, the Supremes, Martha and the Vandellas and En Vogue stem from Kellman’s explicitly articulated comparisons: Aretha sang the 1976 release, the Supremes/Vandellas inform *Running*, and En Vogue famously recorded *Something He Can Feel*. These are album sonic reference points, not credited 2012 performers.

Review contrast: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0002390300) finds the package pleasant though somewhat disjointed and praises Houston’s strong presence; [Allison Stewart, Washington Post (30 Jul 2012)](https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/style/quick-spins-whitney-houstons-last-release/2012/07/30/gJQAJnzZLX_story.html) hears restrained, hushed Whitney and many forgettable midtempo tracks; [James Reed, Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/2012/08/17/among-film-songs-few-sparks/OK9CZLUTC1vPvUyLoEfKyN/story.html) praises the gospel track’s reverence but finds the rest light modern R&B. Don't flatten these contradictory critical reactions into a claim of uniformly show-stopping Houston vocals.

### Track credits, instrumentation and production

[Presto Music’s track-by-track original credits](https://www.prestomusic.com/classical/products/8265551--sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack) ground **keyboards, organ and choir** on Whitney’s solo *His Eye Is on the Sparrow* (Kenneth Crouch; Voices of Praise); on *Celebrate*, **guitar, bass, keyboards, choral/background vocals and programming** (Donnie Lyle, Bigg Makk, Rodney East, collective vocalists, Abel Garibaldi, Ian Mereness). Other tracks explicitly feature **piano**, **acoustic drums**, **bass**, **guitars**, **saxophones**, **trumpets**, **trombone**, a **horn section**, **violin/viola/cello strings**. All these token families belong in `instruments`, but specific instrument families are **not automatically present on Houston’s own two tracks**. Keep this album-wide distinction in the import.

Only `layeredvocals` is coded in `production`: Stewart’s Washington Post critique explicitly calls Whitney’s *Celebrate* vocals “multi-tracked”. The credits’ generic word “programmer” does **not** establish `sequencer`, `sampling`, specific MIDI workstation, drum-machine model, Pro Tools, or audio software; none are populated. Producer list is deduplicated from [AllMusic explicit Producer roles](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0002390300), [IMDb film soundtrack produced-by cue listings](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1876451/soundtrack/) and [FilmMusic music-track credits](https://filmmusic.com/movie/sparkle-2012/): R. Kelly, Bigg Makk, Andrew “Pop” Wansel, Oak (Warren Felder), Chuck Harmony, Claude Kelly, The MIDI Mafia, The Underdogs, Flippa123, Jameel Roberts. **Not** included: Curtis Mayfield (the old songs’ writer), Harvey Mason Jr. (mixing and vocal production role, but not credited plain music co-producer here), Dexter Wansel (horn/string arrangements only), Houston herself (the film’s *executive producer*), Salaam Remi (film score, not necessarily songs), soundtrack executive producers.

### Eight anchored 0–5 judgments and thematic values

* **energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3:** mainly midtempo soulful original soundtrack, with upbeat retro-pop numbers (*I’m a Man*, *Running*, *Celebrate*) set against slower church ballad and solemn Curtis Mayfield covers. Not a full dance album.
* **electronic 2:** live drums, bass, guitar, organ and horn credits on multiple tracks with some arranged programming. Credits do not show a fully acoustic or synth-dominant production.
* **mood 4, theatrical 3:** uplifting group finale and hopeful/romantic film-song repertoire outweigh mournful notes; big gospel performance exists but many vocals are understated and midtempo.
* **experimental 0, polish 4:** familiar retro-soul covers and tidy contemporary R&B arrangements; neither avant-garde nor unprocessed/lo-fi studio performance. Production score concerns heard presentation and documented polished remake context, **not** assumed hardware.
* **voice clear|powerful|soft|raspy|choral:** Jordin/Carmen/Goapele/CeeLo group voices, with late-period Houston's expressive rasp and choir support. This is an **album-wide multi-artist voice aggregate**, NOT a description of Whitney alone.
* **lyrics love|spiritual|identity|heartbreak:** relationship-centred Curtis Mayfield songs, hymn/testimony and uplift songs, cinematic character roles; some themes are track-scoped, not every song.
* **confidence high:** official estate tracklist, multiple original music producer credits, contemporary reviewers and instrument-level personnel corroborate sound and singer proportions. Ratings remain interpretive, hence `draft`.

### Version / catalogue caveats

[AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0002390300) prints **July 30, 2012** release metadata, while the [estate](https://www.whitneyhouston.com/music/sparkle-original-motion-picture-soundtrack/) says **July 31, 2012 US**. Both agree on **2012**; use official U.S. date for reference. No fabricated version-specific credits. Original 11-song vocal soundtrack must not be confused with **Salaam Remi’s 31-minute instrumental original score soundtrack** released separately October 2012 per [FilmMusic.com](https://filmmusic.com/movie/sparkle-2012/). The latter is **not** included in this Whitney-centered package.

## Import-critical controls

1. Strictly incremental **three rows only (2003, 2009, 2012)**. Branch `docs/descriptors/whitney.csv` contains phase01 (1985, 1987, 1990, 1992); prior Phase 02 delivered separately (1995, 1996, 1998, 2002) and might still await import. Don't merge or replace it inadvertently.
2. This Phase 03 creates genre token `retrosoul` **because AllMusic expressly says Retro-Soul**. The token is not listed in the current `scenes.ts` Genre union, `genreFamilies.ts` or validator. Register before validation. No new instruments or production tokens.
3. 2003 and 2009 are original studio albums; 2012 is **various-artist** soundtrack placed in Whitney's directory because it contains her final recorded duet. No 2022+ rows are in this ZIP.
4. Only exact original musical producer roles; no conflation of film score composition, screen credits, songwriting, executive production or sound mixing with credited recorded-song producers.
