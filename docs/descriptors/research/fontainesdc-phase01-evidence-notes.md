# Fontaines D.C. — Phase 01: evidence and reasoning

Research date: 26 October 2026. **Editorial numeric scores** (0–5) apply to entire albums; qualifying sources are referenced by URLs in `fontainesdc_phase01_sources.csv`. The notes below describe how each score is inferred. Sources describe musical attributes; scores are Fandome interpretation. Stronger documentary evidence for equipment is from credits and first-person recording accounts.

## Dogrel (2019) — `fontainesdc-dogrel`

**Genres / priority.** Dominant `postpunk` is explicit in Pitchfork; AllMusic supplies `postpunkrevival`, `newwave` and `indierock`. Pitchfork mentions early garage, surf and rock'n'roll elements; the Guardian identifies surf-punk songs and one Britpop-style ballad; Galway Advertiser explicitly identifies art-punk, new wave and garage rock on specific tracks; Loud and Quiet calls *Hurricane Laughter* garage-psych. Thus those last genres are ranked lower, with scopes documented line by line. Genre evidence is descriptive, not based on influence lists.

**Eight scales.** Energy **4** from fast early songs, insistent bass and twin guitars (*Big*, *Too Real*, *Boys in the Better Land*), with exceptions *Roy's Tune* and *Dublin City Sky*. Electronic **1** because the predominantly played live guitar/bass/drum ensemble nevertheless uses documented analog synths and keyboard accents. Mood **2**: high kinetic excitement but wistful city portraits, class anxieties and nostalgia. Theatrical **2**: direct, almost spoken bark rather than elaborate staging. Experimental **2**: conventional song-focused guitar rock with angular digressions and unusually continuous tape recording. Polish **1**: Carey deliberately retained roughness rather than glossy compression. Tempo **4**, allowing for slower closing songs. Groove **3**: many propulsive, rhythmic pieces, but not a dance-floor album.

**Voice / lyrics / language.** Chatten's rough, declamatory Dublin delivery supports `raspy|spoken|powerful` (Pitchfork, Guardian, NME). Self-mythologising, social gentrification, local Dublin figures, cultural belonging, satirical observations and romantic/nostalgic closing tracks support the ordered themes. English is predominant.

**Instruments.** MusicBrainz's track-level credits document **electric guitar** (including baritone and surf guitar), bass, drums, tambourine, percussion, piano, chord organ, analog synth (Swarmatron), and Buchla Music Easel. The guitar timbre and electric guitars are independently supported by Guitar.com and reviews. `clavioline` is **not** inferred from the organ credit: Carlos says directly in the 2020 Stereogum interview that he had already used the clavioline to add low-end movement to *Hurricane Laughter*.

**Production.** Carlos' 2019 X-Press interview explicitly describes tracking in 15-minute reels, two-to-three song sequences played together and two days of overdubs; Dan Carey's Line of Best Fit interview confirms the batch process. `liverecording|analogtape|overdubbing` captures both facts, without falsely asserting the entire album was recorded in one unedited take. `swarmatron` and `buchlamusiceasel` come from MusicBrainz instrument/model credits. `heavyreverb` is only a limited guitar-feature tag supported by the described reverberant guitar lead on *Sha Sha Sha*, so it is last in order.

**Documented influences.** The band's 2018 So Young interview directly credits The Dubliners, Girl Band, The Pogues and The Rolling Stones; Carlos' 2018 Dork interview credits The Beach Boys/surf. These are early, pre-*Dogrel* musical influences. The Fall, The Strokes, Gang of Four, The Walkmen and The Chameleons are better supported as sonic parallels in contemporary reviews and kept in `sounds_like`.

**Disagreement/caution.** Critics disagree on the strength of the band's post-punk identification: Pitchfork centres it; Ed Nash in The Line of Best Fit calls *Dogrel* more than a post-punk homage. Accordingly multiple subgenre tokens follow specific critical descriptions. *Dublin City Sky* hints at Irish folk but the Galway reviewer explicitly says the group is not a folk band; do not label the whole album folk. Original 2019 LP versions take precedence over earlier singles.

**Landmark and confidence.** **1**, **high**: breakthrough first album and Mercury nomination are independently documented (Stereogum) with substantial contemporary review coverage. Genre microtags individually carry lower certainty than the row's main classification.

## A Hero's Death (2020) — `fontainesdc-aherosdeath`

**Genres / priority.** `postpunk`, `indierock`, `postpunkrevival` and `rock` remain the backbone (AllMusic, Pitchfork). AOTY lists `gothicrock`, `neopsychedelia`, `artpunk`, `crankwave` as album descriptors (medium reliability). Pitchfork's track descriptions supply the more limited `artrock` (*Love Is the Main Thing*) and `punkblues` (*A Lucid Dream*). Dead Air at the Pulpit labels noise rock and garage rock facets. `newwave` follows AllMusic's explicit style label. Do not classify the entire album as surf rock on the basis of one guitar tone.

**Eight scales.** Energy **3**: furious *Televised Mind* / *A Lucid Dream* balanced by many slower songs. Electronic **2**: modular filtered noise, Swarmatron, Moog One and clavioline add substantially to an otherwise guitar-dominated band. Mood **1**: touring exhaustion, detachment, loss of self and an anxious tone, with a qualified glimmer of resilience. Theatrical **3**: incantatory vocal delivery, dramatic contrast and expanded songs. Experimental **3**: repetition, warped low-end and studio manipulation, but recognisable rock structures. Polish **3**: clearer, more sculpted than *Dogrel*, as Stereogum reports. Tempo **3** and groove **2**: mixed pace, many meditative tracks, some motoric pulses.

**Voice / lyrics / language.** Pitchfork hears rough proclamations, murmuring and close ballads; Stereogum discusses intense repetition and gentler singing. The self-isolation, relationships, burnout, societal unease, rueful irony and tentative recovery recur across tracks. English throughout.

**Instruments.** MusicBrainz's individual track credits document guitars including acoustic/classical guitar, piano, celesta, Moog One synth, Swarmatron, clavioline and strings on *Sunny*. Band's bass/drums/electric guitars are independently described by Hotpress, Guitar.com and Stereogum. Strings are documented as an actual performance by Raven Bush, **not** as a credited production role.

**Production.** Dan Carey alone produced the *released* album (MusicBrainz/AllMusic). In a direct Line of Best Fit interview he describes a room amp fed an unusual mixture of programme material through a **modular synthesizer and filter**, supporting `sonicmanipulation`. MusicBrainz track-level credits explicitly identify Swarmatron, **Moog One**, and a device labelled **Enzo FX**; the latter name is kept verbatim as `enzofx`, without guessing a brand. Carlos' Stereogum track commentary describes engineering the low end of *Living in America* around guitar/bass-amp effects and clavioline, and a wall of sound. Live ensemble and analog tape are supported by the producer's recording account and NME's description of *Skinty Fia* as carrying forward the live-to-tape methods of the first two; the evidence for analog tape is indirect for this particular LP, so flag for producer confirmation if a stricter standard is required.

**Documented influences.** Grian explicitly names the Beach Boys/Brian Wilson sound-world, with Lee Hazlewood (NME interview). Carlos says *You Said* was musically inspired by **The Brian Jonestown Massacre**; *Living in America* by **Suicide**, **Cabaret Voltaire** and **Spacemen 3** (Stereogum interview). Not collaborators. Miles Davis only influenced **naming the album title**, rather than musical sound, so excluded from `influenced_by` per the rule against nonmusical inspiration.

**Disagreement/caution.** AllMusic is lukewarm compared with Pitchfork and Stereogum. Stereogum says more structured/processed than debut; Line of Best Fit says Carey kept the band spontaneous. These are compatible (live performance with electronic processing). The band attempted and discarded a version in Los Angeles with Nick Launay; he is **not** a credited producer of this released record. Abbey Road's Christian Wright is a **mastering engineer**, not producer. An online metadata summary claiming Dan Carey produced the final album in Los Angeles conflicts with the first-person London-studio account; do not rely on it.

**Landmark and confidence.** **1**, **high**: acclaimed follow-up with strong coverage; the specific model credits and songwriter interviews offer unusually good source separation. Microgenre sources are medium and clearly identified.

## Skinty Fia (2022) — `fontainesdc-skintyfia`

**Genres / priority.** Dominant `postpunk` with `indierock` and `rock`. AOTY lists `gothicrock` and `shoegaze`; the Guardian describes explicitly shoegaze guitar on *Nabokov*. NME calls the title song a dark **trip-hop** turn, Pitchfork names **industrial funk** there, **gothgaze** on *Bloomsday*, and a **jungle-patterned** beat on the opening track. These are retained as lower-priority genre/texture facets, not presented as the dominant sound. The proposed `gothgaze` and `industrialfunk` new tokens must be imported first.

**Eight scales.** Energy **3**: restrained pulse and hypnotic repetition, then peaks on *I Love You* and *Nabokov*. Electronic **2**: effected guitars, electronic synth/Swarmatron and rhythmic treatments, retaining live bass/drums/guitars. Mood **1**: melancholy and anger about Ireland and dislocation, with warmer refrains. Theatrical **3**: intense climaxes and near-choral vocal arrangements balanced by a quiet accordion solo. Experimental **4**: sharper stylistic leaps than the first two LPs, from ethereal guitar and trip-hop rhythms to accordion storytelling. Polish **3**: bigger room, carefully arranged sonics, still a live tracking core. Tempo **2**: Guardian explicitly notes slowed default pace. Groove **3**: title track pulses with dark dance-associated rhythms, but many slow sections remain.

**Voice / lyrics / language.** Main vocal tone remains rough but includes controlled melodic softer singing and climactic belting (Pitchfork/NME). Choral backing harmonies occur on the opener, but `choral` is **not** added to `voice` because the data field denotes lead delivery. Politics, Irish diaspora and identity, immigration, love, toxic relationships, ageing and nostalgia are confirmed by Grian's Rolling Stone song-by-song account and critics. Most tracks are English; the repeated Irish refrain on *In ár gCroíthe go deo* supports secondary `ga` (Irish Gaelic). No assumption that the entire track is sung in Irish.

**Instruments.** MusicBrainz credits list guitar/electric bass, drums, acoustic 12-string guitars, synths including Dan Carey's **Swarmatron**, accordion and tambourine (Grian Chatten), bouzouki (Deegan on *Bloomsday*), piano, celesta (*Nabokov*), clavioline (*The Couple Across the Way*), and xylophone (*Big Shot*). Percussion is credited to Tom Coll. Guitar.com independently identifies the borrowed **Fylde 12-string** on sessions. Xylophone, bouzouki, celesta and clavioline are track-level details, hence ordered late.

**Production.** NME states recorded **live to tape** over two weeks; Guitar.com confirms a larger residential live room (Angelic, Oxfordshire area) while MusicBrainz credits Angelic Studio at Brackley/Northamptonshire. Stereogum records Curley's claim of drum gating/effects, not a demonstrably precise `noisegating` process. Guitar.com documents the **Industrialectric Echo Degrader** pedal used on *Nabokov*, and Carey Swarmatron appears in MusicBrainz's instrumental credits. These support `sonicmanipulation|swarmatron|industrialectricechodegrader`. Curley mentions a Logic demo; do **not** tag `logic` for the mastered record from this alone.

**Documented influences.** Grian says Sinéad O'Connor's *The Lion and the Cobra* and *I'm Stretched on Your Grave* specifically shaped *In ár gCroíthe go deo* and *Skinty Fia*; Curley directly cites Primal Scream's *XTRMNTR* and guitar playing by Cocteau Twins (Robin Guthrie) and My Bloody Valentine in an album inspiration interview (Stereogum, 2022). He describes adopting a sampling-like approach to guitar from Warm Drag (Guitar.com). No sample of these artists is asserted.

**Disagreement/caution.** Pitchfork calls it comparatively demanding, while NME emphasizes hook-writing and the Guardian slower, reflective tempos. These describe different tracks, and justify mixed energy/tempo. NME's trip-hop, Pitchfork's industrial-funk and gothgaze refer to **individual tracks**, not an album-wide wholesale genre replacement. Avoid converting the abandoned Irish-trad double-album plan into a folk classification for the finished record.

**Landmark and confidence.** **1**, **high**: repeatedly acclaimed and UK number one, important stylistic bridge before *Romance*. Individual genre microtags remain more tentative than instrumentation and basic post-punk classification.
