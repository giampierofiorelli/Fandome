# Simple Minds — Phase 02: evidence and judgment notes

Prepared 28 October 2026. All scores on the 0–5 axes are editorial judgments anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`; sources support their interpretation, not numeric precision. This phase contains ONLY four new albums. The 1981 album split uses the original UK LP track assignments. Genres are ordered by importance and independently justified/qualified in `genre_evidence.csv`.

## Sister Feelings Call (1981) — `simpleminds-sisterfeelingscall`

- **Release identity:** The band's own history expressly identifies *Sister Feelings Call* and *Sons and Fascination* as two separately assembled albums from the same sessions, sometimes bundled as a double LP. Only the Sister seven-track LP is represented here: *Theme for Great Cities*; *The American*; *20th Century Promised Land*; *Wonderful in Young Life*; *League of Nations*; *Careful in Career*; *Sound in 70 Cities*. No tracks from the previous phase's Sons row are copied.
- **Genre and groove:** AllMusic's five album-specific styles are all included, not merely new wave. The instrumental *Theme for Great Cities* puts hypnotic dance bass and drums forward (AllMusic song review); this is more rhythmic than the expansive main 1981 *Sons* LP. `groove=5` is an editorial album-level assessment based primarily on its rhythm-forward material, rather than claiming every track is for club use.
- **Vocals:** Kerr's gritty/clear voice appears on sung tracks; `instrumental` is a minority track-level qualification for the opener and closer, **not** a claim the entire record lacks singing.
- **Instruments:** Original combined-session sleeve credits Burchill guitar, Forbes bass, Brian McGee drums, MacNeil keyboards, Kerr vocals. Synth timbre is supported by period assessments of their rhythm-driven 1981 work, but no specific synthesizer model is sourced. Percussion/drums are supported by the *Theme* song review. The exact keyboard model is unknown; no machine tokens are inferred.
- **Producer/technique:** Steve Hillage alone is the credited producer; Hugh Jones and Alan Jakoby worked as engineers. No independently verified signature processing chain or hardware model for the Sister tracks: `production` is intentionally empty. `influenced_by` remains empty; band-wide influences from Phase 01 are not automatically assigned to this album. `sounds_like` names are non-causal editorial approximations.
- **Lyrics and scales:** Urban social / political unease and abstract personal reflection recur, while the dance instrumental format reduces the proportion of lead singing. Moderate electronic score and experimental score reflect extended arrangements, not full synth-only production.

## New Gold Dream (81-82-83-84) (1982) — `simpleminds-newgolddream81828384`

- **Original scope:** Nine-track 1982 studio album, NOT the 2016 deluxe box with B-sides or live material. Strong original-credits sources: official album page and 1982 release reproductions; drummer credits are distributed across Mel Gaynor, Kenny Hyslop and Mike Ogletree. Sharon Campbell provides backing vocals on two tracks. Herbie Hancock plays a synth solo **only on Hunter and the Hunted**; his guest performance is an instrument credit, not a producer/influence credit.
- **Genre hierarchy:** The new-wave and dance-rock descriptions are album-wide (AllMusic). Synthesisers underpin lush pop; `synthpop` describes sound throughout but is less literal as an AllMusic style tag. `collegerock` and `alternativeindierock` are retrospective AllMusic classifications, not contemporary 1982 self-labels. `funk` is **partial** rhythm texture heard on dance-oriented songs such as *Promised You a Miracle*, independently corroborated by contemporary source recollections of drummer Kenny Hyslop's funk listening. There is no unsubstantiated electropop or post-disco token.
- **Technical evidence:** Producer Peter Walsh's account in *Hi-Fi News* directly names tape / Studer machines, bass flanging, Moog reinforcement/doubling of Forbes's bass, and an effort to emulate highly compressed cassette demo dynamics. Consequently `analogtape|flanging|moog|compression|overdubbing` is supported. Large vocal echo/reverb is discussed, but `heavyreverb` is omitted because Walsh characterises the final integrated effect as subtle; `digitalreverb`, Fairlight, LinnDrum and DX7 are NOT proven for this album.
- **Producer discrepancy:** Official original sleeve says **Produced & Engineered by Peter Walsh**. Some MusicBrainz recording-level entries list **Keith Forsey** with Walsh on particular tracks, but this conflicts with primary album credits; retain **Peter Walsh only** in `producers` pending an authoritative resolution. Don't count Walsh's arranging, or Herbie Hancock's solo, as an additional producer. This is material and should be checked if cross-release aggregation is later added.
- **Documented influence:** Walsh says directly that during the making of the crossover sound the band were listening to The Human League, ABC, Talking Heads, Roxy Music, Grace Jones and Can, among others, and were responding to contemporary vocal-led production. These are producer-sourced formative listening for this specific album-making period. This is more direct than an analyst's likeness, although the Walsh passage does not assert that every band shaped every track. The Associates, Japan and Heaven 17 stay in `sounds_like` as sonic/editorial comparisons, never as album influence on Walsh's testimony alone.
- **Scores and themes:** Transition from earlier claustrophobic music to hope, romance, reflective future-looking imagery justifies `mood=4` (not automatically euphoric 5); `electronic=4` from dense synthesizers with clearly audible real bass/drums/guitar. Strong studio detail supports high polish, moderate tempo/groove and dynamic, occasionally hushed vocal delivery.

## Sparkle in the Rain (1984) — `simpleminds-sparkleintherain`

- **Album scope and style:** Original ten tracks, including the Lou Reed cover *Street Hassle*. The cover is **not** evidence that Lou Reed is an album-level artistic influence and is not included as such. The band's own 40th-anniversary essay and Kerr interviews describe a deliberately more forceful, rock-oriented sound after the dreamy *New Gold Dream*. Genre tokens combine AllMusic album tags, documented pop-rock and new-wave textures, and Kerr's self-described rock direction.
- **Credits/instruments:** Original sleeve credits Steve Lillywhite as sole producer; Burchill acoustic & electric guitars, Forbes bass and vocals, Gaynor drums and vocals, MacNeil keyboards. The official anniversary article additionally documents synth beats and piano flourishes. Kirsty MacColl's voice is on *Speed Your Love to Me* and *Street Hassle*, **not** every track. Thus multiple vocal qualities and occasional backing harmonies but no fabricated choral ensemble credit.
- **Production caution:** Kerr directly says Lillywhite strengthened the impact of Mel Gaynor's drumming, and that recording was significantly more oriented toward a band playing together. It does not prove the whole album was cut in one take, live-to-tape, or entirely without overdubs. No instrument brand, Linndrum or particular synthetic reverb unit is securely named for these final sessions; `production` is deliberately **blank** instead of inventing technique tokens. The drum-loop anecdote on the band anniversary website refers to the **extended 12-inch mix** of *Waterfront*, **not the original album mix**; no `drumloop` tag.
- **Comparisons versus influences:** Contemporary *Rip It Up* critic likens the enlarged sonics to U2 and Big Country; *Washington Post* makes U2, Roxy Music and OMD comparisons. They belong to `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`. Kerr's interest in Steve Lillywhite's earlier Peter Gabriel production was one factor in choosing him; no artist/producer says *this album's songs* derive from Peter Gabriel, and named producer collaborators are forbidden as influences.
- **Mood/lyrics/scores:** *Waterfront* reflects the River Clyde/Glasgow shipyards, with loss and regeneration, while other songs mix personal love and spiritual/political imagery. Energy and drama are high owing to drums, soaring vocals, anthemic reach; `electronic=2` acknowledges synthesizers while preserving the dominant full band. Strong dynamics distinguish the album from the sleeker predecessor.

## Once Upon a Time (1985) — `simpleminds-onceuponatime`

- **Original release:** The eight original songs are *Once Upon a Time*, *All the Things She Said*, *Ghost Dancing*, *Alive and Kicking*, *Oh Jungleland*, *I Wish You Were Here*, *Sanctify Yourself*, *Come a Long Way*. The breakthrough non-album single *Don't You (Forget About Me)* was released in 1985 for *The Breakfast Club*; it is **not** an original album track even though later deluxe anniversary editions include it. No film soundtrack genre or song-specific instrumentation is imported from that single.
- **Producer and players:** Album's original liner notes credit **Jimmy Iovine and Bob Clearmountain** together. Bob Clearmountain is also a mixer, but on this album he is unambiguously credited as a full **producer**, so include both. Moira Marquis and Mark McKenna are engineers, not producers. John Giblin replaces Derek Forbes on bass; Charlie Burchill guitar; Michael MacNeil piano and synths; Mel Gaynor drums/backing; Jim Kerr lead. Sue Hadjopoulos percussion on *All the Things She Said*. Robin Clark and additional named singers create notable choral-like backing vocals; `layeredvocals` is a conservative qualitative description of these multivoice arrangements.
- **Genres/voice:** AllMusic tags dance-rock, alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, college rock, contemporary pop/rock, and describes a stronger guitar focus than the earlier synthpop emphasis. Music Waves independently identifies the evolving new-wave/pop-rock fusion. No evidence for a specific 1985 synth model; no false `dx7`, `fairlight` or `linn9000`. Kerr is more forceful and projected, the supporting choruses high in the mix: powerful/raspy/clear/choral are arranged by prominence.
- **Similarity/influence:** AllMusic hears U2-like uplift; Big Country is a plausible, qualified editorial similarity in the context of 1980s stadium-scale guitar rock, **not** a source-proven causal influence. No first-person album-specific musical influence identified for 1985: `influenced_by` intentionally blank. Neither Iovine's previous clients Bruce Springsteen and Stevie Nicks nor guest backing singers are legitimate `influenced_by` entries.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic welcomes the guitar-led accessible record and presents it as strong, but Trouser Press condemns the same stadium-scale American-radio ambition as bombast. The album's sonic descriptions are supported regardless of evaluative disagreement; do not present consensus on aesthetic quality.
- **Scores/lyrics:** Polished, accessible large-room production justifies polish=5, theatrical=5, experiment=1; energy=5 as driven by assertive bass/drums and chorus writing, with synths present but subsidiary (`electronic=2`). Personal devotion, social observation and spiritual images coexist; no unjustified party or political dominance.
