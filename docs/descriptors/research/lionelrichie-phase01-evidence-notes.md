# Lionel Richie — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes

Verified on 28 October 2026 against the GitHub `descriptors` branch. Original, single-artist **studio albums only**. Our 0–5 numbers are album-level editorial judgments using Fandome's 0–5 anchors, not statistics and not copied from review scores. Unsubstantiated device names have been left off. Each `genre_evidence.csv` line identifies either an album classification or a limited song-level style.

## Catalog, dates and format

- `lionelrichie` does not exist in either `catalog.ts` or `seed.ts`, nor is there a published `docs/descriptors/lionelrichie.csv`. This ZIP proposes the artist and 11 studio-album IDs; the four current rows are `lionelrichie-lionelrichie`, `lionelrichie-cantslowdown`, `lionelrichie-dancingontheceiling`, `lionelrichie-louderthanwords`.

## Lionel Richie (1982)

**Evidence:** [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/20972-lionel-richie-lionel-richie.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/lionel-richie-mw0000195447); [1982 official track credits published by Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/lionel-richie-lionel-richie/0060254729794); [Sessiondays](https://sessiondays.com/2018/12/19/1982-lionel-richie-lionel-richie/).

- **Genres:** Primary AOTY tags `adultcontemporary`, `popsoul`, `contemporaryrnb`; AllMusic adds `quietstorm` and `softrock`; original LP style `soul|funk`. The last two have less influence in the recommendations, because the album emphasizes romantic songs alongside early brisk dance cuts.
- **Energy 2; electronic 2; tempo 2; groove 2:** Ballads dominate the duration, although Serves You Right and Tell Me have articulated rhythm tracks. Keyboard-heavy session work is present; there is no basis for a fully electronic rating.
- **Mood 3; theatrical 2; experimental 1; polish 4:** Love and yearning mixed with buoyant You Are, an intimate lead voice and carefully constructed studio arrangements; classic songwriting forms.
- **Voice `soft|clear`:** Low-key intimate lead rather than a predominantly screamed or belted performance. **Instruments:** Synth/keys and Rhodes, piano, electric and acoustic guitars, bass/drums/percussion, arrangement strings and horns are explicitly named on credits; not every instrument plays on every track.
- **Production `stringarrangements|hornarrangements`:** James Anthony Carmichael and Gene Page orchestration/arrangement credits; no tape or synth model asserted because the available original track credits do not identify those models album-wide. **Producers:** Richie and Carmichael, not Gene Page who is credited as arranger.
- **Lyrics:** Love and loss, self-reflection; no invented overt political topics. **Similarity:** Peabo Bryson is an editorial comparison in adult-contemporary soul; not an asserted historical influence. **Influences field empty:** No source links a named model specifically to the making of this 1982 album.
- **Reception disagreement:** Robert Christgau's original appraisal is distinctly negative, whereas AllMusic's retrospective evaluates the album favorably; `landmark=1` reflects the debut's documented career importance rather than unanimous acclaim.

## Can't Slow Down (1983)

**Evidence:** [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/20973-lionel-richie-cant-slow-down.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/cant-slow-down-mw0000650685); [Qobuz/Motown track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/cant-slow-down-lionel-richie/oqee9aew0s7oc); [Sessiondays instrument credits](https://sessiondays.com/2014/12/18/1983-lionel-richie-cant-slow/); [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=lionel+richie).

- **Genres:** AOTY explicitly lists `popsoul`, `adultcontemporary`, `smoothsoul`, `contemporaryrnb`, `dancepop`, `synthfunk`. AllMusic adds `quietstorm`, `softrock`, `soul`, broader `rnb`; faster songs supply a small amount of funk. Synth-funk is attached to rhythmic sections, not to Hello.
- **Energy 3; electronic 3; tempo 3; groove 3:** The prominent club-like All Night Long and brisk Running With the Night counterbalance several slower adult ballads. Synths and live drums/guitars are both prominent.
- **Mood 4; theatrical 3; experimental 1; polish 5:** Uplifting singalong energy offsets yearning. Elaborate, precise major-label studio work; standard pop structures.
- **Voice `soft|clear|powerful`:** Mostly gentle smooth tenor, with more emphatic refrain delivery; does not imply vocal distortion. **Instruments:** Session credits corroborate Yamaha GS-1, Fender Rhodes, bass, guitar, strings, horn players, percussion, Simmons electronic drums on title track and synth bass. `electronicdrums` denotes that credited Simmons part only.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements`, `hornarrangements`, synth-programming and vocoder are documented on release credits. `emulator` specifically refers to the credited Emulator on Stuck on You (not Emulator II). `yamahags1` is supported by instrument credit. These are album features, not proof they occur on all eight tracks.
- **Producers:** Lionel Richie and James Anthony Carmichael for most tracks; David Foster is a separately credited producer, not merely a keyboard player.
- **Similarity:** Luther Vandross / Peabo Bryson are editorial adult-contemporary soul references, not evidenced input inspirations. `influenced_by` intentionally blank.
- **Reception:** AllMusic and Christgau both praise the crossover progress, with specific complaints about the market-focused music; confidence high for sound/instrumentation.

## Dancing on the Ceiling (1986)

**Evidence:** [AOTY genre styles](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/20974-lionel-richie-dancing-on-the-ceiling.php); [AllMusic review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/dancing-on-the-ceiling-mw0000191586); [label-supplied Qobuz credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/dancing-on-the-ceiling-lionel-richie/0060254730353); [Tower reissue/liner notes](https://tower.jp/item/114273); [Washington Post review](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1986/08/17/recordings/450a0963-1fb2-44ba-bcdb-fe7196ac2262/); [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=lionel+richie).

- **Genres:** AOTY includes `popsoul|adultcontemporary|synthpop|poprock`; AllMusic lists contemporary R&B, soft-rock and quiet-storm styles. Its title track is dance-oriented, while Deep River Woman is *country-shaded* in the Washington Post; `country` belongs last, not a whole-album country claim.
- **Energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3:** The major production opening and long Don't Stop are upbeat; Ballerina Girl and Say You, Say Me are slow ballads. **Electronic 3:** Rich keyboards, analog/digital synths coexist with band instruments and acoustic-led sections.
- **Mood 4; theatrical 3; experimental 1; polish 5:** Exuberant communal chorus arrangements balanced with romantic sentiment. Sophisticated pop production with conventional structures.
- **Voice:** smooth, clear lead with forceful choruses. **Instruments:** Source credits list synth, piano, keyboards, guitar, bass, drums, percussion, saxophone, strings. **Production:** Carmichael string arrangements; Narada Michael Walden drum programming, specific credited Moog and Synclavier. No conjectural remastering, software or tape formulation.
- **Producers:** Lionel Richie, James Anthony Carmichael and Narada Michael Walden. **Similarity:** Sade and J.J. Cale are specifically named in Christgau's discussion of the 1986 record. This is criticism/comparison, not the artist's own stated influences.
- **Disagreement:** AllMusic considers it a weaker successor because of overlong dance tracks and ballads; contemporary Washington Post has reservations about the lavish, commercial production. Sound-level confidence remains high.

## Louder Than Words (1996)

**Evidence:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/louder-than-words-mw0000181889); [1996 label credits published on Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/louder-than-words-lionel-richie/rkiabshb77n5a); [Sessiondays full liner transcription](https://sessiondays.com/2016/01/27/1996-lionel-richie-louder-than-words/); [LA Times contemporary review](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-04-17-ca-59312-story.html).

- **Genres:** The reliable core is contemporary/adult R&B, quiet storm and soul. AllMusic's `contemporarycountry` tag is weakly substantiated by other critical writing: intentionally **last**, not comparable to the central R&B tags. It is *not* silently mapped onto country-pop. Brief new-jack and hip-hop elements are noted in contemporary and retrospective reviews and are therefore low-weight peripheral tags.
- **Energy 2; tempo 2; groove 2:** Ballads predominate, interrupted by programmed rhythmic singles. **Electronic 3:** Keyboard / synth bass / sampled or programmed drum arrangements coexist with conventional band tracks, brass and strings.
- **Mood 3; theatrical 2; experimental 1; polish 4:** Intimate romantic mood with scattered uptempo and sensual passages. Several high-budget recording teams; fewer extroverted dance tracks.
- **Voice `soft|clear|rap`:** AllMusic specifically discusses occasional rap attempts; `rap` is last because these are a minority of tracks, not a main rapping album. **Instrumentation:** The reissue liner transcription explicitly identifies synthesizers, electric guitars, live drums, synth bass, keyboard/piano, percussion, brass, tenor sax, and harmonica. Production `drumprogramming|hornarrangements|stringarrangements` derives from individual tracks and arrangement credits.
- **Producer credits:** Richie, Carmichael, Jimmy Jam, Terry Lewis and David Foster; do **not** infer Babyface was a producer simply because he co-wrote and performed on Ordinary Girl. Not a guest-influence link.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic criticizes rap excursions; Cheo Hodari Coker in the Los Angeles Times offers a more favorable contemporaneous reading of rich crossover soul. Lower confidence reflects peripheral genre metadata and varying editions, not lack of sourced instruments.
- **Similarity:** Luther Vandross is an editorial sonic reference, not a documented influence. `influenced_by` blank across all four album rows; see the artist-level declarations in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`.
