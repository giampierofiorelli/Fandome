Scope: four **new** album descriptor rows only, none from Phase 01. The original national LP program is authoritative when a later international, CD, or website edition differs. ## Evidence standards / decisions common to all four

- The original instrumental performer is the **ensemble** Rondò Veneziano. The `artist` value is not a producer; original producer credit belongs to Gian Piero Reverberi. Laura Giordano composed songs but does not acquire producer credit from composition alone; Harry Thumann and Klaus Strazicky are recording/mix personnel, not producers. P.E.M. GmbH is a company, not a person added to the producer list.
- `voice=instrumental` and empty `lyrics`, `language`: the original programs are predominantly instrumental; a text-bearing lyricist flag in some digital track metadata does **not** establish performed vocals.
- `sounds_like` is an editorial suggestion of adjacent instrumental orchestral/pop artists, NOT claimed influence. Avoid interpreting guests or producers as inspiration. In *Odissea*, two direct musical quotations/tributes are treated separately below.
- Production terms are **all blank**: evidence confirms analogue-era studios, engineers and electronic instruments but does not establish the exact tape medium, synth model, equipment or technical process. We do not equate an arranged orchestral part with `stringarrangements` unless it is credited as that exact production practice for the specific album.
- Ranges of energy, tempo and groove were selected only when the album-specific composition commentary documents tempi or rhythmic changes; they are *not* measured BPM. `polish` is blank on all four because session location does not establish subjective finish; `mood` is blank throughout rather than presume optimism from orchestra image. Similarly no undocumented signature equipment (`moog`, `dx7`, `linndrum`, etc.) is introduced.

## Odissea veneziana (1984) — rondoveneziano-odisseaveneziana

**Original version and provenance.** The 1984 Baby Records LP (BR 56062) contains **nine tracks**: Odissea veneziana, Tiziano, Campo dei Mori, Prime luci sulla laguna, Ca' d'Oro, Mosaico, Rosso veneziano, Fantasia veneziana, Invito alla danza. The French *L'Odyssée de Venise* varies; the present official website *Odissea Veneziana* page lists **14 different titles**, chiefly the *Casanova* group, so its current stream must NOT override the 1984 LP. The similarly titled 1985 German *Odissea veneziana* is a variant of *Casanova*, not a second unique 1984 album. Source: original Baby pressing listing; Italian album overview; official page; MusicBrainz chronology.

**Styles.** The album specifically introduced more substantial electronics and modern rhythmic construction relative to earlier albums; the contemporary-edition genre tags are `Electronic`, `Pop`, `Classical`, `Modern Classical`, `Disco`, and `Synth-pop`. Italian secondary album reference labels it baroque pop and easy listening; the English album entry labels it classical crossover. Preserve all terms in source-derived order but put disco and synthpop **last** as peripheral LP metadata with no evidence every track is dance/synth-pop. Electronic 3 and experimental 2 tentatively reflect documented old/new arrangement changes rather than its genre labels alone.

**Instruments.** Italian album's formation says Reverberi keyboards/synthesizers; drums and electric bass by Antonio Pomara and Giacomo Caliolo are named **uncredited** contributors and require caution. The *Tiziano* suite is specifically described as having oboe and transverse-flute solos; retain `oboe`, `flute`, `keyboards`, `synth`, `drums`, `bass`; `orchestra` reflects conducting and the named orchestral act. **No synthesizer model** is attested, and no drummer's exact drum machine is presumed. Were stricter primary credits mandatory, omit drums/bass (uncredited in contemporary original sleeve).

**Influence abstention.** Secondary encyclopaedic commentary describes a Bach BWV565 motif paraphrase in the title track and *Tiziano* as a tribute to Vivaldi. Neither meets the Fandome requirement for a musician/producer firsthand claim or an attributable qualified critic; the Bach paraphrase is also close to a quotation/adaptation. Consequently **`influenced_by` is empty**, pending authoritative interview or critique. `sounds_like` remains editorial.

**Scales.** Energy 3 / tempo 3: album spans animated sections plus the extended multi-movement *Tiziano* and measured passages. Electronic 3: explicitly described increased electronic accompaniment. Theatrical 3: composed suite and orchestral presentation (tentative). Experimental 2: deliberate electronic/baroque fusion relative to early records (tentative). Groove 2: new rhythms without an evidenced consistently dancefloor program. Other scales empty.

**Landmark=1.** Major 1984 phase stylistically and on international charts; no claim that the German *Casanova* edition is an extra original work. Evidence strength: secondary retrospective for aesthetic detail, original LP listing for contents.

## Casanova (1985) — rondoveneziano-casanova

**Original release.** 12 tracks, Baby Records BR 56081, issued in 1985. Official 12-track sequence aligns with original Italian vinyl. In Germany the title *Odissea veneziana* denotes a 14-track repackaging incorporating some original 1984 pieces; do not create a second album. Official artist webpage *Odissea Veneziana* shows the later 14-track running order and is therefore not evidence for the original *Odissea* 1984 tracks.

**Genres.** `classicalcrossover` and `easylistening` explicitly used by English album discography; `baroquepop`, `pop`, `newage` from Italian metadata. New age is a *secondary classification*, not proof of ambient electronic instrumentation; place it last. Pop-Klassik appears on a recorded original LP listing. Avoid generic classical token here without a source explicitly applying it to the **album**.

**Instruments.** The Italian album-specific personnel listing names orchestra, solo/section violin, viola, cello, double bass, harpsichord, oboe, flute, clarinet, trumpet, French horn, drums and electric bass. Reverberi specifically piano on *Preludio all'amore*, keyboards and synth. Hence use each named token (including new `frenchhorn`). The `orchestra` token groups the ensemble; `doublebass` is acoustic contrabass while `bass` is separately credited electric bass. No model or analog-tape recording process follows from the 1985 studios. Italian reference is not an original sleeve scan, so individual performance tokens remain medium strength.

**Scales.** Album track commentary contains several brisk *allegro* pieces, as well as moderate and slower sections: provisional energy 3, tempo 3. Electronic 2 is source-specific synth/keyboards amid prominently credited acoustic strings and winds. Theatrical 3 and groove 1 distinguish staged orchestral delivery from dance music. Mood, experimental and polish unfilled.

**Landmark=1.** 1985 international charting identity of album; Reverberi is producer, Giordano co-composer only. `influenced_by` empty: Casanova is a literary/historical figure, not musician.

## Rapsodia veneziana (1986) — rondoveneziano-rapsodiaveneziana

**Name and edition.** Italy's 1986 Baby Records BR 56101 *Rapsodia veneziana* has 13 tracks. Official website uses *Fantasia Veneziana* (German/Austrian 1986 title) with essentially identical sequence and music; *Lagune* is another regional reissue. All three are **one musical album**, not three separate creative records. Its opening track *Fantasia veneziana (in la maggiore)* is explicitly **not the same composition** as the 1984 *Odissea veneziana* track *Fantasia veneziana*. Source: Italian album commentary, original pressing and Bertelsmann 1986 original-label archival catalogue.

**Genres.** The Italian album entry labels baroque pop and pop; the English album entry separately calls it classical crossover and easy listening. Original Italian LP is filed Classical, French LP is specifically filed Baroque. Because genre name `baroque` is absent from `Genre`, it is a **new token** rather than mapping this original label to `baroquepop`; keep it late as a specific edition tag. One retailer files original Italian LP Neo-Classical; `neoclassical` is last because seller genre taxonomies are weak critical sources. Do not confuse the album title *Fantasia* with a separate release.

**Instruments.** Album-specific documentation credits Reverberi to keyboards, synths, piano on *Isole* and *Misteriosa Venezia*, and the Rondò orchestra. In contrast to *Casanova*, no reliable original instrument-by-instrument 1986 section personnel available: do **not** auto-copy violin/harpsichord/flute/drums from the 1985 record. Instrument column only `orchestra|keyboards|synth|piano`.

**Scales.** Individually documented tempo labels are predominantly Andante/Adagio/Moderato/Allegretto with some animated passages: conservative energy 2 and tempo 2; groove 1. Electronic 2 from attributed synth plus orchestra, not modern dance. Theatrical 3 is provisional on arranged ensemble form; mood, experimental and polish empty. No identifiable production devices or processes.

**Landmark=1.** Reached Swiss number one in its 1986 run, verified on chart archive; definitive 1986 studio program (not distinct from same-year Fantasia).

## Arabesque (1987) — rondoveneziano-arabesque

**Original vs 1987 alternative.** The original Italian LP BR 56115 has 12 tracks. Original LP and later CD differ in durations for *Affresco*, *Specchio della laguna* and *Rive e marine*. German *Misteriosa Venezia* alters sequence, includes *Cameo* / *Porta d'Oriente*, splits *Fiaba antica*, and removes some Italian LP tracks. **Do not** treat these albums/alternate mixes as a brand-new original master album ID. Current official website tracklist appears to use later timings and has an apparent misspelling *Armione* for original *Armonie* (check source-specific credit). No cross-edition duration is used as a genre/tempo proxy.

**Genres.** Classed classical crossover and easy listening in English album record, pop barocco, pop and easy listening in Italian album record. French LP's Discogs-derived retailer entry specifically categorizes Electronic, Classical, Modern Classical; use `modernclassical` whole-album descriptor and `electronic` texture last because electronic equipment appears but the musical vocabulary remains crossover. Plain `classical` is shown under broad Discogs category, not interpreted independently as a new specific stylistic marker. Genre data less rich than the early 1980s titles, hence medium row confidence.

**Instruments.** Album-specific source lists Reverberi orchestral direction/keyboards/synth and Rondò orchestra. No fully reliable separate section instrument list; thus `orchestra|keyboards|synth` only. Avoid importing piano or the 1985 *Casanova* section list. No production token is supported; Country Lane Studio evidence does not itself attest analog tape, reverb or specific gear.

**Scales.** Electronic 2 reflects the documented synth + orchestra personnel; theatrical 3 is tentative from scored ensemble format. All other scales are left blank rather than inventing track-average intensity/tempo, mood, polish, or danceability from the band's broader profile or the *Misteriosa Venezia* reissue.

**Landmark=1.** Major late-1980s original album with international versions and chart success. Tracklist mismatch across the German edition is material to any track-aware similarity computation.

## Conflicting critical/documentary claims requiring import review

1. `Odissea veneziana` official page currently **does not** match 1984 original LP. Prefer nine-track pressing + label-specific 1984 discography, not website playback sequence.
2. The catalog may be missing ALL Rondò Veneziano titles if Phase 01 not imported. The importer must add the 1980–1983 titles from Phase 01 separately; Phase 02 must **not** repeat them.
3. Historical sources frequently equate *Fantasia veneziana* with *Rapsodia veneziana* and *Misteriosa Venezia* with *Arabesque*. These are editions, not new studio work.
4. `baroque` is a new genre only because the 1986 French original LP expressly has Baroque styling. Genre token parent suggestion: `classical`; distinct from existing `baroquepop`.
5. `frenchhorn` is a new specific instrument based on the 1985 *Casanova* album-specific player listing; distinguish this from existing generic `brass`.
6. 1984 Bach paraphrase and Vivaldi homage are only described in secondary compilation commentary, without artist/producer or named expert testimony. Under strict influence rules, both stay OUT of `influenced_by`; discuss only in evidence notes.
7. 1984 uncredited bassist/drummer named by Italian album reference; original pressing scan has not independently corroborated their roles. Preserve medium confidence; omit if primary credits are obligatory.
8. Scarcity of signed reviews for these four early 1980s recordings is a material constraint. Some genres are **edition tags**, not multi-source sonic diagnoses. Electronic-vs-classical share and mood not quantified; ask reviewer to remove uncertain scale judgments if strict.
