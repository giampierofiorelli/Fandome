# Fandome Miley Cyrus — Phase 02 evidence notes

Scope: four **new** rows after Phase 01. Original-release core only; reviewed as of 26 October 2026. Numerical scales are Fandome editorial interpretations of album-specific reviews and credits, not facts quoted from a critic. Genre evidence is independently itemized in `miley_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`. A source naming an artist as a feature or a sample does not itself create an influence.

## miley-plastichearts — Plastic Hearts (2020)

**Core and edition.** Twelve studio tracks from 27 November 2020. Qobuz's 15-track version adds `Edge of Midnight (Midnight Sky Remix)` and concert recordings `Heart of Glass` and `Zombie`. Those bonuses are excluded from the production, instrumentation and album-level sonics. Sources: [Qobuz label credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/plastic-hearts-explicit-miley-cyrus/m90p0wvmgiepa), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/plastic-hearts-mw0003442465), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/nov/27/miley-cyrus-plastic-hearts-review).

**Scales.** energy **4**: forceful electric rock performances, but ballads bring mean below all-out intensity; electronic **3**: guitars/bass/live drum tracks alongside neon synth pop and programmed percussion; mood **2**: defiance, regret and breakup material; theatrical **4**: roaring rock-siren performance and power ballads; experimental **2**: wide but recognisable 1980s pop/rock idioms; polish **5**: detailed mainstream studio sheen; tempo **3**: uptempo disco alternating with slower cuts; groove **3**: dance-oriented singles do not make whole album a club set. Anchored to [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-plastic-hearts/), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/nov/27/miley-cyrus-plastic-hearts-review), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/plastic-hearts-mw0003442465).

**Instruments and production.** Qobuz tracks 1–12 credit Andrew Watt on bass/guitar/drums and keyboards; Louis Bell on keyboards and piano; Chad Smith, Homer Steinweiss and Taylor Hawkins on drums; `High` credits Ben Lester on steel guitar, Jennifer Decilveo and Leo Abrahams on acoustic guitar; `Bad Karma` features Mark Ronson synthesizers, the Picard Brothers' programming and Angel Olsen guitar; `Golden G String` has violin, viola, cello and strings with Wyatt and Haynie. `programming` is on Louis Bell, Happy Perez, Ronson collaborators and Emile Haynie; the Guardian explicitly criticizes audible Auto-Tune, supporting `autotune` (not every track). Cyrus's full-throated belted singing supports `belting`, with prominent rasp supported independently by Pitchfork and Guardian. NO fake named drum-machine model: gated drum timbre is not proof of specific hardware.

**Producer discipline.** Accept only 2020 core **Producer** or co-producer roles: Andrew Watt, Louis Bell, Mark Ronson, Andrew Wyatt, The Monsters & Strangerz, Happy Perez, Take a Daytrip, The Picard Brothers, Emile Haynie. **Do not import** `Stacy Jones`, whose producer credit in Qobuz is for excluded live covers; `Jon Bellion` and `Brandon Bost` are shown only as **additional studio producer** on relevant core tracks; Miley is executive-only here. `The Picard Brothers` have explicit Producer alongside Ronson on `Bad Karma`. [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/plastic-hearts-explicit-miley-cyrus/m90p0wvmgiepa).

**Genres and disagreement.** Core blend pop-rock/rock/glam-rock/new wave, with episodic disco, synth-pop, industrial pop and country. Pitchfork values the voice and album more than Guardian, who calls the rock guitars excessively sanitised; synthwave/adult-contemporary genre tags from AOTY are lower weight. `industrialpop` is a literal Pitchfork term, **NEW TOKEN**. `influenced_by=Britney Spears|Metallica` follows Cyrus's interview describing her forthcoming record as reflecting the musical influences in her live performances, explicitly ranging from Britney to Metallica. This supports a broad album-wide stated reference, **not** a Metallica sound-alike or use of samples; evidence is **medium**, interpretation conservative. [NME 2020 report](https://www.nme.com/news/music/miley-cyrus-says-new-album-will-be-reflective-of-who-i-am-2751601). Other names are only comparisons/collaborators. **Lyrics**: separation, autonomy and romantic/sexual honesty; [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/nov/27/miley-cyrus-plastic-hearts-review), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-plastic-hearts/).

## miley-endlesssummervacation — Endless Summer Vacation (2023)

**Core and edition.** Original twelve studio tracks March 10 2023 (Flowers through Wonder Woman). `Flowers (Demo)` is a later thirteenth on some digital configurations, **not counted** in the album-level metrics or production inventory; credited separately in Pitchfork's track listings. [Full label credits, Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-endless-summer-vacation-listen/).

**Scales.** energy **3**: relatively restrained pop and selected dancing tracks; electronic **3**: first-half band/acoustic/electric piano, increasingly programmed second half; mood **3**: sunny surface and self-possession balanced by regret; theatrical **2**: comparatively unfussy singing with occasional bigger choruses; experimental **2**: the spoken electronic detour Handstand is an outlier; polish **5**: highly finished pop recording; tempo **3**, groove **3**: disco pulse on Flowers/River offset by slower You, Jaded and Wonder Woman. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-endless-summer-vacation/) and [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/mar/10/miley-cyrus-endless-summer-vacation-review) diverge on quality, not structure.

**Instrument basis.** [Original label credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-endless-summer-vacation-listen/) explicitly show Kid Harpoon drums/bass/guitar/synth on Flowers, Tyler Johnson Wurlitzer/synth, Michael Pollack Rhodes, Rob Moose strings (violin/viola), Greg Kurstin guitars/synths/piano on Jaded, Aaron Steele drums and Josh Johnson saxophone on Rose Colored Lenses, Jamie Arentzen harmonica on Thousand Miles, Kid Harpoon synth bass on River, Keyon Harrold trumpet on Violet Chemistry, and Alex Sutton Farfisa organ on Island. NOTE: the Guardian describes a **Roland-303 acid line** on River, but the official instrumentation lists synthesizers and synth bass without a hardware ID. We **do not** insert `tb303` merely from an ear-comparison. `drumprogramming` is explicitly credited on multiple songs and `stringarrangements` on Flowers. The guide's generic `electricpiano` covers credited Rhodes and Wurlitzer; it does not assert a specific digital model.

**Producers.** Credits distinguish Miley's `Executive Producer` role throughout (excluded) from actual track producers Kid Harpoon, Tyler Johnson, Greg Kurstin, Mike WiLL Made-It, Jonny Coffer, Jay Moon, Maxx Morando, Jesse Shatkin, Max Taylor-Sheppard, BJ Burton, Jerome Williams, and Zwiffa. James Blake contributes as performer/writer, NOT a named track producer; Brandi Carlile and Sia are featuring singers, not producers. Details: [Pitchfork published credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-endless-summer-vacation-listen/).

**Genre scope.** Album-level pop/disco-pop/dance-pop/pop-rock; soul is the individual track You; ambient is a **texture** on Handstand; `contemporarysingersongwriter` mirrors AllMusic's literal `Contemporary Singer/Songwriter` category without changing it to Fandome's broader `songwriter`. **NEW GENRE**. `softrock` is **lower-strength** recurring mid-tempo style assessment, and should be removed on second review if editors require only literal source labels. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/endless-summer-vacation-mw0003900484); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-endless-summer-vacation/). **Lyrics** juxtapose self-love/independence, grief and unresolved intimacy; **influenced_by** blank: reports of Gloria Gaynor echoes are not a direct source-verified musical influence.

## miley-somethingbeautiful — Something Beautiful (2025)

**Core and edition.** The original Columbia album comprises 13 tracks, 30 May 2025. `Prelude`, two `Interlude`s and full-length tracks are all integral studio material. Do not conflate the visual companion film (or later Deluxe tracks) with separate soundtracks, remixes or releases. [Sony release](https://www.sonymusic.ca/press_release/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-something-beautiful), [Pitchfork official track credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-something-beautiful-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/).

**Scales.** energy **3**: some Hi-NRG/disco and instrumental climaxes but many ballads; electronic **3**: multiple analog synths, Fairlight and drum machines but substantial guitars, strings and live jazz players; mood **3**: glamour and release balanced by grief and emotional darkness; theatrical **4**: big orchestrations, spoken prologue, conceptual segue; experimental **4**: tape/foley/abstract opening and song structures exceed commercial pop even though later tracks are traditional; polish **4**: elaborate hi-fidelity arrangements preserve grit/distortion; tempo **3**, groove **3**: kinetic disco sequence balances slow extended work. Sources [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-something-beautiful/), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2025/may/29/miley-cyrus-something-beautiful-review), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/miley-cyrus-something-beautiful-review-3865945).

**Instrument confirmation.** Track-level label credits name Maxx Morando drums/bass/electric guitar and *Pulsar-23* drum machine; Jonathan Rado keyboards, Mellotron, piano, EMS Synthi, Wurlitzer, CS-80, vibraphone and tape loops; Pino Palladino bass and fretless; Adam Granduciel and Brittany Howard electric guitar; multiple saxophonists; string orchestra (violin, viola, cello); Farfisa/organ, clavinet; Sara Watkins on fiddle (represented by `violin`), Matt Pynn pedal steel (token `steelguitar`), Kenny Segal Omnichord, and an explicitly credited **Marxophone** on More to Lose. NEW INSTRUMENTS `vibraphone`, `omnichord`, `marxophone` (do not silently replace with a genre-inferred instrument). [Pitchfork complete liner data](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-something-beautiful-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/).

**Production equipment and techniques.** Official credits on Prelude specify **Fairlight CMI**, **Moog System 55**, **EMS Synthi**, **MS-20**, **Minimoog**, **ARP Solina String Ensemble**, and **OB-6**, plus synthesis and drum programming. Later credits specify **Prophet-5**, **Juno-60**, **TB-303 bass programming**, **Pulsar-23**, **PERKONS HD-01**, **Modor NF-1**, **Lexicon Prime Time**. `fairlight`, `moog`, `korgms20`, `minimoog`, `yamahacs80`, `arpstringensemble`, `prophet5`, `juno60`, `tb303` are current allowed tokens and supported by the credited model descriptions. New exact technical tokens `pulsar23`, `perkonshd01`, `modornf1`, `lexiconprimetime` should be added; they are models, not categories. **Caveat**: `ems` and `emssynthia`/`emssynthiaks` are not conflated, because source just says 'EMS Synthi' without model variant; no EMS-specific token is assigned. Likewise OB-6 is credited but not mapped to `oberheim`/a specific model token without a vocabulary decision. Also `sampling` is justified by the Marie Davidson / Soulwax remix sample on Every Girl You've Ever Loved, **not** an `influenced_by` claim. Tape operator/tape-loop and foley credits underpin `analogtape|tapeeffects|sonicmanipulation`. Distortion/processed vocals on opening tracks are critic-described. `stringarrangements` supported by named orchestrators and arrangers. [Pitchfork credit document](https://pitchfork.com/news/miley-cyrus-releases-new-album-something-beautiful-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/).

**Producers discipline.** Named **Producer** across original 13 tracks only: Miley Cyrus, Shawn Everett, Jonathan Rado, Michael Pollack, Maxx Morando, Max Taylor-Sheppard, Molly Rankin, Alec O’Hanley, BJ Burton, Ian Gold, Ethan Shevin, Kid Harpoon, Tyler Johnson. Brittany Howard is credited **Additional Production** on Walk of Fame and is excluded; Kenny Segal has **Additional Production** on Pretend You're God, likewise excluded. Executive title alone is not the basis for including Cyrus/Everett: actual Producer credits separately confirm both. Full citation above.

**Genre discrepancies and influences.** AOTY categorizes neo-psychedelia and electro-disco; Guardian disputes the claim that record is authentically psychedelic, so `neopsychedelia` is deliberately low-ranked/texture, not treated as prevailing consensus. `discorock` is NME's exact term for Walk of Fame, a **NEW GENRE**. `showtunes` / `spaghettiwestern` track and texture references, not a score or show album; `psychedelicsoul` and `experimentalrock` reflect a separately named title-track style, scoped to that track. **Pink Floyd is the only documented musical artist influence**: Cyrus directly names *The Wall* as a model for the combined musical/visual undertaking in [Harper's Bazaar](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/culture/art-books-music/a62870734/miley-cyrus-new-music-interview-2024/?os=ioxa42gdub5u1enqic0). *Mandy* is a film influence, so not added. Beyoncé's *Cowboy Carter* had been a candidate destination for an early version of Easy Lover; writing for a possible outside album is not automatically a documented musical influence for this one.

## miley-basspersuades — Bass Persuades (2026)

**Identity, dates and edition.** Released 18 September 2026 via Atlantic as **Miley**, the tenth studio album. Four rows retain `artist=Miley Cyrus` to match Fandome's canonical artist ID `miley`, but `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` and catalog audit preserve exact 2026 billing. **Ten tracks = nine original songs + final Model/Actriz remix** of title track. Since it was issued within the original studio album sequence, count the album once, do not also create a separate `remix` album/second row. No lives or compilations. [Warner announcement](https://www.warnermusic.ie/news/2026/miley-unveils-new-single-and-video-bass-persuades-new-album-drops-septemeber-18th/), [Pitchfork release-day full credits](https://pitchfork.com/story/read-the-full-credits-to-miley-cyrus-bass-persuades/).

**Scales.** energy **3**: strong opener/industrial cuts but many ballads; electronic **4**: synthesizers, programmed drums and bass foregrounded; mood **3**: self-assurance and romance alongside sadness and family distress; theatrical **3**: big choruses with quieter phrasing; experimental **2**: isolated industrial/waltz peculiarities in generally conventional song forms; polish **5**: high-control pop production; tempo **3**, groove **3**: advertising dance does not make a front-to-back club album when roughly half of tracks are ballads. [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/miley-cyrus-bass-persuades-review-3970066), [Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/miley-cyrus-makes-a-charismatic-break-with-the-loved-up-bass-persuades), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/miley-cyrus-bass-persuades/).

**Instrument and production documentation.** [Pitchfork's 10-track credits](https://pitchfork.com/story/read-the-full-credits-to-miley-cyrus-bass-persuades/) list Kid Harpoon and Maxx Morando synths/guitars/bass/drum programming; Jack Wetmore guitars, Ruben Radlauer live drums/percussion, James King sax, claps on the opener, organ and synth bass with Andrew Wyatt on Neon Signs, and Mellotron, Wurlitzer electric piano, harp, and *dulcimer guitar* on Orbit. Strings arranger Davide Rossi is named on REDLIGHTS; [independent review](https://www.stilllisteningmagazine.com/reviews/miley-cyrus-bass-persuades-review) hears strings there. `dulcimerguitar` is a **NEW INSTRUMENT**; do not mislabel it as generic dulcimer. `moog` supported by explicit Bass Moog credit on Orbit (source does not specify exact model), not by inference. `remixing` is evidenced by the closing track, Model/Actriz credited as remixer. No firm Auto-Tune, specific DAW, named vintage drum machine, sampling or vocoder claims: robotic vocal sheen in a review is not hardware proof.

**Producers.** Actual producer/co-producer names from release-day full credits: Miley Cyrus, Kid Harpoon, Michael Pollack, Maxx Morando, Model/Actriz, Monsterz & Strangerz, Gregory Aldae Hein, Griff Clawson. **Do not include** Andrew Wyatt, named only **Additional Production** for Neon Signs; Model/Actriz is a **full Producer** on Different Religion but only Additional Production / Remixer elsewhere. Guest Wayne Coyne on Aftermath is not credited Producer. TIDAL's Orbit credit list does not exactly match Pitchfork (which includes Kid Harpoon as producer), but the album-wide Kid Harpoon role is unequivocal across other tracks; preserve source-level difference. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/story/read-the-full-credits-to-miley-cyrus-bass-persuades/), [TIDAL](https://tidal.com/album/561763543/credits).

**Genre and criticism.** Primary pop/pop-rock/dance-pop, secondary soft rock and electropop, with industrial electronica/dance-punk on Different Religion. Pitchfork's disco-pop and glam-rock framing contrasts with NME's industrial and dancefloor enthusiasm; Slant and Best Fit also disagree how well electronic beats fit romantic ballads. New `dancepunk` is literal Best Fit description; lower-volume `dreampop` and `waltz` apply to only one song each. `influenced_by=Cocteau Twins|Fleetwood Mac` is attributable *only to Hot Press critic characterising Let's Get Married*: Cocteau Twins inspiration and Fleetwood Mac-inspired harmonies, **not** to an artist interview or a whole-album intention. Lower that field's confidence if stricter musicological provenance is needed; these references also reside in editorial `sounds_like`. [Hot Press](https://www.hotpress.com/music/album-review-miley-bass-persuades-23162173). **Lyrics** include romance, family crisis and a title-track complaint about disengaged youth; [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/miley-cyrus-bass-persuades-review-3970066), [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/miley-cyrus-bass-persuades-album-review/).
