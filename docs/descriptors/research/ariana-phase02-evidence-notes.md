Sources have stable keys in `ariana_phase02_sources.csv`; genre scopes in the separate genre-evidence CSV. ## `ariana-thankunext` — *thank u, next* (2019)

**Identity and edition:** 12-song 2019 original studio album [T_AM,T_Q]. Not a subsequent clean/deluxe variant. Fandome's `ariana` prefix derives from the existing album catalogue.

**Musical structure:** Pitchfork locates most of the record in pop/R&B, with clipped rap-like passages, group-vocal writing, a trap-informed low end and brighter pop refrains. Guardian expressly hears trap-pop on “break up with your girlfriend, i’m bored”, and dancehall in “bloodline”; neither should imply that all twelve tracks have these identities [T_PF,T_PFT,T_G]. “ghostin” is atmospheric, but no qualified source independently verified ambient as a genre, so it is deliberately not tagged ambient [T_PF]. AllMusic explicitly applies dance-pop to the album [T_AM].

**Scales:** energy **3**: quick rhythmic peaks offset slower vulnerable songs. electronic **4**: synth/drum-machine/processed beat core with some strings and live horn colors [T_MB]. mood **2**: breakup, regret, grief and defiance coexist, with sadness beneath confident singles. theatrical **2**: mostly intimate narrative, not stage-belting throughout. experimental **2**: hip-hop-tinted textures within mainstream songcraft. polish **5**: tightly compressed, high-detail radio production. tempo **3**: midtempo base with faster club tracks. groove **3**: recurring trap and pop-dance beats alongside ballads [T_PF,T_PFT,T_Q]. These values are comparative, not BPM measurements.

**Vocal and lyrical values:** clear|soft|powerful|rap describes varied album delivery including brief half-rapped passages (“7 rings”), not equal prevalence. The writing spans heartbreak, introspection, identity/self-affirmation, relationships and occasional sensuality [T_PF,T_PFT,T_G]. English lead vocals. The topical half-rap should not be mistaken for uniformly rapped vocals.

**Production:** Original music producers named across Qobuz credits: Tommy Brown, Max Martin, ILYA, Pop Wansel, Happy Perez, Charles Anderson, Michael Foster and Brian Baptiste; MusicBrainz credits NOVA Wav as co-producer on “in my head” [T_Q,T_QE,T_MB]. **Ariana Grande, Victoria Monét and Tayla Parx are credited as vocal or additional producers on some sources, but those are not interchangeable with the main/co-producer field.** Therefore excluded pending manual role adjudication, not mistakenly called mixers. Serban Ghenea is a mixer, not a producer here.

**Similarity vs influence:** The-Dream, Beyoncé, Kacey Musgraves come from qualified critic comparisons in [T_PF]. None is listed as a documented causal influence on the *whole* album; `influenced_by` intentionally blank. Songwriting coauthors are not producers by default.

**Disagreement:** Pitchfork weighs the spontaneous, emotionally direct feel positively but also recognizes rough lyrical spots [T_PF]. AllMusic documents the quick turnaround [T_AM], while The Guardian praises sonic coherence and others question the mixed themes [T_G]. These are competing appraisals, not measurement inconsistencies. Landmark=1 for the album's prominence in her core body of work, not an implication of universal acclaim.

## `ariana-positions` — *Positions* (2020)

**Identity and edition:** Original 14-track 2020 studio album [P_AM,P_Q,P_MB]; not the 19-track deluxe reissue used only for credit crosschecking on original tracks [P_QD].

**Musical structure:** Whole album leans towards smooth, 1990s-shaped R&B with trap production and strings. Guardian hears neo-soul on “my hair”, disco/new jack swing specifically in “love language”, and an early-2000s Craig David-style garage reference on “positions” [P_G]; another Guardian critic describes house rhythms specifically in “motive” [P_G2]. They are kept lower in genre order and scope rather than weighted as dominant genres. AllMusic retains pop and dance-pop tags [P_AM], while Pitchfork emphasizes muted, sensual songs over dance-floor bombast [P_PF].

**Scales:** energy **2**, largely cool and unhurried; electronic **3**, drum programming and hazy synths integrated with string/harp and voice; mood **3**, playful intimacy undercut by unease after grief; theatrical **2**, often quietly voiced; experimental **1**, smooth conventional song forms; polish **5**, precise layering, sparkling vocal harmonies and polished textures; tempo **2**, predominantly slow-to-mid; groove **3**, rhythmically engaged but not sustained club dance [P_PF,P_G,P_NME,P_Q].

**Vocal and lyrical values:** soft|clear|powerful. Soft layered leads dominate but strong singing appears on ballads; English. Sensuality, love, fear of getting hurt, heartbreak/recovery are central [P_PF,P_G]. Lyrics only use allowed concept tokens.

**Production:** The list aggregates track-level music-producer and co-producer credits from original master sources [P_Q,P_MB]. Includes lesser-known named producers, not only Tommy Brown and Mr. Franks. **Role caveat:** MusicBrainz includes Ariana as 'producer' for several tracks whereas Qobuz labels her vocal producer/arranger; held out from the CSV until the music-production role is resolved [P_MB,P_Q]. Likewise Peter Lee Johnson is credited as strings, not automatically producer. No cap on the number of *verified* contributors; appearance in a writing credit alone does not qualify. CLAUDE should compare each name to track roles before merging.

**Similarity:** Brownstone and Craig David are critic sonic references [P_G]; they are not documented production influences. `influenced_by` blank. Guardian argues the sound is attractively effortless but safe [P_G]; Pitchfork detects an emotional search for trust [P_PF]; NME finds high points beside relatively indistinct songs [P_NME]. The relatively lower experimental value is a production assessment, not a quality score. Landmark=0 is an editorial 'artist-defining' threshold rather than a judgment of merit.

## `ariana-eternalsunshine` — *Eternal Sunshine* (2024)

**Identity:** 13-track *original March 2024 edition*, not any 2024/2025 deluxe, remix, live, or “Brighter Days Ahead” edition [E_AM,E_Q]. Existing catalogue slug preserves title capitalization in display only.

**Styles and weighting:** Most of the album is subdued pop/R&B, with synth-heavy choruses; explicit AllMusic 'Contemporary R&B' and 'Dance-Pop' classifications [E_AM]. Pitchfork names “bye” as orchestral disco, “imperfect for you” alt-rock strum; its wider critique identifies midtempo R&B [E_PF]. Pitchfork's separate analysis finds trap hi-hats, synth-pop on “supernatural”, Robyn-like Europop on “we can't be friends” [E_PFT]. NME and Harvard Crimson identify a *single* house/ballroom number, “yes, and?” [E_NME,E_HARV]. These narrower genres remain marked `track_only` or `substantial`, not false album-wide dominance. `synthpop` follows Pitchfork’s literal synth-pop term; do not silently convert it to `electropop`, a separate Fandome token [E_PFT]. `alternativerock` is only a track-specific alt-rock strum, not the album’s main sound [E_PF].

**Scales:** energy **2**, majority midtempo and few dance-track peaks; electronic **3**, programmed rhythm and shimmering synths offset by guitar and strings; mood **2**, wounded but increasingly hopeful; theatrical **2**, soft focused performance instead of sustained belting; experimental **2**, coherent pop-R&B with song-level stylistic detours; polish **5**, highly detailed engineered vocals and clean cross-track transitions; tempo **2**, slow-to-mid album average; groove **3**, selected strong dance beats amid slower pieces [E_PF,E_G,E_Q,E_FADER].

**Themes, voice and connections:** soft|clear|powerful for predominantly soft interwoven harmonies and occasional climaxes. Heartbreak, self-reflection, love, reputation/identity and sensuality are traced in reviews [E_PF,E_G,E_FADER]. English. `sounds_like` includes Robyn, Imogen Heap and (track-specific) Brandy and Monica [E_PF,E_PFT,E_AP]. `influenced_by` **Brandy|Monica** is specifically qualified by Associated Press as the 1998 duet underpinning the reimagined “the boy is mine” [E_AP]; that is *track-scoped*, not an invented influence on all 13 songs. The 2004 Michel Gondry film is a verified conceptual inspiration [E_PFT], but it is not an artist/producer so is deliberately excluded from the `influenced_by` field. Other sonic comparisons do not become causal influences.

**Production:** Original Qobuz per-track Producer labels include Ariana Grande, Max Martin, ILYA, Shintaro Yasuda, DaviDior, Nick Lee, Aaron Paris, Oscar Görres, Will Loftis and Luka Kloser [E_Q]. Peter Kahm appears as **composer only** in the original Qobuz credits (e.g. “imperfect for you”), so is excluded even though some aggregated credit lists call him producer. All contributors are track-level; not co-producers of every song. In particular Serban Ghenea is a mix engineer.

**Disagreement:** Pitchfork calls the sound slightly scattered and particular genre digressions less persuasive [E_PF]; NME and The Guardian praise the more controlled, subtle release [E_NME,E_G]. Not a reason to remove attested genres. Landmark=1 for artistic visibility, not universal approval.

## `ariana-wickedthesoundtrack` — *Wicked: The Soundtrack* (2024)

**Eligibility / catalog:** Republic/Verve's original 22 Nov 2024 *film songs* album has major billed performances from Ariana Grande **and Cynthia Erivo**, plus numerous cast members. Music is integral to the film's adaptation, so qualifies under the user rule for notable soundtracks and collaborations [W_UNI,W_AM,W_REP,W_UIT]. The row `artist` must remain `Ariana Grande` because this is a per-artist descriptor pipeline; preserve original multi-artist billing in catalogue UI / credits rather than attributing every sung track to Grande. If Fandome later models collaborative albums natively, link as a shared album (avoid duplicate recording IDs). **It is not the separately issued instrumental score album, a sing-along version, a 2025 commentary release, or Wicked: For Good.**

**Genres:** Soundtrack and musical theatre are album-wide direct classifications [W_AM,W_UNI]. `showtunes` is supported by the original Broadway numbers repurposed for film [W_SCH,W_AM]. New token `musicaltheatre` is the normalized exact style (Qobuz catalog 'Musical Theatre'; AllMusic 'Musicals'); Claude must add it to scenes, genre-family hierarchy and validator. The orchestral playing is a *texture*, so `classical` is not assumed as the album's genre. `opera` would also be misleading given occasional almost-operatic vocal technique.

**Scales:** energy **3** (energetic ensemble climaxes versus reflective solos), electronic **1** (large live orchestra and voices, with supporting keyboards/programmed pre-production), mood **3** (humor/triumph mixed with exclusion/conflict), theatrical **5** (fully staged narrative, emphatic duet/ensemble vocals), experimental **1** (traditional Broadway musical song structure), polish **5** (dense arranged live/orchestral and studio/post production), tempo **3** (mix of brisk set pieces and restrained songs), groove **1** (rhythm exists but songs serve theatrical scenes rather than sustained dancing) [W_AM,W_MIX,W_UIT,W_SCH].

**Voices and themes:** clear/powerful vocal alternation from Grande and Erivo, soft exceptions, substantial full-cast choral passages [W_AM,W_REP]. Narrative storytelling, social exclusion/identity, love/friendship, reflection (Glinda and Elphaba) based on original composer's synopsis [W_SCH]. All English.

**Production:** Official Republic/Universal song-by-song producer credits name **Stephen Schwartz, Stephen Oremus, Greg Wells** [W_UIT,W_Q]. Film Music Reporter calls Oremus executive music producer and Wells music producer, while MusicBrainz and label track masters list Oremus as a producer too [W_FILM,W_MB]. The `producers` column uses *the actual soundtrack recording's Producer credits* (all three), not simply feature names. The more general film roles are documented separately. Grande and Erivo are vocal producers, not thereby full-album music producers. Stephen Schwartz is composer and also **credited producer**, which meets the inclusion rule; his composer role alone would not suffice.

**Similarity vs influence:** Idol/precedent voices Idina Menzel and Kristin Chenoweth are heard through original theatrical roles [W_AM]; sonic comparison, **not** direct artistic influence on this specific recording. `influenced_by` empty. There are no phantom performer/genre values for film score cues outside the 2024 songs album. Disagreement: AllMusic is positive about the performances but describes exposition-heavy songs and irregular melodies [W_AM]; the original Broadway theatrical form is a framework, not an experimentation score.
