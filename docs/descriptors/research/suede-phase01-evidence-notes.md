# Suede — Phase 01 evidence notes

Four proposed album-level descriptors, 0–5 integer scales. The numbers are **editorial interpretations** of sourced descriptions; do not mistake a reviewer's adjective for a quantitative measurement. Genres retain a separate provenance row for every entry.

## 1. Suede (1993) — `suede-suede`

**Core:** glam and early Britpop, along with AllMusic's Neo-Glam, Alternative Pop/Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock and general Pop/Rock tags. AOTY also lists neo-psychedelia. Britpop is a retrospective cultural tag, not a claim the 1993 album sounds like every subsequent Britpop act. **Sound:** Bernard Butler guitar/piano with Mat Osman bass, Simon Gilbert drums; Ed Buller's keys and synths, sax, strings, percussion. Debut label credits and archival liner transcription specifically document the string arrangement for **Sleeping Pills**; this does *not* imply symphonic production on every track.

**Scales:** energy **4** (uptempo glam stomp, aggressive guitar hooks balanced with quieter tracks); electronic **1** (predominantly guitar band despite credited synth/keyboards); mood **2** (swagger offset by troubled lyrics and ballads); theatrical **4** (conspicuously performative Anderson delivery); experimental **2** (striking presentation, conventional songs); polish **4** (Ed Buller well-crafted studio production); tempo **3** (mixed brisk and slower material); groove **2** (stomping rhythm but not dance-focused). `voice=powerful|clear|soft` draws on Anderson's emphatic expressive singing and softer passages, **not** a claim of studio belting technique.

**Lyrics:** desire/androgyny, outsiders, working-class or urban stories, relationships. See contemporary Independent and Washington Post interviews and Guardian retrospective. **Influences:** Bowie and the Smiths are supported in historical criticism and the account of the band's origins, but this is not evidence of direct sample or compositional borrowing. `sounds_like` is editorial.

**Source disagreement:** The Independent's Andy Gill expressed strong skepticism about the debut, whereas AllMusic later praised it. `confidence=high` refers to factual/sonic evidence, not unanimity of critical opinion. Label site gives 30 March; AllMusic gives 29 March; the **1993 year is undisputed**.

## 2. Dog Man Star (1994) — `suede-dogmanstar`

**Core:** art rock, glam rock, long-form progressive tendencies, dark neo-psychedelic arrangements, alongside AllMusic's alternative styles/Neo-Glam. This is an **album with expanded orchestration**, not an all-electronic or dance album. The original Nude liner credits Ed Buller as sole producer, Bernard Butler on guitar, Sinfonia of London, Brian Gascoigne as arranger/conductor, plus flute, cimbalom, ba-wu, sax, trumpet and trombone. Orchestra/strings are specifically concentrated in the large arrangements, especially **Still Life**. No specific synthesizer or DAW model is claimed without a source.

**Scales:** energy **3** (dynamic peaks amid slow, drawn-out ballads); electronic **1** (acoustic/orchestral and amplified rock sources); mood **1** (dark, paranoid, emotionally strained, per AllMusic); theatrical **5** (grandiose orchestral melodrama); experimental **4** (extended structures and unfamiliar timbres); polish **4** (elaborate studio arrangement); tempo **2** (many stately tracks); groove **1** (low dance emphasis).

**Lyrics:** heartbreak, seclusion, alienation, striking characters; review explicitly says nearly every track is troubled and heartbroken. **Influences:** Scott Walker and Bowie are explicitly discussed by critic Erlewine, particularly as comparisons to *The Wild Ones*, *Still Life* and *New Generation*. Treat these as **critic-documented influences, not specific soundalike covers**. This is more introspective than the label Britpop might imply.

Sources: https://nuderecordlabel.com/release/dog-man-star/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/dog-man-star-r207781/review ; https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/5570-suede-dog-man-star.php ; https://www.theinsatiableones.co.uk/discography/dog-man-star .

## 3. Coming Up (1996) — `suede-comingup`

**Core:** overtly melodic Britpop, glam and power pop; AOTY includes neo-psychedelia and noise pop as secondary genres. **Do not extrapolate noise pop to every ballad**. Original credits: Richard Oakes guitar, Neil Codling keys, Ed Buller producer, Craig Armstrong strings arranger. Pitchfork's retrospective discusses guitar/synth-led balance, so synth is included. Mixing credit Dave Bascombe is **not** production credit.

**Scales:** energy **4** (numerous brisk hooky songs); electronic **2** (synth/keyboards integrated into live-band sound); mood **3** (overall brisk euphoria/defiance counterbalanced by melancholy ballads); theatrical **4** (Brett Anderson extroverted glam-pop delivery); experimental **1** (direct radio-ready forms after previous record); polish **4** (focused sheen); tempo **4** (brisker than predecessor); groove **3** (more regular, bouncy pulse but still rock-led).

**Lyrics:** pride in outsider identity (Trash), romance, decadence and urban characters, with more accessible mood. **Influences:** Bowie comparison is specific and critically described, but the *sounds_like* field remains a separate editorial judgment.

**Release confusion:** *Coming Up* UK release **2 Sep 1996** on Nude. AllMusic also displays an **8 Apr 1997** North American release entry: use original first issue, **1996**.

Sources: https://nuderecordlabel.com/release/coming-up/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/coming-up-mw0000019706 ; https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/5571-suede-coming-up.php ; https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15571-coming-up-deluxe-edition-head-music-deluxe-edition-a-new-morning-deluxe-edition/ ; https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/rock-1.83514 ; https://www.salon.com/1997/04/18/sharps970418/ .

## 4. Head Music (1999) — `suede-headmusic`

**Core:** Britpop/glam identity reshaped by Steve Osborne's electronic and groove-based production. Reviewers disagree whether this change is fundamental or largely cosmetic: NME interview documents new **songwriting through drum machines**, AllMusic notes analog synths and drum machines, The Independent finds smoother grooves, while South China Morning Post hears both loops and persistent glam guitars. AOTY indexes art rock and electronic. Do not label the entire album techno, dance, trip-hop or synthpop from production alone.

**Scales:** energy **3** (mixed upbeat and slower tracks); electronic **3** (substantial synth and machine rhythm layers alongside guitars/drums); mood **2** (cooler/alienated lyricism with occasional bright choruses); theatrical **4** (continued character-driven expressive vocals); experimental **3** (different writing/production processes, still conventional songs); polish **4** (Osborne's smooth finish); tempo **3** (mixed); groove **3** (more deliberate beat-driven songwriting, not a dance album).

**Instruments:** source-credited Richard Oakes guitar, Mat Osman bass, Simon Gilbert drums, Neil Codling synthesizer/keyboards; contemporary interview establishes drum machines. **Production token `drumprogramming`** used on direct account of the songs developed via drum machines. Specific synth makes/models, drum-machine models, effects chains and DAWs remain **unknown**, not filled from era/style. Recorded loops do not alone prove sampling; do not add `sampling` without original evidence.

**Lyrics:** contemporary review describes sexuality, drug-fuelled hedonism and alienated urban characters. `influenced_by` **blank**: Slade is a *track-specific* stylistic comparison, not reliable evidence of direct album-wide artistic influence. `sounds_like` editorial, not historical attribution.

## Cross-album comparison

The key recommendation distinction is **1993/96 guitar-led glam pop** vs. **1994 theatrical and dark art rock** vs. **1999 rhythm-programmed electronic sheen**. An electronic rating does not equate to a dance genre, nor does an orchestral instrument automatically imply classical genre. The eight scale values are subjective but each is explained above.
