# Kim Carnes — Phase 03, research and evidence notes

Method: original release and label/discography for dates and credits; liner-note transcriptions for instrumentation; AllMusic for genre and critical description; interview testimony where available. Descriptor numbers (0–5) and `sounds_like` are original editorial interpretations. `influenced_by` remains blank where direct musical influence for the particular album is not documented.

## Barking at Airplanes (1985) — `kimcarnes-barkingatairplanes`

- **Release and editions:** 1985 studio LP, 10 original tracks. CD reissues contain bonus remixes and other performances that are **not** a second album. Release-date sources differ between 29 May and June 1985; both agree on 1985. [B1, B7]
- **Production:** co-producers Kim Carnes, Bill Cuomo for nine tracks and Duane Hitchings for “Abadabadango”. Mike Shipley was a mixer, not producer. AllMusic and sleeve credits support the three album-level producer names. [B1–B4]
- **Genres:** AllMusic's album-wide pop-rock, adult contemporary and soft-rock classifications plus its review of post-new-wave elements, arena-rock guitars and dance. `newwave` is only some songs, and `arenarock` a texture. Do not flatten this into a fully programmed synth-pop album. [B1]
- **Instruments:** Cuomo credited on synths, Chroma, DX7, Kurzweil and drum programming; Craig Krampf on acoustic drums and programmed parts. Also credited: Waddy Wachtel, Lindsey Buckingham, Ry Cooder and others on guitars; saxophone (Jerry Peterson), steel drums (Michael Rogers) on one song, percussion and multiple bassists. `dx7` is the precise documented model, but *Kurzweil* is an unidentified model: no `kurzweilk2000` token. [B2, B4, B6]
- **Technique:** `drumprogramming` and `dx7` are verified. In a later interview, Carnes remembers a Prophet family instrument and prefers tape warmth but does **not** securely identify the precise Prophet model or unequivocally say this record was tracked analog: do not add `prophet5` or `analogtape`. [B3]
- **0–5 editorial scales:** energy 4, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 3, polish 5, tempo 4, groove 4. Anxious hit and spacious ballads make mood darker than mere brightness of synth timbres.
- **Influences:** Carnes praises The Cars' records in the 2020 interview but mentions the wish to use Mike Shipley, who engineered/mixed them; this is a production aesthetic reference, not enough to claim the band musically shaped the entire record. The Cars appear only as a sonic comparison in `sounds_like`.

## Light House (1986) — `kimcarnes-lighthouse`

- **Release and producer:** original May 1986 Capitol LP, 10 tracks, produced by Val Garay; the digital album credits also assign production to Garay. The 2014 *Lighthouse* CD spelling is a reissue variant, **not** a separate album. [L1, L3, L4]
- **Genres:** AllMusic lists adult contemporary and contemporary pop-rock / soft rock. `pop` and `rock` are broad coverage; unlike earlier 1982 work, no unsupported whole-album `newwave` or `synthpop` designation. [L1]
- **Instruments and production:** original credits place Steve Goldstein on keyboards, keyboard bass and *Linn 9000 programming*, with synths by Kathy Kurasch, Randy Edelman, Eric Kaz and Lauren Wood on specific songs. Craig Krampf plays drums/percussion, guitarists include Waddy Wachtel and Craig Hull, plus bass, sax and J.D. Steele Singers on “Dancin' at the Lighthouse”. `linn9000` and `drumprogramming` are documented but not every track is a drum-machine recording. Keyboard bass is credited; do not convert it into a specific synthesizer model or infer a separate synthesizer-bass instrument. [L1, L2, L4]
- **0–5 scales:** energy 3, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 1, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 3. Vocals combine powerful and husky lines, softer ballads and choral backing on some tracks.
- **Influences:** writers of covered songs are not direct recorded influences; `influenced_by` blank.

## View from the House (1988) — `kimcarnes-viewfromthehouse`

- **Original:** 25 July 1988 MCA studio LP. 10 songs, not the later expanded bonus-track CD. Original release is 1988; country and adult-contemporary genres are independently documented. [V1, V4]
- **Production:** Kim Carnes and Jimmy Bowen co-produce; Bill Cuomo is an actual *additional producer on final track “Crimes of the Heart”*, not co-producer of the whole ten-song session. Including him in album producers records this track-level production, with this explicit qualification. [V1–V3]
- **Rationale:** Carnes wanted *live interaction between Nashville musicians*, moving away from LA computer-led sessions. Credited Nashville players include fiddle and mandolin by Mark O'Connor, accordions by Bruce Hornsby and John Cascella, John Jarvis piano, Kirk Johnson harmonica, electric and acoustic guitars, Craig Krampf's drums/washboard, bass and backing vocals. A small amount of synthesizer playing and drum programming still exists, especially last track, therefore `electronic` = 1 rather than 0. `overdubbing` explicitly credited on original sleeve notes, not used to contradict live tracking. [V1–V3]
- **Genre scopes:** country and adult contemporary whole album, country-rock and folk-rock crossover only some songs. Note source calls this a return to folk/country; `folkrock` is a *contextual sonic editorial interpretation* not a documented album marketing tag; re-evaluate under strict source genre tests. [V5, V6]
- **0–5 scales:** energy 3, electronic 1, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. Organic ensemble emphasis, generally warm understated performance; serious content including 'Blood from the Bandit'.
- **Influences:** Carnes specifically credits Rosanne Cash's *Seven Year Ache* with drawing her to Nashville. This is documented career-context inspiration, but does not prove specific musical derivation of the album; `sounds_like` includes her as editorial proximity, `influenced_by` remains blank. Covering John Prine songs does not by itself establish influence.

## Checkin' Out the Ghosts (1991) — `kimcarnes-checkinouttheghosts`

- **Original:** first issued only in Japan on 21 March 1991, Zebrazone (TECP-28767), 10 studio recordings. *Gypsy Honeymoon* later appeared as the title of a 1993 **compilation**; that is not a separate studio-album phase entry. “Hangin' On by a Thread” is a new recording of an older Carnes song, but this 1991 album contains mainly unique studio performances and is not a compilation. [G3, G4, G6]
- **Producers:** Joey Carbone credited on all ten songs; Kim Carnes on tracks 1–6 and 8–10. “Independent Girl” is Carbone-only; Fumihiro Ishikawa executive producer is excluded from album `producers`. [G2, G4]
- **Genres:** AllMusic assigns soft rock, adult contemporary and contemporary pop-rock. Broad `pop`/`rock` categories support the rounded picture without assuming it is club dance. [G1, G2]
- **Instrumentation:** session credits name Steve Goldstein keyboards/synths, Michael Thompson electric guitars, Dennis Belfield bass, Carlos Vega drums, Joey Carbone tambourine and keyboard, programming by Collin Ellingson and Mike Tavera on some tracks, Roger Lebow cello on “Hangin’”, Steve Madaio trumpet on title song, and chorus on “Look Through Children's Eyes”. `synthprogramming` is shorthand for specified keyboard and guitar programming; no particular hardware model credited. [G2, G4]
- **0–5 scales:** energy 3, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. Live rhythm section dominates, but selective programming matters. `confidence=medium` due sparse independent reviews.
- **Influences:** Thom Bell and Linda Creed wrote the song covered on this album; do **not** place them in `influenced_by` simply because it is covered. The resemblance to West Coast session pop is a comparison only.

## QA and limitations

- Excludes earlier Kim Carnes phases (1971–1983) and all live, compilation and remix albums. `Chasin' Wild Trains` (2004) remains for a final phase.
- Only original credited producers appear in `producers`, including track-level producer with qualification in these notes.
- `sounds_like` is original editorial similarity and should not be treated as a statement of influence, endorsement or direct artist quote.
- `folkrock` for the 1988 album is less securely documented as a **genre label** than the primary AllMusic/record-label categories; keep low in order or remove under stricter source-only rules.
