# Cameo — Phase 01 evidence and descriptor reasoning

Research: 28 October 2026. One row per original 1977–79 album; critical evaluation of each album, not application of all-band clichés. Every `confidence`, score and descriptive comparison refers to this particular LP unless explicitly identified as period-wide/contextual.

## 1. `cameo-cardiacarrest` — *Cardiac Arrest* (1977)

**Sources:** Alex Henderson, *AllMusic*, https://www.allmusic.com/album/cardiac-arrest-mw0000112836; specialist album/sessions history https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/; detailed credited instruments https://audio-music.info/htm/c/Cameo_Cardiac_Arrest_and_We_All_Know_Who_We_Are.htm; original producer listing https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/cardiac_arrest; early Cameo critical comparison https://www.allmusic.com/artist/cameo-mn0000944349.

**Genres:** `funk` carries the album: aggressive, tightly played, horn-stabbed grooves ('Rigor Mortis', 'Funk Funk', 'Post Mortem'). `rnb` is the AllMusic classification; `soul` and `quietstorm` reflect the slower soul songs, particularly 'Stay By My Side'. `discosoul` is restricted to the 'Find My Way' crossover track (AllMusic review specifically names disco-soul). Do not represent the entire album as disco.

**Scales:** energy **4** (majority hard-driving funk, but with slow songs); electronic **1** (real rhythm section and horns predominate although Gregory Johnson's synthesizer is in credits); mood **3** (exuberant fast songs versus romantic slower songs); theatrical **2** (assertive lead and group interplay, not staged/operatic production); experimental **1** (recognizable funk-song and extended grooves); polish **2** (hard-edged/gritty playing described by Henderson, not slick late-1980s electronic production); tempo **4** (most key tracks brisk); groove **4** (accented bass/drums and danceable syncopation). Each is an editorial album-level score.

**Instruments:** Drums/percussion (Larry Blackmon), bass (William Revis), guitars (Eric Durham and Charles Sampson), trumpet (Nathan Leftenant), saxophone (Arnett Leftenant), piano/electric piano/synth (Gregory 'Doc' Johnson), congas (Paul Harris) are album-specific AllMusic credits. The source credits identify these instruments even if they do not specify the exact track. `production` blank: original recording location known (Music Farm), but no model/technique evidenced; horns are an arrangement, not evidence for a synth workflow.

**Voice:** blank by evidence standard; Larry Blackmon, Tomi Jenkins and colleagues are credited but no firm approved timbral classification. **Lyrics:** dance/party, romantic love and intimacy follow song/review topics. **Producers:** Blackmon, with disputed supplementary Johnson credit noted in import document. **`sounds_like`:** Henderson explicitly names Parliament/Funkadelic, Ohio Players, Bar-Kays as close styles; 'Find My Way' is compared to Trammps/Double Exposure. No individual album influence attributable to first-person accounts, so `influenced_by` blank. **Landmark 1**: critically singled-out Cameo debut, early signature, multiple singles, compared favorably with later work.

## 2. `cameo-weallknowwhoweare` — *We All Know Who We Are* (1978)

**Sources:** Stephen Thomas Erlewine, *AllMusic*, https://www.allmusic.com/album/we-all-know-who-we-are-mw0000036609; original-label January 1978 vinyl https://www.45cat.com/vinyl/album/cclp2004; specialty chronology and track descriptions https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/; credited musicians https://audio-music.info/htm/c/Cameo_Cardiac_Arrest_and_We_All_Know_Who_We_Are.htm; MusicBrainz original album year https://musicbrainz.org/artist/ef4d808a-2113-44c3-9b44-1b31f026ef1e.

**Genres:** `funk` dominant, `rnb` AllMusic category and `soul` softer numbers. `quietstorm` refers specifically to 'Why Have I Lost You'; `discofunk` is **Erlewine's exact disco-funk phrase** about 'It's Serious', not a whole-record style. This is why both are ordered after the core genres.

**Scales:** energy **3** (driving tracks but slow romantic material); electronic **1** (drums/bass, horns and piano/guitars credited, no definite synthesizer on this LP); mood **3** (communal and exuberant alongside loss); theatrical **2** (large-band horn arrangements without theatrical delivery); experimental **1** (extended funk tracks, conventional foundation); polish **2** (tight band studio with uneven sequencing rather than highly layered pop gloss); tempo **3** (rhythmic, variable); groove **3** (disco-funk and syncopated funk balanced by the ballad).

**Instruments:** Blackmon drums/bass/acoustic guitar; Gary Dow bass; Eric Durham/Charles Sampson guitars; Gregory Johnson keyboards/piano; Arnett Leftenant sax/percussion; Nathan Leftenant trumpet/percussion; Tomi Jenkins timbales. All directly credited. **Production:** blank without credited production technology; Gary Mayo is listed as remixer, not album producer. **Voice:** left blank. **Lyrics:** 'Inflation' is topical/social, 'Why Have I Lost You' expresses heartbreak, dance numbers justify party. **`sounds_like`:** early-era AllMusic biography compares Cameo to Parliament/Funkadelic, both used as period-wide sonic resemblance only. **Producers:** only Blackmon. **Landmark 0**: an important transition but repeatedly judged uneven and less defining than debut/Secret Omen.

**Uncertainty:** AllMusic says 1977, physical first release records and MusicBrainz support 1978. The CSV retains 1978. Do not silently “correct” it based on AllMusic's generic discography year.

## 3. `cameo-uglyego` — *Ugly Ego* (1978)

**Sources:** Rob Theakston, *AllMusic*, https://www.allmusic.com/album/ugly-ego-mw0000467212; credited band and trombone from https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/ugly-ego; a separate guitar sonic comparison from https://markattheflicks.blogspot.com/2026/10/ugly-egosecret-omen-by-cameo-october.html; chronology and track descriptions from https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/; reissue label https://www.cherryred.co.uk/cameo-ugly-ego-secret-omen.

**Genres:** Album-wide `funk` and `soul` are directly named by AllMusic, with parent `rnb`. `ballad` is a lower-weight genre/style for multiple tracks ('Give Love a Chance', 'Friend to Me', 'Two of Us'), cited by Theakston. This 4th classification has track scope and must not displace funk as primary sound.

**Scales:** energy **3** (brisk 'Insane' and 'I'll Be With You' vs multiple slow songs); electronic **1** (traditional live-funk credited instrumentation with unspecified keyboards, no documented synth); mood **3** (party and sentimental material coexist); theatrical **2** (vocal-band interplay, no theatrical character); experimental **1** (standard verse/chorus funk and soul balladry); polish **3** (1979 contemporary source described strong production and tight playing; studios documented); tempo **3** (slow/up-tempo mix); groove **3** (driving syncopated funk tempered by ballads).

**Instruments:** Larry Blackmon drums/percussion; Gary Dow bass; Eric Durham/Anthony Lockett/Cecil Holmes III guitars; Gregory Johnson keys/piano; Arnett Leftenant sax; Nathan Leftenant trumpet; Robert Whimper trombone, supported by credits. **Production:** blank, no verified named studio/engineering technique. **Voice:** blank (lead and backing voice credits, no strict subtype). **Lyrics:** love/heartbreak, party, self-image introspection on the title. **`sounds_like`:** a dated independent review (https://markattheflicks.blogspot.com/2026/10/ugly-egosecret-omen-by-cameo-october.html) compares the guitar on 'I Want You' to The Isley Brothers; period-wide similarity to Parliament/Funkadelic as AllMusic biography notes. No album-specific artist-acknowledged influences. **Landmark 0**: mixed reviews, transitional, outweighed by debut and fourth LP.

**Credit/editorial disagreement:** Theakston calls it the follow-up to debut but this is third LP; Himes/other discographies and original vinyl make sequence clear. Other contemporary album commentary praised production yet questioned originality. No unverified sonic hardware added.

## 4. `cameo-secretomen` — *Secret Omen* (1979)

**Sources:** Alex Henderson, *AllMusic*, https://www.allmusic.com/album/secret-omen-mw0000189241; album personnel/production and arranger list, https://audio-music.info/htm/c/Cameo_Secret_Omen.htm; original 1979 LP production https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/secret_omen; reissue information and breakout hits https://www.cherryred.co.uk/cameo-ugly-ego-secret-omen; JazzRockSoul discography https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/.

**Genres:** `funk` dominant and horn-heavy, `rnb` and `soul` via AllMusic (notably ballad 'Sparkle'); `quietstorm` describes the slow side. `disco` is restricted to the prominent re-recording of 'Find My Way', explicitly identified by Henderson as a disco treatment.

**Scales:** energy **4** (high-powered tracks 'Energy', 'I Just Want to Be', 'Macho', 'The Rock', 'New York'); electronic **1** (instrumental ensemble and keyboards; no specific synthesizer model verified); mood **3** (party exuberance balanced by tender 'Sparkle'); theatrical **2** (staged vocal choruses and horn punctuation, no concept drama); experimental **1** (accessible funk/soul arrangements); polish **3** (professional multimusician sessions, arranged horns and strings); tempo **3** (fast funk plus slow ballad, long disco remix-type performance); groove **4** (most of the record is made around pronounced rhythmic funk grooves).

**Instruments:** Drums/percussion Blackmon and Damon Mendes; bass Gary Dow and Aaron Mills; Lockett/Durham/Fred Wells guitars; Gregory Johnson and Randy Stern keyboards/piano; sax Arnett Leftenant; trumpets Nathan Leftenant/Arthur Young; trombone Carl Harleston; flutes Seldon Powell/George Marge; congas Angel Allende; Irving Spice's Strings is a credited string ensemble. **Production:** `hornarrangements|stringarrangements`, because Sammy Lowe is specifically credited as arranger of horns and strings. No synth model or digital processing extrapolated. **Producers:** Larry Blackmon only. **Voice:** left blank. **Lyrics:** party, love and sensuality on hit/soul ballad material. **`sounds_like`:** early-horn funk evokes Parliament/Funkadelic per AllMusic artist bio, with a more limited critic comparison of 'The Rock' to Earth, Wind & Fire. No album-specific named first-person influence. **Landmark 1**: major 1979 album and durable breakthrough singles.

**Production chronology caveat:** The disco 'Find My Way' is a remake of the song previously appearing on *Cardiac Arrest*; it is not counted as a new album or proof of a wholly disco-style record.
