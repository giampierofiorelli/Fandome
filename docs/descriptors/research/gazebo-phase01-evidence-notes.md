# Gazebo — Fandome phase 01: evidence and decisions

Preflight: Fandome `descriptors` branch, `catalog.ts` and `seed.ts`, checked 27 October 2026. Existing catalog only holds **Gazebo** (1983) and **Telephone Mama** (1984). `docs/descriptors/gazebo.csv` was absent. The later two albums must be added to `catalog.ts` **before** validator. All rows draft. Whole-number scales are first-pass editorial judgments from album-specific sources and documentary evidence; not mathematically measured and not copied from ratings. Instrument/production claims should be stricter than sonic analogy.

## 1 — `gazebo-gazebo` (1983)

- **Genres:** German first-pressing discographic record says Electronic, Italo-Disco, Electro and Synth-pop; check genre evidence table. All four apply to original eight-track album.
- **Sources / sound:** Giombini's first-person history confirms the complete album was commissioned after Masterpiece and the Chopin-inspired piano hook emerged while he was studying Chopin. Original 1983 sleeve transcription identifies Giombini piano and OB-X/Minimoog, DMX programming, Derek Wilson's Simmons overdubs, Civitenga bass and Fabbri violin. Instrument ordering ranks the characteristic electronic accompaniment first; string section occurs on specific tracks, not all. `oberheimobx`/`oberheimdmx` are allowed equipment tokens. `overdubbing` directly follows sleeve Simmons drum **overdubs**. `stringarrangements` follows violin ensemble/arranger credit.
- **Production split:** Giombini produces seven tracks; Paolo/Paul Micioni and Roberto Fusar Poli produce **Masterpiece** (A4). Baby Records is a label/executive, not a person in `producers`. Keep split explicit.
- **Scales:** `electronic=4` follows explicit electronic programmed rhythms beside real bass and violin (not all-electronic=5). `groove=4` reflects several club-oriented tracks but the prominent piano-led ballad. `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `mood=3` account for dance cuts and the dreamy/sentimental centerpiece. `experimental=2` for disco-based arrangements with some texture changes; `polish=4` for coordinated studio overdubs/programming; `theatrical=2` for intimate delivery. These remain editorial, revise if an expert listening audit disagrees.
- **Voice/themes:** Gazebo male lead, described as gentle/breathy in contemporary retrospective artist descriptors. `love|nostalgia` mainly follows **I Like Chopin** lyric/theme and romantic songs; per-track topics are not extrapolated to each track. Similarities to P. Lion and Savage are a later genre-community editorial comparison, not artist testimony.
- **Influence:** **Frédéric Chopin** is retained exceptionally because *the actual producer and melodic composer* Giombini expressly describes composing the song's piano riff while studying Chopin. The documented connection is to the composition of **I Like Chopin**, not proof that all eight tracks mimic Chopin. This is *not* the naive inference that merely naming Chopin in a title means influence. Source: https://giombinimusic.com/about-me/ . No other influence inferred from the name of a musician.

## 2 — `gazebo-telephonemama` (1984)

- **Genres:** Original 1984 LP issue lists Electronic / Italo-Disco, Electro, Synth-pop; other 1984 LP catalogs identify it as Pop/Italo-Pop. The latter is a broader classification, placed last. Artist explicitly recalls moving away from his first album's formula, so similarity to the debut cannot stand in for detailed track analysis.
- **Instruments:** original LP credits Giombini **keyboards**; CD credit transcription identifies **Marco Rinalduzzi guitar** on Telephone Mama and **Gazebo guitar** on Strategy. **Do not invent** OB-X, DMX, Linn, extra piano or strings from the prior album. Though keyboards dominate, their exact models are not confirmed. Production intentionally blank because the precise technique/machine is not documented for *this* release.
- **Producer:** album arranged/produced by Pierluigi Giombini. Baby Records corporate attribution is excluded; Bresciani is engineer, not producer. Original eight songs only.
- **Scales:** Electronic=4 follows keyboard-based synthetic arrangement mixed with documented guitar; groove 3 vs first album 4 because slower and more melodic material shares space with the title track; other scores are provisional editorial judgments. Vocals categorized `clear|soft` as a preliminary reading; lyrics deliberately blank rather than inferred from track titles. English-language eight-song recording.
- **Sources discrepancy:** one unofficial 1984 online sleeve archive claims August 11 German issue; other references give November 1984 for Italy. **Only calendar year** is in CSV; track order and credit fields take precedence over exact month.

## 3 — `gazebo-univision` (1986)

- **Genres:** Italian 1986 vinyl metadata lists Electronic/Leftfield/Synth-pop; an independent sleeve transcription gives Italo-Disco, Electro and Synth-pop. The current CSV uses the Fandome `synthpop` token, not the different `electropop`; the album-wide broad genre `pop` comes from a contemporary German LP catalog. 'Leftfield' appears as a style modifier in one catalog; `leftfield` itself is not currently a standalone guide token, and a single pressing's broad modifier is not enough to introduce a new token (maintainer may review).
- **Instruments and production:** reproduced 1986 disc credits explicitly identify **Gazebo vocals, keyboards and rhythm guitar**, Giombini keyboards and **Linn drum machine**. `drummachine` in instruments; `production` left empty because the sleeve says only Linn, without sufficient evidence to distinguish Linn LM-1 from LinnDrum (or another Linn unit). Do not use `linnlm1`, `linn9000`, `oberdmx` or instrument models not backed by this album's documentation.
- **Producers:** Giombini produced/arranged A1-B3, B5; **Gazebo** produced/arranged **Pain** (B4). Secondary web page often lists only Giombini; liner transcription credits both, track split preserved. Massimo Aluzzi and Marco Lecci are recording/mix engineers, not producers.
- **Scales:** Electronic=4 because named Linn + keyboards vs real rhythm guitar, not purely synthesis; mood 3 and energy/tempo 3 as cautious provisional balances; groove 3 for dance-oriented tracks within varied pop sequences, polish 4 for multilayered studio production. Instrumentation is grounded; exact ratings remain user-editable.
- **No false classical influence:** liner notes for 'Au Moulin Rouge' identify thematic basis in Liszt's **Rêve d'amour**; this is a quotation/adaptation, expressly **excluded** from `influenced_by` under Fandome rules. Do not add Franz Liszt based on this alone. No language/lyric topic derived solely from track title.
- **Date:** original Italian/German vinyl carries **1986**, while some Japan CDs and MusicBrainz list **1987**; other summary pages give an inconsistent 1985. Original issue and official artist discography support **1986**.

## 4 — `gazebo-therainbowtales` (1988)

- **Genres:** contemporary 1988 Italian LP is tagged Electronic/Italo-Disco/Electro/Synth-pop. In addition the *artist's own official biography* identifies this particular record as a shift to **pop and rock** after his earlier Italo disco period. Thus all are retained, priority to synth-pop; avoid assigning **prog** as an album genre merely because he says the shift connected back to his formative prog work.
- **Production and instruments:** Discographic 1988 track credits credit **Gazebo and Denis Haines** with (co-)production. Original LP names Alan Coates, Denis Haines and Gazebo as songwriters; **do not** promote Coates to producer or assume he plays guitar, because the accessible album sleeve transcription only verifies co-writing. A retrospective track-specific listener note about *Ordinary Life* calls out substantial **synth effects**. `synth` is therefore the only instrument backed by source-specific sonic testimony; the absence of detailed track-specific official musician credits means no speculative guitar/drum machine/keyboards models or process tags. Recommend deeper insert/liner audit if more is required before import.
- **Scales:** electronic 4 reflects the synth-centered source testimony with the official pop/rock broad direction. `groove=2` signals the official stylistic move away from dance emphasis; energy 3, tempo 3, melancholy mood 2 and experimentation 2 are provisional listening-style assessments, not statements from an engineer. If strict evidence policy requires it, leave those numerical values empty pending listening panel.
- **Artist influence vs album influence:** Paul documented Genesis, Yes, Gentle Giant, PFM, Banco, Dylan and others as formative across his life, but did not explicitly say **Genesis shaped this album's arrangements**; `influenced_by` stays empty. `sounds_like` also blank rather than equate producer Haines's band history (Gary Numan/Tubeway Army) with a demonstrated sonic match.
- **Release date:** original Japanese CD date 1988, Italian LP Carosello CLN 25130 ©℗1988; MusicBrainz and some summaries misdate a version 1987 (recording date or foreign indexing). Prefer **1988** per official discography and original editions.
- **Track names:** first original 10-track LP, not reissues or extras; titles include *Godbless the Moonshade* on initial transcription (elsewhere *God Bless the Moonshade*).

## Source precedence

Original LP/CD credits and first-person statements > artist official record chronology > independent discographic catalogs > reception/comparison sites. For example, original vinyl credits outrank blanket album-credit summaries on *Univision*; quoted genre terms belong to *each individual album pressing*, not broad genre tag pages about Gazebo's career. Descriptors are editorial draft even when citations for personnel are high-confidence. No live, compilations or unreleased items have been added.
