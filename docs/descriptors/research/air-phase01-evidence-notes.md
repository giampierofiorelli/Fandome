# Air — Phase 01 / source-constrained descriptor judgments

Scope: four chronologically earliest qualifying releases, **original issues only**. Research date 25 October 2026. Genre evidence is a separate row per genre with its scope and mapped source IDs. Numeric values are Fandome editorial interpretations of named evidence, **not numerical statistics from reviewers**. A broad database genre tag (especially Alternative/Indie Rock) is retained because Fandome requests every sourced genre, but explicitly marked weaker than direct, track-specific stylistic evidence. `sounds_like` is comparative; `influenced_by` requires qualified documented influence. `status=draft` throughout.

## `air-premierssymptomes` — Premiers Symptômes (1997, `ep`)

**Release identity:** The AIR official shop labels it the *1997 debut EP* and confirms exactly five tracks [P-OFFICIAL]. A contemporary NME report documents the 1999 expansion to seven [P-NME]. Early tracks had appeared as singles in 1995–97; that does not move the release year of this original EP. Do **not** import 1999 bonuses into the descriptor or change `type` to studio/compilation; it is a historically significant EP of prior singles [P-ORIG, P-NME].

**Genres/production:** John Bush identifies an almost instrumental, downtempo, synth-pop collection [P-AM]; genre databases add lounge, ambient-pop, space-age pop, trip-hop and broad indie/alt tags [P-AOTY, P-AM]. Those latter families are not all dominant: see the evidence CSV. Original recording credits list analog keyboards, Rhodes, bass, tuba, sitar/tambura, samplers, talkbox and vocoder [P-Q]. Godin and Dunckel produce the core recordings; **Étienne de Crécy** additionally co-produces `Modular Mix` and `Les Professionnels` (and mixes those tracks). Production roles are supported independently [P-Q, P-MB]. **Stéphane Briat is mixer, not automatically producer.**

**Eight scales:** `energy=2` and `tempo=2`: largely relaxed, gently driving early instrumentals, not a fast dance album [P-AM]. `electronic=4` because sequenced/programmed/electronic sources lead, yet bass, brass, guitars and other played instruments remain audible in the credited palette [P-Q]. `mood=3` reflects warm but wistful and inward-looking pieces, not uniformly euphoric or dark [P-AM]. `theatrical=1`: subdued delivery, no evidence for large staging [P-AM]. `experimental=2`: interesting analog/recording timbres within largely legible structures [P-AM, P-Q]. `polish=3`: a professional but relatively early-studio-singles set; no evidence of big-budget sheen [P-Q]. `groove=3` because Modular Mix and Casanova 70 pulse, whereas the others drift [P-AM, P-NOVA]. These are medium-confidence global judgments.

**Voice/language/words:** `instrumental` first by track majority [P-AM, P-ORIG]. `soft` corresponds to the one smooth robot croon; voice character is processed rather than a separate lead-singer identity [P-AM]. `language` is deliberately **empty**: AllMusic reports a robotic croon on `Le Soleil est près de moi`, but Radio Nova labels it instrumental, and the original-language performance is not independently established by strong sources [P-AM, P-NOVA]. Do not infer lyrics from its French title. `lyrics` left blank: the one processed hook does not establish a securely sourced set of EP-wide lyric themes. `influenced_by` blank; similarity to Stereolab, Perrey and Dimitri from Paris is not evidence of a documented borrowing [P-PF]. Landmark 1 for an influential and well-reviewed debut EP [P-AM].

## `air-moonsafari` — Moon Safari (1998, `studio`)

**Edition:** Exactly ten original tracks. Original source and MusicBrainz confirm Godin and Dunckel as producers; **Stéphane “Alf” Briat = engineer/mixer** and **David Whitaker = string arranger/conductor**, not producers [M-AM, M-MB]. Beth Hirsch is a featured singer/co-writer, not producer [M-MTECH, M-MB]. Godin recalls Rhodes, synthesizers, played bass and guitar, modest multitrack studio work and London strings; live instrumentation prevents treating it as fully electronic [M-GODIN, M-MTECH].

**Genres:** Electronic/downtempo/pop with synthesizer textures, ambient-pop melody and lounge/easy listening is widely supported [M-AM, M-DJ, M-APPLE]. Trip-hop and indie-pop are AllMusic album tags but narrower interpretations; disco/dance/funk belong chiefly to rhythmic material such as `Sexy Boy`/`La femme d’argent`; `psychedelicrock` is an explicitly named retrospective aesthetic input, **not an album-wide rock-band claim** [M-STEREO, M-LA]. French-touch contemporaneous scene ≠ French-house album: DJ Mag specifically contrasts Air with prevailing dancefloor French Touch [M-DJ].

**Eight scales:** `energy=2` (gentle album, one more upbeat single), `tempo=2` and `groove=2` (club-adjacent pulses but no nonstop dance intent) [M-STEREO, M-AM]. `electronic=4`: synthesizers and vocoders central, yet Rhodes, string section, bass, guitar and hand percussion are real sources [M-MB, M-GODIN]. `mood=3`: not `5` solely because music is lush; Dunckel explicitly emphasizes melancholy and `All I Need` dark afterglow alongside warmth [M-STEREO]. `theatrical=2` for lush widescreen strings with generally understated close vocals [M-MB]. `experimental=2` for subtle studio inventions built on accessible melodic forms [M-GODIN, M-AM]. `polish=4` for careful studio tracking and strings, shy of fully maximalist budget pop [M-MB].

**Voices and lyrics:** Majority instrumental or processed voice, Beth Hirsch intimate singing, background harmonies; `instrumental|soft|clear|choral` describes actual album variety [M-AM, M-MB]. `love|sensuality|introspection` is supported by Hirsch’s account of real romance behind `You Make It Easy`, `All I Need`, and Dunckel’s explicit sensuality/introspection description [M-MTECH, M-STEREO]. Main lyrics `en`; instrumental titles are French, **not evidence of French singing**.

**Influences:** Contemporary first-person Godin cites **John Barry, Nino Rota, Brian Wilson, Beck and Money Mark** as inspiring their own approach [M-LA]. Godin later explicitly names **Burt Bacharach** for `Ce matin-là` [M-GODIN], with the scope limited to that track. **Ennio Morricone** is a documented contemporaneous influence for the duo in their Moon Safari-era research [M-LEMONDE; contemporary genre criticism], but strongest evidence for that name is critical attribution rather than quoted artist interview. Do not treat Jean-Jacques Perrey’s `Remember` songwriting/recording credit as mere influence (different relation). `sounds_like` lists solely sonic comparisons. Landmark 1 is uncontroversial for breakthrough album [M-DJ, M-STEREO].

**Disagreements:** DJ Mag and retrospectives discuss how often the album was filed as chill-out, while Godin resists that reductive genre box [M-DJ, M-STEREO]. The album is **not** 100% electronic or dancing, and its romantic sound has documented melancholic undertones.

## `air-thevirginsuicides` — The Virgin Suicides (2000, `soundtrack`)

**Edition:** The original **Air-composed film score** is the 13-track 2000 album; it is distinct from multi-artist licensed 1970s pop appearing in the film [V-AM, V-MB]. Original `Playground Love` singer **Gordon Tracks** is **Thomas Mars**, not Air's Jean-Benoît Dunckel; the other 12 tracks are overwhelmingly instrumental [V-MB, V-Q, V-AM]. Score producer credit is Godin + Dunckel only. Do not attribute drummer Brian Reitzell or mix/engineering personnel as producers [V-AM].

**Genres:** Film score and electronic atmospherics predominate [V-AM, V-LA]. Ryan Schreiber at Pitchfork argues strongly for `prog` and `psychedelicrock` references (melodic, rhythmic and textural) from late-1960s/1970s rock and names *Abbey Road* and *Dark Side of the Moon* analogies [V-PF]; these are contributions, not a claim that every cue is rock. AllMusic indexes ambient-pop, indie-pop, trip-hop and alternative/indie-rock; some of these are stylistic metadata with weak per-cue fidelity, recorded by scope in the evidence CSV [V-AM].

**Eight scales:** `energy=1`, `tempo=1`, `groove=1` for soundtrack of sparse, generally slow non-dance cues [V-LA, V-AM]. `electronic=3` balances recurring analog synth and organ against credited guitars, bass, brass, strings and snare/drums [V-AM, V-PF]. `mood=1`: Dunckel explains planned dark mood following the suicide storyline, counterpointed by soft moments of romance, not a uniformly positive record [V-KINDA]. `theatrical=3` for cinematic motif scoring rather than bombastic orchestral delivery [V-AM]. `experimental=3` as a stronger structural/rock experiment after Moon Safari [V-PF]. `polish=4` from finely controlled multilayered scoring and recording [V-AM].

**Voice/lyrics:** `instrumental|soft`, `language=en`. `love` refers only to `Playground Love`; **cinematic storytelling should not be coded as lyrical storytelling across twelve instrumentals** [V-AM, V-KINDA]. Bright-pink romance/mourning contrast is a soundtrack *mood*, not evidence for textual `heartbreak` in every cue.

**Influence caution:** Dunckel expressly cites **Pink Floyd** as an influence on the conception of this score in a contemporary statement reproduced by Stereogum [V-STEREO]. Ryan Schreiber identifies explicit **Beatles Abbey Road** influence on several cues [V-PF]. Both qualify under the user rule, but Beatles is *critic-supported* (not artist testimony). `sounds_like` also includes Air’s electronic/space-rock peers as editorial comparisons, not additional proven inputs. Landmark 1 because a major original and critically received film score [V-AM, V-PITCHMETA].

## `air-10000hzlegend` — 10 000 Hz Legend (2001, `studio`)

**Edition:** Use the *original eleven-track* France/UK May 2001 album [H-RG], **not** 12-track Japanese, remastered, or remixed Everybody Hertz variants. Catalogue title uses `10 000 Hz Legend` without punctuation; ID preserved as `air-10000hzlegend`.

**Genre disagreement:** John Bush emphasizes darker, unhurried but lavish synth studio-pop with acoustic guitars, harp, flutes, orchestral arrangements and odd-song structures [H-AM]. Pitchfork explicitly finds it discontinuous and weird, with a Beck folk passage, robotic voices, ambient coda and grating sound design [H-PF]. Godin’s retrospective interview characterizes some sessions as purposely unrestrained experiments and notes live Brian Reitzell drums losing power after overdubbing [H-SHINDIG]. Accordingly electronic/electronica, pop, experimental and rock crossovers are significant, while `folk`, `disco`, `industrial`, `grunge` and `ambient` are deliberately **track-limited**, not dominant [H-WP, H-PF].

**Eight scales:** `energy=3` (more forceful at spots than prior work), `tempo=3` (mixed slow and pulsing passages) and `groove=2` (not consistently club-oriented) [H-AM, H-PF]. `electronic=4` given synthesizers, processed vocal chains and sequencers, plus numerous real live players, guitar, harp, flutes and strings [H-Q, H-SHINDIG]. `mood=2` follows Godin’s first-person description of a distressed creative period and critics' brooding/eerie palette [H-SHINDIG, H-AM]. `theatrical=3` for dynamic choir/string expansions and comic robot dialogue [H-AM, H-PF]. `experimental=4` for progressive song constructions, deliberate recording experiments and volatile soundscape transitions [H-SHINDIG]. `polish=4` because complex polished mixing and arrangements coexist with unconventional textures [H-Q, H-SHINDIG].

**Voices/words:** Many featured human lead singers, soft and clear, processed/robot speech on How Does It Make You Feel?, ensembles/choir and several instrumentals [H-MB, H-Q]. `en|ja`: English dominates, Buffalo Daughter's Japanese vocals contribute on `Sex Born Poison` [H-PF, H-Q]; check Japanese phrasing before second-party review. `introspection|love|irony|social` is inferred conservatively from critic analysis of personal/robot love, urban commentary, mechanization and jokes on particular songs; not all tracks have lyrics [H-AM, H-PF, H-WP].

**Producers:** Godin and Dunckel credited as producers throughout; **Tony Hoffer is a documented additional producer on `Caramel Prisoner` only**; his widespread mixing/editing does not make him co-producer of other tracks [H-MB, H-Q]. Beck, Buffalo Daughter, Jason Falkner and Roger Manning are vocalists/collaborators, not album producers by association. `influenced_by` intentionally **blank**: Air directly deny an intentional Pink Floyd influence on this particular album [H-RFI], despite several critics hearing it; names such as ELO, Kraftwerk, Radiohead, Lee Hazlewood belong to **`sounds_like`** only [H-AM, H-PF, H-WP].

**Landmark/confidence:** `landmark=1`, `high`: major original studio album and documented decisive departure. High confidence of evidence **does not mean critical agreement**; Pitchfork questions coherence, AllMusic and first-person discussions value exploratory craft [H-AM, H-PF, H-SHINDIG].
