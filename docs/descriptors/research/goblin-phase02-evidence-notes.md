# Goblin Phase 02 — Dario Argento-only evidence notes

**Scope, dated 26 October 2026:** 1982 **Tenebre** ORIGINAL soundtrack; 1997 **Phenomena** instrumental-score edition (composed/recorded for 1985 film); 2000 **Non ho sonno** original soundtrack (2001 movie). The 1975 *Profondo rosso* and 1977 *Suspiria* from Phase 01 are deliberately not repeated. Every row remains `draft`.

## Method and limits

- Genre strings are sourced names, not guesses inferred from the fact that a film is a horror thriller. `soundtrack` is release `type`, not sonic genre. Film genre (horror/giallo) is kept in `scene`, not forced into `genres`.
- Scores assess the *album*, not only a title sequence; each 0–5 score is an editorial inference from the cited compositional/arrangement descriptions, and an unverified feature is left blank.
- Recording players and machines require score-specific sources. Artist-wide equipment inventories do not imply a machine on an album.
- No collaborators, filmmakers, composers, guest musicians, performers or record labels are promoted to `influenced_by`. All three album-specific influence fields remain EMPTY: the first-hand sources document collaboration rather than a named musical influence on these three scores. The general artist influences were already delivered **Phase 01** and are not repeated.
- `sounds_like` is **editorial sonic comparison**, never a historical claim of influence, reference, mentorship or sampling.
- Production role and original billing are independently verified. AllMusic engineer/mixer credits alone are insufficient to name an album producer.

## 1. Tenebre (1982), `goblin-tenebre`

**Release identity:** 1982 Cinevox MDF 33.157, eight original LP tracks. Cover/credits are under **Simonetti / Pignatelli / Morante**. The three musicians are all Goblin alumni, but the ORIGINAL release could not use the band name. Walter Martino played orchestral timpani/percussion according to Simonetti. The original 1982 material is DISTINCT from the 1997 expanded album, the 2018 expanded Waxwork release and the newly rerecorded 2026 Claudio Simonetti's Goblin album. Sources: [original LP credits transcribed in Sea of Tranquility](https://www.seaoftranquility.org/reviews.php?id=2206&op=showcontent), [Claudio Simonetti first person](https://www.ondarock.it/interviste/claudiosimonetti/), [artist discography](https://www.goblinsimonetti.com/discography), [Goblin site](https://www.goblinofficial.com/biography.php).

**Genres:** `electronic|electrorock|dance|disco|rock|prog|synthpop`. Waxwork's archival release notes specify electronic/dance/rock/disco. Simonetti independently calls the score electronic rock and explains a deliberate turn toward dance production. The official group history calls out rock/prog/dance/electronics. MusiCulturA uses **synth pop**, hence `synthpop` instead of `electropop`. Genre order reflects dominant synthetic grooves before track-specific traditional prog. No `italodisco` assigned from the **2026 remake**: its description is not evidence that the 1982 issue bore that genre.

**Scales:** energy **3** (propulsive title/Flashing alongside slow reprise, Slow Circus and Jane Mirror); electronic **4** (synth and sequenced/drum-machine basis, despite guitar, bass and piano); mood **1** (dread and melancholy across cues rather than playful dance associations); theatrical **4** (cinematic tension and overt musical gestures); experimental **3** (unusual techno-dance/horror blend, vocoder); polish **3** (professional multistudio LP, not demonstrated top-tier glossy studio techniques); tempo **3** (mixed mid/quick rhythmic cues and slow score passages); groove **3** (danceable core, still many underscoring/slow sections). Sources: Waxwork, Sea of Tranquility, MusiCulturA, OndaRock firsthand.

**Voice/lyrics/language:** principally instrumental. A repeated Italian spoken word **paura** processed with a vocoder in the title cue supports `spoken`; this fragment does not establish Italian as a *main singing language* and is not coded as a lyrical theme. No broad lyrics. [MusiCulturA](https://www.musiculturaonline.it/tenebre-original-soundtrack-di-simonetti-pignatelli-morante/).

**Instruments:** credited [original-era transcriptions](https://www.seaoftranquility.org/reviews.php?id=2206&op=showcontent) support piano, Rhodes electric piano, keyboard synths, vocoder, electric and acoustic guitars, fretted/fretless bass, drum machines, and [OndaRock Simonetti interview](https://www.ondarock.it/interviste/claudiosimonetti/) supports guest timpani. Album review mentions organ-style keys (organ is a credited device in available personnel lists); `percussion` covers Martino. Each belongs to THIS score.

**Production:** source reports **Roland Jupiter-8, Oberheim DMX, TR-808, Roland MC-4, Minimoog**; first three named separately from generic Oberheim/Moog to avoid losing specificity. `vocoder` and `drumprogramming` supported by artist account and title-track review. **Critical equipment contradiction:** in a [Red Bull Music Academy oral history](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/claudio-simonetti-gothic-disco-godfather/) Simonetti retrospectively calls the machine a **Linn 9000**; the machine was introduced after the 1982 session, and the contemporary personnel transcription specifies **Oberheim DMX** and **TR-808**. DO NOT add `linn9000` or substitute `linndrum`. Likewise, source phrase *Roland Vocoder Plus* is NOT used to infer exact model `rolandvp330` vs `rolandsvc350`: retain generic vocoder only.

**Producers:** original sleeve/credit transcription explicitly says *produced by Simonetti, Morante & Pignatelli*; include all three, not Dario Argento (film director). **Influences:** blank; dance milieu is not a named artist influence. **Sounds-like:** Giorgio Moroder (club-derived synth pulses) and John Carpenter (minimal suspense ostinato) are comparisons **only**. **Landmark 1:** a defining 1982 soundtrack and important departure into electronic-dance scores, supported by the band's historic account. **Confidence high:** independent original credit transcriptions plus artist interview and label notes, with machine contradiction disclosed.

## 2. Phenomena (1997 score-only issue), `goblin-phenomena`

**Two different albums, same film.** Dario Argento's film is dated **1985**. A commercial **1985 Cinevox album** contained **VARIOUS ARTISTS**. Examples: Iron Maiden *Flash of the Blade*, Motörhead *Locomotive*, Simon Boswell and Andi Sex Gang; Goblin pieces such as *Jennifer*, *Sleepwalking*, *The Wind* and *Jennifer's Friend*; and a theme credited to Claudio Simonetti solo. That is **not** a 1985 Goblin-only LP. [SoundtrackCollector label/tracklist](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/2281/Phenomena), [AMS original label](https://ams-music.it/wp/phenomena/?lang=&lightbox=true).

**Selected album:** the **1997 Cinevox CD MDF 303**, marketed as *Phenomena (The Complete Original Instrumental Sound Track Album)*. This is the first collected instrumental-score-focused release rather than the 1985 multi-performer selection. For normalized Fandome title/ID use **Phenomena / goblin-phenomena** and the RECORD RELEASE YEAR **1997**. Discographic sources attribute the edition variously to Goblin, Simonetti, or Simonetti & Goblin: it combines group-branded and solo music. This is an import recommendation, not a claim of exclusive Goblin performance. Claude should check actual 1997 sleeve billing before creating additional co-billed artist mappings. [Release data](https://winyle-online.pl/wydawnictwo/a794a271/goblin-phenomena-the-complete-original-instrumental-sound-track-album-cd-reedycja-remastering-caly-swiat-1997), [1997 Cinevox tracklist](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/2281/Phenomena), [2008 score criticism](https://www.colonnesonore.net/recensioni/cinema/909-phenomena-.html). AMS's later 2014 LP is ANOTHER edited selection: do not silently conflate sequencing or music sources.

**Genres:** 1997 release is categorized **Electronic**, with **Electro, Ambient, Experimental** genre tags by issue metadata. These categories are kept distinct. *Prog rock* describes Goblin historically but not necessarily these specific mid-1980s score cues. The original 1985 LP's heavy-metal tracks are not imported. **Experimental** applies particularly to textures and sound effects, **ambient** to extended atmospheric score, not all cues.

**Scales:** energy **2** (often subdued with intermittent forward motion); electronic **4** (synthesized atmospheres/synth bass/drum programming alongside acoustic piano, bass and vocals); mood **1** (sombre, plaintive, uncanny rather than consistently violent); theatrical **3** (cinematic melody and wordless soprano but restrained relative to Suspiria); experimental **3** (unusual vocal/atmospheric layering and film effects; also conventional melody); polish **3** (professional studio soundtrack; recording format unclear); tempo **2**, groove **1** (ambient and suspense cues more common than dancing). Based on [Innsmouth Free Press 2013](https://innsmouthfreepress.com/column-slicing-score-phenomena-1985/) and [Colonne Sonore 2008](https://www.colonnesonore.net/recensioni/cinema/909-phenomena-.html). Both critics see more melancholy than outright terror, and one specifically questions the score's dramatic effectiveness: do not classify every passage as horror shock.

**Instruments and production:** contemporary Goblin score lineup is effectively **Pignatelli: bass/drum programming; Simonetti: keyboards/drum programming** ([GDM](https://www.archiviocolonnesonore.com/goblin/biografia/)). Separate review identifies synthesized bass, piano solo (on *Phenomena*), and guitar at *Jennifer (End Titles)*. Synth/keyboards/drum machine are supported by contemporary-participation credits. Simonetti says this is his first film with computer-based work, recorded during **1984** ([OndaRock interview](https://www.ondarock.it/interviste/claudiosimonetti/)); no exact computer model, keyboard model, software or tape medium is stated, so `programming` only. Wordless soprano vocalises occur across much of the score ([Colonne Sonore](https://www.colonnesonore.net/recensioni/cinema/909-phenomena-.html)); use `operatic` after `instrumental`, leave language blank. The soprano's historically credited identity **Pina Magri** is reported for some original 1985 LP cues ([1985 Japanese LP credits transcribed by SoundtrackCollector](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/2281/Phenomena)); singer is a performer, not a producer or musical influence.

**Composer / contributor caveat:** [AMS track-by-track](https://lightintheattic.net/products/phenomena-original-1985-soundtrack) says *Phenomena* was composed by **Claudio Simonetti**, *Jennifer* by **Fabio Pignatelli**, and *The Wind*, *Sleepwalking*, others jointly. Original 1985 album likewise assigns the main title track to Simonetti, not to Goblin. The 1997 collected score is linked to Goblin as a historic project, but NOT an album wholly performed or composed by the four-member band. **Producers EMPTY:** composing, programming and label compiler/editor credits do not equal credited record producers. **Influences EMPTY:** no album-specific cited musical artist lineage. **Landmark 1** as recognized Argento-Goblin collaboration / prominent score. **Confidence MEDIUM** because the score sources and tracks are strong but album billing is inconsistent.

## 3. Non ho sonno (2000 soundtrack; film 2001), `goblin-nonhosonno`

**Date distinction:** four original musicians re-formed to record the movie score in 2000. Goblin official discography, GDM's history, OndaRock and [label-owner copyright statement](https://goblinrockband.bandcamp.com/album/dario-argento-non-ho-sonno-sleepless-original-motion-picture-soundtrack-horror-movie-soundtrack) use 2000. The FILM premiered in 2001, and [SoundtrackCollector](https://soundtrackcollector.com/title/11030/Non%2BHo%2BSonno) lists a 2001 release variant. **AllMusic's 'December 24, 2002' is a specific database issue, not evidence that no 2000 version exists**. Adopt original **2000** for Fandome; flag actual original first-sale date as **not independently verified**. If Claude obtains primary first-press label metadata proving 2001, it should revise the year and catalog entry together. 14-track program, not a live performance. [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/goblin/), [Cinevox/Beat](https://www.beatrecords.it/en/catalogo/593/non-ho-sonno).

**Genres:** official distributor calls **Classic Rock** and **Progressive Rock** with melodic quieter cues: coded `rock|prog` with `prog` first for musical prominence. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nonhosonno-mw0000342685) explicitly classifies **Hard Rock** and Prog-Rock. [OndaRock](https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/goblin/) calls the two principal identities prog-rock and electronic; its track review says *Killer on the Train* hard rock and *Death Farm* nearly death/progressive metal, hence main categories `hardrock|electronic` follow. AllMusic calls the title cue *prog-metal*, hence final track-level `progmetal` (a new literal token). OndaRock's unusual **'quasi death-progressive-metal'** is a hedged stylistic comparison, not a stable unambiguous formal genre; recorded here but not converted to an assertive whole-album token.

**Scales:** energy **3**: big title, rhythmic *Killer on the Train* and *Death Farm*, but *Endless Love*, *Ulisse*, other quieter film cues; electronic **2**: organic rock drums/guitars/bass with keyboards, synth pulses; mood **1**: suspense, tragic and melancholy sections; theatrical **4** for strong rock-horror climaxes; experimental **2** for relatively straightforward return to signature prog instrumentation; polish **3** professional studio but no source warranting glossy 5; tempo **3** varied; groove **1** predominantly film underscore, not dance. AllMusic's qualified review complains melodies are less memorable than earlier scores; OndaRock also criticizes colder technology-era sessions despite energy, so do not describe as universally acclaimed.

**Instruments:** Cinevox and [AllMusic actual album credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nonhosonno-mw0000342685) identify Simonetti keyboards, Morante guitars, Pignatelli bass, Agostino Marangolo drums, plus **Vesna Duganova** soprano on *Endless Love* and **Antonio Marangolo** saxophone on *Ulisse*. [SoundtrackCollector booklet transcription](https://soundtrackcollector.com/title/11030/Non%2BHo%2BSonno) specifies both acoustic and electric Morante guitars, as well as computer programming. Piano and synthesizer riffs called out by OndaRock and AllMusic. **Strings** are mentioned explicitly by Cinevox distributor as a sound on certain cuts. `instrumental|operatic` records score's instrumental focus and track-limited classical soprano, but there's no comprehensible lead lyric/language. `programming` is supported by explicit 2001-issue computer-programming credit, without unsubstantiated Pro Tools, DX7, Cubase, machines or specific effects. `producers` EMPTY: Pignatelli/Simonetti are engineers/mixers/programmers per AllMusic, not explicitly credited as album producers.

**Influences:** no verified direct artists influencing THIS reunion score; leave empty. **Sounds like:** John Carpenter for score atmospherics and King Crimson for angular prog riffing (editorial comparisons, not causal claims). **Landmark 1** records short-lived full historic-lineup reunion. **Confidence MEDIUM** due to publication year disagreement and absence of verified producer credit.

## Critical attribution exclusions

- **Opera (1987)** is a Dario Argento film with **Claudio Simonetti SOLO score contributions**, alongside other composers; it is not a Goblin band album ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/goblin_(Enciclopedia-del-Cinema)/)). Do not add Goblin-Opera.
- **Inferno (1980)** is chiefly Keith Emerson's soundtrack, not a Goblin soundtrack.
- **La chiesa / The Church (1989)** has Goblin material but is directed by **Michele Soavi**, not Dario Argento.
- **Demoni / Demons (1985)** has Claudio Simonetti music but is directed by **Lamberto Bava**, not Dario Argento.
- **Il cartaio (2004), La terza madre (2007), Dracula 3D (2012)** feature Claudio Simonetti's solo scoring, not Goblin original-group albums; **2026 Tenebre** is newly rerecorded by **Claudio Simonetti's Goblin**, so excluded from original 1982 row.
- **Zombi / Dawn of the Dead** (1978) was directed by **George A. Romero**, despite Dario Argento's involvement with the European edit; outside this user-requested director-only filter.
