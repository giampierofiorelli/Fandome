# Christina Aguilera — Phase 02: evidence and decisions

Scope: **Back to Basics** (original 22-track double album, 2006); **Bionic** (original 18-track standard programme, 2010); **Burlesque (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack)** (2010, original 10-track film album). The eight 0–5 descriptor scales are editor assessments anchored to Fandome's guide, **not** source-published scores.

## `aguilera-backtobasics` — Back to Basics (2006)

**Source triangulation.** AllMusic (Erlewine), Slant (Cinquemani), The Guardian/Observer (Empire, Flynn, Petridis), BMI's Aguilera profile, original Qobuz credits and MusicBrainz track credits; individual genre citations in `genre_evidence.csv`.

- **Scope and sonic contrast:** first disc is record-sampling, hip-hop-derived and contemporary, while the Linda Perry-dominated second disc is blues, swing, jazz-club and theatrical pop performed with live instruments. BMI confirms her historical concept and orchestra, choir and horn use; Slant and Guardian independently describe the two-track approaches. Avoid describing the entire album as wholly 1930s jazz or as fully live-recorded.
- **Genres:** `pop|soul|rnb` are recurring elements; `jazz|blues|swing|gospel|bigband|cabaret` tend to cluster in particular arrangements; `hiphop` is particularly present on disc one. The lower-position `contemporarypoprock` and `europop` are AllMusic's broad style taxonomy, not claims that electric rock dominates. New `cabaret` comes from the second-disc burlesque/revue idiom rather than being a global one-to-one genre substitute for jazz.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` covers upbeat Ain't No Other Man / Candyman against slower Hurt and Save Me from Myself; `electronic=2` reflects samples and studio programming alongside a substantial live-band half; `mood=3` mixes exuberance and serious domestic-violence/grief songs; `theatrical=4` for carnival cues, showmanship and dramatic vocal delivery; `experimental=2` for stylistic juxtaposition with recognisable song forms; `polish=5` for large commercial arrangements; `tempo=3` given rhythmic songs plus slow ballads; `groove=3` because some, but not all, of the 22 pieces target dancing. These apply to BOTH discs, not the lead single alone.
- **Voice:** `powerful|raspy|soft|clear`: Billie Holiday-styled torch-singing and Perry piano passages show quieter delivery alongside the characteristic belts and roughened period numbers. No rap voice assigned merely because producers sample hip-hop.
- **Instruments:** piano and Hammond organ (Steve Winwood on Makes Me Wanna Pray, per MusicBrainz/Qobuz); drums, bass, brass/trumpet/trombone/saxophone/clarinet, strings, guitars, synth, keyboards, Mellotron and tuba all appear in original per-track Qobuz or MusicBrainz credits. `turntables` is grounded in DJ Premier's explicit scratches (Back in the Day), not deduced from hip-hop. Not every instrument is on every cut; main-first order reflects the double album.
- **Production:** `sampling` from disc-one documented samples (including Glad/Traffic and old funk/soul recordings) and the Candyman exception on disc two; `hornarrangements` and `stringarrangements` from explicit players and arrangement credits (E. Gorfain / L. Perry / horn arrangers). `lofi` marks the deliberately muffled vintage vocal-mic sound on I Got Trouble, which Aguilera describes in original interviews. It **does not** imply low-quality mastering or an album-wide lo-fi recording. Do not equate live instrumental performances with confirmed `liverecording` simultaneous tracking: insufficient evidence on tracking method. `protools` withheld despite unsourced equipment summaries; Qobuz credit lists on this investigation didn't justify an entire-album specific DAW tag.
- **Producers:** record-level producer and co-producer roles transcribed from Qobuz per track, including Christina Aguilera (credited for production on original first-disc tracks), DJ Premier, Linda Perry, Rich Harrison, Kwamé, Mark Ronson, Big Tank, Q, L. Boogie (co-producer), Tony Reyes, Ben H. Allen, Charles Roane. Do not insert Steve Winwood (featured singer/organist), Kara DioGuardi (writer), Rob Lewis (arranger on multiple tracks), Ron Fair (executive/A&R) or Eric Gorfain (arranger) without an additional producer credit for the ORIGINAL programme. Qobuz sometimes lists album-level repeated main-artist roles; review per-track credit before changing roles.
- **Influences, not producers:** BMI quotes Aguilera naming Billie Holiday, Etta James, Ella Fitzgerald, Otis Redding; BMI mentions the Andrews Sisters on Candyman. The archived primary 2006 interview and period research also document her reference compilation with Nina Simone, Ray Charles, Eartha Kitt, Millie Jackson, Screamin' Jay Hawkins. `sounds_like` is editorial resemblance only; Amy Winehouse and Duffy were not used as documented Aguilera influence claims.
- **Lyrics:** `love`/`sensuality` from marriage and sexual play; `nostalgia` and `identity` from historical homage and self-definition; `social` limited to Oh Mother (domestic abuse); `spiritual` from Makes Me Wanna Pray and Mercy on Me; `heartbreak` from Hurt. No claim of universal political subject matter.
- **Critic disagreement:** Erlewine (AllMusic) praises the distinct auteur pop statement, Slant finds the second disc an imbalance, Kitty Empire critiques the inflated historical claims, Paul Flynn strongly prefers the emotional Linda Perry disc, and the Guardian stresses the exceptional Premier sampling. `landmark=1` is an editorial career-defining judgment rather than a sales threshold. Confidence `high` for album-specific credits and corroborated period coverage; interpret secondary vintage-style genre tags as track-limited.

## `aguilera-bionic` — Bionic (2010)

**Scope: the original standard 18 songs (59:28), not deluxe 23/24 songs.** This matters: Ladytron-produced Birds of Prey and Little Dreamer, plus Santigold-linked Monday Morning and Bobblehead, are deluxe extras. The standard DOES contain Le Tigre-produced My Girls and John Hill / Switch's Bionic and Elastic Love. **Ladytron are NOT standard-album producers**. The artist can cite them retrospectively as an influence on concept without their deluxe instrumental credits contaminating the standard row.

- **Genres:** the AllMusic review identifies futuristic pop and standard R&B, its styles include Neo-Soul, Dance-Pop, Euro-Pop and adult contemporary; Album of the Year explicitly assigns Electropop, Dance-Pop, Electroclash, Electronic Dance Music, Futurepop, Art Pop and Pop Soul. `electrodisco` is specifically Guardian's description of My Girls. Their scope is not equal: foreground electropop/dance pop, then track/texture evidence for electroclash, art pop and neo-soul. `neosoul` is a **NEW genre token** reflecting the exact wording of AllMusic, rather than silently mapping to existing `nusoul`.
- **Scales:** `energy=4` high-dynamics club cuts vs ballads; `electronic=4` repeatedly electronic beats, sizzling synths and programmed textures, explicitly described by Petridis, yet real strings/piano/drums and guitars are credited on Sia/Perry songs; `mood=3` sexual bravado vs lost-love/motherhood sadness; `theatrical=4` bold persona and highly emphatic vocal presentation; `experimental=3` off-kilter Elastic Love, electro-disco My Girls, unusual voice manipulation, while many R&B songs are conventional; `polish=5` large-scale pop sessions; `tempo=3` club numbers offset by extended ballad sequence; `groove=4` many synth/dance tracks. Distinguish editorial 0–5 scale from the source's review score.
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear|soft|rap|spoken` reflects conventional Aguilera belting, significantly restrained Sia songs, and the semi-rapped/spoken Elastic Love/Bionic delivery described by LA Times and AllMusic. Guest raps (Peaches, Nicki Minaj) corroborate some rap texture, but their appearances are **not** album co-billing.
- **Instruments:** synths (Petridis's explicit synthesizer review), keyboards (Vanity / Linda Perry track label credits), piano (Dixon, Linda Perry and Larry Goldings on All I Need), bass, real drums, percussion, acoustic/electric guitar, strings (Oliver Kraus arrangements and Lift Me Up ensemble), trumpet, trombone, saxophone, flute (Desnudate), celesta (Samuel Dixon), double bass (string-band personnel) all have Qobuz standard-edition support. **Do not** add Ladytron's Alessandro Cortini synth/guitar or Monday Morning's rhythm section: both come from deluxe tracks only.
- **Production:** `sampling` exists on standard Woohoo and I Hate Boys; compare label sample/author credit and credited interpolation. `distortedvocals` is confined to part of Woohoo (contemporary review and contemporary song reporting). `stringarrangements` appears on Sia / Dixon ballads with Oliver Kraus credited. The described “cool effect” on Elastic Love is real (Aguilera, PAPER 2024; Petridis 2010 dubby echo), but no specific vocal-processing hardware is documented, so omit `vocoder`, `autotune`, `talkbox`, named samplers and synthesizer models. `drumprogramming` is **not** extrapolated from electric genre alone.
- **Producers:** Qobuz track roles support John Hill, Switch, Polow da Don, Claude Kelly (both on Woohoo, plus co-credited vocal productions), Tricky Stewart, TheRealFocus, Detail, Linda Perry, Samuel Dixon, Sia (co-producer or credited vocal producer; vocals/piano ballads), Le Tigre (My Girls), Ester Dean (Vanity). `Christina Aguilera` is a writer and singer on many cuts, not a listed producer in the selected Qobuz standard per-track data, so absent. Neither M.I.A., Peaches nor Ladytron should be confused with producer roles on the original 18 tracks.
- **Influences vs guest contributors:** Out's 2010 interview confirms Goldfrapp among artists Aguilera actively pursued for the album's electronic approach; unlike M.I.A. or Le Tigre, no original-standard producer credit to Goldfrapp was found. `influenced_by=Goldfrapp` is a **conservative evidence-backed link to sought-out stylistic work**, but is less direct than a quotation explicitly saying “Goldfrapp inspired this particular track”. M.I.A., Ladytron, Switch, John Hill are documented inspirations in Aguilera's 2024 reflection, but are excluded from `influenced_by` under Fandome's no-collaborators rule; Ladytron are credited exclusively on deluxe tracks. `sounds_like` is editorial, not an attribution of copying or artistic influence.
- **Lyrics:** sexual autonomy, fashion, bravado and partying coexist with loss, motherhood, and friendship. Spanish track title Desnudate doesn't justify `language=es` unless verified Spanish lyrics; bilingual performance is heard/reported on Desnudate, so `en|es` denotes a minor Spanish-language presence, not a Spanish-language album.
- **Critic disagreement:** AllMusic acknowledges risk but finds it compromised; Petridis praises the unusual M.I.A./Le Tigre tracks and dismisses much standard material, Observer emphasises club sexuality; Slant sees cheap synthetics; LA Times identifies a genuine departure in Elastic Love. `landmark=1` is Fandome's sound-era-definition judgment, **not** a claim of original critical acclaim; the mixed initial reception is explicit.

## `aguilera-burlesqueoriginalmotionpicturesoundtrack` — Burlesque (2010)

**Title, ownership and co-billing:** Apple Music lists the original 19 Nov 2010 album *Burlesque (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack)* and main artists **Christina Aguilera, Cher**. The programme is eight Aguilera songs, two Cher songs (Welcome to Burlesque, You Haven't Seen the Last of Me). Qobuz's umbrella storefront calls the whole release `Various Artists` yet its track list shows only these two vocalists. Add one row in `aguilera`, one `coBilled.ts` record with explicit `credit: 'Christina Aguilera & Cher'` and `with: ['cher']`, **do not create a duplicate Cher row**. Alternate punctuation (Burlesque: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) varies; use the original Apple Music punctuation above.

- **Genres:** direct sources describe Broadway/musical numbers, cabaret, jazz-band rhythm, Etta James blues/soul, modern dance pop and Cher's power ballad; distinct `musicaltheatre|cabaret|jazz|soul|blues|showtunes|swing|pop|popsoul|neosoul|dancepop|traditionalpop|powerballad|soundtrack` only to the scope shown in evidence CSV. The **soundtrack** token is last and informational: it cannot substitute for the music's sound. `neosoul` follows Apple’s exact name (Amy Winehouse comparison), and `cabaret` is a new genre.
- **Scales:** `energy=4` for frequent belted stage climaxes; `electronic=1` because credited acoustic piano, horns, drums, bass and strings predominate (but Express / The Beautiful People include electronic/new production); `mood=4` generally cheeky upbeat music tempered by Bound to You and Cher's resilience song; `theatrical=5` as an overtly scripted film musical; `experimental=1` classic structures and modern interpolations; `polish=5` major motion picture productions; `tempo=3` fast-shuffling revue numbers but two major ballads; `groove=4` many stage-ready dance grooves. Scores cover BOTH singers and all ten songs.
- **Voice:** Christina Aguilera's large belted, roughened soul voice and Cher's distinctive low husk both represented by `powerful|raspy|clear`; voice tokens are **not** used to imply the same singer on all ten tracks.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz official recording credits specify Alex Al (acoustic/double bass), Michael Neuble (drums), Eddie Brown / Jim Cox (piano), trumpet, trombone and saxophones on multiple numbers, castanets and violin on Cher Welcome to Burlesque, The Section Quartet and other strings, electric guitars and drums for You Haven't Seen the Last of Me, and additional keys. Specific piano / horn combinations are also in Apple Music's editorial.
- **Production:** `hornarrangements` supported by brass arrangements and multi-horn recording roles; `stringarrangements` by named quartet/strings and arrangers; `sampling` by The Beautiful People's Marilyn Manson sampled song, which IMDb states explicitly; `belting` is the sustained upper-register vocal delivery described in major reviews; `protools` credit is attached to technical recording/editing personnel in AllMusic, **not** evidence that every track used the same DAW. **No** unverified named drum machines, synthesisers or Autotune are claimed.
- **Producers:** Tricky Stewart (early Etta James covers and Express), Steve Lindsey and Matthew Gerrard (Cher Welcome to Burlesque), Mark Taylor (Cher vocal/full production credit), Linda Perry (Guy What Takes His Time), Claude Kelly (Express / Show Me How You Burlesque), Matt Serletic (Cher power ballad), Samuel Dixon (Bound to You), Ron Fair, The Phantom Boyz, Larry Summerville Jr. and Melvin K. Watson Jr. (The Beautiful People, with collective and named-member credits) and Christina Aguilera (credited as vocal producer/arranger, not automatically for all ten songs). Exclude Diane Warren (composer of Cher's song), Sia (co-writer of Bound to You, not its lead producer), and Steven Antin (executive soundtrack producer only). Qobuz and AllMusic vary between supplementary and lead-producer labels: Claude Kelly and Christina Aguilera have production roles on *some* cuts, not the whole soundtrack.
- **Influences:** Aguilera calls Etta James a childhood idol and directly covers two Etta recordings in an AJC interview; `influenced_by=Etta James`. Mae West is a **recording repertoire source** for Guy What Takes His Time, not enough to assert broad album influence. Cher is co-billed performer, not an influence. Amy Winehouse is only a sonic comparison from Apple.
- **Awards and landmark:** the **Golden Globe award** belongs to the original song *You Haven't Seen the Last of Me*, sung by **Cher** and written by Diane Warren, not to Aguilera's performance; Aguilera's *Bound to You* was separately nominated. This supports a notable shared soundtrack. `landmark=1` because this co-billed original soundtrack is a significant film-role album with independent recognition. Not to be counted as Aguilera solo studio album.
- **Critic disagreement:** AllMusic calls the soundtrack a step back toward the splashy jazz-soul Back to Basics period while questioning its fashionability. Slant finds new original stage songs livelier than standards and marks The Beautiful People as an incongruous update. Apple Music foregrounds vintage charm and theatrical energy. Genre categorisation and scores describe a heterogeneous soundtrack.

## Source quality and uncertainties

The genre evidence file distinguishes `whole album`, `some tracks`, `texture` and states narrower support for broad music-database tags. Official Qobuz supplied credits sometimes show duplicate performer/composer roles and omit distinctions between vocal producer, additional producer and overall record producer; entries were manually narrowed to credited producers/co-producers. `status=draft` throughout. The source evidence is stronger for instruments than for exact recording hardware, so unavailable hardware is **intentionally left blank** rather than asserted from production year or musical genre.
