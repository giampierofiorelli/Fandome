# Katy Perry — Phase 01 evidence notes

**Method.** Eight whole-album integer scales are our reasoned descriptors, not instrument measurements; direct credit/critical evidence is distinguished from interpretation. Genre evidence is itemized separately with album/track/texture scope. Catalog title spelling follows Fandome (`Prism`, not the graphic `PRISM`).

## Katy Hudson (2001) — `katyperry-katyhudson`

- **Release and original identity:** MusicBrainz lists *Katy Hudson* in 2001 under Katy Hudson; AllMusic provides 6 March 2001. This is a real studio debut even though the Katy Perry-major-label discography frequently begins in 2008.
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly assigns CCM, contemporary Christian, gospel and describes Christian rock. Christianity Today's *contemporaneous 2001* annual list calls it alternative pop/rock and explicitly hears jazz in two songs. A music-credit index also calls it pop rock. These support `christianrock|ccm|alternativepoprock|poprock|gospel|jazz` in descending prevalence, not a blanket classification as jazz.
- **Scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=2` reflect Erlewine's rolling, largely mid-tempo Christian rock. `electronic=1` reflects credits dominated by live instruments with some synthesizer/keyboards/programmed elements. `mood=3` reflects angst and faith/redemption together; `theatrical=3` reflects the review's note about vocal theatrics. `experimental=2` for jazz/alternative flourishes within conventional songs; `polish=3` for a conventional multi-studio produced album; `groove=1` as the songs are not characterized as dance material. These are interpretive, not verified metronomic averages.
- **Voice:** `powerful` refers to the 'caterwaul' characterization of singing in the AllMusic review, not presumed later pop performance.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic's personnel credits explicitly give Collier acoustic guitar; Graul electric and 12-string guitar; Otto Price bass and synthesizer; several drummers; Browning/Faircloff piano and keyboards; Wurlitzer (mapped to existing `electricpiano`); B-3 organ corroborated in track-level staff inventories; Love Sponge String Quartet, cello/violin/viola, brass, tenor sax and wind players. None inferred from genre. `strings` aggregates the named string section rather than adding a claim of a full orchestra.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` is directly credited to David Browning. Generic programming/loops are **not** equated to drum machines, samplers or famous branded hardware. Producers are **Tom Collier** (also styled 'Tommy'), **Otto Price** and **David Browning**, exactly production-role credits. Executive producer Gary Randall is omitted.
- **Lyrics and comparison:** Christian faith/spirituality, adolescent uncertainty and introspection are noted contemporaneously. AllMusic's strong Alanis Morissette-debt and Fiona Apple-comparison qualify for documented influences; Jennifer Knapp is merely a named stylistic comparator. `landmark=0` reflects the user's criterion 'artist defining' in the Katy Perry identity, not dismissal of the archival debut. `confidence=medium` reflects the limited quantity of independently accessible contemporaneous critical writing.
- **Disagreement:** AllMusic marketing style 'gospel' may imply a much more traditional sound than reviewers describe. It remains a sourced tag but below alternative rock; evidence-scope record explicitly states the limitation. Christian music and broad guitar-rock are compatible, not mutually exclusive.

## One of the Boys (2008) — `katyperry-oneoftheboys`

- **Genres:** Both The Guardian and AllMusic identify pop-rock; AllMusic further specifies teen pop. Pitchfork's retrospective calls the album pop-punk-inflected rather than fully pop-punk, hence `poppunk` appears well below pop-rock. Album of the Year distinguishes power pop, dance-pop, new wave and alternative rock; The Guardian separately hears soft-rock, and Apple Music calls one hit dance-rock. Those track/texture labels appear last in the row.
- **Scales:** `energy=4`, `tempo=4` come from recurrent energetic hit arrangements, offset by slower tracks like *Lost*. `electronic=2` captures a guitar/bass/drum album with programmed beats and new-wave keyboard hooks. `mood=4` reflects lively party songs amid heartbreak; `theatrical=4` is supported by staged, provocative vocal personae; `experimental=1` denotes mainstream structures despite stylistic switches. `polish=4` follows multilayered commercial production and `groove=3` recognizes dance-rock singles alongside ballads.
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear` is a whole-album judgment from descriptions of assertive, emphatic delivery; no rap or Auto-Tune credit is invented.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic personnel name multiple guitarists, live drummers, bassists, Greg Wells on piano/bass/guitar, keyboard/programming staff and the dedicated horn-section players. `electricguitar` is the main idiom, with no proof of a particular guitar make or model.
- **Production:** Stephen Thomas Erlewine's review, republished by Qobuz, explicitly names **Pro Tools overdubs**, giving `protools|overdubbing`. AllMusic credits Jerry Hey with **horn arrangements** on *Ur So Gay*, yielding `hornarrangements`. Do not mistake general `Programming` credits for the separate `drumprogramming` technique in this row.
- **Producers:** The AllMusic and Qobuz production-role credits support Greg Wells, Dr. Luke, Butch Walker, Benny Blanco, the **S*A*M & Sluggo** team, Anne Preven, Scott Cutler, Dave Stewart, and an album-level producer credit for Katy Perry. **Max Martin, Cathy Dennis, Desmond Child, Glen Ballard** are often mentioned because of writing or earlier sessions; don't promote those mentions to album production without a track producer credit.
- **Lyrics/influences:** The Guardian explicitly describes material influenced by **Alanis Morissette, Avril Lavigne and Liz Phair**. Narrative covers romantic conflict, party excess, sexuality, sarcasm and persona. `sounds_like` includes editorial comparator Gwen Stefani; `influenced_by` is narrower. `landmark=1` for commercial stylistic breakout.
- **Disagreement:** Guardian writers saw punchy soft-rock and youth-party pop but criticized self-conscious provocation; Pitchfork later found the power-pop freshness more durable than its commercial successor. Preserving both yields moderate danceability/electronic numbers rather than treating it as a club album.

## Teenage Dream (2010) — `katyperry-teenagedream`

- **Genres:** Pitchfork's retrospective expressly combines **EDM, disco and pop** and calls it guitar-powered bubblegum; AOTY tags **dance-pop, electropop, pop-rock and bubblegum-pop**. AllMusic classifies **teen-pop**. Pitchfork's Pop/R&B heading supports a minor `rnb` texture; it specifically describes *Circle the Drain* as hard rock (track-level scope only). `synthpop` was **not** substituted for `electropop`.
- **Scales:** `energy=4` and `tempo=4` describe dominant singles while allowing slower tracks; `electronic=4` follows programmed synth-based arrangements with retained live guitar/drums. `mood=4` accounts for optimism in smash hits with darker, sorrowful album cuts. `theatrical=5` follows extraordinary maximalism noted in Pitchfork and stage-sized arrangements; `experimental=1` for conventional pop songcraft with an occasional strange sound. `polish=5` is supported by high-profile multinational studio production; `groove=4`, not 5, accounts for strong dance appeal but significant ballads/rock.
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear` for the emphatic melodic hooks. `rap` denotes Perry's half-rapped *Peacock* phrasing described by The Guardian, and is a minority song-level qualifier; featured Snoop Dogg's guest rap is **not** a lead-voice type for Perry.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic credits drums/keyboards to Dr. Luke, Max Martin, Benny Blanco and Ammo; synthesizer to Greg Wells and Fabien Waltman; guitars to Paschke, Malone and Miranda; bass to Daniel Silvestri; piano to Wells; saxophone to Lenny Pickett. No TR-808, LinnDrum or specific DAW asserted.
- **Production:** AllMusic explicitly credits **drum programming** to Tricky Stewart, Brian Thomas and Pat Thrall. Pitchfork characterizes the vocal presentation as **belting** and notes a **talkbox** vocal motif in the *California Gurls* outro (no named talkbox machine). There is no evidence to assign blanket `autotune`, `sidechain` or `tr808` merely because the album is EDM-adjacent.
- **Producers:** Original 2010 core songs credit **Dr. Luke, Max Martin, Benny Blanco, Stargate, Sandy Vee, Christopher 'Tricky' Stewart, Greg Wells and Ammo**. Qobuz's track-by-track credits independently check several. Kuk Harrell is a **vocal producer**, not the recording's producer in this 27-column field. *The Complete Confection* and its additional songs/remixes are different editions.
- **Lyrics and influence:** youthful love and desire dominate, with partying, sexual wordplay, nostalgia and relationship regret, alongside vulnerability on darker tracks. Pitchfork notes a California Gurls response to *Empire State of Mind*, but that is not proven evidence of a musically influential act for the whole album, so `influenced_by` stays empty. `sounds_like` is explicitly editorial. `landmark=1` because five original-album singles topped the US Hot 100; the production style is era-defining.
- **Disagreement:** Pitchfork (2021) acknowledges a pop landmark but considers its compression, lyrics and dated excess glaring. The Guardian (2010) is less favorable, criticizing slickness and insincerity; the chart success and production polish remain objective independently of review scores.

## Prism (2013) — `katyperry-prism`

- **Genres:** The Independent calls the album dance-pop. AOTY identifies electropop and synthpop **as separate genres**, plus alt-pop and adult contemporary. The Guardian's Alexis Petridis directly identifies an early-1990s **pop-house** ambition (`pophouse`, new token), deep-house is specified by Slant on *Walking on Air*, and the Tufts Daily calls that song diva-house dance-pop. *Birthday* is described as disco/funk, *This Is How We Do* as new-jack-swing and *Dark Horse* as trap-inspired. They have **some tracks** rather than **whole album** scope: Prism is not universally house or trap.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` and `tempo=3` represent a sharply split album: uptempo first half and slower reflective second. `electronic=4` is justified by synth, programming, electronic house/trap arrangements; live horns, piano and guitars prevent a 5. `mood=3` follows Perry's explicit 2013 distinction between party songs and introspective ballads plus the divorce/recovery narratives. `theatrical=4` reflects big-sounding empowerment anthems; `experimental=1` fits Petridis's claim there is little adventurous writing. `polish=5` follows multi-producer precision and `groove=3` reflects album-wide diversity despite dance singles.
- **Voice:** The AU Review explicitly hears **falsetto** and **belting** on *Birthday* and several vocal layers elsewhere. Those support `production=falsetto|belting|layeredvocals`; `rap` in voice is Perry's rap-like verses documented in reviews of *This Is How We Do*, distinct from Juicy J's featured rap on *Dark Horse*.
- **Instruments:** Release credits via Amoeba/AllMusic support synth, keyboards, piano, drums, guitar, a distinct synth-bassline noted by Slant, horn players (trumpet and saxophones), Ronobit Lahiri's sitar, Aditya Kalyanpur's tabla, and strings from Gingger Shankar's credited double violin. `guitar` is a pre-existing valid token in `instruments.ts`; do not force an uncredited specific electric/acoustic subtype. `hornarrangements` are explicitly credited to Lenny Pickett. MusicBrainz documents Greg Wells **synthesizer programming** on *By the Grace of God* as `synthprogramming`.
- **Producers:** AllMusic and Amoeba provide roles for **Dr. Luke, Max Martin, Cirkut, Klas Åhlund, Stargate, Benny Blanco, Christian Karlsson, Greg Kurstin, Greg Wells and Katy Perry**. MusicBrainz confirms Perry/Greg Wells as co-producers on *By the Grace of God*; Karlsson is 'Bloodshy' in Slant, not a separate duplicate collaborator. Steve Turre, Lenny Pickett and Sia are not added as producers from horn/writing contributions alone.
- **Themes/influences:** *Roar*, *Love Me* and *By the Grace of God* carry self-worth and post-divorce self-searching, while *Birthday* and *This Is How We Do* supply flirtation and partying. Eckhart Tolle inspired philosophical lyrics, not a documented musical artist influence, so `influenced_by` remains empty. The `sounds_like` Robyn/early Mariah/Sara Bareilles parallels are critics' sonic comparisons (Slant, Guardian, AllMusic), not proof of sampling, copyright or direct influence.
- **Disagreement:** Critics split over the perceived maturity: Petridis finds the self-help overlay inflated and composition familiar, while Slant notes surprising 1990s house throwbacks and more restrained second-half material. The Guardian and contemporary interviews both support a spiritually inflected but still sexually playful record. The 2001 and 2013 records should **not** be treated as a continuous gospel project on the strength of lyrical spirituality alone.

## Source-weight and validator notes

The `sources.csv` file separates named critics, contemporaneous review, interviews, label/retailer credits and more weakly grounded database genre tags. Instrument tokens are tied to specific album personnel lists; production values to explicitly named techniques or cited descriptions. Do not automatically extrapolate `protools` used in 2008 to subsequent albums. Album-level genres that only occur on one track appear late and with `some tracks` scope, preserving dominant genre ordering. Every album's `status` is intentionally `draft` pending Claude's validation and second review.
