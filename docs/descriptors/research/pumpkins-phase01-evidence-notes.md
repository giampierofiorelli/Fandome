# The Smashing Pumpkins — Phase 01 evidence notes

Research scope: original 1991, 1993, 1995 and 1998 album programs only. Reissue bonus tracks, collections, single-boxes and live releases are excluded. Entries are editorial proposals; the 0–5 scale scores judge the whole album, not one single. All factual sources are in `pumpkins_phase01_sources.csv`; genre-by-genre scope is in the evidence CSV.

## Gish (1991) — pumpkins-gish

- **Genres:** AllMusic documents alternative/indie rock, grunge, noise pop, dream pop and heavy metal. Mark Beaumont's Classic Rock assessment distinguishes the acid-folk, shoegaze and psychedelic passages and occasional glam color. Album of the Year also lists neo-psychedelia. Glam is a narrow track-level color, not the foundation.
- **Energy 4, tempo 3:** Heavy riff-based songs are balanced by longer drifting, slower passages including Rhinoceros and Daydream (AllMusic; Classic Rock). **Mood 2:** introspective, often sombre; spiritual passages offer contrast. **Groove 2:** syncopated drum and riff combinations occur without dance-floor design.
- **Electronic 1:** amplified guitars dominate, but original credits show no electronic programming or synthesizers; not a purely acoustic ensemble. **Theatrical 3, experimental 3, polish 3:** AllMusic describes intricate art-rock ambitions and Vig's studio craft, but it is less orchestrated and less processed than the next record.
- **Voice:** Raspy Corgan rasp plus gentler vocals; D'Arcy also sings Daydream (AllMusic). **Lyrics:** introspection and spirituality are documented by Corgan's description of the record's spiritual nature; identity is a cautious thematic classification.
- **Instruments:** The original release relationships in MusicBrainz credit guitars/bass/drums and guest Mary Gaines (cello) and Chris Wagoner (violin). Electric guitar is clear from guitar-heavy reviews, not inferred from the genre. No imaginary synthesizer or flute: reviewers' comparisons to a panpipe-like timbre are not instrument credits.
- **Production:** Producer Butch Vig specifically describes tracking Corgan and Chamberlin then overdubbing guitars and bass (Gearspace). Distorted guitar tones recur throughout expert reviews. No specific model of synthesizer, drum machine or recording software is claimed.
- **Producers:** Billy Corgan and Butch Vig, both listed as producers for original Gish tracks in MusicBrainz.
- **Influences:** Direct Corgan admission links My Bloody Valentine to Daydream (NME 2021). Classic Rock explicitly describes the album's attempted combination of Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Cure, Led Zeppelin and The Beatles. Other artist names in `sounds_like` are editorial similarities, not separate documented influences.
- **Possible disagreement:** Whether Gish should count as grunge or as a psychedelic Chicago alternative rock record varies by critic. We record both when explicitly applied, with broader grunge high in the order and textural subgenres later.

## Siamese Dream (1993) — pumpkins-siamesedream

- **Genres:** AllMusic applies alternative/indie, grunge, noise pop and post-grunge. Pitchfork describes Kevin Shields-derived shoegaze textures; Classic Rock Review describes dream pop, heavy metal and progressive-rock architecture. `prog` uses the guide's broad established progressive-rock token; it is a minor structural color.
- **Energy 4, tempo 3:** Extreme dynamic jumps between fast distortion and ballads are typical. **Mood 1:** autobiographical vulnerability, sadness and stress recur (Pitchfork; Fender). **Groove 1:** rhythmic complexity serves rock performance, not dance music.
- **Electronic 1:** Mellotron appears on Spaceboy, but the record is dominated by guitars and acoustic drums. **Theatrical 4:** towering arrangements contrasted with hushed close-miked sections. **Experimental 3:** layered guitar timbres and unusual structure within recognisable songs. **Polish 5:** Butch Vig details meticulous multi-layer guitar overdubs; the album was consciously maximalist (Guitar.com, MusicRadar).
- **Voice:** Corgan moves between rasp/wail and soft singing. **Lyrics:** introspection, heartbreak, identity, love follow Pitchfork's autobiographical reading and the documented context of Soma (Fender).
- **Instruments:** 2011 liner notes reproduce original personnel: electric/acoustic guitars, bass, drums, Corgan Mellotron, Mike Mills piano on Soma, Eric Remschneider cello and David Ragsdale violin on Disarm/Luna. Pitchfork mentions a sitar present in the studio; it is not demonstrated to be heard on the original album, so `sitar` is deliberately omitted.
- **Production:** Vig details dozens of individual guitar overdubs with different spectral roles on Hummer and an unusual salvaged distortion circuit. The Corgan/Vig/Remschneider/Ragsdale original credits support `stringarrangements`. Do not add the specific Mu-Tron effect pedal as a token without an official Fandome counterpart and clear sonic evidence.
- **Producers:** Corgan and Butch Vig; Alan Moulder is chiefly credited for mixing and must NOT be added to producers here (liner notes).
- **Influences:** Pitchfork identifies My Bloody Valentine and Catherine as actual shaping forces, not just comparisons, and traces Corgan's Black Sabbath Master of Reality sound ideal. Corgan specifically named Rush's By-Tor and the Snow Dog as the template for Cherub Rock; first-album label is `Rush (Fly by Night)`.
- **Critical caveat:** `postgrunge` is a catalog term some listeners would dispute given the simultaneous alternative rock era; retain as source-named rather than editorially replacing with another term.

## Mellon Collie and the Infinite Sadness (1995) — pumpkins-melloncollieandtheinfinitesadness

- **Genres:** AllMusic covers alternative rock, grunge/post-grunge; its review explicitly discusses pop, folk and classical, while contemporary Los Angeles Times criticism highlights punk, glam, industrial rock and psychedelic pop within the varied 28-song program. These latter genre tokens are explicitly **some tracks** only.
- **Energy 4, tempo 3, mood 2:** The album shifts repeatedly between explosive fast rock and subdued piano/acoustic songs; darker writing predominates but includes hope and warmth. **Groove 2:** a few rhythm-led moments, while the album is not predominantly dance-oriented.
- **Electronic 2:** varied studio arrangement, keyboards/Mellotron and sonic manipulation, but largely instrumental rock. **Theatrical 5:** two-disc concept scale with orchestra and elaborate forms. **Experimental 4:** uncommon breadth of genres and formats. **Polish 4:** large professional production, intentionally varying from stripped takes to thick overdubs (MusicRadar and Corgan interview).
- **Voice:** Corgan moves between abrasive rasp and fragile singing. **Lyrics:** identity, introspection, love and nostalgia are cautious readings of the full program; the Los Angeles Times describes yearning for connection and life transition.
- **Instruments:** Sound Credit and MusicBrainz list Corgan's acoustic piano, Mellotron and autoharp, electric guitars, bass and drums. Corgan's original-era Guitar World interview directly names electric and acoustic guitar setups. Orchestral strings on Tonight Tonight are part of the original recordings. Specific synthesizer brand/model is not claimed.
- **Production:** Flood describes tracking a significant proportion on Otari MTR-90 MKII and Studer A820s using 15ips NAB and Dolby SR (MusicRadar): `analogtape` is well documented. Guitar World documents selective overdubs, distorted amplified guitars, and the extensive orchestral/string approach. No Dolby model gets an unsupported custom token.
- **Producers:** Flood, Alan Moulder and Billy Corgan. Credit services attribute the three production credit, while specific engineers/mixers remain excluded.
- **Influence:** Corgan's first-person account of Tonight Tonight describes The Who as the model for its tempo and backing, documented in MusicRadar. The Beatles/Queen references in a Guitar World writer's prose function here as `sounds_like`, not unambiguous album influence.
- **Disagreement:** Genre tags span whole album versus one-song textures. The supporting evidence marks them separately; a flat genre string inevitably gives the deep 28-song stylistic range imperfect weight.

## Adore (1998) — pumpkins-adore

- **Genres:** AllMusic and AOTY cite alternative rock/dream pop and gothic rock; Under the Radar explicitly says goth-pop and synth-pop; Ned Raggett uses electro-rock. Pitchfork and The A.V. Club also document trip-hop, electropop and new-wave touches on specific tracks; Louder identifies electronica as a key element. The categories describe different poles of this hybrid and should not be flattened into just electronica.
- **Energy 2, tempo 2, mood 0:** subdued, slow and elegiac predominance anchored in mother-loss/divorce accounts, with more animated exceptions (Longreads; AllMusic). **Groove 1:** mechanised pulse is not consistently dance-floor oriented.
- **Electronic 3:** drum machines, sequencers and synthesizers coexist with acoustic and guitar songs plus session drummers (NME, Dig, Washington Post). **Theatrical 2:** close/intimate delivery instead of Mellon Collie bombast. **Experimental 4:** major writing/production pivot. **Polish 4:** painstaking electronic editing and multistudio mixing, despite deliberately spare arrangements.
- **Voice:** Corgan's soft and raspy singing contrasts with more aggressive earlier work. **Lyrics:** introspection, heartbreak and memory/nostalgia are defensible within the guide's limited category set; grief is the central critical theme but `grief` is not in the allowed lyric tokens, so it is not inserted.
- **Instruments:** Drum machines, synths and sequencer use are documented in Corgan interviews and contemporary reviews. MusicBrainz release credits confirm guest acoustic drums by Matt Walker, Joey Waronker and Matt Cameron, and Brad Wood's organ on Blank Page. Pitchfork and Louder document the piano on For Martha/Blank Page. Acoustic/electric guitars and bass are corroborated by reviews and performance credits. No hardware model is asserted.
- **Production:** Original MusicBrainz per-track relationships explicitly credit Bon Harris with extra `programming` and `drumprogramming` on selected songs and Bjorn Thorsrud with `digitalediting`; NME reports some tracks recorded live in studio, so `liverecording` applies to part of the program. These are NOT claims that every song has those characteristics.
- **Producers:** The original release track listings explicitly name Billy Corgan and Brad Wood as **producers**, with Flood regularly a **mixer**. Secondary sources often list Flood as a producer for the entire album; to respect the user's strict credited-producer rule he is excluded pending a contrary original release producer credit. Do not infer Bon Harris is a producer merely because of his programming credit.
- **Influenced_by:** Empty: the Corgan interview cites 1980s electronic influences in broad terms but does not document a specific named artist as a direct maker of this album. The Cure/Depeche Mode comparisons are in `sounds_like` only. The `sounds_like` values are subjective similarity judgments; no credit/influence implied.
- **Critical disagreement:** Longreads notes that contemporary critics were cooler toward the record than later reappraisals; AllMusic disputes how radical the stylistic departure actually was. Keep all categorical descriptions properly sourced.
