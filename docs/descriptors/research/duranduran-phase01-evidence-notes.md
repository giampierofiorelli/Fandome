# Duran Duran — phase 01 evidence decisions (27-column draft)

The numeric scales are Fandome editorial evaluations applied to each **original studio album**. These are not critic ratings; see the guide anchors. All specific technical tokens derive from album/song-specific credits or statements, with attribution in `sources.csv`.

## `duranduran-duranduran` (1981)

- **Genre scope:** AllMusic gives `newwave`, `synthpop`, `newromantic`, `dancerock`; the album article adds `dancepunk`. The `postdisco` texture is centred on *Girls on Film* and *Planet Earth*; punk energy concentrates in *Careless Memories*. Do not inflate the whole album into punk rock.
- **Scales:** energy 4 from the relentless bass/drum propulsion; electronic 3 because electric guitar, live drums and bass remain foregrounded despite sequenced keys; mood 3 spans buoyant singles and the dark *Night Boat* / *Tel Aviv*; theatrical 3 for Le Bon's projected, mannered delivery; experimental 3 for unusually atmospheric tracks and synthesis of club/post-punk; polish 4 for Thurston's tight mix; tempo 4 and groove 4 for the danceable singles.
- **Instrumentation:** John Taylor bass, Roger Taylor drums/percussion, Andy Taylor guitar, Nick Rhodes synth/keyboards are documented in the original album personnel and Apple review. No claim of sampling or a model of drum machine is made for this LP.
- **Production:** Rhodes specifically names Prophet-5 sequenced part, Jupiter-4 flanged intro and Crumar Performer strings for *Planet Earth*. `synthprogramming` applies to the documented programmed sequence; `flanging` comes from Rhodes's explicit MXR flanger account. Newly proposed `crumarperformer` is a machine token, not an instrument token. No blanket claim of digital recording.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Le Bon is primarily clear, sometimes emphatic. Official overview identifies youthful self-definition, critique of modelling culture and personal agitation; dark songs justify `introspection`.
- **Producers:** Colin Thurston alone for the **1981 nine-track original**. The apparent Ian Little credit on newer AllMusic metadata stems from the **1983 added single**, so is not included.
- **Similarity vs influence:** Japan and Roxy Music are sonic comparisons in Apple Music. Chic, Sex Pistols and Giorgio Moroder were named as ingredients in the band's original vision; Nick Rhodes explicitly cites Human League *Reproduction* as inspiration for the producer choice. The `influenced_by` set is grounded in direct testimony rather than sonic guesswork.
- **Critical disagreement:** some writers read stylish commercial pop, others hear dark post-punk tension. The genre ordering represents the entire record rather than its most commercial tracks.

## `duranduran-rio` (1982)

- **Genre scope:** AllMusic's core classifications are new wave, synth-pop, dance-rock, dance-pop and new romantic. MusicBrainz/Wikipedia summary names pop rock and funk influence. Duran's official reprint identifies disco, post-punk and gothic-rock textures, which are scoped as such rather than primary genres.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 3 and groove 4 respect the prominent live bass/drum/guitar interplay even though Rhodes builds synthetic soundscapes. Mood 4 is principally radiant songs offset by sinister *New Religion* and *The Chauffeur*; experimental 3 recognises sonic studio detail; polish 5 recognises immaculate multi-layered production.
- **Instruments:** documented guitar/bass/live drums/keyboards/synth; Taylor's occasional fretless bass and Roger's Simmons electronic drums; tenor sax Andy Hamilton on title track; actual piano strings manipulated on title intro; ocarina by Le Bon in *The Chauffeur*; vibes on *New Religion* and marimba on *Last Chance on the Stairway* are both encoded as `mallets`. Producer Colin Thurston independently identifies a **TR-808** on *Rio* title track and a single sequencer running behind real musicians.
- **Production:** `tr808`, `jupiter4`, `jupiter8` and `crumarperformer` are source-named on *Rio* track; grand piano reversed on tape supports `tapeeffects`; analogue multitrack approach is specifically discussed in recording histories. The recorded studio sound effects are **not** inferred to be digital sampling or location field recordings. The separate 1983 David Kershenbaum US radio mixes must not be treated as original 1982 production.
- **Vocals and themes:** dominant clean strong Le Bon voice, soft on *Save a Prayer* and *The Chauffeur*. Official interpretation documents desire, emotional solace, aspirations and self-discovery. `heartbreak` is tied to the melancholy romantic album moments, not every song.
- **Influences:** band's interview history specifically names Roxy Music, Japan and Human League among its early references. The `sounds_like` Roxy Music (Avalon) is an editorial sonic comparison, not an unverified claim that Avalon specifically caused Rio.
- **Disagreement:** initial US mixes were considered too light for FM radio. This does not invalidate the original album's carefully varied dynamics.

## `duranduran-sevenandtheraggedtiger` (1983)

- **Genre scope:** AllMusic album-level new wave/synth-pop/new romantic/dance-rock; Telegraph foregrounds lush pop; Apple Music describes *Union of the Snake* specifically as funk-R&B. The funk and R&B tags are low-ranked, not treated as the sound of all nine songs.
- **Scales:** electronic 4 and polish 5 acknowledge Fairlight and elaborate rhythmic/keyboard arrangements; theatrical 4 for monumental choruses; energy 4, tempo 4 and groove 4 for propulsive tracks; mood 3 varies from thrilling to reflective; experimental 3 for layer-building and sampling without implying avant-garde composition.
- **Instrumentation:** band quintet on bass/guitars/drums/keys/synth; album liner notes name percussionists Rafael DeJesus and Mark Kennedy, saxophonist Andy Hamilton, vocal guests on *The Reflex* and *Union of the Snake*. Fairlight sampling synthesiser specifically documented by Nick Rhodes on album; `sampler` reflects the actual instrument and `fairlight` the actual machine.
- **Production:** 24-track mobile jams near Cannes; Little and Sadkin build arrangements from sequenced/recorded patterns, then overdub in Montserrat/Sydney. Rhodes explicitly named Fairlight, Jupiter-8 and Crumar Performer on *New Moon on Monday*. Digital Fairlight samples include sound and voice parts, supporting `sampling`; do **not** infer drum-loop programming or effects from the 1984 *The Reflex* single remix.
- **Influence:** Classic Pop's session history documents Bowie's *Let's Dance* as a sonic reference for *Union of the Snake*. Store `David Bowie (Let's Dance)` because the named work is specific. It is not a guest credit.
- **Producers:** original insert describes Alex Sadkin 'in association with Ian Little and Duran Duran'; Qobuz and LP metadata give all three producer credits. Row records Alex Sadkin / Ian Little / Duran Duran and flags the credit variance, rather than silently inventing Nile Rodgers.
- **Mixed reception:** received negative reviews at release and later appreciation for cinematic pop sophistication. Commercial/production significance justifies `landmark=1` without calling it a universal critical favourite.

## `duranduran-notorious` (1986)

- **Genre scope:** `funkrock` is explicit in Apple Music and secondary discography; official official reprint discusses funk/soul/R&B throughout; AllMusic labels dance-rock and contemporary pop-rock. `dancepop` is conservatively scoped to *Notorious* title track; the guitar-heavy *Hold Me* gives some rock weight. No claim of a primary *synth-pop* album.
- **Scales:** groove 5 reflects funk-driven syncopated rhythms and arrangements; electronic 2 because Steve Ferrone's drums, John Taylor bass, multiple guitarists and real horn section dominate; polish 5 for Nile Rodgers's tight studio finishing. Energy 4, tempo 3, mood 3, theatrical 3 and experimental 2 are based on the brisk singles versus slower understated *Skin Trade* and *Winter Marches On*.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz and Apple credits: bass John Taylor, synthesizers/keyboards Nick Rhodes, guitars Nile Rodgers, Andy Taylor and Warren Cuccurullo, drums Steve Ferrone, percussion Jimmy Maelen, real brass/horns. Apple song-level credits specify trumpet Mac Gollehon and saxophonists Lenny Pickett, Stan Harrison, Steve Elson; encode them as `trumpet`, `saxophone`, `brass` not a generic synthesised horn assumption.
- **Production:** contemporary *Los Angeles Times* mentions horn charts / horn arrangements; artist-site interview reports Rodgers re-recorded guitar overdubs; `falsetto` is expressly attributed to Le Bon in *Skin Trade*. No unsupported Roland/Fairlight product model added.
- **Producers:** original producer credits are Nile Rodgers and Duran Duran. Do not list Daniel Abraham (engineer/mixer), Steve Ferrone (drums) or Warren Cuccurullo (guitar). A Qobuz track export redundantly tags individual band members as producers; grouped `Duran Duran` is the accurate album credit.
- **Influence:** John Taylor directly acknowledges **Prince's** harmonic funk vocabulary as inspiration for *Notorious* title song; `Prince` therefore goes in influenced_by. Nile Rodgers and the guest players remain collaborators, not influences. Chic is a supported *sound-alike* due to the actual Nile guitar language, not listed as an extra 'guest influence'.
- **Critical disagreement:** a contemporary LA Times review found the funk single stronger than much of the record, whereas the official-site retrospective sees a successful reinvention. The genre list allows both the commercial funk and less dance-oriented ballads to coexist.
