The 1959 George/Ira Gershwin title is only in the catalogue; the 1960 Berlin concert is live and excluded.

The 1959 expanded *Ella Sings Gershwin* reissue is a DIFFERENT source programme (it includes big-band recordings): do **not** copy its personnel, genre ratios or track list to the original 1950 album. [jazzdiscography_decca, allmusic_gershwin_mca_warning, pure_ella_original_liner]
- *Sweet and Hot* is a difficult **studio-vs-compilation boundary**. Discographies call it an original 1955 studio album, but Decca assembled it from newly recorded 1955 sides plus earlier singles (1952) and 1953 *Bing Crosby Show* broadcasts. We include it provisionally under the published studio classification, while declaring the disjoint sources; Claude should confirm it satisfies the owner's strict no-compilations rule before import. No producer is asserted for the aggregate album, because the sources provide arrangers/conductors and different sessions, not one identified album-level credited producer. [jazzdiscog_sweet, jazzdiscog_session, allmusic_sweet]
- Other Decca LP compilations and *Lullabies of Birdland* (recordings from 1945 to 1955) are excluded. An early *Souvenir Album* (1940-41 recordings, later package) is also excluded. The official artist site lists both these reissues and real albums together and is NOT itself proof each is a new studio album. [official_full_discography, historical_discography]

## ella-ellasingsgershwin: Ella Sings Gershwin (1950)

- **Identity/date:** Original eight songs for Decca A-806/DL5300, recorded Sept 11–12, 1950 in New York. The AllMusic ORIGINAL album page displays 1951, while contemporary catalogue/session records and MusicBrainz issue show 1950. Use **1950**; neither the 1959 enlarged MCA reissue nor the 1994 *Pure Ella* compilation changes this. [jazzdiscography_decca, musicbrainz_gershwin, allmusic_gershwin_original, allmusic_gershwin_mca_warning]
- **Genres:** `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing`; last is gentle/lightly swinging rather than hard big-band swing. AllMusic and Stuart Nicholson discuss the Gershwin song treatment, not a new band/rock style. The material is American standards; 'Gershwin' is a composer, not an `influenced_by` artist claim. [allmusic_gershwin_original, jazz_com_essentials]
- **Instruments/personal:** ONLY piano played by Ellis Larkins. No jazz quartet or orchestra for the eight ORIGINAL album masters. This is a strong example of why reissues cannot be substituted for original records. [musicbrainz_gershwin, pure_ella_original_liner]
- **Production:** `Milt Gabler` credited original producer; no documented analog-tape technique or hardware model, thus `production` intentionally empty. Instrument alone is not evidence for one-take production. [musicbrainz_gershwin, pure_ella_original_liner]
- **Scales:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2 (unhurried standards, gently swinging); electronic 0 (voice+acoustic piano only); mood 3 (mixed romantic warmth and yearning); theatrical 1 (close duets), experimental 1 (interpretive subtleties within familiar structures), polish 2 (spare period studio production). These are judgments anchored in Scott Yanow's and Stuart Nicholson's descriptions. [allmusic_gershwin_original, jazz_com_essentials]
- **Voice:** `clear|soft|powerful` reflects clean diction, tender pacing and controlled dynamic power, not a single 1950 album of extreme scat. **Lyrics:** romantic and personal longing, not an inferred political theme. `sounds_like` comparisons are explicitly Fandome editorial similarity to intimate Billie Holiday/Sarah Vaughan interpretations, NOT claimed influence.
- **Landmark=1:** historic first sustained solo Gershwin album and early model for later studio songbooks; not the same scale of cultural landmark as the 1956 Porter LP. [allmusic_gershwin_original, jazz_com_essentials]

## ella-songsinamellowmood: Songs in a Mellow Mood (1954)

- **Identity:** Twelve-track Decca 1954 LP, all recorded Mar 29–30, 1954. The *Pure Ella* liner supplies exact dates. AllMusic may display 1955 owing to another issue date: original Decca catalogue and Jazz Codex 1954 have greater weight for original LP year. [mellow_original_liner, jazzcodex_mellow, allmusic_mellow]
- **Genres / instrument:** `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing`. The latter is low-weight: Leonard Feather specifically says only a few cuts move into an easy moderate swing. The original consists of Ella and **Ellis Larkins piano**, no bass/drums or strings. Romantic Broadway/American Songbook standards. [allmusic_mellow, la_times_feather, jazzcodex_mellow]
- **Producer:** `Milt Gabler` on the original sessions, not Orrin Keepnews (the producer of the 1994 reissue *Pure Ella*). `production` intentionally empty in absence of directly identified studio process. [mellow_original_liner]
- **Scales:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2, electronic 0, mood 3, theatrical 1, experimental 1, polish 2. Feather hears soft, nuanced interpretive understatement and light movement; it is not a uniformly slow/no-groove record. [la_times_feather]
- **Vocals/lyrics:** clear, soft and capable of powerful climaxes; love, longing and playful irony; comparisons to Billie Holiday/Sarah Vaughan are editorial only. **Landmark=0:** precursor to songbooks but not a consistently named defining LP in the same way as the Porter project. [allmusic_mellow]

## ella-sweetandhot: Sweet and Hot (1955)

- **Identity and source difference:** 11 original album entries with music sourced across **1952–55**, unlike one new continuous 1955 session. The LP includes material with André Previn, John Scott Trotter, Benny Carter and Sy Oliver, plus earlier singles and 1953 radio-TV broadcast audio. This matters if Claude decides the album should be retyped or excluded under no-compilation policy. [jazzdiscog_sweet, jazzdiscog_session, squartet_sweet]
- **Genres:** album-level `vocaljazz|jazz|traditionalpop|standards` are explicitly listed by AllMusic. `swing` is also evidenced by *Mr. Paganini* and band-associated cuts, while `bigband` is only a subset/texture, not a claim of one consistent large-band session. Mood and lyrical content change considerably track to track. [allmusic_sweet, jazzdiscog_sweet, jazzdiscog_session]
- **Instrumentation:** across the ORIGINAL track list, AllMusic names saxophones, brass instruments, piano (Buddy Cole/Hank Jones), bass, drums and guitar; orchestra credits vary per track. `orchestra` is cross-session rather than a fixed ensemble. Do not assume specific instruments on individual songs without the session crosswalk. [allmusic_sweet_credits, squartet_sweet]
- **Producer:** deliberately **blank**. André Previn, John Scott Trotter, Benny Carter and Sy Oliver appear as conductors/arrangers on different dates, not proven producers of the compiled LP. This is distinct from intentionally fabricating a single record producer. `production` also blank: no verified consistent per-album process such as analog tape, overdubbing or named machines. [allmusic_sweet_credits, jazzdiscog_session]
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3, electronic 0, mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 3: mixed swing/scat showpieces and slower arranged ballads; stronger orchestral staging than duet albums. 1950s period recordings should not automatically be classified as lo-fi. `confidence=medium` for mixed sessions and production uncertainties.
- **Disagreement:** AllMusic digital issue has a misleading 1953 release value whereas the primary Decca LP DL8155 is documented in **1955**. Album credits can also mix expanded editions and original sessions. A 1953 television show recording among LP masters doesn't turn the whole album into a live concert, but does create the compiled-recordings caveat. [allmusic_sweet, jazzdiscog_sweet, jazzdiscog_session]

## ella-ellafitzgeraldsingsthecoleportersongbook: Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Cole Porter Song Book (1956)

- **Identity/reception:** 1956 original Verve two-LP 32-song project, first in the major composer Songbook sequence, later added to **2003 National Recording Registry**. Do not substitute 22-song abridged compilations or use 1990s remaster producer/engineer credits. [library_congress_registry, official_discography_1956, jazz_journal_porter]
- **Genres:** AllMusic names `vocaljazz|jazz|swing|traditionalpop|standards` and `bop` (last as minor element). `bigband` is confirmed by critic and session large-band charts. Some arrangements have orchestral strings/harp as well; it is not wholly a bare trumpet-section big-band record. [allmusic_porter, jazz_journal_porter, musicbrainz_porter_1956]
- **Instruments:** Buddy Bregman charts and actual credits list piano Paul Smith, guitarist Barney Kessel, Joe Mondragon bass, Alvin Stoller drums, a brass/reed section including trumpet, trombone and sax, string section and harp Corky Hale, plus various flutes, oboe and cello. `orchestra` expresses the large ensemble while specific instruments identify documented components. Original production **Norman Granz**, with **Buddy Bregman arranger/conductor only**; do NOT list Buddy as producer. `production` intentionally blank: 'arranged and conducted' by itself does not verify digital editing, overdubbing, named machines or even a specific per-track horn/strings technique token. [musicbrainz_porter_1956, musicbrainz_porter_collection, qobuz_porter]
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 (swinging songs plus ballads), electronic 0, mood 3 (romance, wit, regret), theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4 (rich studio orchestration). `clear|powerful|soft` reflects impeccable text delivery and supple dynamics; not an operatic voice or 'instrumental' LP. [allmusic_porter, jazz_journal_porter]
- **Critical disagreement:** Al Campbell praises Ella's interpretations and centrality of project; Leon Nock (Jazz Journal) criticizes Bregman's arrangements as pedestrian. The latter review treats an abridged *Essential* CD and says it is not the full original: its judgment of arrangements is useful but not its track count. Preserve difference, don't report unanimity. [allmusic_porter, jazz_journal_porter]
- **`influenced_by` remains empty**: Cole Porter is the composer whose songs are **covered**, not a self-named influence on this album; backing musicians, arrangers and guests are not influences. Editorial `sounds_like` suggests Frank Sinatra's orchestral standards and Sarah Vaughan's interpretations, not a claim that they influenced Ella's sessions. Landmark=1 supported by Library of Congress status.

## ARTIST INFLUENCES (not album-level)

- **Connee Boswell — confirmed.** Ella Fitzgerald personally recalled falling in love with a Boswell record and trying to sing like her. Her official biography explains the Boswell record she performed at the Apollo; Library of Congress recounts her own memory. This documents her as a formative **artist-wide** musical model, but does not document Connee directly shaping each 1950–56 LP; consequently no 1950–56 `influenced_by` cell is populated. [education_ella, official_biography, library_congress_ella]
- Louis Armstrong and Bing Crosby are mentioned as early listening favourites by the same education biography, and Dizzy Gillespie is often named for stylistic context; **not** treated as an album-level named influence without more direct artist/producer testimony. They may be considered for a later artist-wide influences research pass. [education_ella]
