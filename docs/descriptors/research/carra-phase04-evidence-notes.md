# Raffaella Carrà — Phase 04: researched descriptor evidence notes

Research cut-off: 25 October 2026. Every genre in the rows appears in `genre_evidence.csv`, with an exact source URL and scope. `some tracks` can reflect release-metadata genre attribution without proving the specific recording; such cases are identified below instead of fabricating track-level assignments. The 0–5 scores are **human aggregate judgments, not audio-feature measurements**. Blank cells are deliberate uncertainty. `influenced_by` remains blank throughout because no direct artist/producer influence claim was established. Writers, arrangers, mixers and executive producers are not automatically musical producers.

## Raffaella Carrà '82 (1982) — `carra-raffaellacarra82`

**Edition control:** Spanish Hispavox S 90.649 has **12 songs** (including `Tuca Tuca` and the Carrà/Franco Bracardi duet `Esta historia`). Italian Hispavox HVX 70701 has **10** (including `Ballo ballo`, `Una coppia da buttare`, `Mamma dammi 100 lire`, `Che dolor`). This dataset identifies the album campaign, not a single track-for-track universal master. Warner and Raffaella's official site confirm **Spain first in 1982, Italy afterward**. Ignore the isolated secondary claim of an original 1981 release. Italian and Spanish vocals reflect separate editions (`it|es`), NOT the assertion that every physical edition is bilingual. `My love` is only a title, not grounds to add English vocals. [Official Spanish](https://raffaellacarra.com/discografia/raffaella-carra-82/), [Official Italian](https://raffaellacarra.com/discografia/raffaella-carra-15/), [Warner](https://shop.warnermusic.it/products/raffaella-carr-82-cd).

**Style/genre evidence:** Rockol explicitly calls *Ballo ballo* **italo-disco** and hears an *Eleanor Rigby*-like string riff in the introduction. That is **sonic resemblance on one song**, not proof the Beatles influenced the album; therefore `influenced_by` and album-wide `sounds_like` are empty. The original Spanish LP is classified **Europop**, **Ballad** and **Chanson** in issue metadata. `Chanson` and `ballad` do not identify particular recordings from current evidence; they are retained as lower-confidence minority styles, preserving album heterogeneity. Musicbase additionally applies Disco to the *Ballo, ballo* single. [Rockol](https://www.rockol.it/news-723527/raffaella-carra-morta-canzoni-famose-testi-curiosita-discografia), [Spanish LP metadata](https://www.caprarecords.com/release/18012988/raffaella-carra-raffaella-carra-82), [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/danilo-vaona).

**Scores:** `energy 4`, `groove 4`, `tempo 4` are driven by the prominently dance-classified title track and related television singles but moderated by album-level ballad/chanson tags. `electronic 2` reflects the documented studio rhythm section (Massimo Luca, Gigi Cappellotto, Lele Melotti/Tullio De Piscopo) with keyboards from Piero Cairo; it is a cautious band-plus-keyboards aggregate, not a claim of pure electronics. `mood 4` recognises celebratory songs alongside romantic/nostalgic ones. `theatrical 4` is supported by Fantastico performance context, not a stage musical. `experimental 1` and `polish 4` are low-confidence editorial ratings of conventionally structured major-label television pop. `voice=clear` is provisional. `party|love|nostalgia|heartbreak` is based on dance themes, the documented return-home lyric in *Ballo ballo*, and mixed ballad repertory; entire-album lyric analysis remains incomplete. **Producer Danilo Vaona** confirmed by ANSA and Warner; Bracardi's songwriting does not make him producer.

## Fatalità (1983) — `carra-fatalita`

**Original object:** 10-track original Italian Hispavox HVX 70702; official archive prints **24 Nov 1983**. The Spanish-language edition followed: do not merge Spanish re-recordings into original Italian row or mistake the 2025 reissue date for original year. **Danilo Vaona** is documented as producer by ANSA; Paolo Ormi and Franco Bracardi credits in some references are arranger/writer roles, not established whole-album co-producer credits. [Official LP](https://raffaellacarra.com/discografia/fatalita-2/), [Warner](https://shop.warnermusic.it/products/fatalit-cd), [ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/cultura/musica/2025/06/10/warner-music-ripubblica-raffaella-carra-82-e-fatalita_bb940dbe-b4fa-47f1-8ba1-97c2d3624691.html).

**Genre contrasts:** Entire LP classified **pop / europop**, with **ballad** and **chanson** minority tags in contemporary-release databases. The title theme `Fatalità` is classified disco by Musicbase and italo-disco in a secondary original 7-inch entry, not an album-long uniform treatment. **Alberto Moneti**, a conservatory-trained multi-instrumentalist, identifies *Innamorata* specifically as a romantic, sensual **pop ballad**. ANSA confirms Roberto Carlos and Cristiano Malgioglio wrote that song, describes Carrà's affection for it, and separately names Malgioglio compositions *Spera, aspetta e spera* and *Gnam, gnam*. No evidence supports fabricating samba/bossa genre for Brazilian writers alone. [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/danilo-vaona), [LP metadata](https://www.merchbar.com/albums/raffaella-carr/fatalit-745228), [Moneti](https://www.accordiespartiti.it/accordi/italiani/carra-raffaella/innamorata/).

**Scores:** `energy 3`, `tempo 3`, `groove 3` balance the disco TV opening with ballad/chanson evidence. `mood 3` represents mixed celebratory and tender subject matter. `theatrical 4` is TV-theme delivery; `polish 4` is cautiously inferred from large-label professional production; `experimental 1` reflects conventional pop songcraft. **`electronic` blank** because no trustworthy album-wide electronic instrument lineup was established. `clear` voice cautiously; `love|heartbreak|sensuality|party` correspond to the romantic ballad and dance title song rather than every lyric. `landmark=1` is about its defining Pronto Raffaella signature-song context, not its artistic experimental status.

## Bolero (1984) — `carra-bolero`

**Original object:** CGD 20447, ten tracks, official site labelled `19/10/1984`; secondary pages sometimes give 1 December as release date. Keep original **year 1984**; annotate uncertainty rather than treating a recording/manufacture date as a definitive street date. Original Italian titles include `Competition` (some archive pages spell `Competicion`) and `Lovin' You Losin' You`. Titles alone do not establish English lead-vocal language, so `language=it` provisionally. Official site explicitly credits **Danilo Vaona as musical producer**, also responsible for composing/arranging many songs; Vaona is expressly `Producer [Artistic Production]` on sleeve transcription. Sergio Japino is **executive**, not musical, producer. [Official](https://raffaellacarra.com/discografia/bolero/), [original sleeve](https://www.vinileria.it/negozio/lp/raffaella-carra-%E2%80%8E-bolero/), [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/bolero-raffaella-carra/fwplhiaal3jfa).

**Styles vs tracks:** Original LP classifies **Italo-Disco, Europop, Chanson** with wider Electronic/Pop labels; another issue listing also says **Disco**. Musicbase separately files `Dolce Far Niente` 7-inch as **Theme/Ballad/Disco**, a hybrid, which demonstrates why ballad should not be discarded even when dance production dominates. Do not infer musical bolero rhythm merely because `Bolero` is the title; no `bolero` genre token is justified. Do not infer futurist electro just from `Il mio computer` title. [LP metadata](https://www.popsike.com/Rare-LP-33T-Raffaella-Carra-Bolero-ItaloDisco-oritaly-1984-CGD-20447/124669673405.html), [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/danilo-vaona).

**Production and scales:** Sleeve documents **Linn II electronic drum machine** and **PPG 2.3 synthesizer**, programmed by **Stephen Head**, Vaona on keyboards, plus **Luciano Ciccaglioni electric guitar** and multiple backing singers. `electronic=4` is supported by named programmed sound sources but kept below fully electronic 5. `polish=4` is high-finish studio dance-pop, not a source-confirmed budget. `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4` reflect strong disco and televisual repertoire; ballad/chanson lessen whole-album homogeneity. `mood=4` and `theatrical=4` refer to buoyant/performative TV presentation, tempered by `Stupida gelosia`, `Io ti amo` and `Lovin' You Losin' You`. `experimental=1` because no documented structural experimentation. Musical producer solely Vaona; no documented artist influence, so empty.

## Fidati! (1985) — `carra-fidati`

**Original object:** Fonit Cetra STLP 147, **26 Nov 1985**, ten songs. Official site links `Fidati!` and `Bellissimo` to the *Buonasera Raffaella* opening/closing credits, and `Bacio` to the U.S.-broadcast introduction. This is a television-linked **studio album**, not an official soundtrack release. [Original LP](https://raffaellacarra.com/discografia/fidati-2/), [RAI](https://www.radiocorrieretv.rai.it/raffaella-carra-artista/).

**Genre variety:** Sleeve-derived classifications include **Pop**, **Italo-Disco**, **Electronic** and **Ballad**. These are album-level release classifications: particular ballad tracks are **not established** and should not be named from titles such as `Bellissimo` or `Stasera io e te` alone. Do not claim `Quiero cantar` is a Latin-genre arrangement, nor count Spanish as a lead-vocal language, simply because of its Spanish title. [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/fidati), [Vinylzone](https://www.vinylzone.ru/catalog/net-v-nalichii/2715/).

**Instrumental heterogeneity:** Sleeve transcription credits **Danilo Vaona** (producer, arrangements, keyboards), **Giovanni Boccuzzi** (electronic drums), **Francesco Boccuzzi** (guitar, computer, grand piano), **Stephen Head** (programming), **Tonio Napoletano** (electric bass), **Sal Genovese** (tenor sax) and group backing voices. This is an electronics-plus-live-musicians mix, hence `electronic=3`, not a fully synth-only album. `groove=4`, `energy=4`, `tempo=4` lean on the original LP's Italo-Disco classification and TV-theme material, tempered by its Ballad tag. `mood=4` and `theatrical=4` are tentative. `polish=4` suits documented staffed studio production; `experimental=1` is a conventional accessible-pop judgment. `voice=clear` describes the lead, not credited backing vocalists. Lyrics `love|party` are broadly evidenced by original titles and mixed repertoire; full lyrics unverified. **Sergio Japino** is specifically **executive producer**, not in `producers`. **Gary Low** wrote and arranged *Quiero Cantar* but has no documented whole-album producer role and no documented status as an `influenced_by` artist. [Sleeve](https://recordsale.de/en/artists/raffaella-carra/albums/fidati), [Musicbase](https://musicbase.net/fidati).

## Cross-album methodology caveats

- Do not promote an album-level database tag into a fabricated song-by-song genre. That includes Spanish LP `chanson` on the 1982 album, or `ballad` on 1985 *Fidati!*.
- Genre tokens **are intentionally nonexclusive**. `italodisco|disco|europop|pop` co-occurrence is allowed, and minority styles are retained, while genre_evidence scope prevents the recommendations engine from interpreting every track identically.
- Distinct original market issues are **variants of one phase entry** unless future catalogue policy defines separate re-recordings as standalone releases.
- The album year, not the exact release date, populates the 25-column descriptor. All four albums are already released by 25 Oct 2026.
- `confidence=medium` for every row because verified original-issue data and musicianship credits substantially exceed the availability of detailed contemporary *album-by-album* critical listening notes. The sourced styles must not be confused with critic-verified precise audio characteristics.
