# Novecento — phase 01 evidence and decisions

**Preflight (27 October 2026).** Checked `src/data/catalog.ts`, `src/data/seed.ts` and `docs/descriptors/novecento.csv` on branch `descriptors`. This artist does not exist in either catalogue; descriptor file absent. Proposed id: `novecento`. No previous rows to skip. Album ids follow Fandome slug rule. All rows status `draft`.

**Evidence hierarchy:** original 1980s album/single credits, band-owned discography, interview with Pino Nicolosi, historical press > discography aggregators > edition-specific genre listings. Numeric 0–5 values are reasoned editorial judgements on albums/individual tracks, *not* statements of measured attributes. Production entries are intentionally empty where session techniques and named equipment could not be substantiated; missing evidence is preferable to fake machine credits. Vocal labels are editorial and should be verified by a listening panel. Lyrics left blank rather than derived from titles.

## novecento-novecento — Novecento (1984)

- **Edition:** WEA Italiana 240567-1 original 8-track LP. German press changes running order vs Italy (and its rear sleeve reportedly misprints order). No 1981 Divieto di Sosta EP: it was issued under an earlier name, before the Novecento identity.
- **Genre:** Musicbase identifies the self-titled 1984 album as Italo-Disco/Synth-pop, corroborated by German LP catalog. `funk` is explicitly track-level for **Take a Chance**: in a 2014 interview Alessandro Berni called it funky black music. `pop` is only broad track-level classification, not a claim of a uniformly pop album. Do **not** add new wave solely because some sellers list the release as New Romantic/New Wave while others say Synth Pop; such tags diverge and are not decisive for the album.
- **Players:** Qobuz Warner master of album track **Movin' On** individually credits Dora vocals, Lino guitar, Pino keyboards, Rossana bass. The 1984 album includes this recording. No evidence for a specific synth or drum-machine model on the full album: those cells not invented.
- **Producers:** `Novecento` credited on the **The Only One / Take a Chance** single; **Tony Carrasco** explicitly on **Movin' On** (1984 original recording), which appears on LP. Scope is track-specific; no assertion they jointly produced every track. **John Fryer mixed The Only One 12-inch**, not an album producer: exclude him.
- **Sounds-like:** *It's Too Late* receives comparisons to Chic, Change and Curtis Mayfield from interviewer Alessandro Berni. These are comparisons (`sounds_like`), not band-declared influences. The comparisons relate to a specific track and must not imply every song sounds that way.
- **Scales:** energy 3 / tempo 3 reflect 8-track mixture of radio-paced dance cuts and less-driving arrangements; electronic 3 because keyboards coexist with documented played bass/guitar; groove 4 because Italo-disco rhythm and funky Take a Chance anchor several songs; mood 3 neutral owing to mixed material; theatrical 2 for unforced vocal presentation; experimental 2 for some harmonic variety within conventional song form; polish 3 for a professionally recorded early LP. These remain editorial provisional scores.
- **Confidence medium:** pressing, track inclusion and credited personnel strong; a first-LP comprehensive credited equipment inventory and album-level review are missing.

## novecento-dreamland — Dreamland (1986)

- **Album:** Art Records ART 20491, eight songs on original Italian vinyl. Some *Japanese CD versions* contain **Changes** (1987), and later compilations include it. Pino Nicolosi confirms that the track appears only on a Japanese CD edition; not part of original 1986 LP. Apple digital files may show 1985 rights metadata but original physical release is dated 1986.
- **Genres:** whole-LP Italo-disco; `synthpop` supported by original **Excessive Love** single; Pino's interview cites *Excessive Love* as pop-rock-dance and *Dreamland Paradise* as an epic ballad. `poprock`,`dance`,`ballad` represent **specific album tracks**, not whole-program genres; ordered after the main style.
- **Instruments:** original Art LP personnel specifically credit Dora Carofiglio vocal, Pino keyboards, Lino guitar, Rossana bass, **Luciano Goitom drums** and **Angelo Pusceddu percussion**. No source names exact machines or overdub processing; `production` left empty.
- **Producers:** LP explicitly `Novecento`; **Carlo Stretti and Ernesto Tabarelli** are executive producers only, hence excluded.
- **Scales:** electronic 3 because electronic keyboard tones are balanced with documented acoustic drum/percussion plus live rhythm section; energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 are an album-average of rhythmic pop-rock opener and ballad; theatrical 3 for epic Dreamland Paradise; polish 4 supported by original detailed studio production and Pino's description of painstaking mixing. Mood 3 and experimental 2 remain weak editorial summaries; revise after full listening.
- **Influences:** Pino later acknowledged listening extensively to Genesis and Peter Gabriel when discussing harmonic writing echoing Tony Banks and recalling this early sound. Good **artist** evidence; not enough to assert that Genesis directly shaped *all of Dreamland*. Therefore `influenced_by` intentionally blank.

## novecento-shine — Shine (1988)

- **Year dispute:** the band-owned original discography, original Five Record FM 13611 LP and catalog of Italian 1988 release establish **1988**. Several retrospective lists call it 1989 because of Japanese King CD version: **retain 1988**. 8 original tracks; title and durations trace to primary vinyl record.
- **Genres:** Synth-pop and Electronic directly listed for the original 1988 LP. `italodisco` limited to **Broadway** and **Mama Say Ye** on separate original 1988 maxi; `pop` is broad classification of a later Japanese reissue, weaker than first pressing. Do not overstate jazz fusion or funk as album-wide genres from the band's later collaborations.
- **Instruments:** first LP credits specifically identify Rossana Nicolosi bass, Lino guitar, Pino keyboards, **Michael Rosen saxophone, Demo Morselli trumpet**, Dora vocals. Pino independently confirms Demo Morselli's trumpet solo on **Broadway**, which has more direct weight than a later generic band biography.
- **Producers:** `Novecento` credited as producer/arranger. L.P. Nicolosi and Gordon Milne are **mix engineers** (Gordon on Shine and Wild Life), not extra producers. `production` remains empty; mixing locations do not establish specific effects.
- **Scales:** electronic 3 indicates significant keyboard-led synthpop balanced by real rhythm/bass/horns; groove 3 for the mixture of Italo single cuts and melodic songs; energy 3, tempo 3 and mood 3 neutral; polish 4 backed by documented Nicolosi Studios sessions and deliberate separate mix engineering; experimentation 2 and theatrical 2 are conservative editing judgments.

## novecento-leavingnow — Leaving Now (1991)

- **Album:** Discomagic LP 531 original *eight-song* gatefold issued 1991, produced by **Novecento**, Studio Nicolosi. Genres as tagged for that original LP: **Downtempo / Euro House / Italo House** with broader Electronic parent. `house` is substantiated separately by original 1991 **Heart On The Line** single taken from this album; treat as album subset if necessary.
- **Instruments & production intentionally blank:** original LP public credits **do not expose individual musicians or actual synthesizer/drum machine models**. Do not fill with the band's general four-person roles, or transfer 2002 re-recording players/production into the 1991 master. This is a **documented evidence gap** and the row is suitable as a conservative draft only. For a stronger import, inspect original vinyl sleeve / complete LP liner scan for bass/guitar/keyboard and machine listings. The user rule prefers full rows, but it prohibits invented album instrumentation. `production` also blank for lack of source-named gear/technique.
- **Single vs album:** in 1996 **Billboard** reported a new *Leaving Now* single produced by **Arthur Baker**, but it was **not** the 1991 LP production. The 2002 **Featuring...** includes a later track called Leaving Now with Billy Cobham and Eddie Gomez; those names **MUST NOT** be attached to this album.
- **Scales:** electronic 4 / groove 4 based on album-specific house and electronic styles versus gentler downtempo selections; energy 3 / tempo 3 blend dance and softer passages; mood 2 / theatrical 2 for gentle-vocal downtempo material; polish 4 because LP records professional in-house mixing; experimental 2 for standard early-1990s club/pop song structures. If strict source-only judgement is required, clear mood, theatrical and experimental pending expert playback.
- **Confidence medium:** year, genre, producer well-supported, exact instrumental personnel not. Song themes left blank without lyric transcription.

## Artist influences vs collaboration (phase 01 only)

- **Strong first-person influence list**: [Pino Nicolosi speaking to Il Sussidiario (27 Dec 2014)](https://www.ilsussidiario.net/news/musica-e-concerti/2014/12/27/novecento-tutti-i-segreti-della-band-l-intervista-esclusiva/567637/): **Billy Cobham**, **Al Jarreau**, **Eumir Deodato**, **Brian Auger** named youthful idols and musical reference points; **Genesis** and **Peter Gabriel** named as records he listened to deeply. Explicit distinction: later production collaborations with Cobham/Jarreau/Deodato/Auger are not, by themselves, influence evidence; the separate childhood-listening statement establishes it.
- **Contemporary corroboration:** [Billboard, 14 September 1996, Italy news](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/90s/1996/BB-1996-09-14.pdf) quotes Lino Nicolosi's preference for richer harmonic structures, naming **Stevie Wonder**, **Elton John**, **Al Jarreau** as influences.
- **Secondary contextual list:** [Jazzitalia group biography](https://win.jazzitalia.net/recensioni/featuring.asp) also recounts the group's early listening to **Chaka Khan** and **Pink Floyd**, but not a direct attributable first-person quotation in this source. Keep these in `artist_influences` with **secondary/documented biographical** label rather than treating as verified band quote.
- Other instrumentalists listed in Nicolosi Productions' long collaborator roster (Sting, Santana, Billy Preston etc.) must not be turned into influences just because they worked together.

Exclude compilations *The Best* (1990), *Collection* (1993), *Greatest Hits* (2002/2003) and *Through the Years* (2012/2013). Discography later includes collaborations such as *Dreams of Peace* with Stanley Jordan and *Surrender* with Dominic Miller; revisit as potentially landmark co-billed studio releases when relevant, not automatically as solo Novecento albums.
