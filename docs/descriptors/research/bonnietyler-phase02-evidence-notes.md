# Bonnie Tyler — Fandome Phase 02: research and evidence notes

Four **new** studio album rows, separate from the 1977–81 Phase 01. Primary sources include Bonnie Tyler interviews and her official archive, Sony/Qobuz label credits, a CBS sleeve transcription, MusicBrainz release/track relations, and named AllMusic reviews. Scales, `sounds_like` and editorial secondary genres are original judgments; quoted critical genre labels are distinguished from interpretation in the genre evidence CSV. Every `influenced_by` entry requires a direct musical connection.

## Faster Than the Speed of Night (1983)

- **Identity:** original British CBS 1983 nine-track studio album, not a later remaster or the two-album CD pairing. The official fortieth-anniversary post reproduces the nine-track LP: https://bonnietyler.com/news/faster-than-the-speed-of-night-40th-anniversary-edition/.
- **Genre and mood:** AllMusic discusses operatic/gothic-scale guitar arrangements, arena-rock treatment and a restrained-versus-enormous contrast within “Total Eclipse”. Rock/pop-rock and arena rock lead; a power-ballad label belongs to the defining hit, so has high weight. Hard rock, soft rock and symphonic rock describe selected songs or textures, not uniform full-record subgenre classifications: https://www.allmusic.com/album/faster-than-the-speed-of-night-mw0000196124.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 5, experimental 3, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 3. Live band/rhythm and synth colours dominate over dance programming. The heartbreaking material sets mood lower than the bombastic arrangements might suggest.
- **Voice/instruments:** raspy and powerful Tyler singing, with choral/reply vocals in the big songs. Original CBS liner credits Roy Bittan (piano), Max Weinberg (drums), Rick Derringer (guitar), Steve Buslowe (bass), Larry Fast (synth), Jimmy Maelen (percussion) and further keyboards; label metadata independently confirms this: https://yperano.com/product/bonnie-tyler-faster-than-the-speed-of-night-lp-incl-total-eclipse-of-the-heart-check-video-2/?add-to-cart=310 and https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/faster-than-the-speed-of-night-bonnie-tyler/ew09t0ffil1uc.
- **Production:** primary credit Jim Steinman. *Important discrepancy:* the 1983 CBS sleeve calls John Jansen **associate producer**; Sony's digital metadata calls Jansen co-producer on many tracks. Under Fandome's rule excluding associate-only credits, this proposed row retains **Jim Steinman** pending review of the original legal credit; Jansen is not silently recast as album co-producer. Steinman’s wall-of-sound is explicitly named by Bonnie's official site; no specific synthesizer model has been verified.
- **Documented influence:** In her 1986 *Tracks* interview Bonnie explained that she pursued Steinman after loving his songs for Meat Loaf’s *Bat Out of Hell*, and that she wanted songs like those. This directly supports `Meat Loaf (Bat Out of Hell)` for the 1983 Steinman transition (musical project, not merely a critic's resemblance). The same work appears in `sounds_like` as an editorial sonic comparison. Source: https://www.jimsteinman.com/btyler1.htm.

## Secret Dreams and Forbidden Fire (1986)

- **Edition:** first US publication April 7, 1986; original LP has eight tracks, CD nine, cassette ten. “Before This Night Is Through” is included on CD/cassette, “Under Suspicion” cassette only. The 1984 soundtrack recording “Holding Out for a Hero” was incorporated into the original 1986 album: do not treat its earlier 1984 single release as a second album. Official chronology and formats: https://bonnietyler.com/news/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire-at-40/.
- **Style:** sources identify pop/rock, theatrical long-form rock, an AOR sheen and occasional synth-driven dance feel. `hinrg` and `synthpop` describe only selected electronic tracks, especially “Band of Gold”; they are ordered after the dominant rock tags. Editorial album scales are energy 4, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 5, experimental 3, polish 5, tempo 3 and groove 4, with the extra groove reflecting certain programmed dance tracks. Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/secret-dreams-forbidden-fire-mw0000192150, https://musicbrainz.org/release/9977410e-0c12-4879-a152-8f88da715483, https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/bonnie-tyler/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire-album_ffvnve.html.
- **Production/credits:** original LP and 1986 Tyler interview support **Jim Steinman** as credited producer; later aggregated summaries sometimes enumerate arranger/engineer collaborators Roy Bittan, Larry Fast, John Jansen and John Rollo as producers. Sony/Qobuz track-level entries credit Steinman directly. Do not conflate musicians, technical staff, arrangers or executive credits with formal album producers: https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire-bonnie-tyler/trjnryi6p0hfa; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/bonnie-tyler/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire-album_ffvnve.html; https://www.neverlandhotel.dk/album/449/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire.
- **Instruments/techniques:** Bonnie's direct interview confirms Larry Fast synthesizers, Roy Bittan keyboards/piano, Max Weinberg drums, Rick Derringer/Eddie Martinez guitar, drum machine plus synthesizers specifically on “Band of Gold”, overdubbed guitars, and Todd Rundgren's arranged/sung backing parts: https://www.jimsteinman.com/btyler1.htm. Original LP transcription confirms horn players, Linn drums and arrangements on “Holding Out for a Hero”: https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/bonnie-tyler/secret-dreams-and-forbidden-fire-album_ffvnve.html. Those techniques apply to *some tracks*, not the entire album; `linndrum`, `hornarrangements` and `drumprogramming` are song-specific.
- **Influences:** In that same interview Bonnie identified *Bat Out of Hell* as the model for wanting Steinman songs, and said she deliberately waited for him again rather than hiring a different producer. That qualifies as a record-specific musical creative direction, not a general assumption about Meat Loaf: https://www.jimsteinman.com/btyler1.htm. Todd Rundgren (featured singer) and Bryan Adams (covered songwriter) are collaborators and **not** placed in `influenced_by`.

## Hide Your Heart (1988)

- **Edition:** first album *Hide Your Heart* (1988); title *Notes from America* used for North/South American editions, not a second album. AllMusic explicitly documents the renaming: https://www.allmusic.com/album/hide-your-heart-mw0001223580.
- **Style:** AllMusic gives Pop/Rock and Album Rock. The original recording has a more direct 1988 commercial hard-rock/AOR guitar ensemble than Steinman's multi-part epics. The single “Hide Your Heart” and rock songs support hard rock as a selected-tracks genre; melodic ballads support soft rock/power ballad as secondary descriptors, not universal categories.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 4, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 3. Tyler remains dramatic, with tighter conventional song forms than on the 1983–86 albums.
- **Credited musicians:** MusicBrainz tracks confirm John McCurry guitars, Jerry Marotta drums/percussion, Tony Levin/John Regan/Seth Glassman bass, Chuck Kentis synthesizers/keys/organ and additional piano parts; other tracks identify backing singer Joe Lynn Turner and others. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/aba7f332-6b89-4ad0-8792-6bf85b4123be and https://alldp.narod.ru/turner/tyler.htm.
- **Producers/techniques:** Desmond Child only; songwriting credits for Holly Knight, Paul Stanley, Mike Chapman and others **do not** make those people producers. Specific machines or editing techniques are not verified, so `production` is deliberately empty. No direct album-specific influence statement was found, so `influenced_by` is blank; cover choices are not proof of influence.

## Bitterblue (1991)

- **Edition:** Hansa 1991 original, fourteen tracks. Do not confuse with the 1989 *Heaven & Hell* compilation/duets. Source: https://www.qobuz.com/nl-nl/album/bitterblue-bonnie-tyler/mvw13cff0rh3b.
- **Styles:** AllMusic describes mostly accessible pop tunes with pop-rock identity, while original Hansa CD cataloguing includes Synth-Pop and Euro-Disco; both styles apply primarily to the more electronic Bohlen/Moroder productions. Roy Bittan's set uses more conventional guitars/bass/drums, so pop rock and soft rock remain important. Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/bitterblue-mw0001047120, https://musicbase.net/bitterblue, https://sessiondays.com/2022/03/08/1991-bonnie-tyler-bitterblue/.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 4, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 4. Diverse track producers mean these are broad album-level editorial judgments, not a claim every song is electronic.
- **Instruments/production:** credit relationships name Teddy Castellucci, Tim Pierce, John Pierce and Waddy Wachtel (guitars); Randy Jackson (bass), Kenny Aronoff (drums), Roy Bittan (keyboards) and Gary Herbig (saxophone). Giorgio Moroder and Scott Greer have explicit programming credits. `programming` is therefore supported, but no particular drum-machine model is stated and none is inserted: https://sessiondays.com/2022/03/08/1991-bonnie-tyler-bitterblue/, https://www.allmusic.com/album/bitterblue-mw0001047120/credits.
- **Producer split:** Howard Houston (tracks 1,13), Dieter Bohlen (2,9,14), David Yorath (2), Roy Bittan (3,5,6), Nik Kershaw (7), Giorgio Moroder (8,10,11,12); Luis Rodriguez co-produced tracks 2,9,14. **All** qualify under credited producer or co-producer policy. Credits are not blanket per-track attributions. Source: https://sessiondays.com/2022/03/08/1991-bonnie-tyler-bitterblue/. Dan Hartman, a duet vocalist, is not a producer or an influence by virtue of guesting.
- **`sounds_like`** is editorial and is not asserted as a list of Tyler's declared influences. No direct 1991 session-specific influence statement reliably identified: `influenced_by` empty.

## Uncertainties, validation and interpretation

1. **1983 John Jansen:** original sleeve = associate producer, digital label metadata = co-producer. Conservatively excluded until owner/Claude resolves whether the latter should override original pressing. Original 1983 credit is explained above.
2. **1986 associated producers:** generalized metadata sometimes lists five producers. Original sleeve and Steinman/Tyler firsthand sources support Steinman as album producer; arranger/engineer roles remain separate. Owner may re-examine if track-specific co-production evidence surfaces.
3. **1986 format variance:** eight LP tracks, nine CD tracks, ten cassette tracks. No expanded-CD bonus cuts added to the original studio album row.
4. **1988 US rename:** *Notes From America* is **not** an additional album.
5. **1991 multistudio record:** stylistic heterogeneity is genuine. Long-tail electronic genres do not describe the entire fourteen tracks.
6. **Comparisons:** `sounds_like` is original sonic editorial interpretation. Covers, songwriter relationships and featured singers never qualify as documented musical influence on their own.
