# Scorpions — Phase 01: research and evidence notes

Scope: original studio issues 1972–76, not reissues or bonus tracks. Ratings are editorial 0–5 against Fandome anchors. Each `genres` token has a separate citation and scope in the genre_evidence CSV. Negative/absence claims are qualified when appropriate. Most support is original band credits, MusicBrainz recording relationships, AllMusic review metadata, independent interviews, and specialist discographies.

## Lonesome Crow (1972) — `scorpions-lonesomecrow`

**Sound and genres.** AOTY lists hard rock, progressive rock, psychedelic rock and Krautrock; AllMusic retrospective applies hard rock and album rock. The 13-minute title song and loose arrangements weigh the experimental score upward (4), while the dark, meditative feeling makes mood 1. The prominence of guitar improvisation is distinct from later short-form radio singles. Klaus Meine explicitly disputes the *Krautrock* label as the group's self-conception, although commentators hear its psychedelic and experimental proximity; retained with the disagreement visible. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/26469-scorpions-lonesome-crow.php); [band interview](https://thecircle.de/blogs/features/scorpions-im-exklusiv-interview-der-harte-weg-zum-welterfolg); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/lonesome-crow-mw0000201217).

**Instrumentation.** Original band/pressing credits: Michael Schenker lead guitar; Rudolf rhythm guitar; Lothar Heimberg bass; Wolfgang Dziony drums and percussion; Klaus voice, other musicians backing. Thus electricguitar, bass, drums, percussion, **no synth or keyboard token without credits**. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/lonesome-crow-2/); [original LP credits](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Lonesome_Crow/35048).

**Recording.** Conny Plank is sole producer. Dziony says the band tracked complete versions of songs together rather than separately overdubbing. Classify `liverecording` (live *in studio*), not live concert or an invented analog format. Klaus recalls about one week including the mix. [Dziony](https://scorpscollector.wordpress.com/2016/10/05/from-osnabruck-to-hamburg/); [Klaus](https://ultimateclassicrock.com/scorpions-rock-believer-interview/). Polish 2 is an editorial response to the raw live-session recording; electronic 0 means no documented electronic source, not proof that zero electronic sound can ever be heard. Vocals powerful, raspy and clear capture contrasting delivery, not a supposed voice processor.

**Lyrics.** Official track texts include psychological instability and peace-seeking (*I'm Goin Mad*, *In Search of the Peace of Mind*) plus relationship and physical desire (*It All Depends*, *Leave Me*). Main order introspection, love, sensuality. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/lonesome-crow-2/).

**Influence vs resemblance.** AllMusic compares the result to Black Sabbath, Led Zeppelin, Rolling Stones: *sounds_like*, not artist testimony. Rudolf said Pretty Things influenced how his own band should sound; Klaus identifies Led Zeppelin as formative British rock influence, and the group played those songs before recording. Only Pretty Things and Led Zeppelin connected as `influenced_by`. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/lonesome-crow-mw0000201217); [Rudolf](https://musicinsidermagazine.com/rudolf-schenker-scorpions/); [Klaus](https://thecircle.de/blogs/features/scorpions-im-exklusiv-interview-der-harte-weg-zum-welterfolg).

**Reception.** The historically formative debut does not automatically count as the band's core signature album: landmark 0. AllMusic's retrospective is critical while niche collector appraisals are positive; do not treat a single published review as consensus. 

## Fly to the Rainbow (1974) — `scorpions-flytotherainbow`

**Sound.** *Speedy's Coming* is a driving hard-rock single, but long sections in *Drifting Sun* and the nine-minute title track continue psychedelic rock and progressive design. AOTY explicitly labels hardrock/progressive/psychedelic; AllMusic also uses heavy metal and album rock labels. Pressing/fan reviews note one acoustic folk-rock passage (*Far Away*) and flamenco/Spanish-guitar rhythms (*They Need a Million*); low-ranked because not album-wide. Krautrock is a retrospective Wikipedia genre label, despite the band's own rejection of the category, and is the weakest category. Scales: energy 3, experiment 3, groove 2: the record mixes rockers and wandering ballads. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/26470-scorpions-fly-to-the-rainbow.php); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/fly-to-the-rainbow-mw0000199910); [MMA](https://www.metalmusicarchives.com/album/scorpions/fly-to-the-rainbow); [metal reviewer](https://www.metal-archives.com/reviews/Scorpions/Fly_to_the_Rainbow/35051/).

**Members and instruments.** First studio album with Uli Jon Roth, Francis Buchholz and drummer Jürgen Rosenthal. Roth and Rudolf on lead/rhythm electric guitar, various acoustic guitar passages; guest Achim Kirschning on Mellotron, organ and synth (confirmed by [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/76dfec17-d27c-3843-abb9-b02fc956f79d) and [original LP](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Fly_to_the_Rainbow/35051) even though official site may omit guest). Roth and Rudolf share occasional lead singing with Klaus (do not represent all tracks as Klaus-only). The spoken voice category is justified by Roth's spoken segment on title track in original LP credits. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/fly-to-the-rainbow-3/).

**Production and caveats.** The **official artist page** attributes production to **Frank Bornemann and Scorpions**. Track-level MusicBrainz repeatedly lists Frank Bornemann and on some tracks Scorpions; Mack has production or recording references in some metadata, but original release documentation primarily calls him engineer/recording. Preserve official album-wide production, note Mack credit conflict; do not turn every studio staff member into producer. Wahwah explicitly named in [album account](https://theultimatemusiclibrary.net/2022/09/19/scorpions-fly-to-the-rainbow-1974/) is the only production token. No other specific machines claimed.

**Lyrics and influences.** Official texts support fantasy/imagery, social-money critique and romance; Fandome themes social, love, introspection, storytelling. AllMusic directly hears Jimi Hendrix's *Spanish Castle Magic* in Roth's *Drifting Sun*. Roth independently names Hendrix as an influence. `Jimi Hendrix (Axis: Bold as Love)` in `sounds_like` identifies the album containing the referenced song; `Jimi Hendrix` in `influenced_by` identifies the actual musical influence, with distinct roles. Critic elsewhere hears Rainbow; kept only as `sounds_like`, not historic creative influence. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/fly-to-the-rainbow-mw0000199910); [Roth](https://rockmusicstar.com/2019/03/22/uli-jon-roth-interview/); [Metal reviewer](https://www.metal-archives.com/reviews/Scorpions/Fly_to_the_Rainbow/35051/).

## In Trance (1975) — `scorpions-intrance`

**Sound.** Cleaner and more focused hard rock/early heavy metal, with shorter songs, tight riffing, softer ballads. AllMusic labels hardrock/heavymetal/albumrock/arenarock; Warthur describes blues-rock elements and ballads. The bluesrock tag is track-scoped and should not outrank album-wide metal. Hair metal appears in AllMusic's broad retro-tag inventory but is chronologically inappropriate for the actual 1975 musical arrangements: excluded as a non-diagnostic catalog artefact, not translated into a 1970s subgenre. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/in-trance-mw0000204919); [MMA](https://www.metalmusicarchives.com/review/in-trance/337622).

**Personnel.** Roth/Rudolf guitars; Buchholz bass; newly joined Rudy Lenners drums and percussion; Achim Kirschning guest keyboards. [Official site](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/in-trance-5/); [original LP](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/In_Trance/1377627). The title-song's melodicism and energetic *Robot Man* contribute to mixed tempo, but the fast pieces merit energy 4, tempo 3. Mood 2 due to longing/darker narrative, theatrical 4 from Meine's dramatic lead delivery (editorial).

**Producers.** Dierks credited across original album. A MusicBrainz release credits Snuff Garrett on **Dark Lady only**; this is not reliably corroborated as a whole-album production role in original credits. Exclude from general `producers`, and flag for manual comparison of sleeves. The recording at Dierks Studios does not prove an identifiable analog machine or signal-chain technique: `production` empty by design. [Original edition](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/In_Trance/1377627); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/da78f173-5fb2-4bae-a459-53c36aa9019f).

**Themes and similarity.** *In Trance*, *Evening Wind* and *Life's Like a River* cover desire/ageing/inner conflict; not a uniform social/political album. Roth's Hendrix sound was described directly by fellow member Rudolf in a period-spanning interview, and a specialist critic hears Hendrix in Roth's fluid solos, so Hendrix fits both influence and sonic similarity with independent rationale. [Official lyrics](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/in-trance-5/); [Rudolf](https://www.vice.com/en/article/scorpions-rudolf-schenker-interview/); [MMA](https://www.metalmusicarchives.com/review/in-trance/337622). Landmark 1 for first clear classic formula in early chronology, not a sales ranking.

## Virgin Killer (1976) — `scorpions-virginkiller`

**Sound.** Fast hardrock/heavy metal with compact tracks and tense riffs; closes with melodic and psychedelic-tinged pieces. The main style labels are AllMusic/AOTY hardrock and heavy metal, and AllMusic retrospective albumrock/arenarock. The official site describes it as the transition away from psychedelic rock; psychedelicrock remains last as minor track colouring. Ratings reflect more focused heaviness (energy 4, experiment 2), with some softer material (mood 2). [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/virgin-killer-r17519); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/26472-scorpions-virgin-killer.php); [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/virgin-killer-4/).

**Instruments and performers.** Klaus Meine leads; Uli Jon Roth additionally sings on *Hell-Cat* and *Polar Nights*; Rudolf rhythm and Roth lead guitars; Francis Buchholz bass, Rudy Lenners drums/percussion. Achim Kirschning is also credited with keyboards and synthesizer by both the band and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/42c9910a-0eae-49cc-af66-42773a200b35). Rank electric guitars, bass and drums first. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/virgin-killer-4/); [original pressing](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Virgin_Killer/35057).

**Production.** Dieter Dierks, no invented second producer. The original edition credits a **32-track** studio recording. Since the Fandome list lacks a general original-multitrack token and no specific overdubbing or effect chain is confirmed in a primary source, `production` is deliberately empty. It does not imply that the record lacks production techniques. [Metal Archives original vinyl](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Virgin_Killer/35057).

**Influences.** Roth's own acknowledged Hendrix connection and Rudolf's account of the Roth/Hendrix style justify `influenced_by=Jimi Hendrix`. No album-specific critical sonic artist comparison robust enough for `sounds_like` was confirmed, so leave it blank instead of guessing Deep Purple, UFO or Rainbow. The album title is not itself a genre or mood. [Roth interview](https://rockmusicstar.com/2019/03/22/uli-jon-roth-interview/); [Rudolf](https://www.vice.com/en/article/scorpions-rudolf-schenker-interview/).

**Artwork caution.** The original cover contains an unacceptable sexualised nude image of a child. This archive contains only text and CSV metadata, **no album artwork, thumbnails or cover-art URLs**. Release 1976 is corroborated by official album page; do not import unsafe imagery as the default artwork. The US 1977 release refers to a later territory-specific issue. 

## Source hierarchy and integrity

Original-era release and label credits > band's official release page > track-level MusicBrainz > signed critiques > fan review/listening accounts. Genres from anonymous review comments are labelled as weaker/track-scoped. No producer is assigned from a reviewer's generic use of the word producer. A retrospective tag such as arena rock is not evidence that the original album was made for an arena audience. Genre rows flag these differences so Fandome's recommendations remain explainable.
