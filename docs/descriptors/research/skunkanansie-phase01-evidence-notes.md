# Skunk Anansie — Phase 01 evidence notes

**Scope and method.** These are four ORIGINAL studio albums (1995, 1996, 1999, 2010). Main Fandome ID is `skunkanansie` (checked on branch `descriptors`). Numbers on the eight scales are editorial assessments calibrated against the Fandome guide, *not* scores published by critics; supporting critical passages are cited per album below. Items without a defensible album-specific source are left blank. Genre evidence distinguishes album-wide style from track-level touches. Acoustic/electronic instruments are only listed when credited or identified on THIS record. Credit aggregation across deluxe reissues was avoided. `sounds_like` is editorial sonic similarity and **does not** assert documented influence; `influenced_by` is empty for all four albums because specific album-causal testimony was not verified. Documented general ARTIST INFLUENCES are in CLAUDE_IMPORT.md, phase 01 only.

## Paranoid & Sunburnt (1995) — `skunkanansie-paranoidsunburnt`

- **Release and production.** [Official Skunk Anansie listing](https://skunkanansie.bandcamp.com/album/paranoid-sunburnt) confirms the 1995 11-song album; [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/paranoid-and-sunburnt-mw0000645356) and [MusicBrainz's original-release credit transcriptions](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2492f744-58a7-33fa-924d-85cbede47824) explicitly credit **Sylvia Massy AND Skunk Anansie** as producers. Andy Wallace is principally a mixer here, not an album producer. AllMusic erroneously shortens/reconciles release versions elsewhere: use original year only.
- **Genres.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/paranoid-and-sunburnt-mw0000645356) calls its broad category *Pop/Rock* and enumerates alternative metal, alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, heavy metal and hard rock. Genre summary also names alternative rock and punk rock; [Louder's historical article](https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-story-of-skunk-anansies-debut-single) documents the punk/metal-adjacent scene. A sharp **funk edge** noted by AllMusic is carried as late `funk` with scope *texture*, not an album-defining funk genre. `alternativemetal` is a genuinely new Fandome genre token and not silently collapsed into `metal`.
- **Instruments and technology.** Original [MusicBrainz recording relationships](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2492f744-58a7-33fa-924d-85cbede47824) name Ace's **electric guitar**, Cass's **bass**, Robbie France's **drums** and Skin's vocals. I have not imported the *later* drummer Mark Richardson into the 1995 lineup. [Skin's memoir as reported by Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-story-of-skunk-anansies-debut-single) recalls recording **Selling Jesus** as if they were playing live to regain their onstage feel: `liverecording` is justified only by that *song-specific description*, not a claim that every track was taped in a single live take. No manufacturer/console/model or tape medium is established. Aggressive guitar timbre alone is not grounds to invent a distortion effect/machine entry.
- **Scales** **5 / 0 / 1 / 4 / 2 / 2 / 4 / 3** (energy, electronic, mood, theatrical, experimental, polish, tempo, groove). Forceful fast guitar music, low electronics, tense political mood and conspicuously commanding vocals support extreme energy/theatrical. `groove=3` credits the documented rhythmic funk edge without classifying the record as dance music. Polish lower than later records as reviewer emphasizes a direct no-frills attack; it is a professional studio album and not demonstrably `lofi`.
- **Voice, lyrics, and comparisons.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/paranoid-and-sunburnt-mw0000645356) calls Skin powerhouse and [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-story-of-skunk-anansies-debut-single) details aggressive delivery and anti-racist/anti-fundamentalist lyrics. `social|political|identity` lead the theme order; `irony` from **Selling Jesus** satire. Quiet vocal moments justify `soft`, but not a separate operatic/falsetto label. Comparisons to Rage Against the Machine and Living Colour describe intensity and funky riffs; they are not listed as *album-specific influences*, though Skin later documented them as general band influences.
- **Disagreement:** original date reported as 18 Sep on some histories and 21 Sep on official Bandcamp; neither affects the year field. Retrospective metal framing coexists with a punk/hard-rock/alternative reading: tags keep this evidence in different positions rather than present one critical taxonomy as definitive.

## Stoosh (1996) — `skunkanansie-stoosh`

- **Credits.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/stoosh-mw0000021285) and [Riffology's personnel audit](https://vault.riffology.co/album/skunk-anansie-stoosh-cfd79ed2) name **Garth Richardson (aka GGGarth)** as producer. They are the **same person**; list once. **Joe Barresi mixed** the album: do not put him in producer field. Len Arran is a songwriter, not a credited producer. No evidence that the band was also co-producer.
- **Genre hierarchy.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/stoosh-mw0000021285) directly lists alternative metal, alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, heavy metal and hard rock. [Independent reviewer](https://textlambrecht.com/music-and-album-reviews/album-reviews-by-artist/skunk-anansie/) labels alternative rock, grunge and hard rock; [Riffology](https://vault.riffology.co/album/skunk-anansie-stoosh-cfd79ed2) includes rock and pop. Restrict pop and grunge to hook-oriented or grunge-touched **parts**, not the album's principal tag.
- **Instruments.** [MusicBrainz original-edition track credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9618e7b9-3137-46ef-bf5a-764dd1844218) support **electric guitar, bass, drums, percussion, Skin's theremin, programming** (an interstitial instrumental), while [Riffology](https://vault.riffology.co/album/skunk-anansie-stoosh-cfd79ed2) lists **violins, Martin McCarrick's cello, and Mr. Colson's Hammond organ**. `strings` as a *generic* instrument is omitted because `violin|cello` give more precise documented parts; no named string-arranger credit strong enough to justify `stringarrangements` in `production`. Theremin is not extrapolated to other albums.
- **Production.** `programming` arises from the **documented** hidden instrumental/pregap material credited to Darren Vaz and the band; it does *not* imply programmed drums throughout. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9618e7b9-3137-46ef-bf5a-764dd1844218) notes pregaps in this specific CD pressing, while the [official original track list](https://skunkanansie.bandcamp.com/album/stoosh) contains eleven standard songs. Do not create a separate album for hidden tracks; the source's AllMusic 76-minute runtime reflects anomalous bonus/hidden indexing, not the band's official 11-track presentation.
- **Scales** **4 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 2**. High-impact but more melodic and sometimes quieter than debut. The band said the writing was more emotional and inward-looking in [archived contemporary interview](https://skunk.kelv.net/interview.html); [AllMusic's review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/stoosh-mw0000021285) highlights political **Yes It's Fucking Political** against intimate **Infidelity**. Electronic remains low even with interstitial programming/theremin. Mood is dark/mixed, groove modest, refined but not glossy production.
- **Influence and comparison.** The producer's prior work with Rage Against the Machine is **not proof** they influenced this album. `sounds_like` comparisons are editorial only. No album-specific causal quote was located, so `influenced_by` deliberately blank despite documented general RATM and grunge inspiration of the band.

## Post Orgasmic Chill (1999) — `skunkanansie-postorgasmicchill`

- **Credits and album boundary.** The [artist's original album page](https://skunkanansie.bandcamp.com/album/post-orgasmic-chill) specifies 22 Mar 1999, with twelve tracks. The [1999 CD credits as reproduced by Dork](https://readdork.com/album/skunk-anansie-post-orgasmic-chill) credit **Andy Wallace** as producer AND mixing engineer. **Clif Norrell** is engineer, **Howie Weinberg** masters; neither of the latter belongs in `producers`. Do not blend in newly added tracks from the 2024 expanded anniversary set.
- **Genre evidence.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/post-orgasmic-chill-mw0000243100) classifies hard rock, alternative metal, alternative pop-rock, alternative indie rock, heavy metal and club/dance; it also describes the dub-like interlude on **The Skank Heads**. [1999 Independent review](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/pop-jazz-album-reviews-1081912.html) specifically calls out **drum'n'bass and hip-hop** in parts; [Skin's firsthand retrospective](https://www.loudersound.com/bands-artists/skin-shares-stories-behind-10-skunk-anansie-classics) describes the cross-style construction of **Charlie Big Potato** and **Secretly**. New genres must follow these *limited scopes*: the bulk is forceful guitar rock, not a uniformly dance/hip-hop album. `drumandbass`, `hiphop`, and `dub` are at end of `genres`.
- **Instrumentation.** [AllMusic personnel](https://www.allmusic.com/album/post-orgasmic-chill-mw0000243100): electric/acoustic guitars; Cass on bass and programming; Mark on drums and percussion; Andy Wallace on **Hammond organ**; Skin on **theremin, vibraphone and vocals**; Wil Malone arranged/conducted. `mallets` corresponds to vibraphone. Its **strings** are explicit in AllMusic critic's account of the orchestrated texture. In a frequently imported album-credit database, the guitar player is mistakenly shown as **Ace Frehley**; band member is Ace / Martin Kent. Don't add a guest or new producer on that basis.
- **Production.** `programming` follows Cass's documented programming credit. `stringarrangements` combines original Wil Malone arranger/conductor credit with review-confirmed string parts; individual tracks have orchestration, not necessarily all twelve. Despite one review's electronic/dance comparisons, no evidence identifies a specific sampler, drum machine model, sequencer, software, or vocal Auto-Tune. None are entered.
- **Scales** **4 / 2 / 2 / 4 / 3 / 4 / 3 / 3**. Strong riff attacks alternate with mid-tempo, emotive ballads. The electronics/groove score acknowledges some rhythmic crossovers; guitar band dominates. More layered and smoother sound than the debut; [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/post-orgasmic-chill-mw0000243100) emphasizes both shock contrasts and polished strings. Theatrical singing noticed in the [Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/pop-jazz-album-reviews-1081912.html).
- **Disagreement.** [AllMusic's reviewer](https://www.allmusic.com/album/post-orgasmic-chill-mw0000243100) hears an accomplished hard-rock album; [The Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/pop-jazz-album-reviews-1081912.html) finds the stylistic additions distracting and voices overblown; [Skin's own 2026 song-by-song interview](https://www.loudersound.com/bands-artists/skin-shares-stories-behind-10-skunk-anansie-classics) separately highlights the strings in **Secretly** and the song's varied textures. The row includes variety without declaring any one critic definitively right. `sounds_like` Police comes from AllMusic's explicit comparison of one instrumental passage; NOT evidence the Police influenced creation.

## Wonderlustre (2010) — `skunkanansie-wonderlustre`

- **Album/year.** [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/wonderlustre/1519620902) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/wonderlustre-mw0002023363) give 2010 for the original 12-track album. Do not use an expanded thirteen-track or 2014 tour edition. 
- **Producers with track precision.** [Original CD credit transcription](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/skunk-anansie/wonderlustre-album_ffeeee.html): **Skunk Anansie** produced tracks 1,3,5–8,11,12; **Brio Taliaferro** and **Jeremy Wheatley** produced tracks 2,4,9,10. On track 1, both outsiders are credited **additional production**, which *alone* would not suffice, but their credit as full producer on the other four songs does. [Drummer Mark Richardson's firsthand contemporary interview](https://renownedforsound.com/qa-skunk-anansie/) broadly corroborates both production groups while differing in the number of self-produced tracks and the total chronology of work: prefer the **track-indexed sleeve credits** for the precise split. **Chris Sheldon** was engineer, **Cenzo Townshend** a mixer; do not mislabel either as producer.
- **Instruments and production.** [Original credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/skunk-anansie/wonderlustre-album_ffeeee.html) detail Ace's guitars, Cass's bass, Mark's drums/percussion, **Hammond organ (Toby Baker, tracks 1,3,12)**, **piano** (James McMillan/ Skin), keyboards (Taliaferro), and **strings** arranged by Brio Taliaferro, Oliver Kraus and Skin on **Talk Too Much**. Programming by Taliaferro on tracks 1,2,4,9,10; the row uses `programming|stringarrangements` and **does not** invent a string synth, sampler or recording platform. Some supplementary CD editions credit extra piano/programming; broad instruments retained because in core tracks, not bonus tracks.
- **Genre ranking.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/wonderlustre-mw0002023363) explicitly calls the music **post-grunge and alt-rock** and lists alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, and *Britpop* as style metadata. [Sputnikmusic reviewer](https://www.sputnikmusic.com/review/39151/Skunk-Anansie-Wonderlustre/) calls the band hard rock and identifies crunchy guitar on specific tracks; poppier songs don't make the whole album pop. **Britpop is retained low only because a source actually labels it**, but it conflicts with the band's identity: Skin describes their scene as distinct from Britpop ([Radio X](https://www.radiox.co.uk/news/music/skunk-anansie-skin-story-whitewashed-britpop/)). Do not set the `scene` to Britpop; this metadata term is a debated label, not endorsement of the movement.
- **Scales** **3 / 2 / 3 / 3 / 2 / 4 / 3 / 2**: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/wonderlustre-mw0002023363) finds the music unusually cohesive, tuneful, still tense, often post-grunge; [Sputnikmusic](https://www.sputnikmusic.com/review/39151/Skunk-Anansie-Wonderlustre/) stresses melodic ballads over full-speed aggression. Moderate electronic because some documented programming and keyboards, not an electronic-dominant production. Mood mixed: confident with still darker lyrics. Production slicker, experiments subtler. Low dance orientation.
- **Lyrical detail and voice.** [Mark Richardson](https://renownedforsound.com/qa-skunk-anansie/) discusses the self-destructive relationship theme of **My Ugly Boy** and changing lyrical concerns; [Sputnikmusic](https://www.sputnikmusic.com/review/39151/Skunk-Anansie-Wonderlustre/) notes fewer overtly political lyrics and more personal songs. Skin ranges from intimate to forceful, as [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/wonderlustre-mw0002023363) observes.
- **Historical album status.** This is the first full album after the reunion. `landmark=0` says **not a consensus career-defining artistic work** compared with initial 1990s era; not a judgment that the reunion itself lacks importance. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/wonderlustre-mw0002023363) is approving, while [Sputnikmusic](https://www.sputnikmusic.com/review/39151/Skunk-Anansie-Wonderlustre/) is more reserved about its pop leanings. No documented album-specific artist influences established.
