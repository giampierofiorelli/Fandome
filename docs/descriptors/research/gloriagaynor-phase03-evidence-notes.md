# Gloria Gaynor — Phase 03: evidence and editorial decisions

Research: 28 October 2026, public GitHub Fandome `descriptors` branch read-only; original LP programmes used, not remastered bonus tracks. All 0–5 values are editorial judgments guided by observed instrument/genre information rather than claimed measurements. Each `genre` entry has a scoped supporting record in `genre_evidence.csv`; low and medium evidence must not be passed off as primary documentation. All four `influenced_by` fields are deliberately **empty**: performing somebody else's song, collaborating with a songwriter, or sounding similar to another artist does not establish that artist as a documented influence on this album.

## I Kinda Like Me (1981)

- Released June 1981 on Polydor, despite a 1982 Yugoslav LP pressing. **Original producer credits**: Gene McFadden, John Whitehead and Jerry Cohen. The record was made at Philadelphia's Sigma Sound; engineer Dirk Devlin. Sleeve personnel reproduced by Musik-Sammler include Jimmy Williams (bass), Keith Benson (drums), Dennis Harris/Eddie Sierra/Roland Chambers (guitars), Jerry Cohen (keyboards and synthesizer), Daryl Burgee (congas), Sam Peake (saxophone and horn arrangements), Don Renaldo (strings) and supporting singers Barbara Ingram, Carla Benson and Evette Benson. Synth arrangement is credited to Jerry Cohen and Larry Gold; it is not enough evidence for a particular synthesizer model or for drum programming.
- Pop Rescue reviewed the original nine-track vinyl in detail: title song and I Can Stand the Pain combine funky bass, urgent disco rhythm and strings; Lets Mend What's Been Broken and I Love You Cause lower the energy; Chasin' Me into Somebody Else's Arms is specifically labelled disco-pop, so that genre goes **last** in the row, as a single-track component.
- `energy=3`, `groove=4`, `electronic=1`: mostly live rhythm section despite a credited synthesizer and extended disco arrangements. `mood=3`: upbeat self-affirmation and slower/heartbreak moments coexist. Genre `soul` and `rnb` receive medium support from the Philadelphia idiom and review context rather than a stand-alone album taxonomy; remove if an editor requires direct genre label. **No confirmed album-level influences.**
- Sources: https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/gloria-gaynor-i-kinda-like-me-lp-1651075/ ; https://poprescue.com/2023/05/05/review-i-kinda-like-me-by-gloria-gaynor-vinyl-1981/ ; https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/gloria-gaynor/ .

## Gloria Gaynor (1982)

- **Original Atlantic record** (80033-1, 1982), not the 1997/2014 CD programme. The later Hot Productions / Big Break CDs append at least one later version of I Will Survive; **do not treat those recordings as originally on this album**. AllMusic's track listing is based on an expanded edition and is not the original eight-song sequence.
- Four original album producers are **Ollie E. Brown, Amir Bayyan, Rick Stevens and Yves Dessca**, as reported by the specialist reissue analysis and AllMusic credits. Do not accidentally include Linwood M. Simon, who is credited as executive producer/director on some secondary metadata rather than a track producer. Separate songs have different producers. The source sometimes prints “Rick Stevens” twice; deduplicated.
- Instrument credits across the original set: several bassists (including James Jamerson, Wayne Brathwaite), drummers Yogi Horton / Andy Newmark and Ollie E. Brown, Paul Jackson Jr. and Joe Beck guitars, keyboards and synth by Kashif, John Barnes, Lenny Underwood and Amir Bayyan, Carlos Franzetti electric piano, saxophone by David Sanborn and Dave Tofani, plus horns and percussion. Kashif is specifically credited with an OB-X synthesizer in session listings, but **the current Fandome token `oberheimobx` should not be used without confirmation from an original LP detail**; general `synth` suffices.
- Justin Kantor (AllMusic) describes early-80s R&B mid-tempo grooves and pop-oriented dance tracks, calling *Mack-Side* a quiet-storm entry. The Second Disc explicitly calls the production **post-disco electronic R&B**, identifies the funk of Mack-Side and synthesized Tease Me. The 1982 album retains the disco tag, but post-disco R&B heads the genres and the lower tempo reflects the midtempo material.
- `electronic=3` reflects credited synth, keyboards and electronics amid live players; `mood=4` mostly warm/uplifting with more introspective ballads. `quietstorm` is **track-specific** and goes last. Producer/performer participation by Kool & the Gang members does **not** create a documented `influenced_by` entry.
- Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/gloria-gaynor-mw0000235234 ; https://theseconddisc.wordpress.com/2014/06/24/big-break-big-round-up-divas-edition-label-reissues-carolyn-franklin-gloria-gaynor-patti-labelle/ ; https://musicbase.net/gloria-gaynor-3awts2v .

## I Am Gloria Gaynor (1984)

- Original **eight-song** release, published by Silver Blue/Chrysalis, not the 1996 `I Am What I Am` ten-track CD that **added two recordings from Park Avenue Sound**. One primary pressing is catalogued in MusicBrainz as 1984 Chrysalis 206151; copyright is 1983, and the title-track single first appeared in 1983. Retain `year=1984` for the original album; there are mixed 1983/1984 discography claims, but the documented main LP is 1984.
- **Joel Diamond** is the main producer; MusicBrainz credits **Shep Pettibone** as an additional producer/remixer specifically on *I Am What I Am* and Marcus Miller as arranger of that recording. Pettibone belongs in evidence notes and the limited `remixing` production token, **not** as main album producer. Avoid assigning Marcus Miller a bass instrument credit simply because he is known as a bass player: credited as arranger for this title.
- Reissue metadata lists Russell Blake bass, Frank Vilardi drums, Bobby Messano guitar, Ray Chew Fender Rhodes, Marcus Barone keyboards, Stan Bronstein saxophone, Ken Gross trumpet and Ray Mantilla percussion. **Some credits may apply to added 1978 tracks in later reissues**; album-wide confidence stays `medium` pending original sleeve confirmation. Other named instruments have corroborating live/rhythm context but the track-level split remains unresolved. No synth hardware/drum-machine model is documented.
- Opening title *I Am What I Am*, written by Jerry Herman for *La Cage aux Folles*, generates `showtunes` as **one-track** genre last; this is an interpretation of a show song on an otherwise dance-pop / disco / R&B LP, not an entire cast recording. Covers are **not** formal influences. `landmark=1` is an editorial judgement because the culturally prominent single anchors this album; assess at import separately from musical qualities.
- Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/780f04c4-b4d9-414a-8427-60d0c4786d29 ; https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/gloria-gaynor/ ; https://www.eruce.com/store/album/i-am-what-i-am-gloria-gaynor-qo73n132px/ .

## The Power (1986; also known as The Power of Gloria Gaynor)

- **Studio album, not a compilation of past recordings.** Original 1986 UK Stylus LP **The Power** (SMR 618) contains **new recordings** of then-contemporary hits and standards, among them *Eye of the Tiger*, *Everybody Wants to Rule the World*, *Every Breath You Take*, *Broken Wings*, *Suddenly* and *I Want to Know What Love Is*. Two Gaynor co-writes, *Don't You Dare Call It Love* and *Top Shelf*, also appear. The original gatefold package includes a separate bonus disc with a **newly recorded** greatest-hits medley and a remix; do not mistake this bonus for a prior-hits compilation. For album-level scoring, privilege the fourteen new main LP recordings over bonus medley/remix.
- **Canonical first-edition title**: *The Power* per original Stylus release and MusicBrainz; AllMusic and reissues often label *The Power of Gloria Gaynor* or *The Power of Love*. Use `gloriagaynor-thepower`; when importing, **do not create duplicate** for `gloriagaynor-thepowerofgloriagaynor` (tentative ID in phase02 manifest). Preserve only one studio album record. See https://musicbrainz.org/release/31fa7810-5301-4abf-91b6-4eee6e42781a .
- **Steve Rowland** produced at Red Bus Studios, London, engineered by Gordon Milne (with John Smit and Matt Budd credited on vinyl). LP sleeve credits as transcribed by Musicbase: Ray Russell guitar, Andy Pask bass, Neal Wilkinson drums, Jess Bailey keyboards, Ed Starink synthesizer, Phil Todd and Dick Morrissey saxophones, Pete Beachill trombone; Richard Niles arranger. No specific synth machine model verified. The LP includes an actual bonus remix, so `remixing` is justified but *only for the original bonus disc*; set it last/empty if Fandome production explicitly excludes bonus discs.
- *Pop*, *softrock* and *poprock* describe the dominant song choices; *R&B*, *disco* persist in AllMusic style taxonomy, *synthpop* and *ballad* apply to parts of the repertoire and are correspondingly later in the genre list. `energy=3`, `groove=3` avoid treating this cover-driven LP as a return to a full continuous disco suite. These covers do **not** license `influenced_by=Survivor|Tears for Fears|The Police|Foreigner`.
- Sources: https://musicbase.net/the-power-238nq6k ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-power-of-gloria-gaynor-mw0000847362 ; https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/gloria-gaynor/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_of_Gloria_Gaynor .

## Integration / evidence policy

- All 27-column rows marked `draft`. No earlier-phase row is repeated. Existing original album IDs must win if private working branch already includes an album under a different ID; check and reconcile before importing.
- Never paste review copy into the row notes. `sounds_like` are Fandome sonic analogies, **not** claims the artist cited these musicians as influences.
- No verified album-specific influence from a named musician is documented; empty `influenced_by` is deliberate rather than forgotten. No illicit retrofitting of instrumentation or producer hardware.
- New genre, instrument and production tokens: **none** (all proposed labels are already in respective vocabularies). Co-billed releases: **none**. No external repository was changed.
