# Depeche Mode — Phase 03 evidence notes (27 October 2026)

**Scope:** exactly three original studio albums. No compilation, live album or repeat row from phases 01–02. Values are judgements from *these albums*, guided by the Fandome scale anchors. Evidence sources with working article pages are in `depechemode_phase03_sources.csv`. `depechemode` is a **proposed** ID: absent from public `descriptors` catalogue at audit time.

## Violator — 1990 — `depechemode-violator`

- **Original edition and production.** Mute STUMM 64 dated 19 March 1990, nine full songs, not remastered/reissued bonus tracks. Original credits name **Depeche Mode and Flood** as producers. François Kevorkian mixed most of the LP but is not credited as a producer; the `Enjoy the Silence` mix involved Daniel Miller (some sources also credit Flood, while the later Mute catalogue gives Miller and Phil Legg). Do not add any of these mix-only names to `producers`. Compare original sleeve reproductions and Kevorkian interview.
- **Genres and scope.** The album-level AOTY categories `synthpop`, `alternativedance`, `darkwave` are primary. A separate album index names `electropop`, `alternativerock`, `gothicrock`, `dance`, `electro`, `electronic` and `rock`; the last four are broad or particularly evident on selected tracks. `Electropop` must remain separate from `synthpop`. The AllMusic review hears blues guitar and hip-hop-derived funk beats, not a full blues-rock album. AllMusic expressly tags `World in My Eyes` with `dancerock`, `collegerock` and `alternativepoprock`; these are appended as **track-scoped** tags, not claimed as core whole-album classifications.
- **Energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 4.** Mid-tempo melodic electro-pop predominates, with forceful dance grooves on `World in My Eyes`, `Personal Jesus`, `Enjoy the Silence`, alongside quieter songs `Waiting for the Night` and `Blue Dress`. Slower moments prevent energy/tempo 4–5. Critics discuss album's rhythmic and melodic balance.
- **Electronic 5 / experimental 3 / polish 5.** Almost all of the arrangements depend on programmed/sampled electronic sound, despite prominent blues guitar. Technological experimentation includes original drum loops, recontextualised string samples and sequencers; polished production, controlled mix depth and radio crossover justify 5 polish. Distinguish sampling-based synthetic string pads from actual orchestra.
- **Mood 1 / theatrical 3.** Dark desire, guilt and intimate relationship tension dominate, with expansive choruses rather than consistently operatic arrangement. Singer tone varies between forceful Gahan baritone and Gore's gentler leads on `Sweetest Perfection` / `Blue Dress`.
- **Instruments.** `synth|sampler|sequencer|drummachine|keyboards|guitar|electricguitar|drums|steelguitar`: credits document keyboards, Gahan/Gore guitars, Alan Wilder's played drums on `Clean`, synth/drum programming and live sampled inputs. Pedal/steel guitar is documented for Nils Tuxen on Violator in Wilder interview. Do **not** infer a string orchestra from sampled Elgar-style strings.
- **Production.** Sampling and first substantial loop use are described by Alan Wilder. Flood's `arp2600` with its sequencer is explicitly connected to `Halo` and `Waiting for the Night`. Technical retrospective identifies the **E-mu Emulator III** (`emulatoriii`) and Akai S1000 (`akais1000`); the Emulator III is a new token and must not be merged with `emulator` or `emulatorii`. MusicTech explicitly discusses `minimoog`, `emsvcs3` and `oberheimob8`. No `analogtape`: a source's colloquial phrase 'put to tape' does not prove the specific recording medium.
- **Lyrics/language.** English album of desire, guilt, relationships and quasi-religious metaphors (`Personal Jesus`). `sensuality|love|introspection|spiritual|heartbreak` are thematic summaries, not claims that every track covers each theme.
- **Influences.** **Tangerine Dream** is explicitly named by Wilder as the desired atmosphere of `Waiting for the Night` (track-specific). German critical retrospective calls `World in My Eyes` a deliberate-sounding **Kraftwerk homage**; this is a **critic-supported** influence, less direct than Wilder's statement. Samples from Led Zeppelin or classical works are **not** entered as `influenced_by`: a sampled recording is not proof of compositional inspiration. `sounds_like` is a cautious sonic editorial comparison, not historical evidence.
- **Critical disagreement.** High acclaim/landmark is consistent across retrospective critics, but assessment of gothic/rock credentials varies; sonic palette is not a literal guitar-band LP. Original Mute credit vs later booklet mixing description differs on `Enjoy the Silence`; does not alter producer cell. Album-wide mood 1 is a median over some more hopeful dance melodies.

## Songs of Faith and Devotion — 1993 — `depechemode-songsoffaithanddevotion`

- **Original edition.** Original Mute STUMM106 issued **22 March 1993**, ten tracks, not the separate *Songs of Faith and Devotion Live* (1993), reissues or remix singles. Mute press sheet and LP credit **Depeche Mode and Flood**. Mark `Spike` Stent mixed but has no full-album producer credit.
- **Genres.** Primary `alternativerock` is explicit in AOTY; AllMusic tags `alternativedance|alternativepoprock|alternativeindierock|dancerock|collegerock`. `industrialrock` appears in a later Classic Pop assessment, reflecting louder cuts, not all tracks. `gospel` is specified in 1993 Los Angeles Times review for `Condemnation` and `Get Right With Me`. `Bluesrock` and `synthpop` were **not** inserted based solely on a blues-guitar riff or the group's earlier sound; genre labels require album-specific attribution.
- **Scales.** Energy **4** from dense `I Feel You`, `In Your Room`, `Rush`; softer `Judas` / `One Caress` limit higher score. Electronic **3**, since synth and sampled takes share space with drums, guitars, choir and strings. Mood **1** from fraught faith, guilt, desire, longing; theatrical **4** from overt gospel and choral expansion. Experimental **4** from live ensemble takes cut into rhythmic loops and manipulated instruments; polish **3** (the band valued imperfections and a live feel). Tempo **3**, groove **3**: pulsing loops on some tracks, expansive ballads on others. Sources: 1993 Wilder/Keyboard interview, original Mute song commentary, contemporary criticism.
- **Instruments.** Original press/sleeve credits name `uilleannpipes` (Steáfán Hannigan on `Judas`), strings with Wil Malone arrangement (`One Caress`), guest choir (`Get Right With Me`). Wilder played **live drums**, Gore guitars and an **organ**, piano/harpsichord processed through guitar effects on `Walking in My Shoes`, bass instrument on some tracks, handclaps in `Condemnation`; samplers and synthesizers documented in studio testimony. `strings` avoids fabricating quartet section specifics. `choral` voice relates to actual backing gospel choir on a minority of tracks.
- **Production.** **Original Wilder interview, reprinted by mu:zines**, explicitly documents `akais1100` for drum samples and **Roland System 700** (`rolandsystem700`) modular. ARP 2600 and Minimoog noted in 1993 artist discussions. `cubase` used to rearrange live drumming into loops; sampling, room sounds and distortion processors are cited (piano/harpsichord effect on `Walking in My Shoes`, `Condemnation` handclaps and room space). Strings arranged, not synthetic choir assumed. Do **not** insert E-mu Emax: 1993 interviews discuss it primarily as proposed **tour gear**, not a proven studio model here.
- **Influences.** No unambiguous specific *album-making* artist influence is confirmed at sufficient strength for `influenced_by`, so it stays **empty**. The record's gospel/blues references are *styles*, not artist names. U2 and Nine Inch Nails are **sounds-like only**, not documented sources of influence. Do not reclassify soundtrack samples as influences.
- **Critical dispute.** The Los Angeles Times panned the bombast and questioned gospel effectiveness; other sources note formal breakthroughs. Both sides inform `theatrical` 4 and `confidence`, not a false critical consensus. Original sleeve gives release/credit stronger precedence than streaming dates (some American editions 23 March).

## Ultra — 1997 — `depechemode-ultra`

- **Original edition/credits.** Mute STUMM148 released **14 April 1997** in the UK, original LP includes `Junior Painkiller` hidden track on some configurations. Mute credits **Tim Simenon alone** as producer. **Kerry Hopwood** is programmer, **Dave Clayton** keyboards/programming/strings arrangement, **Q** engineer/co-mixer, **Gareth Jones** additional vocal engineer/selected mixes, **Daniel Miller** label founder/guest on `Uselink`; none of them is a co-producer on original release. Distinguish guest musicians from musical influences.
- **Genres.** Album of the Year tags primary `alternativerock|synthpop` and subsidiary `triphop|industrialrock|ambientpop|downtempo`. Classic Pop corroborates trip-hop **especially on `Sister of Night`**. AllMusic tags `alternativedance|alternativepoprock|alternativeindierock|dancerock|collegerock|punknewwave` (last a broad heritage bucket). Original Mute press statement explicitly mentions **blues, gospel, country** influences, restricted to some material; pedal steel on `The Bottom Line` reinforces country colour. `trip hop` is correctly `triphop`, distinct from broad `electronic`.
- **Scales.** Energy **2**, mood **1**, tempo **2**, groove **2** capture languid darker songs/interludes with a few punchier exceptions `Barrel of a Gun`, `It's No Good` and `Useless`. Electronic **4** given dense synth/sample processing balanced by extensive live guest instrumentation. Theatrical **3** for `Home` strings/large choruses against quieter passages. Experimental **3** in less direct electronic song structures. Polish **4** from Simenon's layered production without every song being immaculate/glossy. Contemporary Washington Post review finds atmosphere stronger than melody, contrasting later positive Pitchfork reappraisal.
- **Instruments.** Mute original credits specify guest drum/percussion (Gota Yashiki, Keith Le Blanc, Jaki Liebezeit, Victor Indrizzo, Danny Cummings), Doug Wimbish bass, `steelguitar` from B. J. Cole (`The Bottom Line`), `strings` on `Home`, guitar and keyboards by Gore/Clayton, samplers/synthesizers in Simenon session list. Do not call steel-guitar guest B. J. Cole an influence.
- **Production / model precision.** **Future Music April 1997 list supplied by Tim Simenon**, transcribed by DM Live Wiki, documents the **studio inventory**: `arp2600|korgprophecy|juno106|akais1000|rolandjv1080|logic|cubase` and **Akai S3200XL** (new token `akais3200xl`, **not** S3200). A gear inventory proves presence/use in sessions, **not exactly which notes and tracks** each device appears on. `sampling|programming|synthprogramming|stringarrangements` supported by credits and the producer's team. No unverified guest studio synth attributed to Gore personally.
- **Lyrics/influences.** Press and critics discuss loss, vulnerability, desire, belief. `influenced_by` remains empty: sources confirm genres and a producer, but no named external artist as a direct reference for this album. `sounds_like` is sonic editorial comparison only. Avoid inferring influence because Can's Jaki Liebezeit guested.
- **Disagreements.** Tim Simenon recollects beginning with tentative EP-length sessions; full studio album was issued, so type `studio` only. AOTY gives industrial rock as secondary, whereas later Classic Pop hears a softer record than *Songs of Faith and Devotion*. Both statements can be true of different tracks. Some sources list a US release dated 15 April; original UK 14 April retained.

## Import and exclusion guard

All 3 rows use **27 columns**. All references and genre evidence apply only to these 3 albums. All `influenced_by` artists require documented **direct** influence, never sampling, covers or guest appearances. `sounds_like` is editorial. **No compilation** is to be created or imported from this phase or any later Depeche Mode phase. Also **do not add _The Singles 86>98_**, mistakenly proposed in Phase 02's roadmap. The Phase 02 row for _The Singles 81–85_ was an unwanted compilation: if Claude has not imported it, omit it; if it was imported, remove after checking whether any unrelated user edits depend on it.
