# Sia — Phase 01 evidence notes (1997–2008)

## Method and edition boundaries

Sia's first four original studio albums, not the Zero 7 albums, the 2007 *Lady Croissant* live/EP product, their remixes, or deluxe reissue tracks. Preserve ALL documented track-specific genres even if present in one track; evidence strength and scope explain their frequency. A musical cousin under `sounds_like` is an editorial comparison, NOT an influence claim. `influenced_by` is blank throughout because album-specific artistic influence was not verified. `producers` only contains credited producers and is never filled from writing or mixing credits. The numerical scales are cautious album-level editorial judgments based on the cited track accounts, not automated audio measures. Neither absence of an instrument credit nor a blank scale implies acoustic absence. The original editions control dating; US releases in 2002/2006 and late European metadata must not replace release years.

## OnlySee (1997) — sia-onlysee

- **Object**: The Flavoured Records CD is credited to **Sia Furler**, not the later mononym; for Fandome the artist field follows the artist-room display name **Sia**, and CLAUDE_IMPORT preserves the original credit. MusicBrainz lists 13 tracks and Jesse Flavell as producer. Steve Salvi is engineer/co-mixer, **not** producer; Steve Rooney has a generic membranophone performance credit. The original record is not included in AllMusic's mainstream Sia discography, but MusicBrainz establishes release.
- **Genres**: The *Album of the Year* community metadata lists acid jazz, trip-hop, downtempo, neo-soul and singer-songwriter; *TheAudioDB* adds pop. No cited contemporaneous track-level expert analysis independently verifies these categories. Consequently all are tagged **low** evidence. Former band Crisp's acid-jazz associations are context and cannot independently prove this album's arrangements.
- **Instruments**: `percussion` cautiously subsumes a credited **membranophone**; specific drum-kit/percussion type is not verified. No other instruments or production machines are asserted. Producers Jesse Flavell from MusicBrainz.
- **Scales/voice/lyrics**: all left blank because there is no sufficient record-specific professional sound analysis to anchor numbers, lead timbre or lyric themes, rather than pretending an artist's later style establishes these.
- **Track audit** (13 original CD tracks): Don't Get Me Started; I Don't Want to Want You; OnlySee; Stories; Madlove; A Situation; Shadow; Asrep Onosim; Take It to Heart; Beautiful Reality; Soon; One More Shot; Tripoutro. Track-level stylistic verification **not available** for all 13. See source-level release credits: musicbrainz.org/release/be7e84ca-4c3c-4e75-b6d4-65bd00d83937.
- **Confidence** low because modern catalogue style tags are less reliable than contemporaneous criticism and musician notes. Do not upgrade without audio/archival review.

## Healing Is Difficult (2001) — sia-healingisdifficult

- **Object**: the UK 2001 **11-track** Long Lost Brother original (not 2002 US date or anniversary reissues/remixes). Album of the Year credits Sam Frank, Blair Mackichan and Sia; release-linked credits also identify Nigel Corsbie for *Taken for Granted* and Jesse Flavell on *Sober and Unkissed*. These last two are edition-sensitive. Production credits on original physical edition should take precedence if they conflict; check before merging an unconfirmed comprehensive list.
- **Genre range from the *AllMusic* analysis (Jon O'Brien)**: *Get Me* new jack swing; *Judge Me* drum-and-bass inflection; *Taken for Granted* chiming trip-hop; *Fear* dub-reggae and ambient flute/cello/slap-bass; *Drink to Get Drunk* sax-led jazz; *Insidiously* acoustic nu-soul; *Blow It All Away* string-rich chillout/soul. The GAFFA contemporary review describes bass-heavy jazz-funk/R&B. Styles do NOT apply to every track. `ukgarage` is deliberately low strength: 2-step classification appears in secondary sources and celebrated Exemen *Little Man* **remix** is NOT evidence that the original album master is 2-step. Claude may remove that genre pending original-track confirmation.
- **Instruments**: saxophone, bass, flute, strings/cellos grounded in critic's descriptions; type of programming/drum machine is not confirmed, so `drummachine` is not invented. `sampling` is supported by AllMusic's description of the Prokofiev quotation within *Taken for Granted*. MusicBrainz calls it a quoted musical work; whether every passage is a direct phonogram sample warrants further checking.
- **Scales**: energy 3 and groove 3 reflect groove-heavy R&B alongside restrained songs; electronic 3 reflects sample/beat-based approaches plus acoustic elements; mood 2 reflects bereavement lyric content with musical brightness on several cuts, not a uniformly dark record. Theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 3 provisional editorial interpretation of distinct track descriptions.
- **Lyrics**: love, grief/heartbreak, loss and trying to heal as documented by the AllMusic review. `introspection` and `heartbreak` are thematic map terms, not transcriptions.
- **Track audit**: Fear [dub-reggae/ambient textures]; Drink to Get Drunk [saxophone/jazz]; Taken for Granted [trip-hop and Prokofiev quotation]; Blow It All Away [strings/chillout]; Get Me [new jack swing]; I'm Not Important to You [track-specific genre undetermined]; Sober and Unkissed [earlier-version reworking, producer/edition caveat]; Healing Is Difficult [track-level genre not verified]; Judge Me [drum-and-bass inflection]; Little Man [original version not equal to Exemen remix]; Insidiously [acoustic neo-soul].
- **Disagreement**: Some metadata markets the album as R&B, other professional criticism emphasizes jazz-funk, trip-hop or soul. Represent the union with scope; resist erasing minority songs.

## Colour the Small One (2004) — sia-colourthesmallone

- **Object**: original UK 12-track Island/Go! Beat release Jan 2004. Later 2006 US edition adds *Broken Biscuit*, *Sea Shells*, and/or *Breathe Me* mixes depending on edition. Do not silently use those bonuses in standard-album metrics. Jimmy Hogarth produced the tracks according to MusicBrainz; Tchad Blake is mixer, not producer. Credits list Heliocentric recording and Real World mixing (for available tracks).
- **Genres**: acoustic/downtempo trip-hop as explicit in *Santa Barbara Independent*; slow singer-songwriter pop with jazz inflections (*Guardian*), and soul specifically on *The Church of What's Happening Now* (*Slant*). Record Club groups the album under pop, indie pop, electronic and downtempo; those aggregated descriptors have lower strength. A mostly acoustic record is compatible with selected electronic textures (*Guardian*). Do **not** label this album `rnb` solely because the Guardian describes the earlier career as R&B: it explicitly distinguishes this album from that phase.
- **Instrumentation**: MusicBrainz identifies acoustic/12-string guitar, electric guitar, piano, keyboards, bass, drums, percussion, accordion, clarinet, flute and marimba (mapped to `mallets`). The Independent names electric piano, harmonium, melodica, marimba and understated percussion; *Slant* documents string arrangements. `harmonium`, `melodica`, `chimes` are genuine source-named new tokens, not substitutes for generic synth; list for Claude in import notes. The `chimes` credit appears on MusicBrainz's *Numb*. Do not infer a drum machine from 'beats' or sample programming.
- **Production**: `layeredvocals` supported on *The Church of What's Happening Now* by mxdwn; MusicBrainz documents programming, but user tokens currently lack a general programming technique. No invented DAW or specific synth brand.
- **Scales**: energy 2, electronic 2, mood 1, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 2, groove 1: slow intimate songs plus elaborate arrangement. A 1 for mood does not imply every track is uniformly melancholic; Sweet Potato has character narration and the Church moves towards release.
- **Track audit**: Rewrite [12-string guitar/accordion, keyboards and programming]; Sunday [clarinet, flute, electric guitar]; Breathe Me [piano, marimba and dramatic swell]; The Bully [Beck co-write, **not proof of Beck influence**]; Sweet Potato [soul-leaning melodic story]; Don't Bring Me Down [guitars/bass/drums]; Natale's Song [background vocals]; Butterflies [accordion/clarinet/percussion/marimba]; Moon [no secure individual style classification]; The Church of What's Happening Now [soul and layered choir-like ending]; Numb [chimes and guitars/keys]; Where I Belong [jazzed-up, spacious arrangement]. All credited details are from the cited MusicBrainz original recordings/reviews; otherwise leave genre undecided.
- **Critical disagreement**: Slant celebrates quiet emotional range and cinematic strings; Guardian finds some ballads initially small and repetitive, but recognizes jazz texture. This does not warrant assigning 'experimental' 5.

## Some People Have Real Problems (2008) — sia-somepeoplehaverealproblems

- **Object**: 2008 original, with 13 album tracks and hidden *Buttons* in some CD editions; the Apple digital listing exposes 14 separate entries. Don't count extra digital bonuses or cover artwork as sound. *I Go to Sleep* is a Ray Davies composition, not evidence that he produced the Sia master or that the whole album is Kinks-inspired. Beck appears as **guest vocalist** on *Academia* and *Death by Chocolate*, not producer.
- **Producers**: Jimmy Hogarth is consistently designated producer for the original recordings in MusicBrainz. Dan Carey/Greg Kurstin/Rick Nowels etc may be co-writers, players, or engineers on these tracks; do not promote to credited producers.
- **Style diversity**: Pitchfork calls it pop/R&B and 'easy listening', but specifically notes brass/keys in *The Girl You Lost to Cocaine*; unusually shifting brass in *Electric Bird*, and handclaps/multi-tracked voice in *Playground*. Apple Music calls *Academia* indie pop. mxdwn describes orchestral pop and synth touches on Playground. SF Gate highlights powerful ballads (*Lentil*, *You Have Been Loved*). Guardian sees a post-trip-hop jazz/soul continuity yet judges the arrangements bland. This conflict is partly taste, not an objective contradiction about personnel.
- **Instruments**: MusicBrainz provides drum-kit, bass, multiple guitarists/keyboardists, Oliver Kraus strings, brass ensemble, percussion, accordion, clarinet/flute. Handclaps are described by Pitchfork on Playground. The isolated 'synth bubbles' in Playground do not warrant marking the entire album as electronic; electronic 1 follows prevailing live instrumental credits.
- **Production**: `layeredvocals` grounded by Pitchfork's complaint about recurring vocal multi-tracking. Do not infer Autotune or Pro Tools from era or label.
- **Scales**: 3/1/3/3/2/4/2/2 for energy/electronic/mood/theatrical/experimental/polish/tempo/groove: contrasting bright songcraft, full arrangements and slow ballads. Reviewers disagree on vocal technique and polish; these remain qualified editorial aggregates.
- **Track audit**: Little Black Sandals [jazz-soul/pop band and background singers]; Lentil [power ballad/string arrangement]; Day Too Soon [strings/percussion]; You Have Been Loved [power ballad]; The Girl You Lost to Cocaine [brass-led pop]; Academia [indie pop and Beck vocals, accordion/woodwinds]; I Go to Sleep [Ray Davies cover]; Playground [handclaps and layered vocals, isolated synth texture]; Death by Chocolate [Beck guests, multi-part harmonies]; Soon We'll Be Found [strings/cello/programming; dark dramatic ballad]; Electric Bird [brass/accordion]; Beautiful Calm Driving [strings and restrained mood]; Lullaby [band + background vocals]; Buttons [hidden track in standard CD / separate streaming track]. Source: MusicBrainz, Apple Music, Pitchfork, SF Gate and mxdwn.
- **Conflict of release metadata**: Apple and Metacritic state Jan 8 2008, while some chart-site listings show 2009 for later territory; preserve first 2008 release.
