# Wham! — Phase 01 source reasoning

Date checked: 27 October 2026. All 8 scales are **editorial whole-record judgments** anchored against the Fandome descriptor guide. Sources are linked in `wham_phase01_sources.csv`. No measurements or numerical claims are attributed to critics. `confidence` reflects source richness and structural certainty, not an assertion that scales are objective facts.

## Fantastic (1983) — `wham-fantastic`

**Scope and selection:** original 8-track LP. Apple Music's current digital edition adds remixes/instrumentals and has an alternative displayed upload date; the source-of-truth original UK date is 1 July 1983 (Wham! official site). `landmark=1`: the duo's breakthrough debut and defining early dance-pop phase.

**Genres and ordering:** AOTY tags `dance-pop`, `Britfunk`, `synth funk`, `boogie`, `synthpop` and `disco rap`, which agree broadly with Apple Music's account of synthetic funk-pop and post-disco Club Tropicana; the original 1983 pressings have `pop rap`, `synth-pop` and sometimes `Europop`. AllMusic adds dance-pop, disco and new wave. `discorap` and `poprap` are **not merged**: the latter appears as an original pressing style, the former as an AOTY secondary genre and both are weighted late because rapping is concentrated on Wham Rap. `electronic` is a very broad Discogs umbrella rather than a claim of all-electronic performances. **Do not import every AllMusic auto-style:** alternative/indie rock and post-punk are hard to defend from actual song/credit evidence. Britfunk and disco rap require new Fandome tokens.

**Scales:** energy **4**, tempo **4**, groove **5**: contemporary commentary and Apple editorial describe an upbeat party/dance record, rhythm-section propulsion and club-oriented songs (`Wham Rap!`, `Bad Boys`, `A Ray of Sunshine`, `Club Tropicana`). electronic **2**: live bass/guitars/kit/brass dominate personnel despite synth passages (`Come On!`). mood **5**: playful/optimistic energy predominates; `Nothing Looks The Same In The Light` is softer. theatrical **3**: extroverted vocals, theatrical attitude without rock-opera staging. experimental **1**: novelty of white British funk/rap fusion does not substantially depart from hook-led popular song structure. polish **3**: accomplished early studio arrangements but more brittle/tinny than the 1984 follow-up (Rolling Stone critic Don Shewey's appraisal).

**Voice/lyrics:** George Michael main melodic clear/powerful lead and on Wham Rap rhythmic rapping; George explicitly mentions falsetto on their early recording in the 1982 ZigZag interview; Apple Music also names the falsetto. Album combines `party`, working-life/social themes (`Wham Rap`), ironic bravado/identity (`Bad Boys`), and romance. British early-1980s dance-pop/Britfunk scene and English lyrics.

**Instruments:** Wham's official singles chronology documents bassists John McKenzie / Brad Lang / Deon Estus, drummers Andy Duncan / Graham Broad / Trevor Morell, Andrew Ridgeley and Robert Ahwai guitars, Anne Dudley/Tommy Eyre keyboards, horn sections and percussion. Track-by-track credits transcribed for original/reissue pressings confirm these. Apple describes **bongos, synths, brass and slap bass** on named album tracks. `strings` are credited for **Love Machine** (string arrangement Linton Ace), a one-track arrangement; last in list. Brass is grouped as `brass`, because not every official horn credit states separate trumpet/sax.

**Production:** `falsetto` supported by direct 1982 George Michael interview about Wham Rap recording, and Apple's album essay. `stringarrangements` supported for Love Machine (Wham official archive). No named model of synth or drum machine is firmly established for this original LP, so none is guessed. `Steve Brown|George Michael` co-produce most tracks; `Bob Carter` produces Wham Rap (official and original LP notes). Engineers and arrangers are **not** named as producers.

**Influence vs comparison:** Wham's 1982 George Michael interview explicitly praises disco artist **Sylvester** as a formative favourite. `The Human League` appears **only in Rolling Stone's listening comparison** for this debut (and in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`). `The Miracles (City of Angels)` is an arrangement comparator because the album actually *covers* the Miracles' Love Machine faithfully, as the Motown history explains; the cover is not evidence of conscious musical influence. No claim that these artists share tracks/credits with Wham.

**Critical disagreement:** contemporary Sounds review praises convincing, tightly arranged dance music; Rolling Stone's Don Shewey finds the textures brittle/technopop; AllMusic's William Cooper describes the album more negatively. These contrasts inform `polish=3` and prevent unjustified full-on electronic/synthpop classification. Sources: AOTY contemporary-review index, Apple editorial, original 1983 credits and the primary period interview.

## Make It Big (1984) — `wham-makeitbig`

**Scope:** 8-track original LP, studio. *Not* the simultaneous single `Last Christmas`, which did not appear on this LP. `landmark=1` due to multiple enduring worldwide singles and the duo's breakthrough at global scale. George Michael is album's credited producer (MusicBrainz / original album records); do **not** carry over Steve Brown or Bob Carter from *Fantastic*. George’s alternate Jerry Wexler recording of Careless Whisper is not the album's normal producer credit.

**Genres:** AOTY tags `pop soul`, `sophisti-pop`, `dance-pop` and `blue-eyed soul`; The Shfl's Ned Raggett independently labels pop, pop-soul, blue-eyed soul, dance-pop and new wave. AllMusic identifies new-wave pop-soul in its review, with album styles adult contemporary and contemporary pop/rock. These group into active disco-revival/soul-pop singles contrasted with smooth ballads. `sophistipop` ranks after more central `popsoul` because fewer tracks share its refined soft-arrangement attributes. Do not project a `synthfunk` genre onto the album just because `Everything She Wants` used synth gear; genre claim must come from explicit sources.

**Scales:** energy **3**, tempo **3**, groove **4**, mood **4**: *Wake Me Up*, *Freedom*, *Everything She Wants* drive the bright/dancing half, but *Careless Whisper*, *Heartbeat*, *Like a Baby* pull down tempo, intensity and emotional brightness. theatrical **3**: forceful yet natural lead voice, sentimental delivery, no narrative staging. electronic **2**: electronically generated arrangement on `Everything She Wants`, balanced with prominent session bass, live kit, horns and guitars elsewhere. experimental **0**: classic accessible pop/soul forms with deliberately commercial melodies (1984 Record Mirror interview). polish **5**: refined George Michael-run production and dense radio-ready ballad/hit arrangements.

**Voice/lyrics:** George's main expressive, powerful/clear vocal, with softer singing on romantic tracks. Themes `love`, `heartbreak`, `sensuality`, `introspection`, `party`: different singles and album tracks support those; do not assert an album-long holiday theme.

**Instruments:** Official Wham! 1984 recording credits for *Wake Me Up* and *Freedom* explicitly list electric guitars (Hugh Burns and Andrew Ridgeley), acoustic guitar on *Freedom*, Deon Estus bass, Tommy Eyre keys, Trevor Morell drums, brass/horn section. The Shfl notes the **sax and string** colour of *Careless Whisper*; MusicBrainz shows saxophonist David Baptiste on multiple tracks. *Everything She Wants* is specifically a synth/drum-machine production; retain both `synth` and `drummachine` even though the other songs are often live-player driven.

**Production:** In a direct interview, engineer Chris Porter explains how **Everything She Wants** was made after the band left the French sessions, with George Michael playing/creating the backing himself. The Guardian retells that account; MusicRadar quotes the singer on using **Roland Juno-60** for the whole track and identifies **LinnDrum**. `drumprogramming` comes from Michael's described LinnDrum pattern; models entered exactly `juno60|linndrum`. **Conflict:** official Wham! page labels the synth a 'Roland Juno 80' for *Last Christmas / Everything She Wants*. This conflicts with engineer and performer model identification; do not invent `juno80`, or conflate with `juno106`, which is a separate instrument.

**Influences:** `Elton John` is documented as a musical model for the duo by the contemporaneous Rolling Stone 1985 profile, but there is **no demonstrable direct Elton John song copied here**. If import policy requires an influence to be explicitly attributed to *this one LP*, delete album-row `influenced_by` rather than substituting critics' comparisons. George's 1984 Record Mirror admission that the LP draws from many mainstream pop areas does **not** warrant guessing unmentioned names.

**Critical disagreement:** the 1984 Record Mirror interview characterises this as leaving early disco-funk behind in favour of ballads/pop songwriting; AllMusic criticises the unevenness between standout singles and lesser cuts, while Rolling Stone's Christopher Connelly praises the successful pop execution. Both perspectives explain the album-level median scores.

## Music from the Edge of Heaven (1986) — `wham-musicfromtheedgeofheaven`

**Discography / type CRITICAL:** MusicBrainz and AllMusic place the album under studio/albums, and it was an 8-track **1986 North American/Japanese album** while `The Final` was the UK/European hits compilation. This does **not** mean these were eight new 1986 studio recordings. The 1986 album recycles earlier singles (`I'm Your Man` 1985, `Last Christmas` 1984), includes George Michael's solo-credit track *A Different Corner*, a reworked `Wham Rap '86`, and one live track *Blue (Live in China)*. Catalogue it as studio because discographies often do, but preserve this caveat for recommendations and a future more granular release taxonomy. `landmark=0`: lacks the consistent new-album identity of the first two even though it includes historically significant hits.

**Genres:** AOTY gives `pop`, `new wave`, `synth funk`, `synthpop`, `dance-pop`, `sophisti-pop`; AllMusic adds adult contemporary and contemporary pop/rock. `softrock` is a very late *track-specific* addition for the slower *Where Did Your Heart Go?*, documented in analyses of Wham's understated cover arrangement. More varied than a single smooth synthpop album; strong dance tracks and reflective ballads coexist. The `genre_evidence` file marks scopes; do not propagate every genre to every track.

**Scales:** energy **3**, tempo **3**, groove **3**, mood **3**: *The Edge of Heaven* / *I'm Your Man* are energetic; *A Different Corner*, *Last Christmas*, *Where Did Your Heart Go?* are substantially slower or sadder. electronic **3**: guitar/bass/live kit/brass on fast tracks, machine/synth-dominant older recording *Last Christmas* and synth-led ballad layers. theatrical **3**: emphatic uptempo singing versus intimate ballad voice. experimental **1**: conventional pop productions but a contrasting, edited-together farewell format. polish **5**: highly polished final-era single productions (do not interpret the mixed source material as technically low-fidelity). Confidence **medium**, due mostly to **heterogeneous release structure** and track-to-track variation rather than missing core credits.

**Instruments:** Original late-1985/1986 official Wham! single and EP credits show guitars (Andrew Ridgeley/David Austin/Hugh Burns), Deon Estus bass, Tommy Eyre keys, Trevor Morell drums, horns. MusicBrainz and Apple Music identify **Elton John piano** on *The Edge of Heaven*, **Andy Hamilton sax**, **Paul Spong and Simon Gardner trumpets**, **Rick Taylor trombone**, **Danny Cummings percussion**, and Charlie Morgan drums in alternate detailed credits. Credit both main instrument families but do not infer that every member played on every track. *Last Christmas* reused on the album has confirmed **Juno-60 synth, LinnDrum and sleigh bells**; those instrument and model tags apply only to that song.

**Production:** Apple Music track credits for *The Edge of Heaven* and *Where Did Your Heart Go?* explicitly credit George Michael with `programming`. `drumprogramming|juno60|linndrum` are supported by Chris Porter's detailed account of the *Last Christmas* original recording. `concertrecording` applies **only** to `Blue (Live in China)`; do not describe the rest of the record as live. The album-wide producer remains **George Michael**; Don and David Was wrote the *Where Did Your Heart Go?* original, but did **not** produce the Wham! version (official Wham! release / MusicBrainz credits). Elton John is guest pianist, not credited Wham! record producer nor an album-influencing artist simply by playing on one song.

**Voice/lyrics:** George Michael leads in high-impact energetic soul-pop; *A Different Corner* and *Where Did Your Heart Go?* are soft introspective/heartbreak performances, and `Wham Rap '86` brings rap phrasing. `love|heartbreak|sensuality|party|nostalgia|introspection` are interwoven across the eight recordings, not homogeneous.

**Sounds-like (comparison only):** `Was (Not Was) (Was (Not Was))` because *Where Did Your Heart Go?* covers a song that first appeared on the **1981 Was (Not Was) debut**, and even after George Michael's changes the arrangement remains structurally similar. This is not a documented influence and the 1981 original artists are NOT production collaborators on this album.

**Producer/date/source conflicts:** Actual original 1986 dates vary (late June, early July depending on source, format, territory); choose release year 1986 only in CSV, note Apple Music 1 July 1986 and original pressings. The Wham official site's album section lists the two UK studio LPs but not this North American/Japan title, because the UK catalogue took *The Final* instead; both MusicBrainz and AllMusic document the regional release. Critics William Ruhlmann and AOTY disagree on the album's coherence/genre spread; retain low `landmark` and medium confidence.
