# Belinda Carlisle — Fandome album evidence, phase 01

**Scope:** original studio albums issued in 1986, 1987, 1989, and 1991. All are new to Fandome's current `descriptors` branch. Numeric scales and `sounds_like` are editorial assessments, **not** externally measured facts or proof of artistic influence. None of the four has a verified album-specific artist-influence declaration, so `influenced_by` remains empty; career-wide influences are documented separately in CLAUDE_IMPORT.

## belindacarlisle-belinda — *Belinda* (1986)

- **Issue, collaborators, production:** original May 1986 ten-song I.R.S. album, not the 2003/2014 deluxe issue. Original recordings produced by **Michael Lloyd**. William Orbit is credited for mixing/remixing/engineering on selected tracks but **not** as the album's credited producer. Bernard Edwards is attached to *Dancing in the City*, a separate 1987 recording appearing on later expanded editions, not the original ten-track studio album. Sources: [MusicBrainz original](https://musicbrainz.org/release/5c926e78-650e-412a-8fa7-2a4bd823966f), [Qobuz edition track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/belinda-belinda-carlisle/wcr0mjq9qr6ra).
- **Genres:** AllMusic calls the album adult contemporary and contemporary pop/rock; the original discography also describes pop rock. `pop` (broad melodic pop orientation) and `rock` (guitar-led portion) are lower-priority constituents, **not** an attempt to relabel this as the Go-Go's punk/new-wave album. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/belinda-r3328/review); [album reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_(Belinda_Carlisle_album)).
- **Instrumentation:** original session credits list guitarists Charlotte Caffey, Laurence Juber, Andy Taylor and David Lindley; bassists Dennis Belfield/Paula Brown; Paul Leim drums; Phil Shenale/Nicky Hopkins/Jim Cox and others keys; Paulinho da Costa percussion. Sax appears on 'Gotta Get to You', trumpet on 'Stuff and Nonsense'; original credits include string arrangements by John D'Andrea (selected songs), which supports `strings` and `stringarrangements`. No specific synth model is evidenced. [Sessiondays source reconstructing LP credits](https://sessiondays.com/2026/09/11/1986-belinda-carlisle-belinda/).
- **Values 0–5:** energy 3 and tempo 3 for mid-tempo pop with livelier songs; electronic 2 because predominantly full band with keys rather than electronic rhythm foundation; mood 4 for generally warm/hopeful love material; theatrical 2 for a relatively direct intimate delivery; experimental 1 conventional pop arrangement; polish 4 meticulous professional production; groove 2 because rock/pop pulse outweighs explicit dance-floor arrangements. `voice=clear|powerful` reflects melodic and insistent lead voice. No instrumental vocal lead or rapping inferred.
- **Lyrics/association:** love and regret (including 'Mad About You', 'Since You've Gone', 'Band of Gold'), introspection in ballads. References to Cyndi Lauper, Susanna Hoffs and Kim Carnes in `sounds_like` are **editorial proximity** not citations of collaboration. 'Band of Gold' is a Freda Payne cover; covering it does not establish a documented Payne influence. Landmark 1 for her solo debut and hit 'Mad About You'; confidence high in documented credits, not a claim of an unanimously favourable critical reaction.

## belindacarlisle-heavenonearth — *Heaven on Earth* (1987)

- **Issue/genre:** original 1987 studio record, not bonus remixes from later editions. AllMusic explicitly lists contemporary pop/rock, soft rock and adult contemporary. Genre ordering puts accessible pop-rock ahead of softer romantic material. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/heaven-on-earth-mw0000197045).
- **Credits:** producer **Rick Nowels**. Original-issue credits list Rick Nowels/Charles Judge **drum programming**; Rhett Lawrence **Fairlight**; live drums Kenny Aronoff/Curly Smith; bass John Pierce; acoustic/electric guitars Rick Nowels, Dan Huff, John McCurry and others; Charles Judge acoustic piano; percussion by Jimmy Bralower and Paulinho da Costa; backing voices including Michelle Phillips, Charlotte Caffey and Ellen Shipley. Use `fairlight` as named technology, but **do not infer a Fairlight model**. Neither Ellen Shipley nor Robert Feist are added to producers: the credits mark them as **additional vocal production**, not full producers. [MusicBrainz original pressing](https://musicbrainz.org/release/ef5dc20e-441e-3c79-ac1a-528a3c67b8b1).
- **0–5:** energy 4 for emphatic driving singles; electronic 3 for programmed drums/Fairlight amid live guitar and drums; mood 4 for optimistic romantic affirmations balanced by yearning; theatrical 4 due to large-scale choruses; experimental 1 straightforward mainstream compositions; polish 5 conspicuous multi-studio gloss and orchestration of vocals; tempo 3 mixes up-tempo hits and slow ballads; groove 3 rhythmically active pop but not uniformly club-focused. `voice=clear|powerful|choral` identifies strong melody and notable layered backing choir (not operatic vocal training). Lyrics centre on love, emotional vulnerability and relationship choices.
- **Reception:** solo career breakthrough with 'Heaven Is a Place on Earth', 'I Get Weak' and 'Circle in the Sand'. Retrospective critical commentary acknowledges mainstream pop effectiveness while recording complaints about slickness and distance from the Go-Go's. Landmark 1; `sounds_like` parallels not causal evidence. [AllMusic career history](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/belinda-carlisle-mn0000151549).

## belindacarlisle-runawayhorses — *Runaway Horses* (1989)

- **Genre:** AllMusic explicitly lists adult contemporary, contemporary pop/rock and pop/rock; critic Alex Henderson calls some songs **power pop**, a subset ordered late (`powerpop` with `some_tracks` scope). The album is not labelled synthpop merely because 1980s keyboards occur. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/runaway-horses-mw0000201962).
- **Producers:** **Rick Nowels** is the album's credited producer, including on 'Valentine'. **David Munday** contributed instruments, drum programming and songwriting on that track, **not production**, as the original MusicBrainz release explicitly distinguishes. Do not conflate engineering, arranging, instrumentation or songwriting with credited production. [MusicBrainz original release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2aa5073c-7ad8-3602-bca7-32525546cc53), [Amoeba musician/production credit set](https://www.amoeba.com/ajax/album_credits.php?id=4348367).
- **Instrumentation and production:** guitar-heavy band sessions include George Harrison's guest **slide guitar** on 'Leave a Light On' and 12-string guitar and six-string bass on 'Deep Deep Ocean'. Charles Judge piano/keys, accordion Jimmie Haskell ('La Luna'), violin Sid Page, strings arranged by Paul Buckmaster ('Summer Rain'), Luis Conte hand percussion/bongos; David Munday drum programming on 'Valentine'. Supports `drumprogramming`/`stringarrangements` as album-level techniques used **on selected songs**, not every track. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2aa5073c-7ad8-3602-bca7-32525546cc53), [Amoeba](https://www.amoeba.com/ajax/album_credits.php?id=4348367).
- **Values:** energy 3, tempo 3 because mid-tempo ballads coexist with energetic power-pop cuts; electronic 2 from mostly live playing alongside some programming; mood 3, hopeful romantic surfaces offset by 'Summer Rain' nostalgia/uncertainty; theatrical 3 big choruses but restrained narrative pieces; experimental 2 Latin/folk colour and guest instrumentation within orthodox song structures; polish 4; groove 2 rather than a dance album. `voice=clear|powerful|choral` documents lead and harmonies. `sounds_like` associations (Roxette/Heart/Wilson Phillips) are editorial. **George Harrison is a guest**, not automatically an `influenced_by` entry. Landmark 1 for UK/Australia hits 'Leave a Light On', 'Summer Rain', and 'La Luna'.

## belindacarlisle-liveyourlifebefree — *Live Your Life Be Free* (1991)

- **Issue/genre:** original 11-track 1991 studio album. AllMusic explicitly assigns Adult Contemporary and Contemporary Pop/Rock, while Justin Kantor notes a new **dance influence** on 'Do You Feel Like I Feel?' and 'I Plead Insanity'; thus `dance` carries `some_tracks` scope, not the whole album. [AllMusic review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/live-your-life-be-free-mr0000273421).
- **Producers:** 1991 original release credits list **Rick Nowels, David Munday, Richard Feldman and Eric Pressly**. Multiple production teams are credited on differing tracks: e.g., Nowels on title song, Feldman on 'Half the World' and 'Little Black Book', Munday on 'You Came Out of Nowhere', Pressly co-credited on multiple tracks. Do not convert background vocal/mixer/engineer credits to producers. [MusicBrainz original](https://musicbrainz.org/release/db431fc3-34c8-4d39-a8e8-52e995c8ac4d), [Qobuz track producer breakdown](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/live-your-life-be-free-belinda-carlisle/yv2ym37ji8h7h).
- **Instruments/production:** guitars Rick Nowels, Michael Landau and Ben Schultz, keys Charlie Judge/Rick Nowels, bass John Pierce and Eric Pressly; Denny Fongheiser and Kenny Aronoff documented drums; **programmed drums** by Ben Schultz and Charles Judge on selected tracks. AllMusic explicitly identifies **mandolin, Spanish guitar and oboe** on 'Half the World'; MusicBrainz identifies **cello and violin** on 'Little Black Book', and associated **string arrangement**. List them as specific tracks rather than mistakenly assuming whole-album presence. [Original credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/db431fc3-34c8-4d39-a8e8-52e995c8ac4d), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/live-your-life-be-free-mr0000273421).
- **0–5:** energy 3 and tempo 3 with notable faster singles balanced by ballads; electronic 3 given programming mixed with live instruments; mood 3 varied optimistic liberation and heartbreak; theatrical 3 broad choruses; experimental 2 stylistic variety with acoustic/chamber details; polish 4 professional studio gloss; groove 3 intermittent dance-forward pulse. Clear, forceful singing and background choral harmony; words on romance and freedom (identity). Landmark 0: a regular fourth studio effort, notwithstanding some successful UK singles. Do not mistake **Live Your Life Be Free** for a live release because of the word 'Live' in the title. It is a **studio album**.

## Career-wide declared influences — separate from album rows

Belinda Carlisle recalled loving the **Beach Boys**, especially *Pet Sounds*, shaping her melodically driven music; she also says the **Runaways** showed her that an all-woman rock band was possible. [The Guardian (2021)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2021/feb/11/belinda-carlisles-teenage-obsessions-i-was-going-to-be-anita-ekberg-in-rome-but-ended-up-in-a-band). She has cited **the Beatles** and **Phil Spector** as early musical influences. [American Songwriter (2025)](https://americansongwriter.com/belinda-carlisle-channels-nostalgia-harmony-and-heart-into-a-tribute-to-the-timeless-tunes-of-her-youth/). These are career-wide influences, **not proven for any particular solo album in this phase**.
