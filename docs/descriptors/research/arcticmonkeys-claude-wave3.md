## dylan-roughandrowdyways
- genres: Pitchfork (Sam Sodomsky): "It is blues and folk music that seem to drift in and out of consciousness". NME (Mark Beaumont): "gentle spiritual, lustrous country, Southern blues or gothic Americana"; "sultry vaudevillian blues"; "sparse, flamenco-flecked Western noir" (Black Rider, so flamenco goes at the tail). The Forty-Five (Leonie Cooper): "a jazzy, loungey thing", "lush, backstreet blues feel"; one track a "sparkling torch song". Irish Times (Joe Breen): "the gritty blues trio"; I've Made Up My Mind is waltz-like; the page files the album under singer/songwriter. "Southern blues" mapped to blues.
- scales: energy 1, tempo 1 (Forty-Five "sumptuously laidback", "melodies float rather than batter you"; NME "open-casket hush"). mood 1 (death, "glacial melancholy", NME), lifted from 0 by Irish Times "menacing, playful". theatrical 1 (Pitchfork: "he speaks carefully, quietly, earnestly"). experimental 2 (NME "atmospheres rather than songs", 17-minute closer; yet Forty-Five "far more traditional"). polish 2 (Pitchfork "threadbare and hypnotic", "acoustic instruments"). electronic 0. groove 1 (some blues shuffles).
- voice: Irish Times "croaking character-filled voice"; NME "growls", "grainy low register"; Irish Times calls Murder Most Foul a recitative closer -> raspy|spoken.
- instruments: Pitchfork "a muted string ensemble, a soft pedal steel, some funereal motifs from classical and electric guitars"; NME "acoustic, slide guitar and cello", "piano ambience"; MOJO (Benmont Tench): Hammond organ on Key West, Alan Pasqua piano, Fiona Apple "had already played on the track"; Bobserve session page (fan research, flagged as speculative there): Donnie Herron violin/mandolin/steel guitar, Tony Garnier bass, Matt Chamberlain drums/percussion, Dylan harmonica on track 6. https://mojo4music.com/articles/stories/inside-the-making-of-bob-dylans-rough-and-rowdy-ways https://bobserve.com/olof/DSN40510%20-%20Rough%20And%20Rowdy%20Ways%20session.htm
- production: Tench in MOJO: "we're in there overdubbing". NME's "drowsy steel drums" left out (I couldn't confirm it).
- producers: none credited apart from Dylan (Bobserve lists no producer), so the cell stays empty.
- sounds_like: Forty-Five: vocals "closer in tone and timbre to Tom Waits' scrappy croon"; torch song "hits the midpoint between Dean Martin and Johnny Cash" [1/10 each]; NME: Murder Most Foul compared to Lou Reed and John Cale's Songs for Drella [1/10]. Left out: Billy "The Kid" Emerson and Jimmy Reed (Pitchfork: borrowed riffs and homage = quotation); the Americana Highways comparisons are to Dylan's own songs.
- influenced_by: empty. Pitchfork says Dylan played the band another artist's "prototype" track but names none.
- landmark 0: NME says Tempest was his late-career peak "until now", which ranks it in the late period but doesn't call it career-defining.
- lyrics: Pitchfork (death, love "a delicate pact", prayer, humour), NME ("religion, music, war and civil rights", JFK).

## arcticmonkeys-thecar
- genres: Pitchfork (Matthew Strauss): "a lovesick, debonair lounge act"; "the funk-inspired 'I Ain't Quite Where I Think I Am'"; "'Body Paint,' a blast of baroque pop". MOJO (John Mulvey): "string arrangements redolent of '70s symphonic soul"; Perfect Sense "so fastidiously Bacharach baroque". NME: "The grandiose funk-rock of 'I Ain't Quite Where I Think I Am'". RS UK (via WebFetch): "lounge-pop led sounds". The Skinny: "lounge-laden musical attire". Chamber pop (Telegraph, seen only in a search snippet) left out.
- scales: energy 2 (ballads, "grand, elegiac balladry", MOJO), mood 1 (doubt, paranoia, longing), theatrical 4 (Skinny "sonically grandiose"; RS UK "grander, colourful and cinematic"), experimental 3 (Pitchfork "filled with detours, non sequiturs"), polish 4 (18-piece strings at RAK, NME), electronic 1 (drum machine and Moog on one song, RS UK), tempo 2, groove 2 (funk/wah tracks).
- voice: Pitchfork: "Turner sings much of The Car in falsetto"; RS UK and MOJO: croon -> soft|clear.
- instruments and production: NME: "an 18-piece string section ... arrangements composed by Turner, producer James Ford and Bridget Samuels"; MOJO: "harpsichord to the fore", "gestural wah-wah", "a Turner guitar solo"; NME interview: lead single "vanishing sensations of violin and harpsichord" (search snippet), Turner on piano; Pitchfork: Helders "like a jazz percussionist", "wah-wah riffs"; RS UK: "drum machines and Moog synthesisers"; NME review names bassist Nick O'Malley playing.
- producers: James Ford (NME).
- sounds_like: MOJO: Curtis Mayfield, Isaac Hayes, Al Green (Willie Mitchell sessions), "most strikingly, of Scott Walker's first four solo albums" (the album's strings overall); Bowie "in his Plastic Soul phase" (I Ain't Quite) + Skinny "essences of Bowie in Big Ideas" -> David Bowie [2/10]; Bacharach [1/10]; Mick Ronson guitar solo [1/10]. Pitchfork: "'Jet Skis on the Moat,' which could pass for an Isaac Hayes cover". NME: Hello You "as alluring as anything on Steely Dan's 1980 classic 'Gaucho'" [1/10]. Skinny: "Sinatra-like crooner vibes in Perfect Sense" [1/10]. Left out: The Last Shadow Puppets (Turner's own band); Beatles/James Bond (RS UK, vague).
- influenced_by: empty. The "Del Schwartz" playlist (Andrew Oldham, Os Mutantes, Francis Lai) is attributed to Turner by fans, and he denied it, so it doesn't count. In Big Issue, Turner says "Some of my favourite records are by David Axelrod" while talking about the producer's role, not as an influence on this album: https://www.bigissue.com/culture/music/exclusive-7-things-alex-turner-revealed-to-us-about-new-arctic-monkeys-album-the-car/
- lyrics: Pitchfork "an album of love, longing, and doubt"; Skinny "a motif for a sense of nostalgia".

## florence-dancefever
- genres: Exclaim! (Sun Noor): "anthemic synthpop, baroque pop and folk balladry"; label press release quoted by NME: "dance, folk, '70s Iggy Pop, longing-for-the-road folk tracks a la Lucinda Williams or Emmylou Harris"; The Arts Desk (Guy Oddy): "pop-prog flavours", "a bit proggy, at times a bit folky"; LOBF: the closer is "closer to breezy indie pop" (tail).
- scales: energy 3 (Pitchfork "as propulsive as any Florence and the Machine album"), electronic 2 ("plenty of synths", Arts Desk; synth-heavy Free/My Love, Exclaim), mood 2 (anxiety and death; LOBF "joyous"), theatrical 4 (Pitchfork "sweeping, grandiose statement"), experimental 2, polish 4 (Arts Desk "lush wall of sound", "over-produced"), tempo 3, groove 2 (Arts Desk doubts anyone will dance; My Love a "club banger", Exclaim).
- voice: Pitchfork: "belting over harp", "she rasps", "contorting her voice into a howl or a croak", spoken-word section "in a crisp monotone", "coos".
- instruments: Pitchfork harp, "bass and trumpets", cello, "frenetic drums", finger snaps; Arts Desk "hand-clapping percussion, plenty of synths and even the occasional violin"; Exclaim "layered harmonies and handclaps", "string elements"; LOBF "driving drums and plucked harp".
- production: Arts Desk "wall of sound"; Exclaim "layered harmonies"; Pitchfork "belting".
- producers: NME news: "Produced by Welch along with Jack Antonoff and Glass Animals' Dave Bayley".
- influenced_by: Welch on Apple Music 1 (Rolling Stone UK, Far Out): "In the lower baritones, I'm trying to do a Leonard or a Nick or an Iggy Pop" (about King) and Far Out: "an Iggy Pop was a really big reference". Label press release (NME): "'70s Iggy Pop", "a la Lucinda Williams or Emmylou Harris", and "Nick Cave at the club" (My Love). Iggy Pop and Nick Cave are named twice (song + press release), so no share given; Cohen only for King [1/14]. Lucinda Williams/Emmylou Harris come from the label's description, so they're weaker (see Uncertain). https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/florence-welch-says-she-channelled-iggy-pop-and-nick-cave-for-dance-fever-16685/ https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/florence-welch-embody-nick-cave-new-album/ https://www.nme.com/news/music/florence-the-machine-new-single-my-love-listen-3179624
- sounds_like: Arts Desk: "an almost Kate Bush-like approach to song writing and production", "the mantle of late-period Eurythmics"; LOBF: on Cassandra "she sounds a little like '90s Nick Cave"; Exclaim: "She emulates Nick Cave and Iggy Pop on the experimental track 'Cassandra'" [1/14 each]. Lorde's Solar Power (Pitchfork) is a contrast, not a comparison.
- lyrics: Pitchfork: "Welch's most personal lyrics", "devils and angels", ambivalence about motherhood; LOBF "funny wasn't one of them until now".

## lanadelrey-didyouknowthattheresatunnelunderoceanblvd
- genres: Clash (Robin Murray): "a song-rooted venture laden with country-inflections" / "shot through with buzzing electronics"; The Grants "takes on a gospel hue"; Let The Light In a "swirling salute to the Bakersfield Sound". Pitchfork (Olivia Horn): "gospel backing vocals", "jazzy squiggles", "boom-bap playground rap", "The trap beats are back, at least in the record's final stretch". Irish Examiner: A&W "seven-minute electro-folk steamroller" (lead single; mapped to folktronica); The Grants "a ballad". NZ Herald (Karl Puschmann): "synths and hip-hop beats", Let Light In "cinematic country-tinged duet".
- scales: energy 1, tempo 1 (Irish Examiner "least urgent", "slowly unspooling tempos"), mood 1 (BPM "most melancholic album yet"), theatrical 2 (Irish Examiner "aggressively introverted", but orchestral swells), experimental 3 (Pitchfork "no rhythm, no structure" on Fingertips, sermon interlude; NZH "wild experimentation"), polish 3 (Pitchfork "visible seams and unhemmed edges"; NZH "meticulously crafted while also sounding casual"), electronic 2, groove 1.
- voice: NZH "deep, narcotised croon and whispered falsetto"; Pitchfork "boom-bap playground rap"; Crimson (student paper) "Del Rey raps" on Peppers.
- instruments: NZH "reverb-drenched piano, which leads most of the 16 songs", "dusty orchestral strings"; Pitchfork "the strings and the Wurlitzer"; BPM "strings, drums, guitar and clarinet"; NZH synths.
- production: NZH "reverb-drenched"; Pitchfork samples Tommy Genesis' "Angelina", reuses Venice Bitch; trap beats -> drumprogramming.
- producers: The Forty-Five who's who: Antonoff "Produced every song bar 'The Grants', 'Sweet', 'Candy Necklace', 'Fingertips' and 'Let The Light In'"; Drew Erickson produced five; Zach Dawes four; Mike Hermosa the title track; Jon Batiste "credited as a producer" on Candy Necklace; Benji "a production credit" on Peppers. https://thefortyfive.com/news/lana-del-rey-collaborators-ocean-blvd/
- sounds_like: Clash "Dig This? Dig Deeper: Mazzy Star, Patsy Cline" (Sylvia Plath dropped, a poet); NZH: Fishtail "sounds like any number of breathy minimal-pop tunes that use Lorde's Royals as their guiding light" [1/16]. Left out: Bleachers (Antonoff's band, producer/guest), Leonard Cohen (Kintsugi lifts a lyric = quotation), Harry Nilsson (lyrical reference), John Denver (lyric imagery).
- influenced_by: empty.
- reception note: mainstream 3 (Interscope auteur record, consistent with Ultraviolence and NFR); a case for 2 exists (Irish Examiner "least urgent", diehards only).

## foofighters-buthereweare
- genres: Stereogum (Chris DeVille): "the kind of straightforward, crowd-pleasing arena rock"; "'Under You' a Jawbreaker-esque power-pop gem"; Nothing At All "spunky new wave verses ... with a gargantuan post-grunge chorus"; Beyond Me "a vintage FM-radio power ballad"; Rest "a slowcore/doom metal hymn". Pitchfork (Ian Cohen): "the pure rock'n'roll revivalism of 'Rescued'"; Show Me How "shimmering shoegaze"; Hearing Voices evokes the Cure's "arena rock and frosty post-punk". NME (Ali Shutler): "the surf rock of 'Under You'"; Hearing Voices "a gothic, synth-pop number". Plain rock: Stereogum "loud, catchy, radio-friendly rock music". New token: doommetal.
- scales: energy 4 (NME "searching for noisy catharsis in every track"; Pitchfork "most propulsive ... music of the last two decades"), mood 2 (grief; Pitchfork "life-affirming"), theatrical 4 (stadium epics, 10-minute The Teacher), experimental 1 (Stereogum "largely devoid of left turns"), polish 4 (Pitchfork "Greg Kurstin's reliably cushy production"), tempo 3, groove 1, electronic 1 (one synth-pop track).
- voice: Pitchfork "indefatigable growl", "screams"; Stereogum "searing screams", "agile beauty"; NME Rest "he whispers".
- instruments: NME "Grohl plays drums across the album", "flamboyant guitar solos", "Stuttering acoustic track 'The Glass'"; synth-pop track (NME). Bass/keys not stated in an opened source, so left out.
- production: Pitchfork: Rest "bursts into distortion halfway through".
- producers: NME: "Kurstin returned to co-produce alongside the band" (band credit dropped).
- sounds_like: Pitchfork: Hearing Voices and the Cure; NME: The Teacher "pulls heavy influence from The Cure" (a critic's reading) -> The Cure [2/10]; Pitchfork: title track as "what major label A&Rs heard ... handing six-figure deals to Jawbox and Shudder to Think" [1/10 each]. Stereogum: Jawbreaker [1/10], "new wave verses a la Elvis Costello or the Knack" [1/10], "a guitar solo in the mold of Brian May" [1/10], The Glass "worthy of ... Paul McCartney, and maybe Alex Chilton too" [1/10]. NME: Beyond Me "pulling inspiration from Oasis' more tender moments" [1/10]. Left out: Back in Black / Wish You Were Here (Pitchfork's conceptual analogy about grief, not sound), the band's own debut, and Sunny Day Real Estate/Germs (band members' old bands).
- influenced_by: empty (the "naiveté of their self-titled debut" is a self-reference).
- landmark 0. Grammy: Best Rock Album nominee (grammy.com).
