# Madonna — Phase 06: I’m Breathless / Evita (historical soundtrack backfill)

## Scope and chronology
This phase was expressly requested to fill two *older gaps* after Phases 01–05. It is chronological **within the new rows** (1990, 1996), not a continuation after 2019. The branch `descriptors` already contained 15 Madonna rows, none for these titles. **Only append these two**; never replace or repeat older research. ## I’m Breathless (1990) — `madonna-imbreathless`
**Context and genres:** The 12-track May 1990 record is dominated by jazz-era big-band and swing idioms, retro pop and show-tune writing [IB_OFF, IB_AM, IB_SL, IB_SE]. Three Stephen Sondheim compositions were used in Dick Tracy; the rest were mostly newly composed songs inspired by the film. `Vogue` is a separate house/dance-pop track appended late; `Now I’m Following You Part II` is a distinct programmed dance segment. Their sonic identity is explicitly *track-scoped* in the genre file and must not outweigh the majority of the album [IB_CP, IB_RO, IB_VG, IB_MB]. AllMusic calls the set superficial novelty songs and singles out `Vogue`; Slant argues the dramatic retro idea and singing are stronger than that account allows [IB_AM, IB_SL]. These are competing reviews, not contradictory instrumentation claims.

**Eight editorial scores** (0–5, project anchors):
- `energy=2`: mainly measured torch/novelty songs and ballads, with some livelier swing; `Vogue` not used as proxy [IB_SL, IB_SE].
- `electronic=2`: original horns, bass, drums, piano and vocals dominate, while `Vogue` and Part II use drum-machine/synth programming [IB_MB, IB_QB, IB_RO].
- `mood=3`: playful and cheeky material alongside longing and introspection (especially `Something to Remember`) [IB_SL, IB_AM].
- `theatrical=4`: overt character acting, showgirl delivery, dialogue-style duets and stage-musical compositions [IB_SL, IB_OFF, IB_CP].
- `experimental=2`: deliberate pastiche and era juxtaposition; song structures generally traditional rather than structurally experimental [IB_SL, IB_AM].
- `polish=4`: fully arranged and carefully recorded brass, winds, rhythm section and refined song productions [IB_MB, IB_QB].
- `tempo=3`: substantial mid-tempo swing with slow ballads and one faster dance-house finale [IB_AM, IB_SL, IB_RO].
- `groove=3`: swinging rhythmic numbers and one strong club track, but not predominantly dance-floor programming [IB_SL, IB_RO, IB_SE].

`voice=clear|soft|powerful|spoken`: controlled character-singing, gentler torch delivery and occasional belting, with spoken material on Vogue/Part II. `lyrics=sensuality|love|irony|introspection|identity|party|storytelling`: cheeky flirtation and wordplay recur; `Something to Remember` has reflective content; Vogue's self-expression/club lyric applies narrowly to that song [IB_SL, IB_CP]. `language=en`; English is dominant. `scene` names **two separate contexts**, Hollywood period jazz and New York ballroom house, not one continuous genre [IB_RO, IB_CP].

**Producer audit:** Madonna and Patrick Leonard lead tracks 1/3–7/10; Madonna and Bill Bottrell tracks 2/8/9; Kevin Gilbert joins Madonna and Leonard on track 11; Madonna and Shep Pettibone on track 12 [IB_SE, IB_QB, IB_MB]. Neither Andy Paley nor Stephen Sondheim should be added as producers (writing roles). Brian Malouf is a mixer/engineer rather than producer [IB_MB]. `sounds_like=Marilyn Monroe` is an **editorial timbral/period-persona analogy** only; Classic Pop identifies the Monroe-style presentation but this does not establish an influence. `influenced_by=The Salsoul Orchestra` is narrowly **Vogue only**: producer Pettibone cited the Salsoul era as a production source [IB_BB, IB_RO], with the Orchestra's Love Break also examined in press reporting [IB_BBC]. It does not claim Madonna's whole big-band album was derived from that group.

`landmark=1` is a Fandome editorial choice: unusual film/musical turn and host of enduring repertoire, including Vogue and the Oscar-winning Sooner or Later; it is not a claim of unanimous critical praise [IB_OFF, IB_AM, IB_SL]. `confidence=high` rests on good production documentation and multiple critics, but individual genre associations carry different evidence strengths.

## Evita (1996) — `madonna-evita`
**Edition:** score the original 31-track **complete motion-picture music soundtrack** (double CD), not a 19-track selections/highlights edition, subsequent 1997 CD pages or a remixed Don't Cry for Me Argentina single. Madonna's catalogue page labels its album 30 November 1996, while early music press discusses a 28 October European release and MusicBrainz/Apple list 12 November in the US. The *original year is unequivocally 1996* [EV_OFF, EV_PA, EV_MB, EV_APP, EV_MR]. AllMusic digital metadata sometimes refers to 1997: do not overwrite the 1996 recording year with that later edition.

**The work itself:** This is a sung-through film adaptation of Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice's stage musical, not a Madonna-authored standalone pop album. The cast includes Antonio Banderas, Jonathan Pryce, Jimmy Nail and ensembles; there is also orchestral/instrumental material [EV_AL, EV_MB, EV_QB]. `showtunes` and `rockopera` describe the album-level idiom, `soundtrack` its format, `classical` the orchestral production/catalogue classification, and `latin` some local colour: **the mere Argentine setting must never be treated as evidence of authentic Argentine folk/tango performance throughout** [EV_PA, EV_CA, EV_AM]. `poprock`, `dancepop` and `adultcontemporary` are broad AllMusic catalogue styles, not claims of evenly distributed dance production. Because `rockopera` has a source and is absent from the current Genre union, it is a **proposed new token** [EV_AOTY, EV_PA]. `opera` is a form descriptor used by director Parker, not a claim that this is a traditional classical opera [EV_AL].

**Eight editorial scores:**
- `energy=3`: rising ensemble numbers and busy sequences contrast with intimate ballads and elegiac passages [EV_CA, EV_AL].
- `electronic=1`: predominantly orchestral, acoustic and cast-voice arrangements; modern pop/rock textures do not make the album synth-based [EV_QB, EV_IR].
- `mood=2`: grief, ambition and decline dominate a score that has moments of exhilaration and public triumph [EV_AL, EV_CA].
- `theatrical=5`: fully sung-through narrative with large ensembles, dramatic transitions and climactic showpiece performances [EV_AL, EV_PA].
- `experimental=2`: unusual assignment for a pop vocalist but conventional musical dramatic construction and familiar source material, so do not inflate for career novelty [EV_AL, EV_PA].
- `polish=4`: extensive London pre-recording, elaborate orchestrations and careful postproduction without maximal modern electronic gloss [EV_IR, EV_MB, EV_QB].
- `tempo=3`: considerable metrical variety, from slow laments to animated set-pieces; the whole 31-track work is not uniformly slow [EV_CA, EV_AL].
- `groove=2`: some Latin, pop and rock pulses, but song continuity primarily supports dramatic narrative rather than sustained club dancing [EV_CA, EV_AL].

`voice=clear|powerful|soft|choral|instrumental`: Madonna's studied register expansion and diction are documented by first-hand interviews; ensemble/choir and orchestrated passages belong to the album; don't model the film soundtrack as exclusively Madonna's solo voice [EV_LT, EV_AL, EV_QB]. The main language is **English** even though the historical story is Argentine; isolated Spanish words do not justify `es`. `lyrics=storytelling|political|social|identity|love|introspection|heartbreak`: this is a dramatic biography shaped by politics, class, fame, romantic relationships and mortality [EV_AL, EV_PA, EV_CA].

**Producer credits:** the Madonna official source and original 1996 release track metadata explicitly credit Andrew Lloyd Webber, Nigel Wright, Alan Parker and David Caddick [EV_OFF, EV_MB, EV_QB]. The official page additionally lists Emilio Estefan Jr, Clay Ostwald, Jorge Casas and Lawrence Dermer after an asterisk: their role appears linked to a distinct mix/reworking, **not established as producer credits across the original 31-track standard soundtrack**. Do not mix producer/remix/executive or songwriting roles until original track applicability is checked. Tim Rice is lyricist, not a soundtrack producer; John Mauceri is conductor, not producer [EV_QB, EV_OFF]. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` stay empty rather than treating Andrew Lloyd Webber or the earlier cast performers as Madonna's personal musical influences. Shared compositions do not imply sonic sameness.

**Critical disagreement:** Erlewine credits the polished execution but finds the work less involving [EV_AM]; specialist reviewer Portantiere emphasizes limitations in Madonna's range and transposition compared with more forceful stage casts [EV_CA]; Parker and contemporary interviews emphasize the adaptation's deliberate naturalistic performance and expanded register [EV_AL, EV_LT, EV_PA]. Preserve these separate interpretations. `landmark=1` refers to a documented vocal turning point and significant film-musical career project, *not* an assertion that it is among the user's favourite pop albums [EV_AL, EV_LT, EV_OFF]. `confidence=high` for original credits and the musical style; comparative vocal judgments remain subjective.
