# Moby — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes

**Method.** Source snapshot 25 October 2026. Scales are editorial, album-wide estimates anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`. The 27-column row is a proposal (`draft`) requiring Claude validation. A source naming a broad genre for only a few tracks is marked **some tracks** in the genre-evidence CSV. Sounds-like values are editor comparisons; influence requires explicit artist/critic evidence. Empty instrument and production cells are intentional.

## Moby (1992) — `moby-moby`

- **Identity and label control:** AllMusic dates Moby to July 1992, containing `Go`, `Drop a Beat`, `Next Is the E`, plus closing `Slight Return`/`Stream`. Its contemporary original release has approximately 54 minutes. AllMusic's bio explicitly says the label assembled the early recordings without Moby's involvement; thus `studio` denotes the conventional album category, not a coherent 1992 recording session. Preserve separate British alternate listing `The Story So Far` as an edition only. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/moby-mw0000086878](https://www.allmusic.com/album/moby-mw0000086878); [https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/moby-mr0000027092](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/moby-mr0000027092); [https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116).
- **Genres:** Techno, rave, house, electronica, electronic and club/dance are named by AllMusic. `chillout` is **some tracks** (last two); trance/acid house/acid techno apply only to the original `Go` material; not the album's principal style. This is a higher-energy rave album than the later Ambient. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/moby-mw0000086878](https://www.allmusic.com/album/moby-mw0000086878).
- **Scales:** Energy 4, tempo 4 and groove 5 reflect the club-oriented dance singles, reduced from a maximum due to two calmer closing pieces. Electronic 5 follows the description of programmed dance music. Mood 3 acknowledges euphoric leads alongside restrained passages. Experimental 3 reflects adventurous but recognisable rave structures; polish 3 and theatrical 2 are cautious, unlike later lavish crossover records. These are judgments not measured laboratory values.
- **Voice and lyrics:** Most prominently instrumental; the album does not support a stable album-wide sung language or lyrical theme. Thus `instrumental`, blank `language` and blank `lyrics` are deliberate.
- **Instruments/production:** The first-person 2016 account confirms Moby played the `Go` melodic idea on a **synthesizer and piano** rather than directly sampling the TV recording; they are documented instruments. No signature drum-machine model, sequencer brand or production token is assigned based solely on period or techno genre. [https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/](https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/).
- **Influence vs sample conflict:** Angelo Badalamenti's *Twin Peaks* theme is a confirmed melodic source for `Go` (influenced_by). Moby describes an attempted but unsuccessful sample and then a recreated melody; Pitchfork describes a sample. This discrepancy prevents a confident whole-album `sampling` production token. [https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/](https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/); [https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/1522-10-songs-that-sample-twin-peaks-well/](https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/1522-10-songs-that-sample-twin-peaks-well/).
- **Landmark/confidence:** `1`, `medium`: defining early rave hits but album assembly was externally controlled and versions differ. Sonic comparisons `Orbital|The KLF` are editorial only.

## Ambient (1993) — `moby-ambient`

- **Identity:** AllMusic's Ned Raggett calls it an Instinct-made collection rather than a newly conceived album, despite its normal position as Moby's second album. Some recordings predate 1993 by several years. `studio` follows standard studio-album numbering, not an assertion that recording took place together. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086); [https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116).
- **Genres:** Ambient and ambient techno dominate; techno, electronica and electronic document context and elements. `clubdance` applies to some brighter beat-driven pieces, not the entire record. Raggett explicitly distinguishes the less prominent rhythmic passages from quiet pads and drones; references to progressive house are **comparative** and not grounds to label it progressive house. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086).
- **Scales:** Energy 1, groove 2, tempo 1: mostly unhurried atmospheric pieces, but several songs have a subdued beat. Electronic 5: overwhelmingly synthetic sound bed. Mood 2: brooding atmosphere with luminous passages; theatrical 1 and polish 3 reflect quiet studio pieces. Experimental 3 tracks sound-design sketches and atypical rhythmic patterns but avoids equating ambient with avant-garde.
- **Voice/lyrics/language:** Instrumental emphasis; no dominant evidenced lyric subject or language.
- **Instruments:** Raggett identifies piano and synth-string textures in `J Breas`/`Piano & Strings` and percussion on `My Beautiful Blue Sky`; generic synth, piano and percussion supported, but sampled orchestra or acoustic string ensemble **not established**. Thus do not enter `orchestra`, `strings`, `synthbass` or a named drum machine merely from impressionistic imagery. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086).
- **Production:** Intentionally empty: no demonstrated signature technique/machine for this 1988–91 compilation. The credits attribute **production to Moby** and digital editing to Bob Ward; editors are not producers. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ambient-mw0000111086).
- **Influences:** In a first-person interview, Moby explicitly names Brian Eno, Harold Budd and the instrumental side of Bowie's *Heroes* as his entry into ambient music. These are documented influences on **his ambient direction**, though not a claim that any specific track quotes them. `sounds_like` references to Future Sound of London/Aphex Twin are sonic comparisons suggested by AllMusic, not producer credits. [https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/](https://echoes.org/2016/06/24/moby-in-the-echoes-podcast/).
- **Landmark/confidence:** `0`, `medium`: significant stylistic document, not among his defining crossover records. Critical evaluations vary; Raggett finds it pleasant but less inventive than Aphex Twin.

## Move (1993) — `moby-move`

- **Why included:** *Move* was Moby's first Mute/Elektra release, a critically praised EP with a No. 1 US dance-chart single. The *Washington Post* quoted Moby describing it as the first recent project over which he felt proper artistic ownership after Instinct's archival releases. The contemporary *Los Angeles Times* confirmed its No. 1 Billboard dance single; Christgau described the six-track EP as a substantial work. These accounts justify the landmark EP exception. [https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1993/10/29/moby-sailing-beneath-the-raves/01843719-440c-4f53-8950-f7c832cd1953/](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1993/10/29/moby-sailing-beneath-the-raves/01843719-440c-4f53-8950-f7c832cd1953/); [https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html); [https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html).
- **Format and editions:** Some UK/European releases present four tracks, while the main Elektra edition has six. Include the six-song EP as the fuller reference; do not mistake remixes and country variants for new albums. 1993 release date. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160](https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160); [https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/moby/move-song_hgvfn.html](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/moby/move-song_hgvfn.html).
- **Genres:** AllMusic names electronic, techno, trance, electronica and club/dance; The *Los Angeles Times* describes the rave/techno and classic disco fusion; *Trouser Press* names jungle and gospel elements on individual tracks. Ambient is a genuine **some-tracks** style from the atmospheric `The Rain Falls and the Sky Shudders`. These are not all album-wide dominant styles; tempo and groove are lower than a club-only EP. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160](https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160); [https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html); [https://trouserpress.com/reviews/moby/](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/moby/).
- **Scales:** Energy 3, tempo 3 and groove 4 reflect a dance-centred first half but a quiet closing stretch; electronic 5 because contemporary accounts document synths and keyboard programming. Mood 3 mixes euphoric club vocal refrains with the darker reflective ambient ending. Theatrical 3 reflects diva vocals versus instrumental restraint; experimental 3 the sharp sequencing contrasts; polish 4 the well-formed major-label productions.
- **Voice/lyrics:** Powerful Rozz Morehead and Carole Sylvan performances dominate the title track; softer personal singing and long instrumental passages support `powerful|instrumental|soft`. Love and introspection reflect an affectionate dance chorus and Moby's 1993 explanation of frustration, despair and hope. Lyrics sung in English. [https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1993/10/29/moby-sailing-beneath-the-raves/01843719-440c-4f53-8950-f7c832cd1953/](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1993/10/29/moby-sailing-beneath-the-raves/01843719-440c-4f53-8950-f7c832cd1953/); [https://musicbrainz.org/release/4dbb6bf2-659a-4b6f-8175-4f502794354d](https://musicbrainz.org/release/4dbb6bf2-659a-4b6f-8175-4f502794354d).
- **Instruments:** Moby is specifically credited synthesizer; *Trouser Press* identifies a prominent piano mix and a percussion-driven track, and Christgau describes an electrodrum ensemble. `synth|piano|drums|percussion` denotes sounds **heard in the recording** without asserting whether the drums were acoustic. No drum-machine model, sampler or software is guessed. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160](https://www.allmusic.com/album/move-ep-r225160); [https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-07-ca-54071-story.html); [https://trouserpress.com/reviews/moby/](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/moby/).
- **Production and credits:** Moby is sole producer on the original release; Curt Frasca engineered the title cut, which must not make him a producer. No uniquely evidenced signature hardware or studio treatment is assigned to the production column. [https://musicbrainz.org/release/4dbb6bf2-659a-4b6f-8175-4f502794354d](https://musicbrainz.org/release/4dbb6bf2-659a-4b6f-8175-4f502794354d).
- **Influences versus resemblance:** No direct named influence sufficiently tied to EP production; `influenced_by` intentionally empty. `Orbital|The Orb|Black Box` are tentative **editorial sounds-like** descriptions, reflecting rave, ambient and house/diva sides without pretending all three influenced Moby.
- **Landmark/confidence:** `1`, `high` on documented event, recording credits and critical genre language. Critical coverage is positive overall (Christgau, *Trouser Press*, *Los Angeles Times*), while the EP's deliberately contrasting sides make single-number mood/tempo approximations uncertain.

## Everything Is Wrong (1995) — `moby-everythingiswrong`

- **Identity and guests:** The 13-track standard album is confirmed by Moby's official discography. MusicBrainz/AllMusic credit Moby with writing/engineering/programming/sole production and Mimi Goese, Myim Rose, Rozz Morehead and others with vocals on particular tracks. **Do not mistake a guest vocalist for an album producer.** [https://moby.com/music/everything-is-wrong-album/](https://moby.com/music/everything-is-wrong-album/); [https://musicbrainz.org/release/8ef8f214-9640-35cd-a5ab-722576883268](https://musicbrainz.org/release/8ef8f214-9640-35cd-a5ab-722576883268).
- **Genres/scope:** Album-spanning electronica/techno/house contrasts with ambient/downtempo interludes. AllMusic also assigns rock-adjacent styles; they are included with lower rank because they are less representative of the whole sequence. Washington Post's 1995 review describes disco, reggae and dancehall on **particular songs**, and a contemporary *The Mix* interview describes hardcore and grunge moments. A modern specialist catalogue also applies happy hardcore and post-minimalism to smaller portions; a January 1997 retrospective describes a classical element. `hardcore` reproduces that article's term **without** presuming hardcore techno or a fixed punk substyle. `rave` describes the club side; treat the 1995 article's vocabulary as context. The late-added `postminimalism`, `happyhardcore`, `classical` are **some-tracks**, not album-level dominance. [https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000124002](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000124002); [https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/05/19/mobys-techno-sensers-crash-hop/e6dd41d9-c4d4-4728-a694-6246512f92d6/](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/05/19/mobys-techno-sensers-crash-hop/e6dd41d9-c4d4-4728-a694-6246512f92d6/); [https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140); [https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/8425-moby-everything-is-wrong.php](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/8425-moby-everything-is-wrong.php); [https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-01-19-ca-20000-story.html](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-01-19-ca-20000-story.html).
- **Scales:** Energy 3, tempo 3 and groove 3 balance pounding dance material against extensive reflective material. Electronic 4 rather than 5 acknowledges intentionally recorded distorted guitar; mood 2 is strongly inward/concerned despite rave uplift; experimental 4 follows deliberate stylistic juxtapositions; polish 4 follows sophisticated multi-track workflow; theatrical 3 captures big lead vocals alongside close ambient passages.
- **Voice/lyrical themes:** Powerfully projected dance singers and softer/ethereal Mimi Goese parts are both essential, so `powerful|soft|instrumental`. Lyrics and Moby's stated sleeve concerns cover spirituality, relationships, environmental destruction and introspection; order reflects interview evidence. Lyrics mostly English. [https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/05/19/mobys-techno-sensers-crash-hop/e6dd41d9-c4d4-4728-a694-6246512f92d6/](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1995/05/19/mobys-techno-sensers-crash-hop/e6dd41d9-c4d4-4728-a694-6246512f92d6/); [https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140).
- **Instruments:** Moby specifically says this album uses Yamaha SY22/SY35/SY85, Roland Juno-106 and Jupiter-6 synthesizers, samplers and an Ibanez electric guitar. `synth|sampler|electricguitar|keyboards` is conservative; no sampled choir or orchestral instrument is assumed. [https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140).
- **Production:** Moby says the **vocals** were recorded to three ADAT machines; most masters went from console to DAT; sequencing was **Cubase Audio on Mac**; distorted guitar passed through a backwards distortion pedal and Boss SE-50. Enter `adatrecording|datrecording|distortion|cubase|juno106|sy22|sy35|sy85|jupiter6|bossse50`. We intentionally do **not** enter `analogtape` and do not mistake general samplers for proof of cleared samples from other records. Yamaha SY/Jupiter/Boss SE units are new tokens, not inferred equipment. [https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/get-on-the-party-line/12140).
- **Influences:** Mark Jenkins's contemporaneous Washington Post profile links the *music* of Moby, explicitly discussing this 1995 work, to Giorgio Moroder and Tangerine Dream. Since it is a critic's genealogy, not Moby's admission of composition-specific borrowing, treat as medium-strength. Husker Dü is a comparison from Sound on Sound's later interview (sonic similarity, not credited influence). [https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1995/06/04/a-whale-of-a-difference/e9443f46-35a3-4a72-a5eb-206e808ac1be/](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/style/1995/06/04/a-whale-of-a-difference/e9443f46-35a3-4a72-a5eb-206e808ac1be/); [https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/hall-mn0000477116).
- **Landmark/confidence:** `1`, `high` thanks to multiple contemporary sources and unusual direct machine-by-machine account. Strong critical praise coexists with attacks on its eclecticism; capture conflict without averaging away the differences.
