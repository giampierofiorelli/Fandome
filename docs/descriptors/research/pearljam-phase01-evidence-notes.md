# Pearl Jam — phase 01 evidence notes (research draft)

Data cutoff: 27 October 2026. Four original studio albums, in chronological order. Exact 27-column schema. Scale scores are editorial interpretations of **album-specific** published criticism, not measured audio features. Where technical evidence is absent, `production` is blank.

## Ten (1991) — `pearljam-ten`
- **Genre ranking:** AllMusic labels the album grunge, alternative/indie rock and hard rock. Alternative rock receives the canonical `alternativerock` token; broad rock follows. Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/ten-mw0000265614) and [official band credits](https://pearljam.com/music/album/ten). Genre strength is album-wide, not an inference from Seattle origin.
- **Energy 4, mood 1, tempo 3, groove 2:** The big riffs and vocal crescendos create persistent weight, while Black, Oceans and Release make it less uniformly fast than the hardest records. The lyric emphasis on damage, personal history and alienation makes the album broadly dark. Anchored against Fandome's 0–5 guide.
- **Electronic 1, experimental 1:** Electric-guitar rock with some piano/organ and cello but no documented synthesizer or drum-machine parts. An electric band should not be scored at the fully acoustic 0 anchor.
- **Theatrical 3, polish 4:** Vedder's powerful delivery is expansive rather than classically theatrical. The original Tim Palmer mix is notably reverberant and studio-polished, not the drier 2009 Brendan O'Brien remix; compare [Palmer/Parashar account](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/pearl-jam-ten) and [Ten original credits](https://pearljam.com/music/album/ten). `heavyreverb` is specifically tied to the original recording's sound.
- **Instruments:** Gossard and McCready guitars; Ament bass; Krusen drums; Parashar piano/organ/percussion; Walter Gray cello. Tim Palmer is credited for unconventional percussion on Oceans, not as album producer. [Band album page](https://pearljam.com/music/album/ten).
- **Producers:** Pearl Jam and Rick Parashar only. Tim Palmer is credited as mixer; Brendan O'Brien's later remix does **not** make him an original-album producer.
- **Influences:** [McCready on Ten](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/mike-mccready-ten) specifies Hendrix and Stevie Ray Vaughan voicings/phrasing, including Alive and Black. [Gossard interview](https://stereogum.com/1519972/qa-pearl-jams-stone-gossard-on-songwriting-making-setlists-and-lightning-bolt/interviews) and [Ament on Zeppelin](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/jeff-ament-stone-was-always-writing-from-a-zeppelin-angle-so-seeing-if-fretless-bass-could-work-in-that-realm-was-exciting) support Led Zeppelin as a formative guitar-language influence; [McCready on Ten](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/mike-mccready-ten) connects Aerosmith to the early band's compositional mix. These are attributable artist statements rather than cover-song assumptions.
- **Lyrics:** Identity/family accounts (Alive, Jeremy), story-driven character material and heartbreak (Black); the words are treated as originals, not copied into this file. `sounds_like` is editorial, not an influence attribution.

## Vs. (1993) — `pearljam-vs`
- **Genre ranking:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/vs-mw0000620499) supplies grunge, alternative rock, hard rock; [Hank Shteamer](https://www.theshfl.com/album/Vs-1) explicitly identifies Rats as funk-rock and W.M.A. as groove-based, so `funkrock` is a lower-weight **some tracks** tag. The quieter Daughter/Elderly Woman are folksy, but criticism does not establish an album-level `folkrock` tag, so it is not used.
- **Energy 5, tempo 4, groove 3:** The opening Go–Animal run, Blood and Rearviewmirror are driving and abrasive, interleaved with the much quieter Daughter, Elderly Woman and Indifference; `groove=3` reflects W.M.A., Rats and Glorified G rather than implying a dance record. [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/pearl-jam-vs-album-anniversary); [Shteamer](https://www.theshfl.com/album/Vs-1).
- **Electronic 1, experimental 2:** Electric-guitar rock, without documented synthesizers or programmed rhythm; distinctive rock/funk tracks account for modest structural experimentation.
- **Mood 1, theatrical 3, polish 3:** Abuse, gun culture and social confrontation coexist with empathetic characters; the album is powerful but less reverberantly glossy than Ten. The review and Brendan O'Brien's own discussion of bringing members together in the studio support this assessment. [Producer interview](https://rollingstoneindia.com/qa-pearl-jam-producer-brendan-obrien-on-the-making-of-lightning-bolt/).
- **Instruments:** Core two-guitar/bass/drums setup from official credits. Acoustic guitar and emphatic drum patterns appear in coverage of Daughter, Elderly Woman and W.M.A.; separately played percussion instruments are not established. [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2013/03/1993-pearl-jam-vs/); [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/pearl-jam-vs-album-anniversary). Do not invent a conga or exact drum model. `production` is blank: an account of jamming together does not alone prove live-to-tape capture.
- **Producers:** Brendan O'Brien and Pearl Jam, **not** Nick DiDia (engineering/assistance). [Band credits](https://pearljam.com/music/album/vs).
- **Lyrics:** Daughter (child abuse), Rearviewmirror (escape from abuse), Glorified G (gun culture), W.M.A. (racially biased policing), Dissident and Elderly Woman (narrative); no verbatim lyric quotations. Influenced_by left empty: period descriptions of Zeppelin-like beats or Young-like feel are not enough to establish an *album-specific* stated influence.

## Vitalogy (1994) — `pearljam-vitalogy`
- **Dates:** First vinyl release 22 November 1994; wide CD release 6 December 1994. Both official; descriptor only stores year. [Band](https://pearljam.com/music/album/vitalogy).
- **Genres:** Grunge/alternative and hard rock remain, but [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15263-vs-legacy-edition-vitalogy-legacy-edition-live-at-the-orpheum-theatre-boston/) names proto-punk for Last Exit and folk-rock for Nothingman/Better Man; put track-level genres after the core. `experimental` represents the explicitly reviewed unconventional/noise interludes and collage, a texture rather than a uniform style. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/vitalogy-mw0000121446).
- **Energy 4, mood 1, tempo 3, groove 2, electronic 1:** Jagged explosive rock alternates with subdued songs and unsettling ambient/noise experiments. The album is dark and comparatively less danceable.
- **Theatrical 2, experimental 4, polish 2:** Arrangements are lean and deliberately awkward; Bugs, Aye Davanita and Hey Foxymophandlemama interrupt familiar songs. Critics disagree on value: AllMusic admires the focus while Pitchfork sees some interludes as ill-conceived. Both agree it is atypical for the band.
- **Instruments:** Eddie Vedder accordion; Jeff Ament standup/double bass; Stone Gossard Mellotron; Mike McCready slide guitar alongside rock guitars and drums. The original band credits name Dave Abbruzzese and Jack Irons, without implying Jack performed throughout. Acoustic guitar is not itemised because the retrieved album-level credits specify only guitar. [Band credits](https://pearljam.com/music/album/vitalogy); [MusicBrainz credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/0b0f54ae-7c80-4147-aad3-79083f09ebeb).
- **Production sampling:** The collage Hey Foxymophandlemama uses recorded spoken fragments; `sampling` narrowly describes this track, not digital sampler gear. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15263-vs-legacy-edition-vitalogy-legacy-edition-live-at-the-orpheum-theatre-boston/). No model or software is assumed. Brett Eliason recorded/mixed that track, but is **not** credited as whole-album co-producer.
- **Influenced_by:** Empty. Pitchfork compares a groove with the Stooges/MC5 and a quirky track with Captain Beefheart, but those are editorial *sonic likenesses*, not evidence of the artists purposefully influencing the whole album. `sounds_like` holds those comparisons.
- **Lyrics:** Fame, boundaries, vulnerability and relationships dominate; humor/satire on parts of Satan's Bed. Review sources support the introspective and social balance.

## No Code (1996) — `pearljam-nocode`
- **Genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000185938) labels alternative/grunge/hard rock despite the softer album profile. [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/reviews/album/the-genius-of-no-code-by-pearl-jam/) documents folk/roots and world-music-inspired percussion on particular songs; [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/no-code/157478390) explicitly describes Lukin as punk. Substyles have lower weight and **some tracks/texture** scope.
- **Energy 3, tempo 2, groove 2, mood 2:** Hail Hail, Habit, Lukin and Red Mosquito provide aggression; Sometimes, Off He Goes, Around the Bend and Present Tense are considerably more restrained. The reflective spiritual tone is still troubled, not euphoric. [Contemporaneous review](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1996/08/28/pearl-jam-all-grown-up/c65b107d-2625-4ae1-a516-e9ea3480d7bb/).
- **Electronic 1, theatrical 1, experimental 4, polish 3:** Amplified electric rock, unusual percussion, quieter vocal delivery and Eastern-tinged textures do not make this a synthesizer album. Jack Irons's distinct drums, studio writing and stylistic expansion justify the experimental rating; no particular synth or sampler is documented. [Jeff Ament interview](https://www.musicradar.com/news/jeff-ament-pearl-jam-no-code); [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/reviews/album/the-genius-of-no-code-by-pearl-jam/).
- **Instruments:** Official band page covers the core guitars, bass and drums; [Who You Are track credits](https://readdork.com/track/pearl-jam-who-you-are) document Eddie Vedder's **electric** sitar (not a conventional acoustic sitar) and Jack Irons percussion. [MusicBrainz release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/67a7abf8-56fc-4e5f-aad6-f823e5844364) documents Brendan O'Brien piano and Vedder harmonica; coverage describes acoustic guitars. `production` stays empty because sound-description prose does not establish particular signal processing or named hardware.
- **Voice and lyrics:** Dark-to-gentle singing, a spoken passage in I'm Open, and spiritual/self-examining themes reported by contemporary reviewers. Descriptions apply to the entire album rather than every individual track.
- **Influenced_by:** Neil Young (Smile) and Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (Who You Are) are credited as sonic debts explicitly by contemporary qualified critics in [TIME](https://time.com/archive/6729530/music-identity-crisis/) and [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1996/08/28/pearl-jam-all-grown-up/c65b107d-2625-4ae1-a516-e9ea3480d7bb/). *The albums' collaborations are not themselves the evidence*: the 1996 musical-critical analysis is. Their contribution as guest performers on other records is not misread as No Code album personnel. `sounds_like` comparisons remain separately editorial.
- **Disagreement:** TIME saw the stylistic breadth as unfocused; Washington Post and Guitar.com treat it as an artistic renewal. The genre diversity and experimental score remain supported even where quality judgments differ.

## Discography and scope caveats
- Studio discography through the 27 October 2026 cutoff contains 12 studio albums (1991–2024). The catalog currently omits **Binaural** (2000), **Riot Act** (2002), **Pearl Jam** (2006), **Backspacer** (2009) and **Lightning Bolt** (2013). These must be added by Claude before importing their future descriptor rows.
- **Merkin Ball (1995)**: [officially presented as a 2-track companion *single* to Neil Young's Mirror Ball](https://pearljam.com/music/album/merkin-ball); also often categorized EP in discographies. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/merkinball-mw0000624683) calls it non-essential, and the two-song guest appearance does not justify displacing full studio albums from this phase. Flagged for optional separate non-studio supplement; it is *not* included here and no 1995 row is silently omitted.
- **Mirror Ball (1995)**: band musicians back Neil Young and Pearl Jam's official discography features the collaboration; the original release is led/billed by Neil Young, not a Pearl Jam studio album. Check exact sleeve credit and `coBilled.ts` if a future Neil Young import includes it. [Official companion album listing](https://pearljam.com/music/album/neil-young-feat-pearl-jam-mirror-ball).
- Skip normal live catalogues, anniversary editions, and the Lost Dogs compilation; no repeated recordings as new studio album entries.
