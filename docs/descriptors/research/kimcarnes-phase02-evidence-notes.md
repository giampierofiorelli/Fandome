# Kim Carnes — Fandome Phase 02 research notes

**Status:** Research proposal, not second-person reviewed. Eight scale values are original 0–5 editorial judgments anchored to the Fandome guide. Production-device claims are based on credits or interview testimony; broader sonic comparisons are explicitly interpretive. This phase includes only four albums from 1980–83.

## Romance Dance (1980) — `kimcarnes-romancedance`

- **Original edition:** EMI America LP SW-17030, June 1980, nine songs. *St. Vincent’s Court/Romance Dance* is a later paired reissue and must not be added separately.
- **Producers:** original sleeve and Billboard advertise George Tobin, "in association with Mike Piccirillo"; discographies also credit Daniel Moore for one track. All three names are retained in the row, with Tobin first. Val Garay was brought in to complete/mix, as confirmed in his interview, but is **not** added to this album's producer field.
- **Genres:** `poprock|softrock|adultcontemporary` describe the album's dominant mainstream L.A. writing and arrangements per AllMusic. `soulpop` and `synthpop` are narrower characteristics of “More Love”, itself a remake of a Smokey Robinson composition. `rock` applies to selected guitar-forward songs. A cover is not evidence of Robinson as an album influence.
- **Instrumentation:** original credits show Bill Cuomo keyboards/organ/synth and ARP String Ensemble, Kim Carnes piano, rock rhythm section, mandolins, percussion and saxophones (including Raphael Ravenscroft / Joel Peskin). These are album-level presence, not claims for every track.
- **Production:** `arpstringensemble` refers to a credited instrument; `stringarrangements` to Cuomo's credited arrangement, not necessarily a live string orchestra. No audio-processing model or drum programming asserted.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 1, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 1, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. The sound is largely session-player pop with selective keys rather than programmed dance music.
- **Comparisons:** Rita Coolidge/Linda Ronstadt reflect the adult-contemporary vocal setting; Smokey Robinson reflects the soul-oriented single as an editorial comparison only. `influenced_by` empty.

## Mistaken Identity (1981) — `kimcarnes-mistakenidentity`

- **Original:** April 1981, EMI America, ten tracks; recorded December 1980–January 1981 at Record One. Subsequent *Mistaken Identity Collection* is a reissue with bonuses and is **not** included.
- **Producer:** Val Garay is the clear producer and recording engineer on original releases. A retailer's digital metadata also labels Kim Carnes “producer”, conflicting with original credits; keep the LP's Val Garay attribution and explain ambiguity here.
- **Genres:** AllMusic assigns pop/rock, soft rock and adult contemporary. Its review discusses reaching a new-wave audience; the collection review calls some original songs synth-pop and distinguishes folk and hard rock tracks. `newwave`, `synthpop`, `hardrock` and `folkrock` are deliberately lower-ranked than album-wide pop-rock styles. The hit "Bette Davis Eyes" is an original *1981 arrangement of an existing song*, not an original Carnes songwriting composition.
- **Instruments:** original LP and producer interview confirm guitars (Waddy Wachtel/Josh Leo), Craig Krampf acoustic drums and Synare electronic percussion, Bryan Garofalo bass, Bill Cuomo synthesizers, piano/mandolin, saxophone and other players. `electronicdrums` is a limited electronic-percussion presence, NOT a blanket assertion of programmed drums.
- **Production:** Val Garay says all instruments and lead vocal of “Bette Davis Eyes” were cut **live in the studio without overdubs**. `liverecording` only references that hit, not the whole album. Garay calls the hit the first take; Carnes in a later interview calls it the second. Accordingly **do not use `onetake`** or assert a definite take number. `synare` documented from Garay directly. Original credits say Prophet synthesizer, but a particular model is not sufficiently corroborated in original sources to add `prophet5` as a guaranteed album-specific model.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 4; synth hook and tight drums offset softer acoustic numbers. Whole-album electronic 5 would be inaccurate.
- **Influences:** Jackie DeShannon and Donna Weiss *wrote* “Bette Davis Eyes”; this is not a confirmed direct musical influence. Pat Benatar, Rod Stewart and The Motels are sonic analogies only.

## Voyeur (1982) — `kimcarnes-voyeur`

- **Original:** EMI America LP, September 1982, ten tracks. Garay is album producer; Duane Hitchings is credited recording a track at Hitchings Manor but must not automatically be elevated to producer.
- **Genres:** original discographies identify synth-pop, new wave and rock; Stephen Thomas Erlewine (AllMusic) explicitly hears drum machines, sequenced synths and arena-rock guitars within pop-rock songwriting. Adult-contemporary/soft-rock ballads keep the album from being purely synth-pop. `electrorock` is our crossover inference; `arenarock` is **only guitar texture**, not a blanket album genre.
- **Instruments / models:** MusicBrainz's track-level credits name Craig Krampf playing/programming an Oberheim DMX drum computer on the title track; Bill Cuomo on Oberheim and Prophet synthesizers, with ARP string synth from Duane Hitchings. It also confirms guitars, six-string bass, organ, keyboards, piano, double bass, drums and sax. Do **not** convert generic “Prophet” into Prophet-5 or confuse the DMX drum machine with the unrelated studio effects unit AMS DMX.
- **Production:** `drumprogramming` and `oberheimdmx` are directly credited on a track; `oberheim` and `arp` are generic credited synthesizer families. `sequencer` is not promoted to a precise production-model token because AllMusic reports sequenced textures but does not identify a specific machine.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 4, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 4, groove 4. Stronger electronic emphasis than *Mistaken Identity*.
- **Lyrical themes:** relationship strain / observation / tension in narrative songs; `social` is limited to narrative and public/private perspective, for review.

## Café Racers (1983) — `kimcarnes-caferacers`

- **Original:** EMI America album, October 1983, ten tracks. Keith Olsen (also written Olson in some third-party transcriptions) is the credited producer. Do not include standalone “Invitation to Dance” as an album track: it belongs to a soundtrack and was released separately.
- **Genres:** AllMusic calls the album pop/rock, contemporary soft rock and adult contemporary, with new-wave styling on singles and adult-contemporary ballads. `synthpop` / `electropop` apply to electronic pop singles; `aor` is an editorial observation from west-coast radio rock instrumentation. No unsupported synth-pop claim for all songs.
- **Instrumentation:** original track-by-track player transcription includes Steve Lukather / Waddy Wachtel electric guitar, Mark Andes bass, Denny Carmassi / Craig Krampf drums, Bill Cuomo / Steve Porcaro and David Paich keyboards, piano, saxophone and Keith Olsen percussion. Multiple keyboard configurations vary by track.
- **Production:** the album's “Invisible Hands” is documented in a transcription of original credits as involving a Jupiter-8 played by Martin Page; hence `jupiter8` refers **to one track** and carries lower source certainty than interview-verified machines. Avoid asserting electronic-drum programming on the entire album: the majority of credited drums are live. If Claude cannot reconcile the Jupiter-8 annotation to an original LP copy, remove the token rather than guessing.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 4, groove 4. High polish from radio-oriented recording, but still a live rhythm section.
- **Influences:** no qualifying first-person documentation that a specific artist musically shaped these four albums. Do not treat covered songs, guests or producers as influences. `sounds_like` is editorial sonic similarity, not proof of influence.
