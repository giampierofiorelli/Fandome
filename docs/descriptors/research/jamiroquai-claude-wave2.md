## bjork-medulla (Medúlla, 2004)

Sources: Pitchfork (Dominique Leone, 30 Aug 2004) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/737-medulla/ ; The Guardian (David Peschek) https://www.theguardian.com/music/2004/aug/27/popandrock.shopping5 ; LA Times (Richard Cromelin) https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2004-sep-05-ca-rack5-story.html ; Rolling Stone (Barry Walters) https://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/medulla-20040916 ; BBC (David Hooper) https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/mh63/ ; PopMatters https://www.popmatters.com/review/bjork-medulla/ ; Tiny Mix Tapes https://www.tinymixtapes.com/music-review/bjork-medulla ; Coke Machine Glow https://cokemachineglow.com/records/bjork-medulla-2004/ ; mxdwn https://music.mxdwn.com/2004/09/15/reviews/bjrk-medlla/ ; The New Yorker (Alex Ross, "Björk's Saga", 2004) https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2004/08/23/bjorks-saga ; The Observer (2005 interview) https://www.theguardian.com/music/2005/mar/13/popandrock ; Crack (2024 retrospective) https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/bjorks-medulla-retrospective/ ; MusicCritic https://musiccritic.com/bjork/medulla

- genres: `acappella` (new): Guardian "Almost entirely a cappella"; Pitchfork "her a cappella record"; Crack "acapella album". `experimental`, `electronic`: TMT "Styles: electronic, experimental". `pop`: PopMatters "In the realm of pop music (which Björk still creates within...)". `house` (closer and single Triumph of a Heart): Pitchfork "the house-y closer 'Triumph of a Heart'". `doowop`: LA Times "it's Bjork's version of doo-wop, with voices overlapped and layered". `disco` (one track): CMG on Triumph of a Heart, "a tweaked disco boogie".
- energy 2, tempo 2: LA Times "austere and demanding, more church than club"; mxdwn "Most of the album is in a minor key". Guardian adds "sometimes surprisingly violent".
- electronic 2: built from voices, but RS "plenty of sublime computer manipulations" and BBC "many tracks still have a distinctly electronic edge".
- mood 2: Guardian "often sombre but always full of awe"; mxdwn "sweet 'n sour melancholy feel".
- theatrical 3: grand choral passages ("a considerably grand soundscape of epic proportions", PopMatters) next to minimalist songs ("Many songs have a minimalist feel", BBC).
- experimental 5: this album is the guide's own anchor for 5. Pitchfork "her most musically adventurous record since Post".
- polish 4: CMG "each voice is clear, the production, honey"; mixed by Spike Stent (New Yorker). BBC calls it "disjointed", so not 5.
- groove 1: BBC "This is not a radio-friendly album... The only really immediate tunes are... 'Who Is It' and... 'Triumph of a Heart'"; mxdwn "an almost danceable closer".
- voice `powerful`: LA Times "effortlessly powerful"; Pitchfork "as powerfully resonant as anything I've heard from her".
- instruments: MusicBrainz credits list bass synthesizer (Björk #6, Mark Bell #12), piano (Björk #11, Nico Muhly #9), synthesizer (#1) and gong (Peter Van Hooke #1). `keyboards`: RS "some subtle keyboards"; New Yorker on a keyboard-played voice sample.
- production: `layeredvocals`, `overdubbing`: Pitchfork "thickly layered and overdubbed"; LA Times "voices overlapped and layered". `sampling`: AllMusic excerpt (MusicCritic) "singing and vocal samples". `programming`: MusicBrainz programming credits (Bell, Matmos, Alary, Sigurðsson). `sonicmanipulation`: New Yorker, Bell "processing the voices in ways that sometimes rendered them unrecognizable". `beatboxing` (new): Rahzel, Dokaka and Shlomo (Pitchfork, TMT). `throatsinging` (new): Tanya Tagaq (New Yorker, Guardian).
- lyrics: `political`, `social`: Observer, "Medulla was certainly her most political", made to counter "stupid American racism and patriotism". `spiritual`: Guardian "playful and hymnal"; LA Times on "nature, myth, metaphysics, love and a dash of politics". `love`: LA Times; Crack "structured around a series of trials between lovers".
- language `en|is`: Pitchfork "one of two songs sung in Björk's native Icelandic".
- producers: Mark Bell is credited as co-producer (#3, #7, #9). Björk's own credit is dropped. Matmos, Alary and Stent are credited for programming or mixing, not production.
- sounds_like: `Meredith Monk [4/14]`: Pitchfork says Ancestors "reminds me of the work of... Meredith Monk", and New Yorker says "The short interludes are not so much songs as studies in vocal texture, in the manner of Meredith Monk". Ancestors plus the three short interludes (Show Me Forgiveness, Öll Birtan, Miðvikudags) make 4 of 14; the count of interludes is my reading of the tracklist. `Kate Bush`, `Donald Byrd`: Guardian "an esoteric lineage to be traced back, from Kate Bush's work with the Trio Bulgarka to Donald Byrd's collaborations with arranger Coleridge Perkinson".
- influenced_by (Björk's own statements and choices, New Yorker):
  - `Meredith Monk (Dolmen Music)`: Björk played "a track or two from Meredith Monk's 'Dolmen Music'" in the mixing studio to illustrate what she wanted. Tagaq's vocal games "recalled the sensuous avant-gardism of Meredith Monk, whom she had long admired".
  - `Ariel Ramírez (Missa Criolla)`: "Björk mentioned Ariel Ramírez's 'Missa Criolla'... This was played and discussed."
  - `Hamrahlid Choir`: "While working on 'Medúlla,' Björk sometimes listened to CDs of choral songs by Reykjavík's Hamrahlid Choir".
  - `Karlheinz Stockhausen`: "A little Justin, a little Karlheinz", after she played Stockhausen's "Stimmung". **Uncertain:** Ross writes that "Björk played the entire exchange for laughs, but... she was driving at something serious and specific". Because of the joke rule I left out Justin Timberlake, who comes from the same line. Stockhausen is kept because Ross also calls him "one of Björk's heroes". Drop him if the owner treats the whole line as a joke.
  - Left out: Robert Wyatt and Mike Patton (guests); Joan La Barbara (played singing a Cage piece, so the target is unclear); Kraftwerk (Rahzel freestyling over Kraftwerk made her accept beatboxers, which is not a sound influence); Jón Leifs (his recordings decided her choice of choir, not the music).
- landmark 1: Crack "It's a landmark work in her discography".
- Reception: importance 3. Crack: "a landmark work" but "signalled the end of Björk's imperial phase" and fans rank it low. acclaim 4: Guardian 5/5, AllMusic 4/5, TMT 4.5/5, praise from Pitchfork and RS, BBC mixed. mainstream 2: a cappella on a major label; BBC "not a radio-friendly album".
- Legacy 3: Crack names Timbaland, Gazelle Twin (Pastoral) and Holly Herndon (PROTO).

Sources: Pitchfork (Mark Pytlik, 13 Oct 2011) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/15915-biophilia/ ; The Guardian CD review https://www.theguardian.com/music/2011/oct/06/bjork-biophilia-cd-review ; The Observer https://www.theguardian.com/music/2011/oct/09/bjork-biophilia-review ; BBC (Mike Diver) https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/5zq4/ ; The Quietus https://thequietus.com/articles/07157-bjork-biophilia-review ; Drowned in Sound https://drownedinsound.com/releases/16555/reviews/4143691 ; Consequence of Sound https://consequence.net/2011/10/album-review-bjork-biophilia/ ; PopMatters https://www.popmatters.com/review/149726-bjoerk-biophilia/ ; Rolling Stone https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-album-reviews/biophilia-252513/ ; Stereogum interview https://stereogum.com/744502/stereogum-qa-bjork-talks-biophilia/interviews ; MusicCritic https://musiccritic.com/bjork/biophilia

- genres: `electronica`: DiS "strafing electronica"; CoS "blistering electronica". `artpop`: PopMatters "A sneakily infectious art-pop number, 'Virus'". `experimental`: PopMatters "the experimental and ahead-of-its-time qualities"; Pitchfork "an experimental music pioneer". `pop`: Guardian "you want to talk about pop songs?". `drumandbass` (lead single Crystalline, so it may sit high in the list): Guardian "a fizzing, old-fashioned drum'n'bass breakbeat"; PopMatters "frenetic, off-kilter drum'n'bass crescendo". `jungle`: BBC "Crystalline's jungle-flavoured breakdown". `techno`: PopMatters "glitchy, twitchy techno beats". `breakbeat`: Guardian (above); TQ "a furious Amen-breaked hissy fit". `modernclassical`: PopMatters on Hollow, "its cold modern classical stylings". `ambient`: BBC "Mutual Core slips from blissful ambience".

- mood 2: DiS "this treads dark themes throughout"; Observer "what emerges here is hope", "a sense of wonder".
- theatrical 3: choirs and Hollow's theatrics (DiS "the theatrics of Selmasongs") against an intimate whole.
- experimental 4: irregular structures (TQ "bizarre, shifting rhythms of 'DNA'"); Pitchfork "lack of any real structure"; custom instruments.
- polish 4: Guardian "exquisitely controlled"; DiS "production sheen".
- tempo 2: PopMatters and DiS describe mostly slow, spare songs.
- voice `powerful|soft`: TQ "her formidable voice shoots for the stars"; Observer "spectacular swooping vocals" and "croons... in her sweetest lullaby mode".
- instruments: `harp`: MusicBrainz credits harps on Moon; TQ "delicate, silvery harp". `gameleste` (new): BBC "the gameleste – a fusion of gamelan and celeste". `organ`: TQ "pipe organ chords"; Björk, in Stereogum, "a pipe organ that just receives MIDI". `teslacoil` (new): Observer "a giant Tesla coil"; PopMatters "a Tesla Coil used as a bass". `gravityharp` (new): CoS "a set of four 'gravity harps'"; TQ "the gravity-driven pendulum harp on 'Solstice'". `brass`, `frenchhorn`, `trombone`, `trumpet`, `tuba`: MusicBrainz credits on Cosmogony; BBC "Cosmogony commands brass into action". `drummachine`: MusicBrainz "programming (drum machine)". `sharpsichord` (new): PopMatters "the Sharpsichord" on Sacrifice. `hang` (new): TQ "the hang, a turtle-shaped steel drum"; MusicBrainz credits "handpan".
- production: `programming`, `drumprogramming`: MusicBrainz credits (Damian Taylor, 16bit, Matthew Herbert, El Guincho, Mark Bell). `maxmsp` (new): Björk in Stereogum, "me and Damian [Taylor] started programming with Max program". `generativemusic` (new): Björk in Stereogum, Virus "is mostly about generated music"; CoS "employing generative music". `ipad` (new): BBC "certain tracks are partly recorded on an iPad"; Observer on tracing "hillocks of bass... on touchscreens".
- lyrics: `love`: Björk in Stereogum calls Virus "sort of a love song"; TQ "nearly all of these songs come across as much as love songs". `spiritual`: PopMatters on Cosmogony, "a simmering spirituality". `heartbreak`: Observer "Anyone recently heartbroken really ought to belt up for 'Mutual Core'". `introspection`: Björk in Stereogum, "'Moon' is very melancholic and about rebirth".
- producers: 16bit is credited as producer on Crystalline. Björk's credit is dropped. Herbert, El Guincho and Bell are credited for programming only.
- sounds_like: `Nico (The Marble Index)`, whole album: RS "like a haunted digital sister of Nico's 1969 album, The Marble Index". `Aphex Twin [1/10]`, `Reprazent [1/10]`: BBC on Crystalline, "part-Aphex Twin, part-Reprazent". `Squarepusher [1/10]`: DiS on Crystalline, "Squarepusher beats". `Thomas Newman (American Beauty) [1/10]`: DiS, Crystalline is "like a fever-dream rendering of the American Beauty soundtrack". `Antony and the Johnsons (The Crying Light) [1/10]`: CoS, "Dark Matter" plays "like the warped stepsister to... Antony's 'Dust and Water'", a song from The Crying Light. Antony worked on Volta, not on this album. `Steve Cravis [1/10]`: BBC, Solstice "recalls... Quell and its Steve Cravis soundtrack".
- influenced_by: empty. The interviews name only non-musical inspirations: Oliver Sacks's Musicophilia, David Attenborough, nature and the Icelandic crash.
- landmark 0: reviews call it "ground-breaking" (Observer) as a project. No source calls it one of her defining albums.
- Reception: importance 3, a notable project (the first app album) but not career-defining. acclaim 4: MusicCritic 81 from 23 critics, with Pitchfork 6.2 as the dissent. mainstream 2: Nonesuch one-off, self-built instruments, "fragile and demanding" (DiS).
- Legacy: no row. I found no professional source naming later music it influenced.

Sources: Pitchfork (Stephen Trouss) https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/10794-overpowered/ ; The Observer https://www.theguardian.com/music/2007/oct/14/popandrock.shopping1 ; Coke Machine Glow https://cokemachineglow.com/records/roisinmurphy-overpowered-2007/ ; Drowned in Sound https://drownedinsound.com/releases/11492/reviews/2525491-roisin-murphy-overpowered ; The Skinny (title-single review) https://www.theskinny.co.uk/clubs/reviews/roisin-murphy-overpowered ; Gigwise (Michael Baggs, 2014) https://www.gigwise.com/features/91607

- mood 3: Observer "the perennial glory of feeling lost and lonely at the disco"; CMG "incredibly fun".

This is the critic's claim, not Murphy's, so it goes here rather than in influenced_by. `Bobby Orlando|Giorgio Moroder`: Pitchfork "a gleaming shrine to the spirit of Bobby O and Giorgio Moroder". `Goldfrapp [1/11]`: Pitchfork, Movie Star "laces itself... into Alison Goldfrapp's glam pop corset". `Prince [1/11]` and `Michael Jackson (Off the Wall) [1/11]` (Footprints): CMG "indulging some Prince fantasies", "reaches Off the Wall Quincy Jones-ness". `Kylie Minogue (X) [1/11]`: DiS, Cry Baby is "in the style of the new Kylie effort" (2007). `Robyn (Robyn) [1/11]`: DiS, You Know Me Better "mirrors Robyn's recent chart achievements". `Amy Winehouse [1/11]`: DiS on Tell Everybody.

- Legacy 2: Gigwise says its influence "can be heard in today's current crop of electronic artists" but names none, so the score stays moderate.
