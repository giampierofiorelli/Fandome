# Rihanna — Phase 02: evidence notes (27-column format)

**Scope.** Four released standard-edition studio albums, 2010–2016. Every genre in the album row has a corresponding evidence entry. Genre evidence differentiates the whole record, some tracks, and texture; entries with `low` strength are not certified whole-track genre assignments. Songs below are mapped to individual source keys found in `sources.csv` (source index appears in the package import notes). Scale scores are editorial aggregates based on album-specific expert reviews and arrangement credits; not track-average measurements.

**Instrument rule.** Instrument names are included only where an original album credit or an expert musical description identifies them; generic `instruments` credits do not establish a specific instrument. `production` records verified techniques rather than inferring DAWs/machines from synthesizer timbre.

**Genres:** pop, rnb, dancepop, contemporaryrnb, edm, electronic, dancehall, reggae, triphop, dubstep, powerballad, acousticpop, hiphop, poprock. The CSV is deliberately inclusive across songs, not a claim that all styles apply to every song. The genre evidence file records limits.

**Scales** (energy 4, electronic 4, mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 4, groove 4): these are comparative judgments from the source reviews for this specific album. Electronic and groove are assessed across both beat-led and slower songs; songs with contrasting moods are retained in lyric themes. Blank not required given sufficiently detailed album reviews.

**Instrumentation:** synth, keyboards, acousticguitar, electricguitar, bass, piano, drums. Evidence is distributed over individual tracks; no claim that all instruments appear on every track.

**Production:** sampling. Only source-attested techniques. See album-specific detail below.

**Producer credits:** Stargate, Sandy Vee, The Runners, Sham, Mel & Mus, Tricky Stewart, Soundz, Alex da Kid, Kuk Harrell, Makeba Riddick, Awesome Jones!!!, Willy Will, Veronika Bozeman. Track-based producer and co-producer credits only. Guest performers, lyricists, mixers and recording engineers do not qualify without producer credit; vocal production is distinguished where named.

**Production / instrument evidence.** `sampling`: the Avril Lavigne vocal excerpt on *Cheers (Drink to That)* and Enya reference on *Fading* are described in criticism and editorial. Acoustic and electric guitar are credited on *California King Bed*; guitar on *Skin*; piano on the closing ballad per Apple. Synths supported by Pitchfork and electronic club tracks. No unverified drum machine model, DAW or Auto-Tune is assigned.

**Critical disagreements:** Pitchfork values reggae and dark near-dubstep departures, whereas AllMusic calls the album uneven and foregrounds its hit-driven dance tracks. Genre diversity is intentionally preserved. `sounds_like` is an explicitly editorial resemblance list, not a claimed influence. The quoted comparison with Alanis Morissette or Beyoncé does not establish `influenced_by`.

**Critical disagreements:** The Washington Post calls *Where Have You Been* house and the Guardian stresses huge rave synths; album still includes midtempo R&B, Caribbean cuts, and ballads. We omit standalone `electrohouse` and `triphop` for this record because an xx sample or four-on-the-floor rhythm does not establish those narrower genres.

**Genres:** rnb, pop, dubstep, electronic, synthpop, dancepop, hiphop, house, disco, reggae, dancehall, electro, rave, avantpop, ballad, edm, contemporaryrnb. The CSV is deliberately inclusive across songs, not a claim that all styles apply to every song. The genre evidence file records limits.

**Scales** (energy 3, electronic 4, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3): these are comparative judgments from the source reviews for this specific album. Electronic and groove are assessed across both beat-led and slower songs; songs with contrasting moods are retained in lyric themes. Blank not required given sufficiently detailed album reviews.

**Instrumentation:** synth, piano, strings. Evidence is distributed over individual tracks; no claim that all instruments appear on every track.

**Production:** pitchedvocals, autotune. Only source-attested techniques. See album-specific detail below.

**Producer credits:** David Guetta, Giorgio Tuinfort, The-Dream, Stargate, Benny Blanco, Oakwud, Flippa, Pop Wansel, Mike Will Made-It, J-Bo, Future, Chase & Status, Nicky Romero, Parker Ighile, Nathan Cassells, Mikky Ekko, Elof Loelv, Justin Parker, Carlos McKinney, Brian Kennedy, No I.D., Labrinth. 8. **What Now** — Piano-based dramatic pop with electronic bass contrasts. Sources: [U_nme](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/UK/New-Musical-Express/2012/NME-2012-11-24.pdf).

12. **Get It Over With** — Moody strings documented by Guardian critic. Sources: [U_g2](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/nov/25/rihanna-unapologetic-review).
13. **No Love Allowed** — Reggae/dancehall track with muted-guitar accents; sometimes called electro-ragga. Sources: [U_g](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2012/nov/15/rihanna-unapologetic-review), [U_nme](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/UK/New-Musical-Express/2012/NME-2012-11-24.pdf).
14. **Lost in Paradise** — Final pop/R&B song, electronically arranged. Sources: [U_pf](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/17369-unapologetic/).

**Production/instrument evidence.** `pitchedvocals` for *Numb* and *Loveeeeeee Song* comes from critics describing altered/slow voices, and `autotune` applies to the Future feature as specifically called out by Pitchfork, not automatically to Rihanna. Piano on *Stay*; strings on *Get It Over With*; synthesizers on *Numb* are stated in criticism. *Jump* interpolates Ginuwine *Pony*: interpolation ≠ sampling, and does not independently trigger the production sampling token.

**Credits nuance.** Official site separates production from composition; for instance Sia is a *Diamonds* writer, not a credited producer. `Nobody's Business` and the *Love Without Tragedy/Mother Mary* suite cite The-Dream and Carlos McKinney as producers. The official standard is 14 tracks; *Half of Me* (deluxe) is excluded.

**Critical disagreements:** Pitchfork calls the record a synth-pop slog and emphasizes monotony, while Guardian and NME value unpredictable stylistic collisions and dubstep/piano contrasts. Our score `experimental=3` recognizes that variance without suggesting avant-garde composition across the album. NME uses the descriptor avant-pop; it appears as `avantpop` only at some-tracks scope.

12. **Higher** — Rasping soul ballad with keys/strings and actual sampled Soulful Strings. Sources: [A_pg](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/17947-rihanna-higher/), [A_off](https://www.rihannanow.com/music/anti/).
13. **Close to You** — Spare piano/keyboard-based ballad. Sources: [A_off](https://www.rihannanow.com/music/anti/), [A_bg](https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/music/2016/01/28/album-review-rihanna-anti/iDNsRThrxDIw7dc8N8STUN/story.html).

**Primary track credits.** Official artist page is unusually detailed: Carter Lang organ/synth bass and Shea Taylor keyboards on *Consideration*; Nuno Bettencourt guitar on *Kiss It Better*; Mick Schultz guitar and Brian Schultz bass on *Desperado*; Fred Ball/Joseph Angel keyboards/drums on *Love on the Brain*; No I.D. keyboards on *Higher*; Brian Seals keys on *Close to You*. Acoustic guitar on *Never Ending* is described by critics, while the official page credits Chad Sabo with its instruments without specifying a particular subtype. `sampling` is evidenced by licensed Soulful Strings elements in *Higher*. *Never Ending* uses a Dido composition interpolation, distinct from use of the original sound recording.

**Influence vs resemblance.** Co-writer Fred Ball expressly identified Prince and Al Green as sonic goals for *Love on the Brain*; these appear in `influenced_by` with a source. Tame Impala is a **covered composition with Kevin Parker producing/performing**; this is not an uncorroborated album-wide declared inspiration, so it does not go in influenced_by. The rest of `sounds_like` contains only editorial sonic comparisons.

**Credit caution.** Rihanna is credited *executive producer*, not all tracks' recording producer. Kuk Harrell is credited *vocal producer*; neither role is silently promoted to whole-album track producer. Additional production (Glass John, Travis Scott) and co-production (Twice As Nice, Frank Dukes) are included because the official page labels them as such. Some official credits are clipped or oddly typeset (e.g. *Kiss It Better* incomplete “DRUM” line); this is not enough to invent a drum credit for that track.

**Critical disagreements:** Slant objects to the album's cohesion, whereas Pitchfork sees value in its restraint, and SPIN singles out unlikely synth-rock, Tame Impala and retro soul choices. Genre evidence includes each separately. `alternativernb` and `psychedelicsoul` are **new genre tokens** rather than forced into existing unrelated ones.
