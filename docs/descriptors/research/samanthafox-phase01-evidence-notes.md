# Samantha Fox — Phase 01 evidence notes

Research: 27 October 2026. GitHub branch `descriptors`, read-only. Four original Jive studio albums. This batch is incremental. All scores are Fandome editorial judgments, **not** sourced quantitative measurements.

## 1. Touch Me (1986)
Cherry Red remaster confirms 10 original tracks, not its 27 bonus tracks. AllMusic calls the original album dance-pop with pronounced vocal reverb and synth lead on “Wild Kinda Love”; its Cyndi Lauper remark is a **critic comparison**. Pop Rescue documents synths on “I'm All You Need,” guitars on “Do Ya Do Ya,” sax on “It's Only Love,” and synthesized/vocal samples on “He's Got Sex” and “Drop Me a Line”. Gary Barnacle also has an original-track saxophone recording credit on MusicBrainz. There is insufficient evidence to identify exact synthesizer or drum-machine models. Sources: https://www.cherryred.co.uk/touch-me-deluxe-edition ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/touch-me-mw0000193848 ; https://poprescue.com/2015/03/27/pop-rescue-touch-samantha-fox-vinyl-1986/ ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/068cf161-df9d-4fcc-8f4e-1263d54b76d9 . The 1986 genre `pop` is broader than its better-supported `dancepop`.

## 2. Samantha Fox (1987)
Cherry Red identifies the ten original tracks; Stock Aitken Waterman provide “Nothing’s Gonna Stop Me Now”, while Full Force produce “Naughty Girls”. Crack's **specific album review** identifies rock guitars and a hip-hop passage on “Naughty Girls”; 'hiphop' is placed after more representative styles because it is a **track-scope** distinction. Album of the Year tags synthpop and pop rock, the discography cites freestyle. `synth` and `keyboards` are supported by the album-specific synthpop discussion and production context, but no specific synthesizer model has a verified original sleeve credit. No production technique or signature model was reliably documented for the album itself; the **production field deliberately stays empty**. The label's producers are credits, not influences. Sources: https://www.cherryred.co.uk/samantha-fox-samantha-fox-deluxe-edition ; https://crackmagazine.net/article/news/samantha-fox/ ; https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/72090-samantha-fox-samantha-fox.php ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/75052b13-d66d-49dd-947e-bb769c2bbeb0 .

## 3. I Wanna Have Some Fun (1988)
The original **1988** U.S. CD is documented by MusicBrainz and original digital rights records dated 1988-11-01. Cherry Red refers to **1989**, its **UK release**. Original LP = 12 tracks: Cherry Red's deluxe set and non-album B-sides/Full Force guest singles are excluded from the scope of this row. Source-by-track Pop Rescue review supports synths, drums, bass, piano, sax, rock guitar, vocal sampling/layering; MusicBrainz credits the title track with samples of Loleatta Holloway's “Love Sensation” and Yazoo's “Situation”. **These are samples, NOT an `influenced_by` relationship.** Producers include Full Force, Kevin Saunderson, Bolland brothers, SAW, Fred Zarr and Chris Tsangarides; do not assume all produced all songs. Hip-hop is a track-level idiom in Full Force material and comes after album-defining pop/house. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/80684665-b1c5-46f9-9781-23705967bd79 ; https://www.cherryred.co.uk/samantha-fox-i-wanna-have-some-fun-deluxe-edition ; https://poprescue.com/2024/05/14/review-i-wanna-have-some-fun-by-samantha-fox-vinyl-1988/ .

## 4. Just One Night (1991)
Original album is eleven or twelve tracks depending on market; do not equate the 2012 2CD deluxe issue with the 1991 original. A **transcription of the 1991 Jive CD booklet** enumerates the instruments precisely, e.g., Paul Pesco guitar and C&C percussion/drums on “Pleasure Zone”, saxophone on the title track, sequencer and programmed drums on “Another Woman”, live bass and drums and Glenn Tipton guitar solo on “Spirit of America”. Drum programming, generic programming, and album-track remixing are credited, hence the production tokens. The album includes disco covers by Donna Summer and Andrea True Connection: **cover compositions do NOT establish an influence**. AllMusic describes dance-pop, urban contemporary/R&B, and certain polished pop-rock tracks; house is documented in original issue style metadata (less strong). For `sounds_like`, Prince is a critic's *Pleasure Zone* comparison; Janet Jackson and Madonna are contemporary Los Angeles Times *stylistic comparisons* and not documented creative influences. Sources: https://jp-mania.ru/publ/samantha_fox/1991_just_one_night/just_one_night_cd_jive_usa_1357_2_j/632-1-0-1679 ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/just-one-night-mw0000263737 ; https://www.cherryred.co.uk/just-one-night-8211-deluxe-edition ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-06-09-ca-609-story.html .

## ARTIST INFLUENCES with sources (artist-wide, separate from album row)
- **Debbie Harry / Blondie**: in a first-person interview with *El País*, Fox calls Deborah Harry her favourite artist since childhood; when discussing the 2015 cover of “Call Me”, she lists “Heart of Glass”, “Atomic” and “Call Me” as favourites. https://elpais.com/elpais/2015/07/14/tentaciones/1436862711_383163.html . In her 2017 *Guardian* interview, she says she wanted a Debbie Harry-like onstage video for debut single “Touch Me”, **visual rather than evidenced sonic inspiration**: https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2017/dec/12/samantha-fox-glamour-model-fame-16-stalkers-david-cassidy .
- **No album-specific musical influences reliably established from first-person testimonies for these four albums.** The `influenced_by` column is consequently **empty** on all four rows. Cyndi Lauper, Prince, Janet Jackson, Madonna, Kylie Minogue etc. are critic `sounds_like` relations, **not** attributed direct influences. Dusty Springfield / The Rolling Stones / Donna Summer / Andrea True Connection are **covers**, Full Force/Stock Aitken Waterman are **producers**.

## Source-confidence caveats
- Early original LP liner notes identify producers but generally lack session-level synth or drum-machine models; never add Fairlight/DX7/LinnDrum by association with PWL.
- Pop Rescue is a competent detailed **listener review**, not an original studio credit. Instrument names from that source are track-level listening observations.
- A 1987 record and some track editions differ by territory; retention here uses the original studio-release group, not an arbitrary expanded CD.
- Title `Just One Night` includes producer credited remix of original album track 3; `remixing` is based on the **1991 CD sleeve** not later 2012 bonus mixes.
- Fandome uses scores 0–5, whole integers, and `draft` status. `confidence` refers to the row's evidence as a whole, not certainty for every soundtrack detail.
