# David Bowie — Phase 01 evidence notes

Method: original editions, official Bowie discography, track-level MusicBrainz credits, retrospective reviews. Source IDs below identify distinct entries in `bowie_phase01_sources.csv`. No instruments or machines are assigned by genre alone. All 8 scale values are editorial ratings against the Fandome guide, not claims of objective measurement. Source strength differs across albums.

## `bowie-davidbowie` — David Bowie (1967)

**Identity/release:** `OFFICIAL67` confirms 1967 UK Deram album and Mike Vernon as sole credited producer; `MUSIC67` confirms original track sessions. Do not conflate with 1969 album of same name.

**Genres:** `REVIEW67` explicitly describes English **showtunes**, a strong Anthony Newley imprint and pervasive **baroque pop**; `MUSICGROUP67` names **music hall** as the idiom. `ALLMUSIC67` additionally classifies it as **psychedelic pop**, despite reviews stressing distance from contemporary psychedelic rock. Hence psychedelic pop is lower ranked and texture-scoped. `pop` is generic parent. New `musichall` requires an approved token addition.

**Scales:** energy **2** because arrangements are lively but largely not forceful; electronic **1** because acoustic/orchestral sources predominate but an electronic organ is credited; mood **3** for humour offset by morbid/dystopian tales; theatrical **5** for narrated character songs and dramatic persona shifts described by Jon Savage; experimental **2** for eccentric lyrical premises within familiar song structures; polish **3** for arranged professional studio work; tempo **2** for mainly moderate songs and ballads; groove **1** because character-driven numbers predominate over dance rhythms. These are cautious whole-album judgements.

**Voice:** `clear|spoken` reflects Bowie singing and narrative recitation (especially the dramatic closing piece); no evidence for exceptional digital vocal effects. **Instruments:** `MUSIC67` documents session piano, acoustic guitar, bass, drums, organ and handclaps. `MUSICGROUP67` documents prominent arranged brass and woodwinds at album level. No brass subtype is imposed; backing vocals are not an instrument. **Production:** intentionally blank (no proven specific devices or techniques for this issue). **Lyrics:** storytelling first; irony, love, social/dystopian commentary, early gender/persona narratives and spiritual themes present in specific songs (`REVIEW67`, `GUARDIAN67`). **Influenced by:** Anthony Newley and Syd Barrett are *explicitly* described as direct stylistic influences by Jon Savage in `GUARDIAN67`; The Kinks are kept only as similarity, since `REVIEW67` characterises resemblance to Ray Davies' sketches rather than a documented transmission. **Landmark:** 0, historically the debut but not generally identified as a defining full album. **Confidence:** medium, since personnel provenance is partial and reviewers disagree about psychedelia.

## `bowie-davidbowie1969` — David Bowie (1969), later titled Space Oddity

**Identity/release:** `OFFICIAL69` and `OFFICIAL69B`: November 1969 original UK title *David Bowie*, US *Man of Words/Man of Music*; reissued as *Space Oddity* in 1972. Original song sequence may include unlisted **Don't Sit Down**; the 1972 sequence differs. ID must suffix later eponymous album year **1969**.

**Genres:** `ALLMUSIC69` calls it **British folk-rock**, **art rock**, **British psychedelia**, **experimental rock**. `PITCH69` uses **prog-folk** for the album and **psych-folk** for the song 'An Occasional Dream'; the latter is not a uniform property. `bluesrock` is limited to 'Unwashed and Somewhat Slightly Dazed', which Pitchfork characterises as blues-boogie (editorial broad grouping: should not outrank named album-wide styles). A **psychedelia** tag is broader than synth-based psychedelia: texture emerges from folk arrangements and effects, not club electronics. Reviews disagree about the quality and cohesion; Pitchfork stresses a disjointed overreaching record whose title song is extraordinary.

**Scales:** energy **2** for long, often reflective acoustic songs with occasional forceful crescendos; electronic **1** because Stylophone, Mellotron, chord organ are present amid mostly played acoustic instrumentation; mood **2** given isolation, breakup and political disillusionment despite celebratory moments; theatrical **4** for astronaut narrative, long-form allegories, orchestra and performative shifts; experimental **3** for the mixture of long narrative songwriting and unusual keyboard timbres; polish **3** professional studio but disparate approaches; tempo **2** mostly moderate; groove **2** some extended rhythmic blues-rock alongside folk ballads.

**Voice:** clear lead, often emphatic/powerful on extended songs. **Instruments:** `BIBLE69` directly lists twelve-string guitar, electric guitar, bass, drums, acoustic instruments, Rick Wakeman's Mellotron and **electric harpsichord**, Bowie Stylophone, kalimba, electric chord organ, flute, harmonica, handclaps. `MUSIC69` corroborates Stylophone, Mellotron and cello on title track. `harpsichord` is used as the closest *instrument family* for electrically amplified harpsichord (not silently substituted for a genre); this is noted for Claude. **Production:** intentionally blank: release mix, studio and mastering are known, but no signature production technique or machine beyond the named instruments is sufficiently verified. **Producers:** `OFFICIAL69`: Tony Visconti except the single 'Space Oddity' by Gus Dudgeon. **Influences:** `PITCH69` critically places Beatles, Stones and Love within the LP's songwriting approaches; not claiming these are Bowie's own firsthand declarations. **Landmark:** 0 for the *album*, despite landmark single. **Confidence:** medium due conflicting assessments and some instrumentation drawn from secondary archival source.

## `bowie-themanwhosoldtheworld` — The Man Who Sold the World (1970)

**Identity:** `OFFICIAL70` specifies original US 1970 date versus 1971 UK appearance. **Producers:** only Tony Visconti (`OFFICIAL70`, `MUSIC70`). Mick Ronson plays guitar, not production credit.

**Genres:** `ALLMUSIC70` classifies art rock, experimental rock, glam rock, hard rock, proto-punk, and calls most music **hard blues-rock or psychedelic folk-rock**. Its *glam* and proto-punk tags are retrospective traits, lower priority than the album's heavy riff-based rock. Album also has quiet eerie passages; it is not an uninterrupted metal record. The new `psychedelicfolkrock` is exact source-phrase normalization. **Critical disagreement:** `ALLMUSIC70` stresses relatively conventional hard blues-rock structures combined with strange lyrics, while the official history `OFFICIAL70B` highlights its embryonic metal/glam impact. Avoid representing retrospective metal ancestry as 1970 genre consensus.

**Scales:** energy **4** driven by Mick Ronson guitar riffs and live drums; electronic **1** synthesizer exists but heavy electric band predominates; mood **1** oppressive/paranoid themes; theatrical **4** bizarre narratives, changes in singing and theatrical arrangements; experimental **3** extended structure and mixing strangeness within blues-rock; polish **3** distinctive and professional but rougher than later Bowie; tempo **3** mixture of fast heavy riffs and slow sections; groove **3** bass-driven rock pulse, not dance-floor composition.

**Voice:** strong / occasionally harsh vocals per AllMusic review. **Instruments:** `MUSIC70`, `ALLCRED70` document Ronson electric guitar, Visconti bass and piano, Woodmansey drums/percussion, Bowie twelve-string guitar, organ, saxophone, harmonica and Stylophone; Ralph Mace Moog. More limited sax/harmonica appear on specific tracks, not a defining full-album texture. **Production:** `distortion` based on `ALLMUSIC70` fuzz-heavy bass and edgy thin guitars; `moog` based on explicitly credited Ralph Mace use (`MUSIC70`). Do not infer an uncredited specific tape-machine or synth model. **Themes:** paranoia, madness, social/political violence, mysticism and identity crisis in songs, documented song titles and AllMusic thematic account. **Influences:** blank; Black Sabbath/Led Zeppelin are *sounds_like* editorial comparisons, not claimed direct inspiration. **Landmark:** 1 for beginning of Ronson/Visconti heavy-rock transformation (official bio). **Confidence:** high.

## `bowie-hunkydory` — Hunky Dory (1971)

**Identity/producer:** already catalogued as `bowie-hunkydory`; original official source `OFFICIAL71` credits **Ken Scott and David Bowie**. `MUSIC71` often lists Ken Scott alone on *particular tracks* (for instance Life on Mars?), and sometimes both on other songs. Album-level credit is both, with disagreement documented rather than silently omitting Bowie. Ronson arranged and played; he is not a producer.

**Genres:** `ALLMUSIC71` names art rock, British folk-rock, singer/songwriter, experimental rock, glam rock and proto-punk; generic `poprock`/`rock` reflects the pop-rock characterization in `PITCH71` and the album-based genre classification, while *glam* and *proto-punk* are especially concentrated on 'Queen Bitch'. `PITCH71` also stresses ballads, proto-glam stompers and love letters. Order places piano-led art/pop before the smaller crunchy guitar subset.

**Scales:** energy **3** contrasts driving Queen Bitch with intimate piano material; electronic **1** Mellotron appears in some credits but music is overwhelmingly a live band, acoustic keys and orchestration; mood **3** alternating wonder, anxiety and introspection; theatrical **4** dramatic songs and grand string flourishes; experimental **3** lyrical ambiguity and stylistic juxtapositions in recognisable songwriting; polish **4** elaborate but organic Trident recording and orchestrations; tempo **2** many moderate ballads; groove **2** a few rhythmic rock songs amid listening-focused piano writing.

**Voice:** clear, often powerful or gentle in softer sections. **Instruments:** `MUSIC71` confirms Rick Wakeman piano, Ronson electric/acoustic guitar, Bolder bass/trumpet, Woodmansey drums, Bowie twelve-string and saxophone, orchestra and Mellotron (Ronson on Life on Mars?). `QUIETUS71` independently discusses Wakeman piano, Ronson's arrangements and layered acoustic guitars. **Production:** `stringarrangements` based on credited Ronson strings arrangement and orchestral sessions; `overdubbing` restricted to documented layered acoustic guitars noted by `QUIETUS71`, rather than any asserted double-tracked lead-vocal technique. **Lyrics:** identity and self-reinvention, private/biographical family life, love, stories and spiritual/occult/philosophical imagery are supported by `QUIETUS71`, `PITCH71`, and song accounts. **Influences:** `VELVET71` gives Bowie's explicit description of Queen Bitch as an homage to Lou Reed, corroborated by `NME71`; `QUIETUS71` connects some layered guitars to George Harrison's *All Things Must Pass* as mediated by Ken Scott's production work. This latter *production influence* is critically reconstructed, not a Bowie statement, and may be omitted on strict review. **Landmark:** 1. **Confidence:** high, with track-level instrumentation and multiple original/critical sources.
