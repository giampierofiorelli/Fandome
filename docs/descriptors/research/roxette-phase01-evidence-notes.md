# Roxette — phase 01 / album evidence and decisions

Scope: original album versions, not reissues with bonus tracks. Numeric scale judgments follow Fandome anchors and are not empirical measurements. Genre names reproduce source distinctions; some redundant geographic/era tags come from AllMusic's published style taxonomy.

## 1986 — Pearls of Passion (`roxette-pearlsofpassion`)

- **Genre:** AllMusic applies Adult Contemporary, Contemporary Pop/Rock, Euro-Pop and Swedish Pop/Rock, and its written review identifies much of the music as dance-pop. Album of the Year separately categorises it as Pop Rock; Qobuz supplies the broad Pop label. Therefore dance-pop is first and the more rock-oriented label second, despite the latter's comparatively restrained guitar sound. These are source genre terms, not assumptions from the artist's nationality.
- **Personnel and sounds:** Label-supplied Qobuz credits on original songs identify Clarence Öfwerman on keyboards, Jonas Isacsson and Staffan Astner on guitars, Tommy Cassemar on bass, Pelle Alsing and Per Andersson on drums, Mats Persson on percussion, and three named trumpeters on 'Soul Deep'. The original-pressing database also lists Matts Alsberg on acoustic bass. Hence the mapped `doublebass` is **not safely demonstrated**: the original source calls it acoustic bass, which can mean an acoustic bass guitar rather than upright bass. **Do not include `doublebass` unless the credit can be resolved.**
- **Production:** No clearly supported named signal-processing method or specific machine for the 1986 album. `production` is deliberately blank rather than guessing drum programming, studio technology or effects from the sound.
- **Voices/themes:** Marie Fredriksson's full-power melodic lead alternates with Per Gessle's clearer, lighter lead; themes centre on attachment, loneliness and romantic loss. Descriptive scales: modest-to-strong rhythmic propulsion, mid-range electronic contribution, relatively natural rather than fully mechanised studio sound.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic's critical appraisal is significantly less favourable than some fan accounts; numerical descriptors do not equate review approval with energy or quality. Retain the distinctive dance-pop character even if later albums are more guitar-led.
- **Edition:** Qobuz 'Extended Version' and MusicBrainz's 1997 'The First Album' have extra tracks. Instruments draw on explicitly named original 1986 recordings; extra 1997 recordings are excluded.
- **Sources:** https://www.allmusic.com/album/pearls-of-passion-mw0000462822 ; https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/pearls-of-passion-roxette/p3hftyprow1ic ; https://thelookforroxette.com/releases.html?id=1842

## 1988 — Look Sharp! (`roxette-looksharp`)

- **Genre:** The 1988 pressing describes Synth-pop and Pop Rock; AllMusic lists Adult Contemporary, Contemporary Pop/Rock, Euro-Pop and Swedish Pop/Rock. The emphasis shifted from the debut's dance-pop to rhythmic pop-rock structured around electric guitars, synths and high-impact ballads; the arrangement of 'The Look' is specific evidence of that collision.
- **Instrument detail:** Original label and MusicBrainz credits: Jonas Isacsson and Morris Michael guitars, Erik Borelius Spanish guitar (map to acoustic guitar), Janne Oldaeus slide guitar, Anders Herrlin bass, Pelle Alsing drums, Erik Häusler/Adam McCulloch saxophone, Jalle Lorensson harmonica, Clarence Öfwerman/Andy Wright keyboards. Marie Fredriksson is also credited with keyboards and piano. ESQ-1 and electronic drum elements follow firsthand statements by Gessle and Isacsson.
- **Machines and method:** Gessle says he composed 'The Look' while programming an **Ensoniq ESQ-1** around a sequenced bass-groove, after deliberately adopting synthesizers and more technical arrangements. Guitarist Isacsson describes a desired drum-machine + rock-guitar combination. The new `ensoniqesq1` production token denotes that concrete instrument on that track, not a claim that it is all over the LP. `talkbox` is credited on 'Dangerous' by MusicBrainz, a **single-track** detail. No specific Linn or Roland drum-machine model is established.
- **Producers:** Clarence Öfwerman handles the majority. **Adam Moseley** (often misspelled Mosley) produced only 'Cry', 'Chances', and 'View from a Hill'. Both therefore belong in `producers` as track-credited producers.
- **Influences:** Gessle explicitly says he asked Jonas for a guitar part modelled on 'Taxman' by The Beatles, from *Revolver* (1966). Isacsson independently recalls Gessle demanding a ZZ Top-like sound for 'The Look'. These are **documented musical aims for a track**, not merely reviewer comparisons; listed in `influenced_by` with the album tag for the Beatles. The broader resemblance to Heart and the Bangles remains subjective and is kept in `sounds_like` only.
- **Scales:** Glossy studio pop, strong electric/processed bass combination and hook density; big vocal and guitar crescendos increase theatricality, not experimentalism.
- **Sources:** https://www.songwriteruniverse.com/per-gessle-roxette-2018/ ; https://www.fireworks-magazine.com/post/roxette-blast-from-the-past-jan-2021 ; https://www.dailyroxette.com/jonas-isacsson-meets-the-daily-roxette/ ; https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/look-sharp-roxette/fh9ouaonmsaca

## 1991 — Joyride (`roxette-joyride`)

- **Genre:** Original record style categorises rock, pop, Pop Rock and Europop. AllMusic applies adult-contemporary and European/Swedish pop-rock variations. There is pronounced power-pop immediacy to guitar hooks, but `powerpop` was intentionally **not** inserted as a hard genre without an unambiguous album-level source listing that exact term.
- **Instrumentation:** Original LP credits name Anders Herrlin on electric bass and programming, Jonas Isacsson on acoustic/electric/slide guitars, mandolin, Clarence Öfwerman on keyboards, piano, Hammond organ and barrel organ; Pelle Alsing drums; Gessle rhythm guitar/harmonica/tambourine; other credits list Jalle Lorensson harmonica, Kjell Öhman accordion, a brass contingent and string arrangements by Henrik Janson. Sources are album-wide roles; no claim that every instrument occurs on each track.
- **Production:** 'Programmed by' is explicit on the original record. `stringarrangements` is independently supported by Henrik Janson's arranger credit. No particular drum machine, digital workstation or tape format verified, so none appears in the row.
- **Producer:** Original LP lists Clarence Öfwerman as producer. AllMusic may label Anders Herrlin 'Producer' in its broad album personnel list, but the original release identifies him as bassist/programmer/engineer and not a named overall co-producer. Follow the original LP rather than extending producer scope.
- **Musical reference caution:** A 1991 interview describes 'Joyride' as evoking *Magical Mystery Tour* and Aerosmith, but that is comparative language rather than unequivocal disclosure of a formative influence. Those names stay under `sounds_like`. Gessle's general influences, such as the Kinks and T. Rex, are listed in the artist-level dossier, not applied to this album without album-specific evidence.
- **Mood/voice:** Festive title song and 'The Big L.' sit beside 'Spending My Time', 'Fading Like a Flower' and the comparatively restrained 'Perfect Day', so an across-album mood of 3 (not 5) is justified.
- **Sources:** https://interrecords.net/roxette-joyride-emi-disc593981n1.html ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/86174d0e-024c-4c25-96ed-9d218ed61c74 ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/r16999/review

## 1992 — Tourism (`roxette-tourism`)

- **Classification:** Historically marketed as a **tour album**, expressly neither an ordinary live album nor a hits compilation. Many contemporaneous discographies include it as the fourth studio album, because it introduces many new studio recordings, but Album of the Year calls the format a 'Compilation'. Fandome has no `hybrid` type. We retain `studio` provisionally so the substantive new recordings are represented, marking this exception for Claude's review rather than hiding the conflict.
- **Material:** Original 16-track programme includes new studio recordings ('How Do You Do!', 'Fingertips', 'The Heart Shaped Sea', 'The Rain', 'Keep Me Waiting', 'Cinnamon Street', 'Silver Blue', 'Come Back (Before You Leave)', 'Queen of Rain'); Sydney live renditions ('The Look', 'Joyride'), other concert songs, Chilean concert/audience blend for 'It Must Have Been Love', plus hotel-room recordings ('Here Comes the Weekend', 'So Far Away'). `concertrecording` is therefore definitely present but **not a whole-album technique**.
- **Gear/personnel:** Original sleeve compilation transcribes Marie Fredriksson piano; Gessle acoustic/electric guitar and harmonica; Jonas Isacsson acoustic/electric guitars and mandolin; Greg Leisz pedal steel; Bo Eriksson oboe; Vicki Benckert accordion; Anders Herrlin bass/programming; Clarence Öfwerman keyboard/piano/programming; drum/percussion, maracas, tabla, tambourine. Sound varies widely by site and track: hotel-room/acoustic and live sounds should not all be treated as polished Stockholm studio sessions.
- **Production:** `programming` is documented on multiple new studio tracks and record credits. `concertrecording` is documented for Sydney, Zurich and Santiago material. No inferred machines or portable recording system models.
- **Producer credit caveat:** Clarence Öfwerman appears as main producer on original pressing and MusicBrainz track listings. AllMusic additionally lists Anders Herrlin as 'Producer' on its album credits. Retain Herrlin as a credited album contributor with medium confidence, but verify specific tracks on a scanned original jacket if Claude maintains a narrower album producer rule.
- **Scales/confidence:** A composite album is inherently difficult to summarise. The moderate values reflect swings between stadium concert performances and sparse hotel acoustic material. Comparisons in `sounds_like` are only listening judgments; there is no album-specific documented `influenced_by` entry.

## General audit notes

- Do not infer musical influences from band membership, guest credits, the Swedish scene or critic comparisons.
- Album `sounds_like` entries are non-factual, explicitly editorial similarity candidates, not sampled/collaborating artists.
- The artist's 1984 appearance of the band name on Gyllene Tider's export project **Heartland** predates the duo: it is not a Roxette album here.
- Do not use later PG Roxette releases as if they were original Per Gessle + Marie Fredriksson Roxette albums.
