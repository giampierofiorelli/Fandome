If adding a `CO_BILLED` entry for it, use `{credit:'Ella Fitzgerald with Duke Ellington and His Orchestra'}` with no `with` ID, and do NOT create an unknown artist link.

**Release issue and recording dates:** official Ella discography assigns Rodgers/Hart to **1956**, Apple/Verve 1956 copyright and October 1956 issue confirm it; AllMusic's album display reads 1957 despite its exact **August 21–31, 1956** recording dates. Keep 1956. *Ella and Louis Again*'s Apple digital page shows a February 1957 date that conflicts with the original **July–August 1957 sessions**; ignore digital metadata for exact original release day. Label specialist uDiscover and physical-discography listing indicate October 1957 double-LP issue. *Ellington* songbook was built from orchestral AND small-group sessions in 1957; some digital tracks carry a 1956 copyright and different remaster packaging. Maintain the original album as **1957**, not as a 2017 "compilation" or standalone small-group LP. ## armstrong-ellaandlouis — *Ella and Louis* (1956)

- **Identity:** Verve studio LP of eleven standards, August 1956 Capitol Studios (Hollywood); **Norman Granz** the producer. Original `armstrong-ellaandlouis` already catalogued; Ella crosslink pending. [official_discography, udiscover_ella_louis, allmusic_ella_louis_review]
- **Sound / instruments:** Fitzgerald’s clear, light-toned voice with Armstrong’s grainy, raspy vocal delivery and occasional **trumpet**. Oscar Peterson **piano**, Herb Ellis **guitar**, Ray Brown **acoustic double bass**, Buddy Rich **drums**. Not a large orchestra and not a synthesized record. Apple distinguishes mellow ballads from buoyant midtempo swing. [apple_ella_louis, allmusic_ella_louis_review, udiscover_ella_louis]
- **Genres:** `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing|bop|earlyjazzvocals|americanpopularsong`. AllMusic names Bop, Early Jazz Vocals and American Popular Song (latter two as missing Fandome tokens). Low-weight tail styles describe Ella’s improvisational approach and Armstrong’s older jazz vocal manner, not a claim that the album is primarily bebop. [allmusic_ella_louis, apple_ella_louis]
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 (swing tunes alongside sustained ballads); electronic 0 (fully acoustic unit); mood 4 (playful affectionate exchanges with wistful love standards); theatrical 2 (unforced conversational singing); experimental 1 (familiar Songbook songs); polish 3 (tidy minimal 1956 studio small ensemble). `voice=clear|soft|raspy` represents both lead singers. `production` blank: no supported particular studio effect, hardware or confirmed one-take claim. [apple_ella_louis, allmusic_ella_louis_review]
- **Themes:** love, heartbreak, some playful irony/longing. `sounds_like` pairs Louis Prima & Keely Smith and Sarah Vaughan as **Fandome editorial similarity**, not artist-stated influence. `influenced_by` empty: the guest performer, collaborators and covered composers must not be counted as influences. `landmark=1` is supported by label history and longevity of the duo record.

## ella-ellafitzgeraldsingstherodgersandhartsongbook — *Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Rodgers and Hart Song Book* (1956)

- **Release/session:** original 1956 two-LP Song Book; Aug 21–31 Hollywood sessions. Its AllMusic *Release Date 1957* display conflicts with 1956 dated label and the official artist discography. Norman Granz produced; **Buddy Bregman arranged and conducted**, not a record producer. Do not mistake recent remaster credits, engineer Alan Emig or individual additional arrangers for producer. [official_discography, apple_rodgers, qobuz_rodgers, public_library_rodgers, jazz_times_granz]
- **Genres:** `vocaljazz|jazz|traditionalpop|standards|swing|bigband|americanpopularsong`. AllMusic names Vocal Jazz, Jazz, Traditional Pop, Standards and American Popular Song, and critic Scott Yanow explicitly distinguishes swinging moderate numbers from near-pop ballads. JazzTimes historical session account documents large band, strings, and smaller settings; `bigband` is important but not every track. [allmusic_rodgers, jazz_times_granz]
- **Instruments:** studio orchestra is well evidenced, with a warm **string** section, **brass/woodwinds**, piano, rhythm section, guitar, reeds, harp and some cello/flute (see restored original *Manhattan* score and detailed MusicBrainz personnel). Do not assert the same section plays every single track. `production=hornarrangements|stringarrangements` reflects demonstrated original musical arrangements, not imagined tape processing. [jazzlines_manhattan, musicbrainz_songbooks]
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 (ballads vs swinging medium numbers); electronic 0; mood 3 (romantic yearning and witty irreverence); theatrical 3 and polish 4 (orchestrated cabaret/stage-song treatment); experimental 2 (Ella departs from melodies but presents standard song structures). `voice=clear|powerful|soft`, lyrics love/heartbreak/irony/introspection. JazzTimes' close analysis of *My Funny Valentine* singles out improvisational phrasing even over sometimes heavy orchestra. [allmusic_rodgers, jazz_times_valentine]
- **Disagreement:** Yanow hears some overly pop-like Bregman backing; Michael J. West hears a strongly sentimental orchestra on *My Funny Valentine* but praises Fitzgerald’s invention. Thus `bigband`/`jazz` coexist with `traditionalpop`; not all tracks are pure swing. [allmusic_rodgers, jazz_times_valentine]
- `landmark=1`: second celebrated original Song Book, influential in her core Verve repertoire. Comparisons Frank Sinatra/Sarah Vaughan/Doris Day are editorial sounds_like, **not artist-stated album influences**. Rodgers and Hart are writers of covered songs and must not be added to `influenced_by`.

## ella-ellafitzgeraldsingsthedukeellingtonsongbook — *Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Duke Ellington Song Book* (1957)

- **Album/session:** original 1957 Verve multi-LP songbook, not a 2017 digital "compilation" despite Apple reissue labels. Norman Granz produced. Duke Ellington and Billy Strayhorn participated in planning and performing, with large orchestral settings **and** smaller settings. Historians describe short-notice scoring and improvised solutions, not one homogeneous big-band run. Original album title retains Ella's main billing; official discography explicitly says *with Duke Ellington*. [official_discography, allmusic_duke, jazz_times_duke, jazz_journal_duke]
- **Instruments:** Ellington piano, woodwind/reed and brass sections (trumpet, saxophone, trombone, clarinet), acoustic bass and drums, and smaller-studio units with guitars; Ray Nance violin and Stuff Smith violin appear on specific tracks. MusicBrainz and AllMusic track credits confirm these, but **not every instrument is on all tracks**. [allmusic_duke, musicbrainz_duke]
- **Genres:** `vocaljazz|jazz|swing|bigband|standards|traditionalpop|bop|blues|americanpopularsong`. AllMusic expressly names blues as genre and Bop/Swing/Traditional Pop/Vocal Jazz/Standards/American Popular Song as styles. Big Band is backed by on-record Ellington-orchestra sessions but cannot describe the whole 3-LP programme. `bop` remains from Phase 01's requested missing token. [allmusic_duke, jazz_times_duke]
- **Scales:** energy 3, groove 4 (more vigorous jazz rhythmic interplay than Ella's restrained Peterson quartet), tempo 3; electronic 0; mood 3 (swings between bright excitement and dark ballads); theatrical 3 (orchestra mixed with intimate combos); experimental 3 (long jazz improvisations/scat and varied original arrangements); polish 3 (accomplished players, but historians note sometimes hurried/reworked charts). `production=hornarrangements` is supported by ensemble-arrangement descriptions, but no analog-tape machine/technique is assumed. [jazz_times_duke, allmusic_duke, jazz_journal_duke]
- **Themes:** romance, blues-based heartbreak, irony, narrative songs; `influenced_by` empty because Duke Ellington is the composer AND guest, and performers/musicians do not count as direct testified influences under Fandome rules. `landmark=1`, important unique Song Book where composer took part. [allmusic_duke]
- **Uncertainty:** AllMusic lists recording ending 17 Oct 1957, JazzTimes highlights the main June sessions, and some 1956-dated individual tracks in digital credits might be included on later versions. These data describe DIFFERENT session/reissue scopes. Preserve original release year 1957 and do not infer a single ensemble or session from the modern track listing. [allmusic_duke, qobuz_duke, jazz_times_duke]

## ella-ellaandlouisagain — *Ella and Louis Again* (1957)

- **Identity:** original Oct 1957 double LP, nineteen cuts with **seven solo vocal tracks**; never replace with later abridged one-LP "duets only" editions. Norman Granz produced. [official_discography, udiscover_ella_again, jazzimages_again_caveat, lescharts_again_issue]
- **Personnel:** Ella vocals, Louis vocals and **trumpet (only a subset of songs)**, Oscar Peterson piano, Herb Ellis guitar, Ray Brown acoustic double bass, **Louie Bellson drums** (Buddy Rich belongs to *Ella and Louis*, not this album). [apple_ella_again, udiscover_ella_again, jazzcodex_again_vol2]
- **Genres:** `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing|americanpopularsong`. AllMusic explicitly names the first, Standards, Traditional Pop and American Popular Song; Apple and uDiscover highlight swing, vocal scat and warm acoustic interplay. Although Ella scat-sings on *Stompin’ at the Savoy*, there is insufficient source basis to label the ENTIRE album bop. [allmusic_ella_again, apple_ella_again]
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 from alternating upbeat performances and slow standards; electronic 0; mood 4 affectionate, witty despite pensive love themes; theatrical 2 for conversational small ensemble; experimental 2 for spontaneous exchange/scat on a few numbers; polish 3 acoustic studio production. `production` blank: uDiscover describes one released **rehearsal performance** on *Stompin' at the Savoy*, but that does not validate `onetake` for the entire album. [apple_ella_again, udiscover_ella_again]
- **Critic limitation:** Alex Henderson would prefer more Armstrong trumpet, while label editorial emphasizes the singers' synergy. Guitar/piano/bass/drums are stable, but trumpet is documented on only six songs. [allmusic_ella_again, udiscover_ella_again]
- `sounds_like` Louis Prima & Keely Smith / Sarah Vaughan is Fandome editorial comparison; not direct album influence. `influenced_by` left empty, for the same guest/cover/composer exclusion. Landmark 1 reflects the canonical second original collaboration, not chart claims.

## Genre/production vocabulary and import issues

**Potential new exact genre tokens**, because AllMusic explicitly names them: `americanpopularsong` (all four rows) and `earlyjazzvocals` (Ella and Louis only). Fandome's current guide lists `standards`, `traditionalpop`, `vocaljazz` but these are distinct wording/genre classifications; do not silently map exact AllMusic named styles to a different token. Claude should add both to `src/data/scenes.ts`, `src/data/genreFamilies.ts`, and `scripts/validate-descriptors.ts`, or explicitly resolve whether Fandome treats *American Popular Song* as a non-genre repertoire label. **Do not merge a CSV with unrecognised genre tokens before the schema is updated.** `bop` was already requested in Ella Phase 01, and still requires checking its addition before import. All instrument tokens and production tokens used here exist in the present guide.

Across the four rows, `production` is intentionally empty for the two Peterson-quartet duet projects because source evidence proves personnel, not particular recording machines/effects; the two orchestral projects use only clearly evidenced `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` (Bregman) and `hornarrangements` (Ellington). All four `influenced_by` cells are intentionally empty because no album-specific artist/producer testimony supports naming a musical influence. **Do not treat Louis Armstrong, Duke Ellington, Rodgers, Hart, the covered songwriters, or accompanists as influences solely by collaboration, writing, or performing.**
