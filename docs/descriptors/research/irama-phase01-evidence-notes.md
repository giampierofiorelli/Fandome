# Irama — Fandome Phase 01 evidence notes

Scope: original releases **Irama (2016)**, **Plume (2018, EP)**, **Giovani (2018)** and **Crepe (2020, EP)**. The repository branch `descriptors` had no `irama` entry in `catalog.ts` and no `docs/descriptors/irama.csv` when checked on 26 October 2026. This package proposes data; it does not modify the repository. 2019 *Giovani per sempre* is an expanded reissue of *Giovani*, not a wholly separate new studio album. Retail editions/2024 vinyl repress dates must not replace original dates. The 2023 collaborative album *No Stress* must be treated separately, with co-billed artist Rkomi.

## Methodology

All eight ordinal values (energy, electronic, mood, theatrical, experimental, polish, tempo, groove) are **editorial comparative judgements** based on album-specific sonic descriptions and instrumentation, not scientific measurements and not a direct claim from a source. Scores apply to the complete release, and the genre-evidence file marks song-scoped colors. Production tokens denote explicitly credited techniques or machine models; no inferred DAW, Auto-Tune or exact drum-machine model. `sounds_like` is an editorial recommendation hypothesis, **not** evidence of influence. `influenced_by` is left blank: a documented career-wide inspiration (Italian songwriters / hip-hop) cannot automatically be attributed as the formative musical influence of a particular record. The eight scores below are intentionally explained individually.

## Irama (2016) — `irama-irama` — studio

Core sources: [Warner album notes](https://shop.warnermusic.it/products/irama-cd); [Rockol contemporary review](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/v-9172/irama-irama); [Qobuz per-song instrument credits](https://www.qobuz.com/it-it/album/irama-irama/0825646378197); [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/irama/).

- **Genres:** `poprap` and `italianpop` dominant, with conventional `pop` melodies, `hiphop` as rhythmic arrangement and some tracks, and `songwriter` reflecting lyrical craft. Warner specifically juxtaposes Italian cantautorato and hip-hop; Rockol defines the project as their combination. These aren't independent subgenres for every single track.
- **Instrumentation:** piano, acoustic/electric guitars, bass, drums, keyboards, violin/cello, accordion and mandolin are in Qobuz original album-level track credits. Song-scoped extra instruments are not assumed to be continuous across all nine tracks. Neither a named sampler nor an identifiable synth model is supported.
- **Production:** the credits include additional programming; `programming` is used conservatively. Producers **Giulio Nenna and Andrea DB Debernardi**; producer role is supported by the label and Qobuz. Mastering and engineering responsibilities do not entail extra producer names.
- **Voice:** mostly articulated sung `clear` voice, rhythmic `rap` phrases and forceful choruses `powerful` in Rockol's pop-rap account.
- **Lyrics:** experiences and self-understanding, love, generational observation; `storytelling` is a modest interpretive tag grounded in title-by-title thematic review.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` because brisk rhythmic tracks and restrained ballads coexist; `electronic=2` for hip-hop arrangement within predominantly live-acoustic/electric credits; `mood=2` for more reflective and uncertain songs than celebrations; `theatrical=3` for emphatic hooks with otherwise direct storytelling; `experimental=2` for genre mixing but conventional pop-rap song forms; `polish=3` for finished professional studio arrangement without maximal layering; `tempo=3` for a broadly mid-paced album; `groove=2` because rhythmic rap is important but it is not principally club dance music.
- **Similarity and influence:** sonic recommendations Coez, Rocco Hunt and Ultimo are editorial hypotheses only. None of them is asserted as a documented influence on this album. `landmark=0`: debut is historically relevant but eclipsed by the breakthrough records.

## Plume (2018) — `irama-plume` — EP

Core sources: [Warner seven-track EP description](https://shop.warnermusic.it/products/plume-vinile-blu-trasparente); [Rockol review](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/8037/irama-plume); [All Music Italia review](https://www.allmusicitalia.it/recensioni/irama-plume-2.html); [Qobuz original-track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/it-it/album/plume-irama/d4szrzzc6kzgc); [AllMusic production/instrument credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0003182136).

- **Genres:** broad `poprap`, Italian melodic `pop`, and `songwriter` across the EP; `latinpop` applies especially to **Nera**; `dancepop` to **Che ne sai** and **Nera**; `acousticpop` to quieter songs, `hiphop` only to the rap phrasing notably near **Un respiro**. **Do not call the full EP reggaeton.** `Nera` has a documented Latin flavor and melodic offbeat groove; the genre file scopes it at track level.
- **Instrumentation:** Qobuz credits piano, electric piano, electric guitar, bass, drums/keyboards and electronic programming, plus recorder (on **Che ne sai**) and a bassoon (on **Che vuoi che sia**). AllMusic confirms Moog synthesizer, cello and a distinct drum-programming contribution. `bassoon` and `recorder` do not yet exist in Fandome and need schema extension rather than unfaithful substitutions.
- **Production:** Producers **Giulio Nenna, Andrea DB Debernardi, Fausto Cogliati**; Fausto's contribution is track-specific. Matias Keskiruokanen is credited with composition/arranging/programming on **Un giorno in più**, **not** indisputably with original-EP production: do not infer production solely from his arrangement. Tags `programming`, `drumprogramming`, `moog` and `falsetto` (explicit reviewer description of **Voglio solo te**).
- **Voice:** articulated lead with high/emphatic refrains, occasional `rap`, softer ballads and a documented falsetto passage; token `falsetto` is in production rather than voice token list.
- **Lyrics:** relationships, separation, reconciliation and personal reflection. Warner and Rockol both position lyrics within a young-generation emotional life.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` for ballad-heavy album balanced by dance-oriented Nera; `electronic=2` for the mixture of acoustic keys/guitars and programmed rhythms; `mood=3` mixed melancholy and brighter Latin pop; `theatrical=3` because big emotive hooks do not amount to full stage-show grandiosity; `experimental=2` for conventional pop-rap with few style contrasts; `polish=4` because mainstream studio mix and clear, foregrounded vocals; `tempo=3` as a mid-tempo center; `groove=3` for Nera/Che ne sai offsetting multiple ballads.
- **Similarity:** Coez, Ultimo and Carl Brave are editorial resemblance markers supported contextually by Rockol, not influence claims. `landmark=1` because this 2018 breakthrough EP is defining even though not a studio album.

## Giovani (2018) — `irama-giovani` — studio

Core sources: [Billboard Italia interview](https://billboard.it/interviste/irama-nuovo-album-giovani-intervista/2018/10/1914452/); [La Stampa 2018 reporting](https://www.lastampa.it/spettacoli/2018/10/18/video/irama_diventa_grande_con_giovani_-180082/); [Radio 105 interview](https://www.105.net/lo-showcase-di-irama-a-105-mi-casa-giovani-e-un-disco-ricco-di-colori/); [Rockol review](https://www.rockol.it/recensioni-musicali/album/8265/irama-giovani); [Qobuz original tracks in expanded version](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/giovani-per-sempre-irama/w0fnuidb4uiza).

- **Genres:** pop-rap and Italian pop are the organizing structure. Band-based rock/pop-rock, soul, funk and retro eighties dance pop have **explicit artist/interview evidence** but mainly specific tracks. Do not describe the complete album as a funk record or disco album. `songwriter` and `hiphop` reflect autobiographical tracks and rap-rooted passages.
- **Instrumentation:** Qobuz credits electric/acoustic guitars, bass, drums, piano, synth, electric piano, Hammond organ, drum-machine percussion and keyboards. **Escort** explicitly credits a **talk box**, without implying the voice is predominantly vocoded. The **Giovani** title track includes a live-recorded band (guitars/bass/drums), confirmed by Irama; not a claim that every track was tracked live. No unproven tape format or DAW.
- **Production:** Nenna and Debernardi are primary; Matias Keskiruokanen and Kalle Lindroth are credited as producers on Finnish-made songs. Qobuz 2019 *Giovani per sempre* republishes original 2018 cuts **and adds extra tracks**: use original ten-song track list as scope, not 2019-only additions (e.g. songs with additional guests). Distinguish a writer or vocal producer from a full producer. `liverecording` (specific track), `programming`, `drumprogramming`, `talkbox` (specific track).
- **Voice:** alternating direct sung `clear`, loud impassioned `powerful`, occasional rapped `rap`, intimate `soft` tracks.
- **Lyrics:** relationships and youthful identity, generational conflict (Rockstar), candid first-person accounts (Non mollo mai), humour or irony on retro-styled material.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` because tender pop ballads alternate with driving rock and funk; `electronic=2` for analog band instrumentation together with synths, programmed beats; `mood=3` because affirmative defiance and love coexist with sadness; `theatrical=3` due to big sung hooks but no continuous dramatic conceptual structure; `experimental=3` for stylistic range and a band-tracked track alongside programmed retro-dance, without avant-garde forms; `polish=4` for multi-location professional recording/arrangement; `tempo=3` for mixed medium-paced ballads and faster numbers; `groove=3` for funk and eighties dance excursions, with many non-dance songs.
- **Critical disagreement:** Rockol's contemporary rating is **5/10** and it questions the artist's ability to go beyond mainstream romantic songwriting, despite other press emphasizing the eclecticism. Do not use commercial success as evidence of sonic novelty.
- **Similarity:** Coez, Ultimo, The Kolors are comparative hypotheses, **not** historically evidenced album influences. `landmark=1` editorial: a central 2018 breakthrough-era studio album with distinctive breadth, notwithstanding mixed critical reception.

## Crepe (2020) — `irama-crepe` — EP

Core sources: [Warner EP track descriptions](https://shop.warnermusic.it/products/crepe-vinile-colorato-naturale); [Billboard Italia interview](https://billboard.it/musica/pop/irama-presenta-ep-crepe-spirito-rock/2020/08/2539562/); [Sky artist interview](https://tg24.sky.it/spettacolo/musica/2020/08/25/irama-intervista); [Newsic review](https://www.newsic.it/recensione-irama-crepe/); [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/crepe-mw0003418876); [original seven song tracklist](https://tg24.sky.it/spettacolo/musica/2020/08/27/irama-crepe-tracklist).

- **Genres:** mainstream pop-rap with a prominent Latin/reggaeton dance axis (`Crepe` and `Mediterranea`, plus `Arrogante` in the surrounding summer material). `urbanpop` and `hiphop` particularly **Flow** and **Eh mama eh**; `trap` **Flow** is explicitly called trap by one lower-strength outlet, so it is **texture/track-scoped and less certain**. `rock` **Bazooka**; `acousticpop` **Dedicato a te**; **never** project rock/reggaeton/trap onto every track. AllMusic's broad alternative/indie-rock styles are more expansive than critics' song-level account; keep them limited to sound, not album-leading genre.
- **Instrumentation:** original credits and AllMusic show synth, guitars (including electric on Bazooka), bass, piano, keyboards and Hammond organ, with vocal programming. Drum-machine model names are **not** verified, so none added. The guitar scratch/take in **Dedicato a te** was played by producer Nenna with a phone card as a pick, according to Billboard; do not mark a specific guitar effects pedal.
- **Production:** official/credit aggregations identify **Giulio Nenna, Andrea DB Debernardi, Mace and Encore** as producers across the EP; producers contribute to different tracks rather than all four making each song. `programming` is justified by formal programming and bass-programming credits. No separate drum-programming token is assigned without direct evidence, and bass programming isn't proof of a named synthesizer-bass model.
- **Voice:** direct melodic choruses (`clear`), forceful rock moments (`powerful`), explicit rap verses (`rap`) and the quiet intimate finale (`soft`). `belting` and `autotune` left unset in absence of a technical source.
- **Lyrics:** love, romantic conflict, inward emotional scars and youth identity; party references belong especially to summer pop songs.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` due to upbeat Mediterranean hits interleaved with acoustic introspection; `electronic=3` given strong electronically arranged urban/reggaeton songs but retained guitar/piano; `mood=3` mixed light summer rhythm and distressed lyrics; `theatrical=3` expressive vocals without conceptual theatrics; `experimental=3` because it deliberately juxtaposes disparate genres across tracks within accessible structures; `polish=4` for radio-oriented, controlled mixes; `tempo=3` moderately upbeat on average; `groove=4` because Latin dance hits dominate its identifiable rhythm character, even though it includes a rock and acoustic exception.
- **Source disagreements:** AllMusic shows a date of 14 August 2020, but multiple contemporary reports and label context identify the release **28 August 2020**. Year is unaffected. The eight-track digital listing includes a variant/remix: base EP has **seven core songs**.
- **Influences versus resemblance:** Prince/Queen are mentioned by the artist as examples of freedom from genre categorization, **not** documented source artists for this EP; leave `influenced_by` empty. The editorial proximity to Fred De Palma and Rocco Hunt is not a documented influence. `landmark=1` because Crepe marks a major Latin/urban crossover.
