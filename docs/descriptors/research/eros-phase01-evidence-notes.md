# Eros Ramazzotti — phase 01 evidence notes

This file is a research log. The 8 numerical scales and `sounds_like` comparisons are tentative editorial judgments; direct discographical facts and credited instruments take precedence. Do not treat machine capabilities as evidence that an *uncredited* device was used.

## 1. Cuori agitati (1985) — `eros-cuoriagitati`

- **Original issue:** Italian 1985 vinyl has nine songs, as the original-vinyl listing shows. Digital issues with ten songs insert 1984 “Terra promessa”. The listed producer, **Piero Cassano**, refers to the 1985 album; the 1984 single has different track-specific credits. Compare [original LP credits](https://musicbase.net/cuori-agitati), [official track listing](https://ramazzotti.com/album/cuori-agitati/) and [Sony/Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/cuori-agitati-eros-ramazzotti/4007192568147).
- **Genres:** `poprock`, `synthpop`, `pop` expressly listed in [SensCritique](https://www.senscritique.com/album/cuori_agitati/5844409); `italianpop` from [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/cuori-agitati-mw0000767798). The prominence of synthesizer-oriented style is *not* proof that a particular synth model was used.
- **Instruments:** original sleeve transcription and Sony per-track metadata name multiple keyboards, Eros / Claudio Bazzarri / Paolo Gianolio guitars, Stefano Cerri and Roberto Costa bass, Alfredo Golino and Giovanni Pezzoli drums, Amedeo Bianchi saxophone. These are **credited album instruments**. Do not invent `prophet5` for 1985 or enter `synth` solely from the genre label.
- **Production:** no specific recording technology, tape format or processing proved on the nine original tracks; leave this CSV cell empty. Cori/backing vocals do not alone prove the `layeredvocals` *recording technique*.
- **Voice and mood:** [AllMusic's artist biography](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/eros-ramazzotti-mn0000601079/biography) describes a sometimes gritty, reedy tenor. `raspy` is a conservative approximate category, not an assertion of constant gravelly singing. The numeric scores are moderate tentative judgments from acoustic band alongside keys and single-focused arrangements; low experimental (0) reflects the conventional song forms.
- **Themes:** love, youth, identity and aspiration from the titled 1985 material, with a social/aspirational component in the repertoire; apply to album generally with less certainty than instrumentation. `sounds_like` Raf/Tozzi is editorial shorthand for mid-1980s Italian melodic pop-rock only; *not* an influence claim.
- **Caveat:** the exact original release day differs across secondary sites (January 31 vs February 9); **1985** is secure. Sources with reissue dates (1987, 1995) must not override the original year.

## 2. Nuovi eroi (1986) — `eros-nuovieroi`

- **Original LP:** nine 1986 compositions, not the 13-track expanded CD. [Original LP entries](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/eros-ramazzotti/nuovi-eroi-album_ffvnee.html) and [official listing](https://ramazzotti.com/album/nuovi-eroi/) document the difference. The four added CD tracks are from *Cuori agitati*; no new genre should be introduced solely because of them.
- **Genres:** `poprock`, `synthpop` in the 1986 LP entries; `italianpop` from [AllMusic album style](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nuovi-eroi-mw0000422795); `pop` on [Italian discography](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuovi_eroi).
- **Instruments:** [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/nuovi-eroi-mw0000422795) specify electric/acoustic guitars (Giorgio Cocilovo), bass (Dino D'Autorio), drums and Simmons (Gabriele Melotti), keyboards and piano (Maurizio Bassi), synths/Fairlight/Prophet-5 (Bassi, Gaetano Leandro, Serse Mai), saxes and some brass in [Sony/Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/nuovi-eroi-eros-ramazzotti/0884977901146). The Fairlight provides an attested sampler instrument. Sax and trumpet are lower-weight instruments, not claimed to dominate every song.
- **Production:** `drumprogramming` grounded in credited programmed drums, `fairlight`, `prophet5` and `simmonsdrums` named in album credits. “Linn” alone is **not** enough to assign a `linndrum` model: several Linn drum devices existed. **Do not add `digitalrecording` without a direct credit.**
- **Producer discrepancy:** original label credits Piero Cassano. AllMusic's combined expanded-edition credits include producer Celso Valli; due to possible track-level/reissue aggregation, **Cassano alone** in row pending original sleeve adjudication. Valli is certainly an arranger.
- **Themes/shape:** [title-song publishing metadata](https://www.angolotesti.it/E/testi_canzoni_eros_ramazzotti_111/testo_canzone_nuovi_eroi_6265.html) supports the youth solidarity/social theme; romantic ballads and Sanremo “Adesso tu” account for `love`. The original album has a mixture of mid-tempo pop-rock and ballads. Scores energy 3, groove 2, electronic 3, polish 4 are **tentative**, grounded in hybrid acoustic and programmed instrumentation.

## 3. In certi momenti (1987) — `eros-incertimomenti`

- **Genres:** contemporary records list `synthpop` and `poprock` [1987 pressings](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/eros-ramazzotti/in-certi-momenti-album_ffhrug.html); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/in-certi-momenti-mw0000767800) lists Italian Pop; [Italian discography](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_certi_momenti) lists Pop. `europop` is lower-weight because a period *single* is filed that way in the [collector discography](https://musicbase.net/eros-ramazzotti), not because the album is uniformly Europop.
- **Instruments:** sources distinguish arrangement sessions: *Celso Valli* piano/keys, *Maurizio Bassi* keyboard/sampler/programming; Mo Foster electric bass, Dino D'Autorio fretless, Stuart Elliott and Gabriele Melotti drums/programming, Giorgio Cocilovo and Paolo Gianolio guitars, Rudy Trevis congas, Jacopo Jacopetti sax, Crazy Morning Section brass. [Original LP transcription](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/eros-ramazzotti/in-certi-momenti-album_ffhrug.html) and [Sony/Qobuz track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/at-de/album/in-certi-momenti-eros-ramazzotti/4007192587414).
- **Machines/production:** original sleeve explicitly names `Fairlight II`, `Emulator II` and `Emax`, plus drum programming. `fairlight|emulatorii|emax|drumprogramming|programming` therefore directly sourced. Sleeve `Prophet VS-2002` is ambiguous across actual models (VS, Prophet 2002); leave out rather than assert `prophet2002` or `prophetvs`. Other brand-wide Roland/Yamaha/Akai system credits are not specific enough for model tokens.
- **Guest/language:** Patsy Kensit sings and co-wrote English lyrics on “La luce buona delle stelle”, supporting `it|en`; the rest of the primary ten-track album is Italian. Do **not** treat Kensit as album co-billing or a cited musical influence.
- **Mood / scales:** substantial digital electronics suggest electronic 4; a mixture of acoustic instruments and Fairlight prevents 5. Energy 3 and groove 2 remain qualitative estimates. Eros's contemporary [1988 live review](https://elpais.com/diario/1988/10/07/cultura/592182006_850215.html?outputType=amp) mentions a sometimes rough voice, which qualifies but does not quantify `raspy`.
- **Themes:** adult affection, family (“Ciao pà”), observations (“Cose che ho visto”) and intimate conversation (“Libero dialogo”); introspection/social balance less secure than the equipment.

## 4. Musica è (1988) — `eros-musicae`

- **Core five-track release:** [Estatica transcribes original notes](https://www.estatica.it/it/musica/eros-ramazzotti/disco/musica-e) with five songs, original recording personnel and producer. The 1988 [Italian picture disc](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/eros-ramazzotti/musica-album_frpguu.html) reproduces five tracks. Elsewhere, 7- and 8-track editions append earlier hits/remixes. This row describes **the original five original recordings**, and therefore is marked `studio`, not `remix` or `compilation`.
- **Genres:** `poprock|synthpop` on 1988 pan-European pressings; `europop` on the original Italian picture disc; `italianpop` in [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/musica-%EF%BF%BD-mw0000541906); `ballad|easylistening` also labelled on the collector index [musicbase](https://musicbase.net/eros-ramazzotti) but are less authoritative; and `pop` from [Italian album discography](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musica_%C3%A8). The long suite does not prove a genre `prog` or `symphonicprog`: an artist influence is not a genre classification.
- **Musicians:** Eros guitars; Paolo Gianolio acoustic/electric guitars and bass; Mo Foster bass; Stuart Elliott drums; Celso Valli keyboards/piano; Serse Mai Fairlight/Akai/Emulator II/Emax programming; Jacopo Jacopetti soprano sax. The 11-minute title track additionally has **Münchner Philharmoniker** and four singers, orchestrated/conducted by Celso Valli. [Transcribed liner notes](https://www.estatica.it/it/musica/eros-ramazzotti/disco/musica-e).
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` on the orchestral title, `drumprogramming` on the mini-album, Fairlight, Emulator II, Emax. No asserted `analogtape`, `digitalrecording` or precise `akaibigsystem` token: Akai Big System is named but the precise model is unspecified (there is no generic token). No `remixing` because that applies to foreign bonus content.
- **Artist testimony and influence:** he said he wanted to create a *suite* because he loved **Genesis**; this is a rare **direct album-specific influence** supported by [Corriere della Sera, 29 July 2022](https://www.corriere.it/sette/incontri/22_luglio_29/eros-ramazzotti-finisce-l-amore-non-desiderio-che-sia-felice-chi-hai-amato-6abe0d74-0b15-11ed-9f77-5a61f2af535a.shtml). Add `Genesis` only on this album. Do **not** add classical crossover genre based only on an orchestra on one track.
- **Scales:** relative to preceding LP, more orchestral and suite-like (theatrical 4, experimental 2, polish 5), yet mostly romantic, mid/slow and not dance-oriented (energy 2, tempo 2, groove 1). These are editorial judgments, not empirically measured averages.
- **Edition notes:** Italian original `it` only. English duet with Patsy Kensit exists on a *remix of an older song* on international editions; Spanish *Música es* is a separate language version. The later Andrea Bocelli performance on the 1997 **compilation** is a **different recording**.

## Cross-album disagreements / uncertainties

- **Genre disagreement:** record dealers and databases describe the same 1980s albums as pop, Italian pop, pop rock, synth-pop or Europop. Instead of flattening these into one genre, all sufficiently source-attested styles are included; listen-based study could adjust order later. `electronic` is reserved as a sound-source scale rather than automatically imported from disc-shop broad categories.
- **Original vs expanded editions:** without the LP-first rule, reused 1984/1985 tracks and 1987 remixes would distort producer credits and instrumentation.
- **Influence discipline:** `influenced_by` remains empty for the first three albums; general artist influences in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` are not silently copied into every record. `sounds_like` remains an *editorial* comparator, so verify with listening before promotion to reviewed.
- **Review disagreements:** contemporary Spanish live critics [El País 7 October 1988](https://elpais.com/diario/1988/10/07/cultura/592182006_850215.html?outputType=amp) and [3 October 1988](https://elpais.com/diario/1988/10/03/cultura/591836409_850215.html?outputType=amp) differed in emphasis and musical appraisal; neither is album-by-album recording evidence. Those reviews were **not** used to invent instrument credits. Limited dedicated contemporary LP criticism makes confidence medium, not high.
