### kabzadesmall-iamthekingofamapianosweetdust (I Am the King of Amapiano: Sweet & Dust, 2020)

Sources:
- The Native, 15 Jul 2020 (byline "The Native"; author note: Dennis Ade Peter). https://thenativemag.com/kabza-de-small-king-amapiano/
- Telekom Electronic Beats, The Top 20 Records of 2020 (entry by Whitney Wei, quotes Kabza from Mixmag). https://www.electronicbeats.net/top-20-records-2020
- Rolling Stone, South African Star Kabza De Small's Key Amapiano Tracks (2021). https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kabza-de-smalls-key-amapiano-tracks-1191542/
- Mixmag, Scorpion Kings interview (2022). https://mixmag.net/feature/scorpion-kings-interview-sonar-festival-amapiano-dj-maphorisa-kabza-de-small-barcelona
- OkayAfrica, 1 Dec 2020 (Spotify). https://www.okayafrica.com/kabza-de-small-is-the-most-streamed-south-african-artist-on-spotify-in-2020/239103
- Qobuz credits. https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/i-am-the-king-of-amapiano-sweet-dust-kabza-de-small/pozpclb25c75b

Cells:
- **genres**: amapiano (every source). jazz, soul, kwaito: Kabza via Electronic Beats, "'Dust' is mainly the hardcore drum pattern; the kwaito influenced sound ... 'sweet' is the more soulful and jazzy amapiano"; The Native, "he plays up the importance of Jazz to the subgenre, especially on ... 'Mapiano Blues' and 'Many Faces'". disco: The Native, "the Disco-style swing of 'Duze'" (one track). synthpop and house: The Native, "the synth-pop swing meets '90s house vibe of 'Jwaleng'" (one track). Electronic Beats also defines amapiano by "deep, thrumming house basslines". The Native calls amapiano a blend of "Kwaito basslines, Deep House, Jazz and Bacardi", but that describes the genre, not this album, so deephouse is left out.
- **energy 3, groove 4, tempo 3**: the two halves differ. 'Sweet' leans "towards head bops"; 'Dust' is "dance-charged", "geared towards inspiring full-bodied swings", ridden "for 76 straight minutes" (The Native). Tempo 3: the Native notes iLog Drum's "strident tempo" on the Dust side only.
- **electronic 4**: "quivering synths", "buzzing synth loop", "electric zaps and sci-fi effects", but also live guitar by XolaniGuitars and vocal scats (The Native).
- **mood 4**: "a beat that sounds like a breezy evening on a tropical island" (Sponono); Sweet's themes "revolving around longing and romantic intimacy"; Electronic Beats, "as much heartfelt and contemplative as it is unwaveringly effulgent".
- **theatrical 2, experimental 3, polish 4**: "immersive and gorgeously layered beats", "the beats are full compositions in themselves" (polish); "flamboyant and experimental" solo work, the album shows amapiano can "either be avant-garde or accessible—or both" (experimental 3). Theatrical 2: DJ-set delivery, no staging described; low weight.
- **instruments**: piano ("very radiant piano chords", "a dominant, floating piano chord"; EB "lilting piano keys"); logdrum (Kabza on iLog Drum, Rolling Stone: "a love letter dedicated to the root of amapiano, the log drum"); synth; percussion ("buoyant percussion"); drums ("Building out from the drums", "table top drum pattern"); keyboards ("strobing keys"); guitar ("sublime guitar work from XolaniGuitars"); bass ("digitised bass guitar riffs"); glockenspiel ("a glockenspiel riff underneath Wizkid's bridge").
- **production**: scatsinging ("vocal scats by Howard"); soundeffects ("electric zaps and sci-fi effects", "beeping sports whistle").
- **voice**: empty. The lead voices are many guests; no source describes one lead voice for the album.
- **lyrics**: love ("longing and romantic intimacy"), party ("he still brings the party").
- **language**: empty. No opened source says which languages are sung (guests include Zulu-, Xhosa- and Pidgin-singing artists, but I found nothing to cite).
- **producers**: Kabza De Small. The Native calls it "the producer's" set, Kabza "in the director's seat". Qobuz lists the producer as "Not Documented" on all 27 tracks; Kabza (Kabelo Motha) and Themba Sekowe (I believe this is DJ Maphorisa's real name, not verified) are credited as composers on every track. Composer credits are not producer credits, so Sekowe is left out.
- **sounds_like**: David Guetta [1/27]: The Native, "'Ndofaya'—a song that sounds like David Guetta Amapiano mimic".
- **influenced_by**: none documented.
- **landmark 1**: The Native, "a creative landmark", "a creative milestone for the entire subgenre"; Mixmag, "the seminal I Am The King Of Amapiano".
- **Reception**: importance 5 (The Native: "the highest stakes for a Kabza project since 'Scorpion Kings'", Mixmag "seminal", double platinum, most-streamed SA album on Spotify 2020); acclaim 4 (The Native rave, Electronic Beats top 20 of 2020; few professional reviews exist); mainstream 3 (artist-led three-hour set, "indulges the producer's creative whims").
- **Legacy**: no row. "Creative landmark" is praise at release, not a statement that it shaped later music.

- **influenced_by**: none for this album. Artist-level statement in artists.md.

- **Reception**: importance 4 (the Grammy record; Pieces of Me remains his SAMA Album of the Year); acclaim 4 (Pulse 8.7, Albumism Album of the Month, Grammy, but Pitchfork mixed); mainstream 4 (Pitchfork "falters when it tries to provide what it thinks the people want"; StarGate, Guetta and Diplo pop crossover; Mixmag reports fans' backlash over his "creative choices").

Cells:

Left out: dream pop, which is only in the headline of the Guardian's original review, seen in search listings; that review did not open. reggae, funk, rock, rap, afroswing (Clash: "swaggering reggae, syrupy funk, distorted rock, polyphonic rap and sensual afro-swing"). afrofusion (Clash "pace-setting afro-fusion"). softrock also Clash: "the soft-rock closer 'Come Home To God'". dancehall (whynow "the dancehall-infused, synth-heavy 'Princess Going Digital'"). indiepop (whynow, Sociopathic Dance Queen "a groovy indie-pop banger"). punkrock (Exclaim "guitar-driven punk rock"). funkbr (Pitchfork, Angels in Tibet "the dreamy baile funk-inspired reverie"; baile funk = funk carioca). surfrock (Exclaim, Sociopathic Dance Queen "a neo-beach rock track", "embraces her inner surfer"). japanesefolk (Exclaim "fuses Japanese folk, Spanish flamenco, rugged American rock"). jazz (whynow "jazz-infused medley of horn embellishments"; Crack "the groovesome sounds of African jazz" on one track).
- **energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3**: mixes ballads ("somber ballad", "midtempo burner") with punk bursts and dance tracks.
- **electronic 3**: whynow, "Whilst her previous album was laden with more computer-based beats, Fountain Baby goes global with its instrumentals" (orchestra, kora, harp), but "crystalline synths", "synths simmering" (Pitchfork).
- **mood 3**: playful pleasure ("having too much fun") against "the dark side of abundance" (Pitchfork); Guardian "gloriously confident romp".
- **theatrical 3**: "lavish" (Guardian), whynow "The theatrical 'Wasted Eyes'", but a whispered, intimate delivery.
- **experimental 3**: Exclaim "far more experimental than her last effort"; Guardian "constantly shapeshifting".
- **polish 5**: Pitchfork "Amaarae is sounding expensive", "her music lavish with instrumentation"; Guardian "putting global pop musics through a sumptuous sheen until they gleam like opulent jewellery".
- **voice soft|rap**: Pitchfork "Amaarae's sweet soprano", "she nearly whispers", "Amaarae raps"; Guardian "sugar-sweet voice"; Crack "seductively whispered vocals".
- **instruments**: Pitchfork, "violins and cellos, a Japanese koto, ethereal harps, West African dounoun and kora, steel pan drums, an authoritative horn section", "a 13-piece orchestra and a percussive glock", "acoustic guitar lament", "a distorted guitar anthem"; Exclaim "koras, horns, steel pans and textured guitars"; Crack "the soaring strings", "the powerful bassline"; synth (Pitchfork). dounoun → `dunun` (new instrument).
- **production**: autotune (Guardian "a delicate Auto-Tuned ballad"; whynow "each auto-tuned note"); falsetto (Guardian "weary, whispery falsetto"); sampling (Guardian "the Neptunes themselves are sampled on ... Counterfeit"; Amaarae, Complex: "There's actually a clip sample for the song 'Counterfeit'"); soundeffects (Guardian, Wasted Eyes "its percussion wrought from gunshots"); compression (whynow "her use of compression"); drumprogramming (Qobuz: Kyu Steed and KZ Didit credited "DrumProgrammer").
- **lyrics**: sensuality ("Desire in its many forms runs rampant", Guardian), love, heartbreak ("a heartbreak song disguised as pillow talk", Pitchfork), party, irony ("no one's more aware of the irony than Amaarae herself", Pitchfork).
- **language en|ja**: English throughout; Japanese on Wasted Eyes (Crack: "a Japanese folk song (Battaki by Umeko Ando) reinterpreted by contemporary Japanese vocalists"; Guardian: Crystal Kay asked "to reinterpret" the song). That it is sung in Japanese is my reading; flag.
- **producers** (Qobuz): KZ Didit, Kyu Steed, Cracker Mallo, Yves Rothman (producer on Come Home to God, co-producer on Sex, Violence, Suicide), Tochi Bedford, Leonardo Dessi, Cadenza, Pheelz, Geo Jordan, Christian "CG" Gregory. Left out: Amaarae (executive producer only), additional producers (S-Type, Cadenza on two tracks, Yves Rothman elsewhere).
- **sounds_like**: Timbaland (Guardian: "The clearest comparison is with Timbaland and the Neptunes' work at the turn of the 2000s"; the Neptunes are left out because they are sampled on the album). Janet Jackson (Guardian "breathy Janet Jackson-esque trills"). M.I.A. (Kala) [3/14] and Britney Spears (Britney) [3/14]: whynow, Wasted Eyes, Counterfeit and Disguise "evoke a noughties nostalgia ... (think of MIA's 'Paperplanes', Brittany's 'Slave 4 U', ...)". The third name there, Rihanna's "Run This Town", is a Jay-Z song, so it is left out.
- **influenced_by**: Janet Jackson (The Velvet Rope): Clash interview, "Her vocal reference across much of 'Fountain Baby' was Janet Jackson ... Amaarae channelled the 'The Velvet Rope'"; Amaarae: "I thought about my own range, and how I could use my lower register as a kind of vocal weapon the way she did." Britney Spears, Timbaland, The Neptunes: Amaarae to Complex, "I took a lot of inspirations. A big one for me was Britney Spears, Timberland, Pharrell and The Neptunes", "being inspired by Timberland, who would take a lot from Arabic music". Missy Elliott, Madonna: Exclaim, "Amaarae cites Missy Elliott, Madonna and Janet Jackson as being some of Fountain Baby's influences"; Pitchfork, the press release namechecks "Missy, Janet, Britney". Uncertain: The Neptunes are also sampled on Counterfeit, and Pharrell has a writing credit through that interpolation. I kept them because she names them as an inspiration apart from the sample. Drop them if the owner reads the sample rule strictly.
- **landmark 1**: Guardian No 9 album of 2023; Pitchfork "one of the best pop albums of the year".
- **Reception**: importance 4 (her major-label leap; The Angel You Don't Know was the breakthrough); acclaim 5 (Guardian top 10 of the year, Pitchfork, Clash 9/10, whynow 4/5); mainstream 3 (artist-led, "Refusing categorisation").

## New tokens

Genres:
- `alte`: West African alternative scene/genre (Guardian on Fountain Baby).
- `afroswing`: UK/African afro-swing (Clash).
- `afrofusion`: Afro-fusion (Clash). Burna Boy's label; not afrobeats or afropop.
- `melodicrap`: melodic rap (Guardian).
- `japanesefolk`: Japanese folk song (Exclaim). Could be mapped to `hougaku` if the owner prefers; I kept it separate under the no-mapping rule.
- `hiplife`: Ghanaian hiplife (Afrocritik, Pulse, Kasahare).
- `kwaito`: South African kwaito (Pitchfork on Black Coffee; Kabza via Electronic Beats).
- `azonto`: Ghanaian azonto (Music in Africa).

Mapped to existing tokens: baile funk → `funkbr`; neo-beach rock / surfer → `surfrock`; emo (rap beats) → `emorap`; afro-house → `afrohouse`.

Instruments: `dunun` (West African dounoun bass drum, Pitchfork on Fountain Baby). `logdrum` and `steeldrums` already exist.
