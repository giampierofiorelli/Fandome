# John Waite — Fandome phase 01 evidence notes (research checked 29 October 2026)

## Scope and approach
The original 1982–87 solo studio albums are in scope: *Ignition*, *No Brakes*, *Mask of Smiles*, *Rover's Return*. Use the original nine/ten-track editions. Do not add The Babys or Bad English albums to his solo discography; those are separately billed groups. Do not create an album from the 2001 two-record CD reissue *Mask of Smiles / Rover's Return*, even though some databases present it as a single disc. The individual album pages on Waite's official site confirm the four original titles and sequences. All numeric scores and artist comparisons are editorial Fandome descriptors rather than factual quotations. **Every row is draft, not human-reviewed.**

## Ignition (1982)
Original Chrysalis 10 tracks, produced by **Neil Giraldo**, recorded at Power Station NYC. Waite names Frankie LaRocka (drums), Donnie Nossov (bass), Ivan Kral (guitar); original session credit transcription adds Tim Pierce guitar, Bruce Brody keyboards and Kral piano, with horn section. `brass` covers Arno Hecht, Crispin Cioe and Paul Litteral; do not infer specific trumpets/trombones without their instruments listed. The label `newwave` is specifically an **album review production-texture** rather than a pure new-wave record. No synth make or processing unit documented; production empty. AllMusic describes processed guitars but does not document `guitarfilter` or `digitalrecording`; do not infer either. Slightly high electronic score reflects production sheen, not a drum-machine claim. Official Waite reminiscence calls the album his first autobiographical work, supporting identity/introspection; heartbreak is also a broad reading of romantic track titles. Similarity to Pat Benatar and Rick Springfield is a **provisional editorial** sonic comparison, never documented influence. The producer Giraldo also worked with Pat Benatar, but that alone does not establish Waite's influences. 

## No Brakes (1984)
Original EMI America 9 tracks. **John Waite + David Thoener + Gary Gersh** credited as producers (Gersh spelled *Gersch* in one credits transcription). Core band: Gary Myrick guitar, Bruce Brody keyboards, Donnie Nossov bass, Curly Smith drums, Steve Scales percussion. No named synth or special vocal-processing machinery documented for original album; production blank intentionally. `softrock` and `albumrock` are explicit AllMusic styles, original LP tagged `poprock|hardrock` by Musicbase: hardrock belongs **last** as the more forceful material, not a blanket sound. Heavy Harmonies uses AOR; do not collapse into an empty umbrella term. Missing You is a strongly downbeat relationship song, alongside harder material. The track title Euroshima may imply a social theme, but it is not enough to tag the album as socially themed without lyric evidence. A 1984 digital-streaming metadata entry lists `DigitalEditingEngineer` for the song Missing You; because the edition/credit is track-level, do not infer whole-album `digitalediting`. **Do not attribute the song Tears to Vinnie Vincent as a performer:** he is a composer. The album's producer Gary Gersh must not be mistaken for songwriter/guitarist Gary Myrick.

## Mask of Smiles (1985)
Original EMI 9 tracks; **John Waite + Stephen Galfas** producers. Album-specific 1985 instrumental credits: Ritchie Fliegler 12-string guitar, John McCurry lead, Johnny Thunders guitar guest; Chuck Kentis electric piano; Tom Mandel synthesizer and keyboards; Nossov, Carmine Rojas and Joey Vasta bass; Frankie LaRocka/Alan Childs drums and percussion; Lenny Pickett tenor saxophone; Steve Elson/Stan Harrison horns. Source documents some musicians on parts of album; do not claim every instrument is audible throughout. AllMusic describes mainstream pop-rock, synthesizers and heavily processed guitars; Musicbase original 1985 vinyl tagged `poprock|synthpop` and reissue overall AOR. Keep synthpop below main rock categories and aor last, with evidence scope explicit. AllMusic review says “Lust for Life” **is not** an Iggy Pop cover: it is a Waite/McCurry original. “Ain't That Peculiar” is a Motown standard cover, not evidence that Marvin Gaye or Smokey Robinson shaped the recording as a named influence. The title track “No Brakes” here is **a distinct track**, not a reissued copy of the preceding album. No signature synth model identified; production left blank.

## Rover's Return (1987)
Original 9-track EMI release. Album's principal credited producers **John Waite + Frank Filipetti**. **Desmond Child** has a production credit specifically on opener “These Times Are Hard for Lovers” and **Rick Nowels** on two tracks, “Don't Lose Any Sleep” and “Sometimes”; preserve all four in producer field but **must annotate per-track scope**. John McCurry acoustic/electric guitar, John Regan and John K. bass, Gary Myrick guest guitar on track nine, keyboards/synths by Stead, Kentis, Mangiafico, Mandel, drums by Fig, Price and Braun. **Jimmy Bralower drum programming is documented**, no drum machine model, sampler or sequencer. Waite's account says initial sessions were discarded and record restarted with Filipetti; this does not document a specific technology (`digitalediting` or `overdubbing`) and should not create those tags. AOR is a contemporary record-index classification; `arenarock` and `softrock` are **editorial tail genres** interpreting high-impact radio-rock and ballads, NOT literal genres listed by AllMusic (which only uses Contemporary Pop/Rock). AllMusic is critical of the album's formulaic polish, whereas Waite frames the rerecording as a difficult personal restart; neither source alone establishes musical influence. The opening title is about relationship difficulties; it does not justify a social or political album tag.

## ARTIST INFLUENCES vs album-specific influences
Direct first-person interview material is retained in CLAUDE_IMPORT.md with source URLs. Some are musical childhood influences (Hank Williams, Marty Robbins, The Shadows, Nina & Frederik), others explicitly inspire his musical career (Neil Young, Beatles, soul artists). **None are claimed to have directly shaped any of these four individual studio albums**, so all four `influenced_by` cells remain empty. The producer relationship and a cover song are never influence evidence by themselves. `sounds_like` values are editorial judgment; keep optional until validated.
