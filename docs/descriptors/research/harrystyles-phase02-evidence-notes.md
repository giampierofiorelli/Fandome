## `harrystyles-kissallthetimediscooccasionally` — *Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.* (2026)

### Identification and catalogue

The official Harry Styles store and Sony/Columbia confirm a **6 March 2026** release, 12 tracks, studio album, and primary Harry Styles artist credit. The branch `descriptors` catalogue currently lists the 2017, 2019 and 2022 albums but **not this fourth album**; import must insert `['Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.', 2026]` for `harrystyles` before validating the descriptor. The exact id is the existing Fandome slug convention. No co-billed release and no live or compilation versions. [sony26, store26]

### Genre evidence and ordering

The recording's **pop/dance-pop/electronic/electropop** core is identified by Apple Music, Pitchfork, The Scotsman and Rolling Stone UK. Album-level, dancefloor-focused **house** is grounded in the Guardian's mid-tempo house rhythms (particularly *American Girls*), and **disco-pop** by an Independent review of the original album's live premiere. Use **dance** as a broader grouping without conflating house, disco and electronic. [apple26, pf26, rsyorke26, scots26, g26, indlive26, rsuk26]

Specific tracks contribute **synth-pop** (*Pop*), **funk** (*American Girls*, *Pop*, *Dance No More*), **disco** (*Dance No More*), and **dance-punk** (Pitchfork says *Are You Listening Yet?* attempts that approach). The Independent explicitly describes *American Girls* as Italo disco touched by **French house**: preserve separate `italodisco` and `frenchhouse` tokens, not the different `frenchtouch` term. [blend26, ind26, nme26, pf26]

Tracks and details with restricted scope stay at the end: *Season 2 Weight Loss*'s **techno** surface, *Aperture*'s **electro/Balearic** mixture, breakbeat rhythms, *Are You Listening Yet?*'s post-punk inflection, Slant's **noise-pop** judgment about two tracks, choir-based **gospel** accents, and the orchestral **ballad** *Coming Up Roses*. The review calls it **Balearic**, not *balearic beat* or *Balearic house*: request the exact new genre token `balearic` instead of mapping to an existing near-synonym. Genre evidence marks these as 'some tracks' or 'texture' rather than all-album labels. [rsuk26, blend26, g26, nme26, sl26]

**Deliberate exclusions:** dance-rock, synth-funk, singer-songwriter, techno-house and R&B are not added by inference from influence, general artist tag, or overlapping words. Being associated with a dance-punk reference act does **not** on its own prove dance-punk on the album; Pitchfork separately supplies song-specific evidence. Likewise, four-on-the-floor does not mean every track is techno. [pf26, g26, ap26]

### Scales (integer guide anchors)

- **energy 3:** several vigorous club-minded tracks, slowed by *Paint By Numbers* and *Coming Up Roses*; not the consistent intensity of an all-night dance record. [g26, pf26, ind26]
- **electronic 4:** synthesizer-led studio arrangements and TR-909 rhythm coexist with Tom Skinner's live drumming and a full orchestral ballad. More electronic than *Harry's House*, not wholly electronic (5). [mr26, pfcredits26, g26]
- **mood 3:** hopeful communal dance themes conflict with anxious love, self-observation and nighttime melancholy. A balanced emotional centre. [apple26, sl26, rs26]
- **theatrical 3:** choir and the 39-piece orchestral crescendo supply dramatic lift, offset by muted, spoken, restrained vocal delivery. [pfcredits26, ap26, g26]
- **experimental 3:** synthesizer modulations, dance-punk touches and unusual album sequencing stretch Styles's conventional pop, but hooks and ballads remain accessible. [ind26, sl26, pf26]
- **polish 5:** carefully layered and mixed major-label record; glossy presentation emphasized even by sceptical reviewers. Live drums do not make this a raw album. [pf26, ind26, pfcredits26]
- **tempo 3:** largely midtempo house/electro patterns, with faster-leaning dance grooves and slower ballads. [g26, rsuk26]
- **groove 4:** four-on-the-floor, pulsating bass and drum-machine patterns are central; acoustic and orchestral songs prevent 5. [apple26, mr26, g26, ind26]

### Instruments: only credits or album-specific observations

- **synth**, **drummachine**, **synthbass**: Kid Harpoon specifically describes making *Aperture* with a TR-909, patched modular synth and played synth-bass part; the Guardian, NME and Rolling Stone document the synth-driven sound on other tracks. **No exact Moog, Sequential or Prophet model** is imported from later live-show rigs. [mr26, rsuk26, g26]
- **drums**: Tom Skinner is credited on *American Girls*, *Ready, Steady, Go!*, *Season 2 Weight Loss*, *Pop*, *Dance No More* and *Paint By Numbers*. **bass**: album-review bass-guitar observations, in particular on *Ready, Steady, Go!* and *Paint By Numbers*; bass may be both electric and synthesized across this album. [pfcredits26, sfl26]
- **piano**: Yaffra on *Aperture*, *Coming Up Roses* and *Carla's Song*. **keyboards**: Independent's described keyboard sounds and *American Girls* chords. **organ**: Yaffra's explicitly credited B3 on *Paint By Numbers*. [pfcredits26, ind26]
- **electricguitar**: *Taste Back* and *Pop* electric guitar textures, guitar solo on *Are You Listening Yet?*; **acousticguitar**: *Paint By Numbers* and the quieter passage of *Ready, Steady, Go!*. **spanishguitar** is the AP critic's specific instrument description for *Ready, Steady, Go!*, proposed as a new exact token, not applied indiscriminately to all acoustic guitar. [pf26, blend26, sl26, ap26]
- **orchestra**, **strings**, **violin**, **viola**, **cello**, **doublebass**, **celesta**, **marimba**, **vibraphone**, **tuba**, **flute**, **englishhorn** (cor anglais), **clarinet**: the **Coming Up Roses** original credits name conductor Jules Buckley and these actual sections/instruments, including percussionist Sam Wilson (marimba/vibraphone). `vibraphone` is a new instrument token; `marimba`, `englishhorn`, `celesta` and the others are already included in the current Fandome instruments file. [pfcredits26, mb26]
- **trumpet**: Mark Crown's performance on *Paint By Numbers*. **brass**: trumpet and orchestral tuba are explicitly credited. **percussion**: described percussive backing on multiple tracks, especially the rhythm-oriented songs. [pfcredits26, hotpress26]

### Production: documented techniques, not assumptions

- **tr909**, **drumprogramming**, **modularsynth**: Kid Harpoon recreates the *Aperture* sound with his own Roland TR-909 and patched modular hardware, and explicitly explains programming the opening rhythm. `modularsynth` is a proposed new production token. He says the apparently electric-guitar-like motif in *Aperture* is **modular synthesizer**, so this particular motif is **not** evidence of electric guitar on that track. [mr26]
- **Eventide model uncertainty:** MusicRadar's 24 Oct 2026 transcription calls the delay unit **“HS3000 SE”**, but Eventide's own model history lists **H3000 SE**. The exact model is therefore **omitted** from the machine tokens under Fandome's conflicting-model rule. The fact of an Eventide delay in the *Aperture* rhythm chain is recorded here, not silently converted to another model. Do not map it to `eventideh910`. [mr26, eventide26]
- **vocalfilter**: Guardian’s original *Aperture* review documents lightly filtered vocals. **vocoder**: The Independent specifically describes vocodered voice on *Pop*. **distortedvocals**: album-track descriptions of altered vocals in *Ready, Steady, Go!*, but cannot establish a proprietary plugin. **autotune**: Fiona Shepherd's Scotsman critique specifically identifies an Auto-Tune voice on that same track; treated as a critic observation, not session-paperwork verification. **layeredvocals**: double-tracked voices and stacked choruses are discussed by critics, corroborated by the named backing choir. [g26, ind26, sfl26, scots26, pfcredits26]
- **stringarrangements**: credited to Jules Buckley, Kid Harpoon and Harry Styles on *Coming Up Roses*, 39-piece orchestra, not an inference from the orchestral genre. [pfcredits26, ap26]
- **NOT included:** `garageband`: Styles told Thom Yorke in October 2026 he sketches *ideas* on his phone with GarageBand, not that the final 2026 album was made in GarageBand. Likewise a sampler, Prophet-6, Space Echo or other equipment spotted at a **live performance** is not evidence of the original studio recordings. No analog tape, Pro Tools, specific Auto-Tune version or sidechain setting is claimed. [rsyorke26, mr26]

### Voice, lyrics, producer credits and similarity

- **voice** `clear|soft|spoken|rap`: his relatively understated lead, intimate singing on *Paint By Numbers*, half-spoken style on *Are You Listening Yet?*, and a critic's explicit report of rap-like delivery there. Female backing voices and the House Gospel Choir are credited performances, **not** an automatic reason to assign `choral` as the lead voice type. [g26, nme26, blend26, pfcredits26]
- **lyrics**: introspection, love, identity, sensuality, heartbreak, party/community, nostalgia, irony and vignette-like storytelling all appear in the artist's track explanations and reviews. Do **not** infer politics from dance-club liberation or `spiritual` just from a gospel choir. [apple26, ind26, sl26, rs26]
- **producers**: Kid Harpoon genuinely produces **all 12** tracks; Tyler Johnson is a co-producer on *American Girls*, *Are You Listening Yet?*, *The Waiting Game*, *Pop* and *Paint By Numbers* (the two labels agree). Tyler's **additional production** on *Ready, Steady, Go!* and *Taste Back* is *not* counted as co-production. Other guest performers, string arrangers, mixers and the Kid Harpoon *executive producer* role alone are excluded; he qualifies because of his actual track-producer credits. [sony26, pfcredits26]
- **sounds_like**: LCD Soundsystem and Hot Chip recur as editorial comparisons; Metronomy is specifically compared on *Ready, Steady, Go!*, Phoenix on *Taste Back* / *Carla's Song*, Jessie Ware on *Dance No More*, New Order/Depeche Mode/Talking Heads on synthesizer palette, Daft Punk and Chic in disco-funk tracks; Postal Service and 1975 on *Taste Back*. These are **sonic resemblances**, not claims they all shaped recording decisions. [nme26, pf26, rsuk26, rs26, sl26]
- **influenced_by**: LCD Soundsystem (Styles cites witnessing gigs as affecting his conception of this project); Floating Points and Jamie xx (directly identified by him in the Murakami interview as core electronic listening during the 2025 creative period); **The Durutti Column** (AP reports Styles directly citing it as an *Aperture* influence); **Radiohead** (Fiona Shepherd identifies it as a cited influence for this project). Four Tet and DJs Ben Klock/Fadi Mohem are documented as artist's electronic listening during the development period, with DJ Mag explicitly calling the latter influences on the record; this is **weaker** evidence of individual track influence. Hot Chip, Daft Punk and Talking Heads are named by qualified critics as influences, rather than just musician credits. [rw26, rs26, ap26, scots26, djmag26, rsuk26]
- **not `influenced_by`**: Ellie Rowsell (backing vocalist), Tom Skinner (session drummer), Jules Buckley (orchestrator), producer Kid Harpoon and co-producer Tyler Johnson. *Carla's Song* was inspired by a friend's experience hearing a Simon & Garfunkel song, but that anecdote and the lyrical allusions alone do not prove a **musical** influence for this recording; leave Simon & Garfunkel in the documented contextual notes only. [apple26, rs26, pfcredits26]

### Reviews conflict without invalidating the sonic evidence

NME, The Independent and Rolling Stone UK praise the departure toward dance-oriented production; Pitchfork argues the tracks merely gesture at dance-punk before returning to safe big-chorus pop, and The Guardian finds many tunes very quiet and lyrically vague. Slant sees exciting sections but questions how far the record actually ventures beyond reference points. Hence the record is **electronic 4**, but **experimental 3**, not 5, with a mixed emotional **mood 3**. `landmark=1` reflects this album's distinctive fourth-career chapter, not unanimity or sales. `confidence=high` refers to the many independent reviews and definitive instrumental/production credits; the specific DJ-influence and track-level genre qualifications remain visible here. [nme26, ind26, rsuk26, pf26, g26, sl26]
