# Oasis — Phase 02: evidence notes and rating rationale

**Scope:** original studio albums only, first releases 2002–2008. Previous four albums are already present in `docs/descriptors/oasis.csv` on branch `descriptors`, as checked on 26 October 2026. Source links accompany every album and genre classification. These rows remain editorial `draft`.

**Method:** Preserve all album-specific styles explicitly named by sources with a clear scope. Broad AllMusic style classes are taxonomic, not necessarily discriminative. Use the Fandome guide for eight integer scales (0–5). Leave unknown technique tags blank. No new releases or 2025 reunion live recordings are included.

## Heathen Chemistry (2002)
**Core source set:** Oasis official album page and retrospective, Oasis Recording Information album-credit transcription, AllMusic original review/credits, NME, Observer and Apple Music editorial.

**Eight independent scale judgments (whole original album)**
- **Energy 3:** *The Hindu Times*, *Hung in a Bad Place* and *Better Man* are driving electric rock, balanced by *Songbird*, *She Is Love* and expansive ballads. Less relentless than the group’s 1994 debut.
- **Electronic 1:** guitars and real drums predominate. Hammond, pump organ and Mellotron are documented; they add colour but are not electronic dance grooves. Pro Tools usage is a *recording process*, not a synthetic musical sound source.
- **Mood 3:** reassurance in *Stop Crying Your Heart Out* and *Little by Little* sits next to ruminative and sometimes unsettled material in *Born on a Different Cloud*.
- **Theatrical 3:** widescreen choruses and orchestral support without the maximalist extended bombast of *Be Here Now*.
- **Experimental 1:** recognisably conventional verse/chorus guitar-rock and ballad structures, despite psychedelic texture on selected tracks.
- **Polish 4:** Mark “Spike” Stent’s big, spacious mixing and orchestrated ballad reflect elaborate conventional studio production; polish is not an assessment of critical quality.
- **Tempo 3:** average midtempo to moderately brisk, with soft and slow material; do not use the fastest single to represent the whole.
- **Groove 2:** guitar-led rock backbeats, not club-oriented rhythm; *Songbird*’s country-rock feel is one track.

**Genres.** Seven AllMusic style terms are preserved in the genre-evidence CSV but inherently broad and not all equally distinctive. `psychedelia` is an explicit texture around *Born on a Different Cloud*; `countryrock` pertains to *Songbird*, not the record’s centre. `poprock` is the broad AllMusic Pop/Rock umbrella. None of these imply a new genre token.

**Voice/instrument evidence.** Liam Gallagher and Noel Gallagher sing in different registers, from raspy projecting rock to more tender ballad delivery. Oasis recording credit transcription lists guitar and rhythm section; Paul Stacey on Mellotron, piano and Hammond, Mike Rowe on piano/Hammond/pump organ, Johnny Marr on guest guitars and London Session Orchestra strings. `organ` consolidates Hammond and pump organ also has its own allowed token. There is no blanket synth/sampler credit. Single instrumental track *A Quick Peep* does not make the LP primarily instrumental.

**Production.** `protools` is supported by Jan Kybert being identified as Pro Tools engineer. `stringarrangements` derives from orchestral string recording and credited Wil Malone (arranger, not producer). Mark Stent is **mixer only** in these credits; **Oasis** is the collective producer. Do not infer `wallofsound`, drum machine models or analog tape from sonic resemblance.

**Influences versus similarity.** NME directly traces particular songs to John Lennon's solo work and compares *Hung in a Bad Place* to Stooges material, while other criticism evokes Faces-style rock. These are qualified critical influence claims; `sounds_like` is a broader editorial sonic relationship. `The Stooges` and `The Faces` are not album collaborators.

**Uncertainty:** `countryrock` track-only, `psychedelia` partial, `protools` a known workstation rather than a determination of electronic music genre.

## Don't Believe the Truth (2005)
**Core source set:** Oasis official page; Oasis Recording Information original per-track credit transcription; MusicBrainz; AllMusic; NME contemporary review and Noel interview; Guardian, Pitchfork and musicOMH.

**Eight independent scale judgments (whole original album)**
- **Energy 3:** vigorous *Lyla*, *Turn Up the Sun* and *The Meaning of Soul* counter soft, reflective *Love Like a Bomb*, *Guess God Thinks I'm Abel* and *Let There Be Love*.
- **Electronic 1:** electric and acoustic guitars, bass, live drums, piano, Mellotron and ancillary percussion dominate. No reliable source identifies a defining sequencer/drum-machine album signature.
- **Mood 3:** wistful, warm and reflective themes but multiple upbeat singalong tracks; neither consistently dark nor euphoric.
- **Theatrical 2:** Guardian describes a softer and more humble record; occasional anthems persist but sweeping bombast is reduced.
- **Experimental 2:** more varied songwriting roles and some folk/musical-hall/psychedelic inflections; largely familiar guitar-rock forms.
- **Polish 3:** cleaner, more compact yet somewhat unpolished/stripped mix character compared to the preceding 1990s records. Multiple studio sessions do not automatically entail maximal production polish.
- **Tempo 3:** moderate overall pace, fast guitar rock balanced with acoustic, quieter material.
- **Groove 2:** drums are a central pulse, and Lenny Castro adds percussion on *Part of the Queue*, but this is not a dancefloor-oriented album.

**Genres.** AllMusic album styles make up the six primary classes. `folk` and `psychedelia` are documented by musicOMH as elements; `musichall` identifies specifically *The Importance of Being Idle* from Pitchfork. `poprock` represents the very broad Pop/Rock umbrella. Do not elevate any one of these limited-scope elements to an overall dominant style.

**Voice/instruments.** Liam's forceful nasal/raspy lead and Noel's relatively clearer/softer lead and harmonies account for varied `voice` tokens. Per-track documentation confirms acoustic/electric guitars, bass, drums from Zak Starkey and Terry Kirkbride on *Mucky Fingers*, Martin Duffy piano, Paul Stacey Mellotron and piano, Lenny Castro percussion, plus harmonica from critical review. **Drummers differ by track**: this is not a blanket claim that Zak plays every cut.

**Production and producers.** The official site and per-track credits jointly support `Dave Sardy|Noel Gallagher`; specific Noel-produced cuts include *Mucky Fingers*, *Lyla* (Sardy additional production) and *The Importance of Being Idle*. Sardy has other song-specific producer credits and mixed many tracks. Failed/abandoned Death in Vegas sessions are **not on the issued record** and that team must **not** be listed as producer. `production` is blank intentionally: the sources describe recording, arrangements and mixing but do not prove any named model or allowed recording technique consistently appears on the *released* songs.

**Influences.** NME cites Kinks-era English acoustic songwriting and Noel likens *Lyla* to The Who; musicOMH identifies a recognisable Velvet Underground antecedent in *Mucky Fingers* and Dylan in the album's folk/blues underpinning. These are directly attributed critic/artist references. Editorial comparisons in `sounds_like` are not claims of personal debt.

**Counter-evidence:** Guardian evaluates the record favorably as a softer, more democratic development; Pitchfork is skeptical of its conventional arrangements. Confidence in instruments and producer credits is high; `folk`/`psychedelia`/`musichall` are scoped to individual material, not a new blanket genre.

## Dig Out Your Soul (2008)
**Core source set:** Oasis official album page; MusicBrainz and TIDAL track credits; Dave Sardy interview in Rock Cellar; Noel Gallagher statements via NME; NME toy-instrument account; AllMusic, Guardian/Observer and Hot Press.

**Eight independent scale judgments (whole original album)**
- **Energy 4:** driving and loud *The Shock of the Lightning*, *Bag It Up*, *Waiting for the Rapture* and *Ain't Got Nothin'* outweigh quieter closing material, though sustained tempos remain mixed.
- **Electronic 2:** core sound is amplified rock instruments but keyboards, Mellotron, electronic treatments and a sampled speech excerpt add clearly audible non-acoustic layers; not electronica in aggregate.
- **Mood 2:** darker, brooding, bluesy and contemplative psychedelia, with *I'm Outta Time* as a particularly vulnerable ballad despite more optimistic rock bursts.
- **Theatrical 3:** dramatic psychedelic ensemble arrangements but a repeated motoric feel rather than *Be Here Now* arena-bombast.
- **Experimental 3:** stated Noel goal of hypnotic grooves and less traditional song structures, unusual timbres (toy electric sitar, tambura) and sample-based vocal cameo; review disagreement precludes score 4–5.
- **Polish 4:** Sardy's carefully engineered Abbey Road studio production and dense, controlled loudness with extensive re-recordings; not a home-recorded rough demo set.
- **Tempo 3:** driving *The Shock of the Lightning* balances the more moderate *Falling Down*, *I'm Outta Time* and drone-heavy tracks.
- **Groove 3:** producer/artist explicitly emphasized repetitive grooves and rhythmic hypnosis; still a rock LP, not designed for dancefloor use.

**Genres.** Broad AllMusic stylistic labels retained and `psychedelicrock` front-loaded because a separate AllMusic review characterizes the whole record around psychedelic guitar rock. `psychedelia` appears as explicit repeated texture in Guardian/Observer. `bluesrock` corresponds primarily to *Bag It Up* (AllMusic labels it blues-psych); do not treat every song as blues. `poprock` is generic AllMusic Pop/Rock classification. No `sitar`/Indian-classical genre token: Indian-inspired rock timbres are not a separate album-wide genre.

**Voice/instrument evidence.** Primary raspy/powerful Liam vocals coexist with Noel's clearer delivery and softer moments. Per-track MusicBrainz records electric guitars, bass, drums, keyboards, `tambura` (a string drone instrument credited to Andy Bell), Mellotron on *Falling Down*, and electronic interventions. NME quotes Noel clarifying the supposed sitar heard on *To Be Where There's Life* was **a plastic toy electric sitar**, explicitly not a normal acoustic sitar; `electricsitar` is a proxy instrument tag for this toy, annotated here. Track-specific handclaps were reported for *(Get Off Your) High Horse Lady*. `tambura` **new token** must not be conflated with `sitar`, which has a different physical construction and function.

**Production.** Official and track credits name **Dave Sardy only** as album producer; Noel's production influence and equipment ownership do not make him an accredited co-producer here. `sampling` is documented by Liam confirming the BBC archive audio of John Lennon on *I'm Outta Time*. `protools` (DAW), `overdubbing` (extra US guitars and vocals), `analogtape` (stereo mix print) are described in an engineer-equipment narrative reproduced secondhand on Oasis Recording Information; mechanical details should be reviewed against primary studio notes if available. `heavyreverb` is demonstrated in Noel's first-hand description of recording the toy sitar through Abbey Road's large echo chamber; this is specific to a part, not the LP overall. **Do not assert a named guitar pedal, sampler, software plug-in or instrument model absent from a source.**

**Influence vs similarity.** Critics explicitly describe mid-period Beatles and early Verve psychedelic sources, Noel's long Beatles/Harrison reference points and Lennon sample, while Kitty Empire likens *Falling Down* to Chemical Brothers/Noel collaboration *Setting Sun*. `influenced_by` conservatively retains Beatles, Chemical Brothers and George Harrison, but these references are critics' grounded readings rather than studio admissions; `sounds_like` is editorial only.

**Contrary view:** AllMusic praises a newly forceful psychedelic sound, Guardian/Observer identifies successful grooves in parts, whereas Alexis Petridis disputes the extent of the experimentation. These disagreements justify `experimental=3` and distinguish factual credit confidence from interpretive confidence.

## Cross-album comparative reading
The 2002 LP is band-produced, radio-scaled guitar rock with Hammond, piano, Mellotron and selective orchestration; the 2005 LP is more stripped and song-oriented and has distinct Noel versus Sardy track producers; 2008 foregrounds heavier psychedelic grooves under a single credited producer. A critic-led sonic resemblance never substitutes for a first-person statement of influence. All source and style assignments are subject to editorial review.
