# Nada — Fandome phase 02 | evidence notes

Method: original studio LP recordings only. Descriptor judgements are editorial synthesis from sources listed in the CSV, not extracted ratings. Blank means unavailable evidence. `draft` means unreviewed. Exact instruments and production hardware are never reverse-engineered from band membership or period fashion.

## Nada (1976) — `nada-nada1976`

- **Version identity:** RCA TPL 1-1226 circulated at the end of **1976**, initially as a promo/limited run. Commercial PL 31248 appeared in **1977**, replacing the Lou Reed adaptation **Sarà** with Luigi Tenco's **Lontano lontano**, and rerecording or rearranging **Arte**. These are versions of *one* studio LP: original year **1976**. Archival blogs sometimes write `Sara` without accent. Use `1976` for album entry, document both versions separately; do not count 1977 as new studio album.
- **Genre:** contemporary Italian songwriters are the main focus, with tracks by Paolo Conte, Piero Ciampi, Renzo Zenobi and Luigi Tenco. `canzonedautore` is a direct conceptual match for the source's musica d'autore; `italianpop` and `musicaleggera` are secondary historical-context classifications; `ballad` is track-level editorial, not a quote. These genre assignments are interpretations, not claims that original liner notes applied all four labels.
- **Instruments and production:** `orchestra` is supported by Marchetti's documented orchestra and orchestration. Do **not** add accordion because of the title *La fisarmonica di Stradella*: a title is not instrument evidence. Studio devices/processes not established: `production` empty. Do not automatically propagate the 1973 personnel to this record.
- **Producer:** **Gianni Marchetti** (or Giovanni Marchetti), confirmed by historical album credits and Qobuz track metadata. Paolo Conte, Piero Ciampi, Luigi Tenco and Renzo Zenobi are songwriters here, not producers and not album-specific `influenced_by` simply for having composed material.
- **Scores:** mostly reflective songwriter-oriented listening, with low electronic and groove claims unsupported. Leave `electronic` blank rather than deducing acoustic/electronic ratio. Mood 1 and energy 2 are restrained readings from repertoire and period description, lower confidence `medium`; may be adjusted after hearing full original LP.

## Nada (1979) — `nada-nada1979`

- **Identity:** distinct nine-track Polydor LP **2448 089**, released May 1979, not the RCA LP from 1976/77. This is the *third* studio LP titled *Nada*, after the 1969 and 1976 records. Six track lists and original recording-session credits corroborate.
- **Genres:** `disco`, `funk`, and `rock` are explicitly named for this album in the 1979 discography; Nada herself calls the album disco/funky in a 2019 interview. `countryrock` is only for **Anche se fosse**, appropriately the fifth/last genre. `pop` reflects the contemporary pop shift discussed in the album entry. Disco/funk is the main stylistic distinction from 1976, not generic 1970s Italian pop.
- **Instruments:** unusually detailed track credits identify electric piano and other keyboards (Alessandro Centofanti), bass (Dino Kappa), drums/percussion (Walter Martino), guitars (Pennisi, Fera, Mancuso), acoustic guitar, piano, Hammond organ, saxophone (Antonio Marangolo), harmonica (Mauro Chiari) and tambourine. `instrument=organ` maps to credited Hammond; exact Hammond model not identified. `electricpiano` from original Italian **pianoforte elettrico**. **No synth or drum machine is credited**, so neither is inserted.
- **Producer:** **Cesare Gigli**, not the arrangers **Alessandro Centofanti** and **Walter Martino**. Recording engineers Gaetano Ria et al. are not producers. Some material debuted as 1978 singles; original LP is 1979.
- **Mood/energy/groove:** comparatively dance-oriented, corroborated by disco/funk characterization. The numbers are editorial 0–5 anchored approximations; not listening-test measurements.

## Smalto (1983) — `nada-smalto`

- **Genre:** *Rockol* describes pop, rock and an experimental ambition. Nada calls the **Amore disperato** period punk/new wave in her 2019 interview. Thus `newwave` is a contextual tail genre and **not** a claim that all eight tracks are pure new wave. `ballad` relates to a subset of intimate songs, especially **Per aspettare te**. A member of Goblin arranging the LP does **not** make the whole LP `prog` or a soundtrack.
- **Instruments:** original LP credits confirm bass (Fabio Pignatelli), electric guitars (Marco Rinalduzzi), keyboards (Guarini, Gianni Mazza, Mike Fraser), drums/percussion (Derek J. Wilson and guests), sax (Maurizio Giammarco), backing voices. Source description identifies sax as a major recurring colour. Some sources credit a Lyricon; as this unusual instrument's attribution is isolated and its exact taxonomy is not in Fandome, it is excluded pending direct sleeve/recording verification.
- **Producer:** **Gerry Manzoli** (sometimes spelled Gerri). Fabio Pignatelli, Rinalduzzi, Wilson are **arrangers and/or instrumentalists**, not co-producers per LP credits. Studio recording at Trafalgar Rome and Stone Castle Carimate is documented, but no specific effects/models are; `production` is empty.
- **Lyric themes:** mainly romance, troubled relationships, a daughter in **Charlotte**, imaginative growing up in **Da grande**, and jealousy/aggression in **Cara**. Editorial scales represent a varied eight-song set, not only **Amore disperato**.
- **Landmark:** 1 because the source documentary centers the LP and hit single as part of Nada's identifiable eighties breakthrough; this is an editor's artist-level judgement.

## Noi non cresceremo mai (1984) — `nada-noinoncresceremomai`

- **Identity:** original EMI eight-track LP, released autumn (November) **1984**, never a live album or compilation. **Balliamo ancora un po’** was a previous single **not** on the original album. Vinyl catalogue numbers show inconsistent transcriptions between historical resources: double-check sleeve prior to permanent source metadata.
- **Genres:** Nada herself calls the recording *completely electronic* and says she rented numerous machines. `electronic` is therefore core. `synthpop` is **editorial interpretation** of keyboards+programming and the Kraftwerk/Yazoo influence; neither a studio equipment inventory nor a contemporary source explicitly applies the word to the record. Digicult calls it electronic but wrongly dates to 1985. `newwave` is less prominent, not equivalent to synthpop. The music also retains the Italian-pop songwriting framework.
- **Instruments:** original recorded personnel lists keyboards, guitars, bass, piano, percussion, saxophone, with **programmazione** credits for Bernardo Schiano and Varo Venturi. `synth` is inferred from Nada's explicit contemporary-machine/synth-oriented account and programme, while **no identifiable model** (e.g. DX7, Fairlight, Linn) is claimed. `production=programming` is supported; **do not put drumprogramming, tr808, linn or any model** without evidence. The article has a likely typo **Bernando** for **Bernardo Schiano**.
- **Producer dispute:** original credits and specialist discography specify **Gerry/Gerri Manzoli**. A festival biography loosely describes production by **“Goblin”**, and Nada separately confirms involvement of former Goblin members (including Pignatelli and Guarini). These are not sufficient to supersede the named formal album producer. Retain only Gerry Manzoli in `producers`, explain the context here.
- **Direct influence:** **Kraftwerk|Yazoo**, from Nada's firsthand 2019 VICE interview discussing **this specific album** and what she had been listening to. Not retroactively attached to any other album or classified as mere comparison.
- **Confidence:** medium because machine models, arrangements and some themes remain less specifically documented than the 1979 and 1983 records. `production` carries only the verified broad programming technique.
