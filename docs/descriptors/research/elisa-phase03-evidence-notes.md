## Ivy — `elisa-ivy` — 30 Nov 2010 — `studio`
**Album identity:** despite being built from older-song acoustic re-recordings, fresh material, and covers, contemporary apcom and Sugar release metadata classify the 17-track project as a studio album. Accompanying film/documentary and its live material are separate media, not grounds for a `live` album type [I10_APCOM, I10_SPOTIFY, I10_ROCKOL_REVIEW].
**Production:** AllMusic directly credits **Elisa Toffoli** as `Producer`, with **Andrea Rigonat, Gianluca Ballarin, Simone Bertolotti, Andrea Fontana** explicitly labeled `Additional Production` [I10_ALLMUSIC]. These are distinguishable from arranger-only credits such as Max Gelsi. French is sung on the Camille cover duet *Pour que l'amour me quitte*; there are English and Italian tracks [I10_SPOTIFY].
**Genres:** see genre-evidence CSV. AllMusic labels Pop/Rock, Italian Pop, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock and Alternative/Indie Rock, but its **actual review** stresses muted piano-and-voice ballads. AOTY's Soft Rock and Classical Crossover labels are explicitly secondary and disputed/weak; Claude may omit `classicalcrossover` on category-fit review, without disguising its published label [I10_ALLMUSIC, I10_AOTY]. **No new unsourced 'acousticpop' token** merely because recording is acoustic.
**Scores:** `energy 1`, `electronic 0`, `tempo 1`, `groove 0` because slow piano-and-voice arrangements dominate, analog instrumentation and unhurried acoustic rhythm [I10_ALLMUSIC, I10_TGCOM]. `mood 2`: elegiac, nostalgic but gentle. `theatrical 1`: subdued, close intimate performances. `experimental 1`: traditional ballad structures despite arrangement revisions. `polish 3`: deliberate natural/organic presentation yet carefully recorded. **Voice:** soft and clear prevail, with occasional powerful passages: not a monolithic voice. **Lyrics:** reflective/romantic/nostalgic strands follow record concept and texts. **sounds_like Tori Amos** is an AllMusic critic's explicit album-level stylistic analogy—not a causal documented influence [I10_ALLMUSIC]. **influenced_by blank:** covers by Smashing Pumpkins, Queens of the Stone Age, Camille, Ligabue do not prove they shaped the album; Rockol only speculates about 1979 → Nostalgia [I10_ROCKOL_REVIEW]. `landmark 0` is cautious: valuable acoustic chapter but not obviously her single defining record. `confidence medium` because two genre classifiers suggest stylistic categories much broader than album's sound.
**Critic dispute:** AllMusic calls the ballad approach repetitious, whereas Rockol favors warmth and reinterpretations. These are evaluations; underlying acoustic instrumentation is not contested [I10_ALLMUSIC,I10_ROCKOL_REVIEW].

## L’anima vola — `elisa-lanimavola` — 15 Oct 2013 — `studio`
**Edition:** original 11-track release, Italian throughout [A13_ROCKOL_REVIEW, A13_PANORAMA]. Album is not the soundtrack for *Django Unchained* despite including *Ancora qui*, which originally appeared in that film; production/arrangement of the final album cut differs [A13_ROCKOL_INT, A13_MB].
**Production:** **Elisa Toffoli, Davide Rossi, Andrea Rigonat, Ennio Morricone** identified as producers by MusicBrainz; Elisa also describes her own production and arrangement with **Christian Rigano**, so he is included as additional album collaborator under production but MusicBrainz explicitly credits Rigano for programming, not its release-level Producer relation [A13_MB,A13_ROCKOL_INT,A13_RADIO2]. Morricone's role concerns the included *Ancora qui* and not the whole album. Ligabue, Tiziano Ferro and Giuliano Sangiorgi are songwriters/guest collaborators, **not album producers and not automatically influences** [A13_ROCKOL_INT].
**Genre weight:** critics consistently foreground Italian vocal pop and lighter English-influenced rock, with cinematic strings [A13_ROCKOL_REVIEW,A13_PANORAMA,A13_AAB]. One clearly electronic 80s-flavored cut (*Maledetto labirinto*) supports `electronic` only at track-texture level [A13_PANORAMA]. Resist stamping the whole record 'synthpop' or 'electronic' based on this one track. `poprock` is a catalog alias already proposed in earlier phases.
**Scores:** `energy 3`: mid-tempo pop and rock lift but numerous reflective passages. `electronic 2`: keyboards/programming and isolated 1980s electronics balanced by rock ensemble and real strings. `mood 3`: warm love and parental themes plus darker uncertainty, not relentlessly euphoric. `theatrical 3`: expansive, cinematic arrangements and strong vocal peaks. `experimental 2`: some inventive arranging with conventional song forms. `polish 4`: detailed production, strings and high-quality studio sound. `tempo 2`: slower-to-midtempo overall; `groove 2`: rock/pop pulse but not a dance-led record. **Voice** clear/powerful/soft, **lyrics** love, introspection, life/identity, nostalgia and heartbreak; subjective extrapolations from critics, not exhaustive lyrical coding. **Language `it`** throughout. `landmark 1`: major first fully Italian studio album, long career milestone beyond an awards proxy. `sounds_like`/`influenced_by` left blank; orchestrations by Rossi are credits, not proof of a Goldfrapp/Coldplay influence.
**Disagreement:** critics emphasize accessible melodic pop while praising artful layered arrangements; these are compatible on sound; opinions about album quality diverge less than on *On* [A13_ROCKOL_REVIEW,A13_PANORAMA,A13_AAB].

## On — `elisa-on` — 25 Mar 2016 — `studio`
**Edition:** original **13 tracks**, 11 English and two Italian (*Bruciare per te*, *Sorrido già*); all bonus tracks and later tour remixes excluded [O16_ROCKOL,O16_ALLMUSICITALIA].
**Production:** **Curt Schneider** named primary record producer independently by Rockol's contemporaneous review and biography [O16_ROCKOL,O16_ROCKOLBIO]. Secondary release metadata attribute production to **Elisa Toffoli, Andrea Rigonat, Christian Rigano, Michele Canova Iorfida** as well; Rigonat is also credited specifically on 3 tracks [O16_RIFFITER]. **Review flag:** these additional album credits are less individually substantiated from primary original-edition liner notes; **Claude should check physical credits or complete track-by-track before elevating certainty**. (We do not assert that all five produced every track.) Do not promote Victor Indrizzo from drums/percussion to producer, nor Jack Savoretti from co-writer/guest to producer [O16_ROCKOL].
**Genre weight:** pop/electronic/dance-pop lead. Rockol explicitly labels *With the Hurt* and *Bruciare per te* with **trip-hop** and **drum'n'bass** accents; soul (*Hold On for a Minute*), funk (*Peter Pan*), electric rock (*Catch the Light*) are particular-track textures and lower ranked [O16_ROCKOL]. No ungrounded all-over dubstep/trap/EDM; the later tour's dubstep-style *Ti vorrei sollevare* arrangement is outside this studio album [O16_ROCKOL_TOUR]. **New token:** `drumandbass` is a named expert genre, absent from current validator; do not silently map it to breakbeat.
**Scores:** `energy 4`: emphatic dynamic choruses and faster programmed pop; `electronic 4`: drum machines/synths/programming at center. `mood 4`: energetic, bright despite ballad and dramatic introspective moments. `theatrical 3`: strong range/power ballad scale, not continuous musical-theater staging. `experimental 3`: genre switches, rhythmic glitches and octave leaps noted by Mattia Marzi, while structurally accessible. `polish 5`: glossy current-state pop production. `tempo 4` and `groove 4`: record created for kinetic enjoyment, but not a continuous mix [O16_ROCKOL,O16_SOUNDS]. **Voice:** powerful and clear lead with quieter song passages. **Lyrics:** mixed relationship/introspective themes, not solely partying. **Sounds like** Sia (expert comparison of power ballads) and Adele (soul intro on one track): these are limited local sound analogies, not established influences. **Influenced by** Diplo and Skrillex directly attributed to Elisa by Rockol *as inspirations for this album's electronic tracks* [O16_ROCKOL]. **Madonna** is a self-declared model in Elisa's **stage** pop presentation in later 2016 interview [O16_ROCKOL_TOUR], not automatically a producer-level album influence; therefore omit from `influenced_by`. `landmark 0`: influential career shift, but landmark rubric deliberately selective.
**Critical dispute:** Marzi calls the eclectic pop risk successful while other critics hear a more commercially accommodating rather than exploratory record [O16_ROCKOL,O16_SOUNDS]. Sonic facts and value judgments kept separate.
