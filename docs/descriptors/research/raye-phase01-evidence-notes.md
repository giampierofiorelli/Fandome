# RAYE — Phase 01: album-level research and uncertainty

Research cut-off: **25 October 2026**. This covers two studio albums plus one historically important EP/mini-album. No live releases; no single-only, remix-only or deluxe-reissue rows. Numeric values are editorial 0–5 classifications from sourced descriptions, **not actual recorded acoustic measurements or independent critics’ scores**. `draft` means second-person audit required.

## 2020 — Euphoric Sad Songs

**Evidence:** E20_GU, E20_AP, E20_PR, E20_TIVO, E20_Q. All nine tracks and producers checked against one original nine-track digital edition. Not a rearrangement of 2021 remixes.

- `genres`: `dancepop|pop|house|synthpop` recurring or governing palette; `disco` comes notably from 'Natalie Don't' and 'Love of Your Life'; `funk` substantially a finale texture, **not** a universal album identity. Broad `dance|electronic` anchored by synth and club beats. Source and scope in genre-evidence, not inferred from visiting recording studio.
- `energy=4`, `electronic=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4`: repeated four-on-floor and radio dance arrangements, moderated by dramatic opening heartbreak ballad; these are *relative ordinal judgments*, not BPM or LUFS findings.
- `mood=3`: *central methodological caveat*: upbeat club grooves are offset by many grieving and jealous lyrics; nine stages progress from distress toward self-acceptance. Setting mood to 4–5 solely because the music is danceable would be wrong. `3` reflects trajectory and contrasting affect; a stricter interpretation of *lyric* affect may justify 2.
- `theatrical=2`: occasional dramatic power ballad but no album-scale concept staging like 2026. `experimental=1`: specialist reviewers describe accessible rather than adventurous dance-pop; `polish=4`: commercial synth and crisp beat production.
- `voice=clear|soft|powerful`: interview and contemporary criticism describe assured/cool delivery and a power-ballad opener, but **the `soft` and `powerful` distribution is less securely established at whole-project scale**. Keep in draft and consider reducing to `clear` if reviewer cannot independently hear/verify broader prevalence. Avoid treating whistle, vibrato, or vocal range as available typed tags (the voice enum doesn't contain them).
- `lyrics=heartbreak|love|introspection|identity`: artist directly lays out nine phases of grief and eventual self-healing; identity is lower-order self-love conclusion.
- `producers`: transcribed strictly from Qobuz track credit role Producer / CoProducer / AdditionalProducer; writer, mixer and vocalist credits excluded. Includes producer teams such as TMS, Rudimental and Punctual. `Mark Ralph` additional, `Johan Lenox` co-producer, no producer credit for a mere featured DJ except where specifically present.
- `influenced_by=Dolly Parton`: 'Natalie Don't' is explicitly framed in specialist/artist materials as a modern 'Jolene' reference (E20_GU). This is a **single-song, not whole-record**, influence; Claude should drop the entry if Fandome requires influences to affect a large share of an album. `sounds_like` are transparent editorial comparisons, not statements by RAYE.
- `landmark=1`: a self-contained transitional 9-song concept and an important public body of work before independence, *not* a chart award claim.

## 2023 — My 21st Century Blues

**Evidence:** B23_PF, B23_NME, B23_GU, B23_GU2; credits B23_Q, B23_MB, B23_DORK. Assessment on 15-track original, **not** Escapism's sped-up variant.

- `genres`: pop/R&B recurrent; jazz-rooted vocal writing and several funk/soul instrumentals; Guardian explicitly mentions house, blues and dancehall. `triphop` is restricted to 'Escapism' (NME), whereas rap-adjacent expression is on multiple pieces including 'Flip a Switch' and 'Five Star Hotels'. A long tag list is not a claim that every song belongs to all genres.
- `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: energetic floor-fillers coexist with ballads and spoken segments. Not a continuous dance album. `electronic=3`: synth/electronic rhythm palette on 'Escapism' and 'Black Mascara' offset by brass, strings, band soul and jazz. `polish=4` for sophisticated electronic/live blend.
- `mood=2`: difficult intimate narratives, dependency, heartbreak, abuse and anxiety dominate. The defiant resolve and occasional brighter groove prevent a fully bleak value 0 or 1. The dark song 'Escapism' is *not* joyful because the beat drives.
- `theatrical=4`: introductory MC, cinematic orchestral arrangements, spoken passages, stage narrative and high-intensity performance. `experimental=3`: genre switching and episodic formal structure, but most tracks retain accessible pop songcraft.
- `voice=powerful|soft|spoken|rap`: critics explicitly point to a powerhouse voice with soft harmonies, semi-rapped phrases, sung-spoken 'Escapism' and spoken MC framing. Guest 070 Shake's voice **must not** count as RAYE's `rap` without her own examples; Pitchfork cites RAYE's semi-rapped 'Flip a Switch'.
- `lyrics`: social critique ('Ice Cream Man', 'Environmental Anxiety'), intimate addiction and body-image struggles alongside relationships; political secondary only to clearly documented topical songs, no inference of party affiliation.
- `producers`: Mike Sabath dominant; Punctual ('Black Mascara'); RAYE additional/co- or full credited; Di Genius confirmed Dork; Pete Miller and Gianluca Buccellati from album Qobuz metadata; be aware aggregator may split vocal engineering and production differently. `sounds_like` Amy/Jorja is independent editorial music similarity, not actual collaborators; 2026 Elle testimony is *not* reason to retroactively add Amy to 2023 influence if causality specific to record wasn't established.
- `influenced_by=Nina Simone|Jill Scott`: qualified critic explicitly describes RAYE's musical upbringing in those terms. No songwriter guest automatically becomes influence.
- **Critical disagreements:** Pitchfork finds the collage uneven, NME calls it triumphant. Their shared descriptions of stylistic diversity are more reliable than either review's value judgment; `confidence=high` applies to factual/overall record descriptors, **not** universal agreement of critical quality.

## 2026 — THIS MUSIC MAY CONTAIN HOPE.

**Evidence:** H26_OFF, H26_OCI, H26_AP, H26_PF, H26_NME, H26_GU, H26_Q, H26_MB, H26_ELLE, H26_MERC.

- `genres`: jazz, pop, R&B, big band, swing jazz, gospel-inflected soul and brass funk are discussed throughout, with a new precise label **`orchestralpop`** directly named by Pitchfork. `house` (Guardian), `disco` ('Skin & Bones'), `hiphop` ('The WhatsApp Shakespeare'), `gospel` ('Fields'), `easylistening` (Guardian) and `powerballad` (critic narrative) are more limited. **Do not assign `classical` or `opera` merely because of an orchestra or 'operatic' voice quality.** `orchestralpop` is proposed new enum, so Claude must extend both `scenes.ts` and `genreFamilies.ts` and validator.
- `energy=3` and `tempo=3`: high energy sections plus slow orchestrated ballads. `electronic=2`: some modern synth/club tracks but acoustically recorded big band and strings predominate. `polish=5`: extensive credited orchestra, brass, professional recording and compositional production. Electronic score differs sharply from pop polish.
- `mood=3`: original four-act arc moves explicitly from dark/damaged autumn into hopeful summer; neither a uniformly sad album nor happy dance-pop. `theatrical=5`: explicitly staged dramatic concept, narrator, huge arrangements, ensemble. `experimental=4`: extended 17-song, 70+ minute cross-style theatrical narrative; not an avant-garde claim.
- `voice=powerful|soft|clear|spoken|rap`: direct critic descriptions of extremes of vocal range and intimacy, narrated/recited passages and jazz scatting/rap. Background choir does not make RAYE's personal lead voice `choral`.
- `lyrics`: hope, relationship pain, self-doubt, love, recovery, friendship, narrative storytelling; `spiritual` interpreted specifically through healing and gospel passages, hence human review encouraged. Joyful final songs should not erase sorrow and self-loathing sections.
- `producers`: original recording credits verified via H26_Q/H26_MB. Co- and additional production should count; avoid guest Al Green as a producer without a credit; Hans Zimmer **is** specifically credited as producer on 'Click Clack Symphony.' Alongside RAYE, list Sabath, Richards, Hill, Clements, Riley, Punctual, Hans Zimmer, Toneworld, Buenck, Russell Emanuel. Alex Robinson and Marvin Tony Hemmings are additionally named as producers by Dork but **Qobuz usually lists Alex as Engineer**: human must reconcile their exact per-track production before import, or omit Alex Robinson rather than guess. `Marvin Tony Hemmings` appears in Dork aggregate; check individual-track credit before final.
- `influenced_by`: 2026 interview ELLE directly quotes Amy as inspiration and discusses same foundational jazz icons Nina Simone, Ella Fitzgerald, Dinah Washington and Sarah Vaughan. These are RAYE's documented *artistic roots*, not necessarily explicit inspiration for a particular 2026 song. A cautious alternative is `Amy Winehouse` only; other names require reviewer adjudication of direct vs editorial attribution.
- `sounds_like`: album-level reviewer comparisons are not inserted as historical influence unless corroborated by artist/producer/critic's influence claim.
- **Critical disagreements:** Guardian and Pitchfork find some overstuffing and stylization, NME and Rolling Stone UK laud ambition. Four-act darkness-to-hope concept and the wide range of genres are corroborated independently.
- `landmark=1` additionally supported by contemporaneous 22 Oct 2026 Mercury Prize winner report (H26_MERC); prize not evidence of sonic genre and doesn't dictate score.

## Source independence, safeguards, open checks

These are source-informed human editorial proposals, not a trained acoustics classifier. Some same-label Qobuz/Apple credit descriptions are correlated, so count distinct *outlets and review authors* rather than URLs. Producer names must be verified with `Producer`, `CoProducer` or `AdditionalProducer` roles. Track-only genres permitted but their scope must be preserved if Fandome later weights tags by prevalence. Empty feature permitted for insufficient evidence. Confirm full `influenced_by` is appropriate at whole-album scale before importing, with `H26_ELLE` as context rather than blanket attribution.
