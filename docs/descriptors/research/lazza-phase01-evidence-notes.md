# Lazza — evidence notes, phase 01 (research draft)

**Scope**: Four original studio albums (2017–2024) and the 2020 **J** mixtape only. Research checked on 2026-10-24. Original-release editions drive all sonic judgments. The files are editorial proposals, NOT second-person reviews. Scales are editorial estimates, **not** measured BPM/audio signals. Empty `influenced_by` is deliberate: liking an artist, using a named producer, or sampling a composition does not automatically establish album-level artistic influence.

## Zzala (2017) — laz za / `lazza-zzala` [NEW]

- Sources: Rockol 2017 review, Rockol retrospective (2024), Qobuz catalogue, The Crate 2025 retrospective. See sources CSV. 11 tracks; debut.
- **Genre** `hiphop|trap`: both named for the work. Classical-style piano phrases are documented *instrumentation*, not enough to classify the whole album as classical.
- **Sound/scale**: energy 4 and tempo 4 reflect driving, forceful rapping; electronic 3 recognises heavy programmed trap production with real piano parts; mood 1 follows sources on dark and burdened tonal colour; theatrical 2, experimental 2 and polish 3 are tentative anchored editorial judgments; groove 2 avoids equating trap groove with dancefloor danceability.
- **Voices/themes**: rap lead, punchlines, personal disclosure and drive to establish identity. No supported named sonic comparison/influence is inserted.
- **Production**: Low Kidd and Lazza are directly connected to production; Rockol retrospective also identifies DJ Slait among the music-production team. Claude should inspect whether DJ Slait is classified as direct track producer or production/direction supervisor before marking `reviewed`.
- **Uncertainty**: qualified sources discuss technical craft but provide no calibrated tempo, polish or theatrical rating. Row `confidence=medium`.

## Re Mida (2019) — `lazza-remida` [EXISTING]

- Sources: Rockol contemporaneous review, Universal original-release tracklist and shop notes, Qobuz track credits.
- **Genres** `hiphop|trap`: contemporary descriptions of hip-hop, trap and Auto-Tune. Do not infer `classical` merely because piano is featured; the 2019 *Piano Solo* and *Aurum* versions are **not** included in the original album's score.
- **Sound/scale**: energy 4, tempo 4 for forceful rhythmic technique; electronic 4 from trap beats and vocal processing, offset by occasional live piano; mood 2 from aggressive/materialist lyric framing with some other textures; polish 4 and experimental 2 from the review's detailed yet accessible production; theatrical 2, groove 3 tentative calibrated judgments.
- **Voice/lyrics**: mostly rapped with sung/Auto-Tuned hooks. Identity/ironic references dominate. `clear` describes melodic, more conventionally sung lines, NOT a change in vocal identity.
- **Producers**: Low Kidd and Lazza supported by original credits. `landmark=1` due to breakthrough #1 album; still editorial.
- **Caveat**: Distributor Qobuz sometimes lists original and later expansion under the same title. Use Universal original March 2019, not Oct 2019 *Aurum*.

## J (2020) — `lazza-j` [NEW; `type=mixtape`]

- Primary interviews: Billboard Italia and Rolling Stone Italia; contemporaneous critical reviews: Newsic and La Casa del Rap; Sky TG24 gives a ten-song credited producer list.
- **Genres** `trap|hiphop|drill`: trap is album-dominant; `drill` **only on Moncler**. This distinction is explicit in genre evidence CSV. No claim that every track is drill.
- **Sound/scale**: energy 5 from reviews describing maximal aggressive rap and hard beats; electronic 4 from programmed trap/drill production; mood 3 from swagger/club orientation, not euphoric positivity; theatrical 2 and experimental 1 from largely conventional club track structure; polish 4 from assembled studio producers; tempo 4 and groove 4 follow artist's statement he made the songs for dancefloor/club use (though no measured tempo is available).
- **Producers**: Low Kidd, Drillionaire, Marvin Cruz, AXL Beats, Lazza, Sick Luke and Andry the Hitmaker taken from Sky TG24's ten-track listing. Critical review uses 'Luke Skywalker' for one contributor, but official/trade article calls that track's producer Sick Luke; keep trade credit pending Claude verification.
- **Lyrical focus**: club-facing bravado, irony and self-identification rather than introspective writing. `landmark=0` is editorial relative to his breakthrough studio albums.
- **No false influence**: Rolling Stone mentions admiration for Kanye West, but it does NOT by itself establish documented sonic influence on this particular tape. `influenced_by` remains blank.

## Sirio (2022) — `lazza-sirio` [EXISTING]

- **Edition**: original 17 songs of Apr 2022, excluding the 2023 addition 'Cenere' and piano/orchestral *Concertos* variants.
- Sources: OndaRock, Rockol, Newsic, artist interview in Billboard Italia, Universal official track credits and official release note, Qobuz track credits.
- **Genres** `poprap|trap|hiphop|pop|trappop|posttrap|reggaeton|hardcorehiphop`. Pop-rap/trap describe album's broader strategy; reggaeton (Cinema), post-trap (Molotov), hardcore hip-hop (Topboy), trap-pop (Uscito di galera) are **specific-track** observations. New genre required: `hardcorehiphop`; others are in the guide, including `posttrap` and `trappop`.
- **Sound/scale**: energy 4 but tempo 3 thanks to a significant melodic midtempo component; electronic 4 for synths/drum machines, with meaningful piano openings; mood 2 and theatrical 3 for intimate/melodramatic lyrics; experimental 2 for a mix critically described as familiar rather than radically innovative; polish 4; groove 3 due to some danceable tracks, not a fully club-oriented record.
- **Lead voice/themes**: both rapid rap and clear melodic refrains; love, sensuality, introspection, identity.
- **Producers**: Universal's per-track credits support Low Kidd, Drillionaire, Young Miles, Lazza, Takagi & Ketra, 808Melo, CashMoneyAP, PK, Mirko808. Qobuz additionally credits Bigbadbeats for Molotov. Producer spelling/aliases `Miles` / `Young Miles` should be normalized by Claude; no duplicate aliases intentionally.
- **Sonic comparators**: OndaRock explicitly compares elements of Sirio to Blanco, Sfera Ebbasta and Ghali. These are *critical comparisons*, not asserted historical influences.
- **Critical disagreement**: Rockol admires greater intimacy, whereas OndaRock and Newsic criticize formulaic trap/pop-rap production. This does not remove observable sonic traits. Do not conflate score with critical acclaim.

## Locura (2024) — `lazza-locura` [EXISTING]

- **Edition**: the original 18-track 2024 album. The 2025 *LOCURA JAM + OPERA* is a substantially rearranged project and not included in scores. Universal/Qobuz can show '19 September' for digital stores; FIMI/Rockol use Italian-market 20 September 2024; year 2024 is unambiguous.
- Sources: signed reviews in OndaRock (Antonio Silvestri) and Rockol (Claudio Cabona); official Universal per-track producer credits; Qobuz and FIMI metadata.
- **Genres** `poprap|trappop|hiphop|trap|pop|electronic`: OndaRock explicitly tags pop-rap and pop-trap, distinguishes trap, melodic pop, and repeated electronic synth-based arrangements. `electronic` is a supporting style, not equivalent to the entire album being electro.
- **Sound/scale**: energy 4, tempo 3, groove 3 because hard trap alternates with introspective midtempo ballads; electronic 4 for synth-driven cinematic beats, offset by piano/acoustic detail; mood 2 contrasts relationship anxiety and fame with braggadocio; theatrical 4 captures grand, elaborate arrangements; polish 5 is an editorial interpretation of conspicuously lush, layered production; experimental 2 because qualified reviews find the pop-trap formula familiar.
- **Producers**: Universal credits Drillionaire, Young Miles, Low Kidd, Lazza, Foreign Teck, Nightfeelings, Kiid, Sadturs, Finesse on documented tracks. Track with 'MILES' is probably the Young Miles alias but final alias resolution belongs to Claude. This list does not claim producer exclusivity on tracks with missing official credits.
- **Critical disagreement**: Rockol gives a positive 7.5/10 for musical production but criticizes lyrics; OndaRock rates the overall album 5/10 and calls it elaborately produced yet formulaic. Distinguish sound evidence from approval.

## Cross-release rules and omissions

- `Zzala` and `J` are the only newly proposed catalogue entries in this batch; `Re Mida`, `Sirio`, `Locura` must retain existing catalog IDs.
- Earlier mixtapes **Destiny** (2012) and **K1 Mixtape** (2014), piano variants and *LOCURA JAM + OPERA* (2025) are NOT analysed or added here. Do not classify them as new studio albums or import them blindly. The 2025 variant could merit separate analysis and an appropriate type under the current schema.
- `hardcorehiphop` and `drill` are **new genre identifiers** (not present in the guide inspected 2026-10-24): Claude must extend `src/data/scenes.ts` and descriptor validator first, or apply a controlled exception.
- None of the ratings is an objective measurement. Empty evidence columns are intentional, not accidental omissions.
- `status=draft` for every row; do not mark reviewed based only on CSV structural tests.
