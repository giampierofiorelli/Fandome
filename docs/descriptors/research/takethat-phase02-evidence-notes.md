## `takethat-thecircus` — *The Circus* (2008)

**Edition and catalog ID.** Original 2008 UK studio album, 12 songs, ending in ‘Here’; excludes the subsequent *The Circus Live* concert release, editions with a hidden ‘She Said’ bonus and the *The Circus* tour. The album is already in Fandome under `takethat-thecircus`. Official Take That timeline and UK shop document the release and original sequencing.

**Genre reconciliation.** AllMusic’s broad retrospective tags dance-pop, Euro-pop, teen pop and pop are retained; however, several contemporary reviews describe the 2008 recorded sound more specifically as piano-led band pop, soft rock and glossy guitar rock. The Guardian’s John Burgess describes chiming guitars and strings, and Ally Carnwath describes soft rock rather than the group’s earlier club style. The common pop-rock label is primary; alternative rock is listed last because that specific tag derives principally from secondary discography genre classification. `adultcontemporary` is an editorial broad-category inference from the period reviews, **not a verbatim source label**; it can be dropped by the reviewer if strict named genre sourcing is required.

**Production and instrumentation.** The original release track credits list **John Shanks as producer**, Jeff Rothschild as recording engineer/mixer and programmer, and Ryan Carline as additional programmer. Neither Jeff Rothschild nor Ryan Carline is therefore included in `producers`. MusicBrainz explicitly documents Gary Barlow piano and keyboards; Shanks guitar and bass; Jason Orange acoustic guitar; Rothschild acoustic drums; the London Studio Orchestra; Wil Malone strings and brass arrangements; trumpet, trombone and tuba; and track-level Ben Mark electric guitar, Matthew Ward violin and Philip Sheppard cello. The 15 tokens reflect documented presence (even if some appear on only one song), not all equally prominent. No specific synth model or drum machine is evidenced, so none is entered. Main production tokens: `programming|stringarrangements|hornarrangements`.

**Rating rationale.** Energy 3 and tempo 3 represent a mix of anthemic mid-tempo singalongs, quicker jaunty songs and sustained ballads. Electronic 1 reflects live drums/guitars/piano/orchestra, despite documented background programming; groove 2 indicates limited club emphasis. Mood 4 is broadly hopeful/celebratory, qualified by introspective songs; theatrical 4 captures swelling arrangements and communal choruses, not circus *concert* staging. Experimentation 1 / polish 5 indicate conventional highly finished mainstream rock/pop. Voice `clear|powerful|soft` represents Barlow’s lead, Owen’s gentler nasal timbre and shared vocal choruses; no undocumented vocal processing is added.

**Lyrics/comparison/influence.** `love|introspection|nostalgia|identity|storytelling` is a conservative thematic description based on the original songs and period commentary. The Guardian compares it to Coldplay’s *A Rush of Blood to the Head*, Keane’s piano-pop and ELO-like songs ‘Hello’/‘Up All Night’; these are **sounds_like** not `influenced_by`, since reviewers’ analogies are not members’ documented inspiration testimony. No direct album-level musical influence supported the stringent `influenced_by` rule.

**Critical disagreement.** Polly Vernon found the songs broad and winning, while reviewers Carnwath and Burgess criticised the reliance on anthemic rock formulas and over-polished ballads. These differences explain the high theatrical/polish values but do not justify an inflated experimentation score.

## `takethat-progress` — *Progress* (2010)

**Edition and formation.** The original ten-track studio album from 2010 is used; no *Progress Live*, later *Progressed* reissue, or duplicate of the new eight songs. It was the group’s first studio recording after Robbie Williams rejoined, and the last Take That studio album featuring Jason Orange. Fandome’s current catalog ID is `takethat-progress`.

**Genres.** NME calls the release arena synth-pop; the Guardian highlights Stuart Price’s electropop palette and calls the record club-pop; AllMusic retains the additional Pop, Euro-Pop, Dance-Pop, Contemporary Pop/Rock, Adult Contemporary and Teen Pop categories. The top five terms capture the record’s genuine sonic departure; the lower tags give broad/review-label continuity. `poprock` is secondary to electronic pop, especially on the guitar-forward ‘Underground Machine’ and the expansive ‘The Flood’. There is no evidence that a named brand of synth or specific drum machine was consistently used. *Glam stomp* from critics is a sonic rhythmic description, not automatically a `glamrock` album genre. Avoid conflating synthpop/electropop as identical tokens.

**Instrument sources and production roles.** Label credits (Qobuz) and MusicBrainz original track-level liner relationships consistently specify **Stuart Price producer and mixer**, Gary Barlow and Price programming and keyboard work; Price bass and guitar, Ben Mark guitar, Karl Brazil and Howard Donald on real drums, and Wil Malone’s string orchestration with London Studio Orchestra on selected songs. Only `programming|stringarrangements` is included. Generic programming is documented, but no source is sufficiently precise to justify `drumprogramming` on this specific album. The `synth` token is supported by contemporaneous NME and Guardian descriptions of a synth-driven album, not inferred from the producer’s discography. No piano token is asserted without a track-specific credited instrument, even though keys carry ballad arrangements.

**Rating rationale.** Energy 4, tempo 4 and groove 4 are justified by strong beats (‘SOS’, ‘Kidz’, ‘Happy Now’), contrasted with softer finale material. Electronic 4 is strong synth use alongside verified electric instruments and live drums, not fully electronic. Mood 2 is based on critical accounts of anxious, even paranoid, lyrical subjects, despite moments of sentiment. Theatrical 4 / experimental 3 follow the unusually dramatic glam stomp and texture-conscious stylistic break for this band; polish 5 reflects Price’s gleaming studio finish. Voice `clear|powerful|soft` includes Barlow, Williams, Owen and Donald with changing leads; the clear vocal mode remains primary.

**Lyrics and comparisons.** `social|introspection|identity|love|heartbreak|political`: societal anxiety and conflict feature in ‘Kidz’ and ‘SOS’; Barlow/Williams reunion and relationships thread other songs. The Guardian hears Killers, Scissor Sisters and Muse; AllMusic suggests Gorillaz; reviewers also hear Queen. They stay in `sounds_like`. These are explicitly **not evidence** of album influences. No confirmed direct musician/producer influence testimony is available for `influenced_by`.

**Source/edition warning.** AllMusic shows November 22 in some territories, official Take That history says release on **15 November 2010** UK; the year is unequivocally 2010. ‘Eight Letters’ and possible hidden ‘Flowerbed’ timing should not produce new catalog rows.

## `takethat-progressed` — *Progressed* (2011, EP)

**Crucial scope: eight NEW songs only.** The official band site calls *Progressed* its first EP with eight additional songs and dates the UK release **13 June 2011**; MusicBrainz models a distinct eight-track EP release group. The commonly sold 2011 two-disc **deluxe repackage** appends the whole 2010 *Progress* album. Therefore Fandome should add **one standalone `ep` row** `takethat-progressed`, based on these eight unique tracks only: ‘When We Were Young’, ‘Man’, ‘Love Love’, ‘The Day the Work Is Done’, ‘Beautiful’, ‘Don’t Say Goodbye’, ‘Aliens’, ‘Wonderful World’. It must NOT re-import the original 2010 songs, despite AllMusic’s 82-minute deluxe-runtime metadata. There is no separate co-billed artist despite soundtrack placements of singles.

**Genre hierarchy and disagreements.** Release metadata lists electronic/pop and synth-pop; contemporary review excerpts describe disco electropop. AllMusic’s review identifies vivid **track-only contrasts**: the acoustic folk beginning of ‘When We Were Young’; the specific dubstep texture of ‘Man’; a new-wave synth-pop segment on ‘Beautiful’; and a mini rock opera on ‘Aliens’. Genres `folk|newwave|dubstep|rockopera` are intentionally ordered last and tagged `some tracks` in genre evidence, to avoid overstating 8-song EP-wide coverage. AllMusic’s generic Euro-Pop/Dance-Pop tag is for the marketed expanded reissue rather than solely EP; the review of the eight new tracks and original-release metadata secure more specific terms. Do not infer an entire dubstep album from one song.

**Instrument and production verification.** MusicBrainz EP track relationships credit keyboards (Gary Barlow and Stuart Price), bass/guitar (Price/Ben Mark), real drums (Karl Brazil), **mandolin on ‘When We Were Young’ by Steve Lipson**, and the London Studio Orchestra with Wil Malone string arrangements. AllMusic also explicitly references a piano intro on ‘Beautiful’ and synthesizers on ‘Love Love’ and ‘Beautiful’; acoustically strummed guitar on ‘When We Were Young’. Hence `synth|keyboards|guitar|bass|drums|piano|acousticguitar|mandolin|orchestra|strings`. Programmed parts and string arrangements are credited; a specific synth/drum-machine model, sampling apparatus, sidechain compressor etc. is **not proven**. Stuart Price is the sole album/EP producer in label and release credits; Gary Barlow is co-programmer, **not** thereby co-producer.

**Rating rationale.** Energy 4 / groove 4 reflect forceful ‘Love Love’, ‘Man’ and others; tempo 3 accounts for sentimental acoustic and midtempo songs. Electronic 4 is moderated by mandolin and strings. Mood 2 reflects themes of age, nostalgia, crisis and separation, despite brighter moments; theatrical 4 captures the stylised ‘Aliens’ and dramatic chorus arrangements. Experimentation 3 is higher than mid-2000s pop-rock but still within mainstream structures; polish 5. Voices `clear|powerful|soft` are documented band members with Robbie/Barlow shared leads and Jason Orange’s rare lead on ‘Wonderful World’ (AllMusic).

**Sonic references are not causal influences.** Contemporary critical comparisons include Depeche Mode (‘Love Love’), Pet Shop Boys (‘Don’t Say Goodbye’), ABBA (‘Beautiful’ and melody of ‘When We Were Young’), the Killers (‘Beautiful’), Mumford & Sons (‘When We Were Young’), Michael Jackson’s ‘Beat It’ (*Thriller*) (‘Aliens’), Vangelis (‘Wonderful World’). These belong only in `sounds_like`. A film placement for ‘Love Love’ and ‘When We Were Young’ does not change EP classification to `soundtrack`. No direct verified album-level musical influence from a band member or producer justifies `influenced_by`.

**Caveat on release timestamps.** Some retailer streaming pages use a generic January 1 year placeholder, and the double-CD can reverse the disc sequence. Use the official 13 June date and the separate eight-track EP as canonical. The source file documents the discrepancy.
