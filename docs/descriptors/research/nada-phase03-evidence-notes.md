# Nada — Phase 03: research and editorial reasoning

Research date: 29 October 2026. English-language original descriptor notes (not quotations). Date and metadata checked against the original record catalogue and historical listings. Scores are editorial comparisons with the Fandome anchors, not measured laboratory values; uncertain scores are left empty.

## Baci rossi (1986) — `nada-bacirossi`
- **Edition:** May 1986 EMI Italiana original LP catalogue 64 1187491. **Eight** tracks. This is distinct from *Noi non cresceremo mai* (1984) and the single *Bolero* (1987); do not introduce either recording into this LP. Some concert biographies report 1985, but original EMI pressing and monograph document 1986.
- **Genres:** original pressing catalogues give **Rock, Pop Rock**; music journalism describes the continuing electronic lean of the 1984–86 period. `poprock` is the album's band-pop framework, `electronic` its distinctive programmed texture, `italianpop` is contextual Italian-language taxonomy, `pop` an original record classification. Do not infer an uncredited synth model or claim the whole LP is dance music.
- **Instruments:** original LP notes (Passione Musica Italia, derived from liner notes) name Mauro Paoluzzi on keyboards, electric guitar, drums and percussion; Franco Cristaldi and Giovanni Tommaso on bass (Giovanni specifically on Tic-tac blues); Demo Morselli trumpet and Amedeo Bianchi sax. The order weights keyboards and guitars first due to electronic shift and central arrangements.
- **Production:** programming and **drum programming** attested by Musicbase release credits. Instrument-specific electronic model remains unidentified; no Fairlight, DX7, TR-808 or LinnDrum claimed. Producers: Gerry Manzoli (also spelt Gerri/Gerardo) and Nada; Paoluzzi is an arranger/player/mixer, not the named producer.
- **Lyrics, scales, voice:** love and heartbreak obvious across Schiava d’amore, Baci rossi and Amo te; sensuality and inward unease partly derived from those titles and reviewed lyric characterization. The raspy/powerful voice is persistent in contemporary descriptions; raw electronics plus expressive lead justify an intermediate experimental value. Energy/mood/etc are estimates from available contemporary framing, to verify by expert listening.

## L’anime nere (1992) — `nada-lanimenere`
- **Edition:** original RCA/BMG PL 75283, **nine** tracks; 1992 is supported by original catalogue, multiple specialist references and 1992 recording copyright. Do not assign the 1994 compilation *Malanima* to this album.
- **Genres:** sourced pop melodies with nostalgic rock, a restrained acoustic-leaning review, all tracks co-authored by Nada and songwriting partners. `poprock`, `canzonedautore`, and `ballad` are *explicitly editorial classifications*, not exact quotations from the sleeve. Spanish-language title *Adios amor* does not justify `es` language; track is from an Italian-language studio album, and no original Spanish-language vocal has been independently established.
- **Production dispute:** **Hit Parade Italia** transcribes producer credit as **Nada & Gerri Manzoli**, whereas the Wikipedia album entry names **Varo Venturi & Gerardo Manzoli**. Venturi/Hipnos is also a writer on the album. Without access to a readable facsimile of the original liner, retain the specialist discographer's Nada/Manzoli credit provisionally at `confidence=low`; **Claude should verify the actual RCA booklet before marking reviewed**. Do not merge contradictory sources into a four-person producer credit.
- **Instruments left empty intentionally:** dedicated search found no verifiable album-specific named-instrument credit. A critic describes *sonorità acustica* and a rock atmosphere, but that does **not** establish piano vs acoustic guitar vs strings or identify individual players. Fandome instructions prioritise leaving fields blank to avoid hallucinating; additional booklet scan is needed. `production` is also blank for lack of technical evidence.
- **Voice/lyrics:** close rasp and introspective, melancholy material described in the contemporary era; numerical `electronic`, `polish` and `groove` are intentionally left unknown.

## Dove sei sei (1999) — `nada-doveseisei`
- **Edition/credits:** Mercury 538 819-2, **11** tracks. Producer **Mauro Pagani** (artistic production); Marcello Villella = executive production, Gerry Manzoli = artistic coordination/pre-production, NOT additional producer fields. A commonly repeated claim that **every song** was written by Nada is **not strictly accurate**: *Piccoli fiumi* (track 10) is written by **Gianmaria Testa**; Nada wrote ten other tracks.
- **Genres:** *la Repubblica* reviewed this record in May 1999 as broad rock atmosphere with ironic Lou Reed-like songs and three specifically named pop ballads. A later *SENTIREASCOLTARE* critic explicitly dates her alternative-rock songwriting phase from this LP. `Lou Reed` is in `sounds_like`, **not** an influence (journalist's comparison, not Nada's personal statement). The album is landmark as the new phase and first largely self-written original LP.
- **Instruments and production:** Hit Parade Italia supplies full per-track original credits: Pagani acoustic/electric/MIDI guitar, violin, keyboards, percussion, flute, celesta, Hammond organ, Rhodes electric piano, mandolin, harpsichord; Flavio Zanol bass; Marco Pancaldi guitar; Roberto Martinelli clarinet on *Piccoli fiumi*. Max Costa credited with programming except track 10, Pagani string arrangement on tracks 1 and 11. `stringarrangements` and `programming` only, no guessed effects or model numbers. Percussion not necessarily a full drum-kit credit, so `drums` not included.
- **Language:** Italian; English word *Glu glu* does not imply another sung language.

## L’amore è fortissimo e il corpo no (2001) — `nada-lamoreefortissimoeilcorpono`
- **Edition:** Original Storie di Note SDN016 studio CD, **nine** new songs, all written by Nada. Strongest producer source is original album-credit transcription on Hit Parade Italia: artistic production **Pasquale Minieri, Fausto Mesolella and Nada**; Gerry Manzoli handles **executive** production and is excluded as producer. Short promotional biographies mention just Minieri/Mesolella, but this is compatible with the longer three-person liner credit.
- **Genres:** Musicbase release styles **Alternative Rock / Indie Pop / Ballad**; original-history summary describes electronic, punk, folk dance, rock and pop pieces. Therefore no all-album punk/electronic claim. `punk` is a **tail genre for some tracks**, based on an album-history account explicitly naming punk among its mixed styles, not a claim that the full album is punk. No `jazz` blanket genre inferred merely from the presence of jazz session players.
- **Instruments:** Rita Marcotulli piano/keyboards; Mesolella guitars (type unconfirmed: use `guitar`, not acoustic/electric); Marco Siniscalchi bass, Ferruccio Spinetti doublebass, Michele Rabbia drums/percussion, Javier Girotto sax on *In generale*. Claudio Coccoluto supplied groove parts on *Gesù* and the title song; neither turntables nor a specific drum machine is credited. Maurizio Martusciello performed sound/electronic manipulations, and Midiware Roma did electronic software work. **No specific plugin/hardware** named. `programming` is an interpretation of credited digital/electronic elaboration; not an attribution of a specific machine.
- **Voice and themes:** distinct, emotional raspy/powerful delivery; love enduring through years, spiritual imagery (*Gesù*), personal self-reflection. November 2001 review in Gay.it hears a comparison with Loredana Bertè, including her *Un pettirosso da combattimento* album. It belongs in `sounds_like`, never in `influenced_by`. Non-musical images of the G8 in the music video **do not** establish album-wide political lyrics.
