# Lady Gaga — Phase 03 (strict increment, four original album releases)

## Method / release boundary

- As of **2026-10-24**. This batch contains only gaga-dawnofchromatica, gaga-loveforsale, gaga-harlequin, gaga-jokerfolieadeuxmusicfromthemotionpicture; no prior Gaga rows and no other artists' rows. Fandome artist id: `gaga` (not `g`).
- Branch `descriptors` inspected: `docs/descriptors/gaga.csv` already contains eight preceding rows: `g-fame`, `gaga-thefamemonster`, `g-btw`, `gaga-artpop`, `gaga-cheektocheek`, `gaga-joanne`, `gaga-astarisborn`, `g-chromatica`. None of these four albums has a descriptor yet. Existing `g-mayhem` is a seed catalogue entry reserved for Phase 04.
- Genres are only traceable labels from named source(s). See *genre_evidence.csv* for album vs substantial vs subset vs weak taxonomic context. The sequence prioritises album salience rather than frequency of genre mentions. Weak **track context** genres should not be presented as if they define the entire album. New tokens need TypeScript schema + genre-family updates **before** validation.
- Scores (0–5) are editorial descriptions of the *whole original album edition*, not laboratory metrics. A number requires this album's reviews, credits or musician testimony. Unknown stays empty. Notes are newly authored and at most 15 words. `status=draft`.
- The `producers` column is especially strict: production, not executive production, remix credits, composers or mixers. Vocalists and collaborators do not automatically become producers.

## 1. Dawn of Chromatica (2021), `gaga-dawnofchromatica`

**Release / relevance.** Released September 3, 2021, full remix record of 2020 *Chromatica*, curated by BloodPop with a notably varied cast (Pitchfork, [D_PFNEWS]; original credited remixes in [D_QOB]). A documented collaborative project worthy of an individual album description, rather than overwriting `g-chromatica`.

**Genre precision.** Dance pop, hyperpop, bubblegum bass, deconstructed club, house, electronic dance music, electro house, electropop and diva house are explicitly named in Album of the Year's taxonomy [D_AOTY]. Pitchfork describes digital maximalism: Charli XCX/A. G. Cook `911` hyperpop, Arca `Rain on Me` avant-pop, Planningtorock `1000 Doves` new wave, Doss `Enigma` floaty dance, and riffs/heavier metal in Rina Sawayama/Clarence Clarity and Dorian Electra interpretations [D_PF]. Slant identifies `Plastic Doll` as **trap-adjacent**, but that is insufficient to classify the track or album as trap [D_SL]. The genre taxonomy's niche labels have moderate confidence due to sparse song-specific evidence.

**Eight anchored scales.** energy=4 (strenuous, noisy remixes, with relaxed Doss and other exceptions); electronic=5 (electronic programming and reconstructed audio dominate nearly every track); mood=3 (bright and defiant rewrites of themes of trauma and vulnerability); theatrical=4 (big dramatic edits, extreme dynamics, vocal features); experimental=4 (Arca structural transformations and hyperpop deconstructions); polish=3 (some deliberately harsh, overloaded or dissonant sound against sleek remixes); tempo=4 (generally energetic, but tracks vary); groove=4 (frequent club pulses, yet disruptive sections and experimental remixes cannot be called continuous dancefloor house). Evidence: [D_PF,D_SL,D_AM].

**Other fields.** `voice=powerful|clear|raspy|soft|rap` recognises Gaga's varied leads plus Ashnikko's rap-inflected guest turn [D_PF,D_SL]; *not* a claim every voice type dominates. Themes remain those of the original underlying songs, reframed: introspection, identity, heartbreak, love, social alienation and sensuality [D_PF]. English main vocals [D_QOB]. `sounds_like` are **editorial** affinities to SOPHIE, 100 gecs, The Knife and PC Music's maximalist strand, not a claim of documented musical influence. `influenced_by` is intentionally empty: remixer participation is collaboration, not proof Gaga drew direct inspiration from them for *this* project. `landmark=0`: culturally notable remix experiment, not treated as one of the core Gaga artist-defining albums.

**Production attribution caution (important).** [D_AOTY] labels LSDXOXO, Arca, A. G. Cook, Mura Masa and even Charli XCX as `Producer`, while the label's [D_QOB] assigns remixer/programmer/vocalist roles and does *not* identify each as album track producer. Because this contradicts the user's strict producer-role rule, `producers` is **left EMPTY** until individual original liner/label producer credits resolve who actually produced the remixes. BloodPop is documented as *executive* producer, not automatically a credited track producer [D_PFNEWS]. This abstention is intentional, not an omission of artists. The remixer roster is preserved in source and evidence files.

**Critical dispute.** Pitchfork [D_PF] sees inventive, coherent widening of a pop project; Slant [D_SL] argues many treatments are tuneless and cluttered. The higher experimentation score does not assert higher aesthetic quality.

## 2. Love for Sale (2021), `gaga-loveforsale`

**Release / original issue.** October 1 2021: Gaga and Tony Bennett recorded a **12-track** Cole Porter songbook duet album (not the deluxe variant) [L_APPLE,L_AM]. Independent collaboration and latter-day high-profile jazz moment.

**Genres.** Jazz, vocal jazz, swing, traditional pop, standards repertoire and big-band sound are supported by Apple Music, qualified reviews and producer's track personnel [L_APPLE,L_GU1,L_QOB]. The Observer explicitly calls this easy listening [L_GU2]; the token refers to *listening context* more than a new compositional idiom.

**Eight scales.** energy=2 (restrained standards with upbeat tracks); electronic=0 (traditional acoustic vocal jazz bands/orchestral accompaniment); mood=4 (mostly affectionate, witty, warm despite melancholy); theatrical=3 (expressive singing but rarely maximalist performance); experimental=1 (conventional Cole Porter arrangements); polish=4 (careful clean studio arrangements); tempo=3 (swung mid-tempo songs, some slower ballads); groove=2 (some danceable swing, largely listening oriented). [L_AM,L_GU1,L_GU2]

**Voices / themes.** Gaga and Bennett alternate clear, resonant/powerful and soft intimate phrasing. Lyrics of Cole Porter originals revolve around romance, lost love, double entendre and wit; coded `love|heartbreak|irony|sensuality` [L_APPLE,L_GU1]. Sung in English [L_APPLE]. `producers=Dae Bennett` from original-track producer credits on Qobuz and AllMusic [L_QOB,L_QOB2,L_DAE]. Not the arranger/conductor Marion Evans/Jorge Calandrelli and not Cole Porter, who composed the repertoire. `influenced_by` blank: honoring Cole Porter through repertoire is separate from documenting Gaga's personal artistic influences on the *sound of this album*. `sounds_like`: editorial stylistic comparison to Ella Fitzgerald, Frank Sinatra, Diana Krall, Michael Bublé in a vocal-jazz repertoire sense. `landmark=1` for the second and final Tony Bennett/Gaga studio collaboration, a significant phase of Gaga's jazz work.

**Critical tension.** Petridis [L_GU1] welcomes relatively unforced jazz performances while recognizing that such a project cannot supplant classic Ella Fitzgerald songbook recordings; Cragg [L_GU2] reads it as polished and warm, not ground-breaking. Distinguish respect for interpretation from novelty.

## 3. Harlequin (2024), `gaga-harlequin`

**Release / identity.** Gaga's original **September 27** 13-song studio *companion* record to *Joker: Folie à Deux*, including two original compositions, not the October 4 film soundtrack or Hildur Guðnadóttir's score [H_OFF,H_APPLE,H_GU]. Label and credited personnel identify `Lady Gaga|Benjamin Rice|BloodPop` as producers [H_OFF,H_QOB]; BloodPop is not necessarily a producer of every track. Michael Polansky has songwriting and co-arrangement credits but is **not** entered solely on that basis.

**Genres.** Jazz and traditional/vocal pop standards plus big-band swing and stage show tunes are primary. The record's *intentionally hybrid* palette includes surf-rock on `World on a String` [H_APPLE]; Gaga explicitly names surf punk, funk, soul, rock and jazz [H_CAP]; soul on `Gonna Build a Mountain` [H_SL]; gospel influence and New Orleans jazz arrangements [H_AM]. `Happy Mistake`'s acoustic guitar does not establish *folk* as a named genre, so that token is omitted. `showtunes` is a **new Fandome token** for theatrical song repertoire, not `soundtrack` because this is an independent companion studio album.

**Eight scales.** energy=3 (alternating brassy high-energy band numbers and piano ballads); electronic=1 (live acoustic big-band and guitars predominate, with some electronic texture on Happy Mistake); mood=3 (campy joyful standards meet ominous/fragile song reframings); theatrical=5 (performative character and dramatic voice at every turn); experimental=3 (old standards deliberately altered with surf/punk/rock, but familiar structures remain); polish=4 (professional crisp large-ensemble studio work); tempo=3 (slow waltzes and ballads interleaved with brisk songs); groove=3 (rhythm-heavy jazz and rock subset, not continuous dance-floor music). [H_APPLE,H_AM,H_GU,H_SL]

**Voices / topics.** Powerful belts, rasp, clear jazz croon, occasional tender delivery and choral/ensemble sections are specific to this record [H_APPLE,H_QOB,H_AM]. Songbook lyrics and character-written original material are coded love, heartbreak, identity, storytelling, irony, introspection. *Happy Mistake* explicitly explores the spectacle of feminine suffering [H_APPLE]. English lead recordings. `sounds_like`: editorial kinship to Judy Garland, The Cramps, Bette Midler and Amy Winehouse based on theatre/surf/jazz timbres; **the artist explicitly invokes The Cramps while describing `World on a String`**, so only The Cramps enters `influenced_by`, a *track-limited, qualified* artistic touchstone, not full-album dependence [H_APPLE]. `landmark=0` for an inventive companion project, not a central mainstream career statement.

**Critics disagree.** AllMusic [H_AM] is highly enthusiastic about free and playful transformation. Guardian [H_GU] regards much of the big-band work as unsurprising or talent-show-ish. Slant [H_SL] praises some vocal performances but dislikes portions of the interpretation. This dispute informs medium experimental rather than a blanket 'masterpiece' framing.

## 4. Joker: Folie à Deux (Music From the Motion Picture) (2024), `gaga-jokerfolieadeuxmusicfromthemotionpicture`

**Release / edition.** Official *October 4 2024* **16-song cast soundtrack**, credited to Joaquin Phoenix, Lady Gaga and cast; qualifying Gaga co-lead soundtrack [J_LABEL,J_APPLE,J_MUSIC]. The ID is the complete Fandome `gaga` + punctuation-stripped **official album title**. This soundtrack must remain independent of Gaga's *Harlequin*, which re-records overlapping repertoire. Also **exclude the Hildur Guðnadóttir instrumental score album** from this row [J_MUSIC_CREW].

**Genres.** Film soundtrack and traditional vocal pop are central. Film and theatre **show tunes** are prominent [J_MUSIC]. Standards have jazz/crooner colour; Bee Gees, Carpenters and Daniel Johnston songs add pop/soft-rock/folk source repertoire, but these are heavily altered *on-screen* performances and do not make the whole soundtrack a conventional rock or folk record [J_APPLE,J_QOB,J_STEREO]. Those songs have origins in different styles, but `softrock`, `folk` and `soul` are deliberately **omitted**: the sources do not categorise the particular soundtrack performances that way.

**Eight scales.** energy=2 (mostly conversational/slow, some brassy sequences); electronic=0 (piano-led live sung performances and acoustic orchestral expansion; electronics not central); mood=1 (largely uneasy or doomed, even when standards originated as joyful songs); theatrical=4 (fantasy-musical framing and character acting, less grandly sung than Harlequin); experimental=2 (unusual live-on-set recording arrangements for familiar songs); polish=3 (sound finishing is cinematic, but intentionally vulnerable/imperfect takes remain); tempo=2 (slow and unstably phrased most of the time); groove=1 (rare persistent dance beat). [J_STEREO,J_QOB,J_SOUND,J_MIX,J_APPLE]

**Voice / themes.** Gaga and Phoenix deliberately unlearned conventional polished vocal techniques and sang live to accompanists who *followed their tempo* rather than a click [J_PHOENIX,J_SOUND,J_MIX]. Multiple subdued/raspy/clear/spoken/occasional strongly projected voices; emotional dialogue motivates `storytelling|love|heartbreak|identity|irony`. Language **en**: Jacques Brel's If You Go Away uses the English adaptation; no evidence the film soundtrack sings in French [J_APPLE,J_QOB]. `sounds_like` is editorial comparative *vocal/cinematic aesthetic*, not claims of actual original composition influences: Judy Garland, Frank Sinatra, Nick Cave and Daniel Johnston. `influenced_by` blank: covering songs doesn't automatically certify an influence chain. `landmark=0` for a co-star cast soundtrack, not a defining Gaga studio album.

**Producer audit.** [J_LABEL] directly credits Lady Gaga, Joaquin Phoenix and Jason Ruder as soundtrack producers (Todd Phillips and music supervisors also appear as **executive producers**, excluded). [J_QOB] credits Stewart Lerman as producer for `To Love Somebody`, so he is included as an album-contributing producer, not just arranger. Qobuz inconsistently tags some executives as producers too; individual film-credit list [J_MUSIC_CREW] distinguishes orchestra arrangers, mix engineers, and the Hildur score. Never add Hildur as producer of this *song soundtrack* based on score credits. A modest confidence reduction accounts for cast-wide scoring and mixed credit granularity.

**Separate documentary evidence.** Mix engineers describe live vocal recording, piecing takes together and building accompaniment to un-clicked performances [J_SOUND,J_MIX]. This is direct evidence of sound-source and polish choices, not a guess from the movie genre. Stereogum [J_STEREO] specifically calls the soundtrack's duplicate songs darker than Harlequin performances.
