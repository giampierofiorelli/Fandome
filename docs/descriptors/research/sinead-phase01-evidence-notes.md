# Sinéad O’Connor — Phase 01 evidence notes

Scope: original studio LPs only, 1987–1994. No compilations, bonus tracks, subsequent alternate mixes or songs on later reissues are used to support instrumentation/production. Token and scale decisions below apply **only** to the original album. All eight numeric scales use Fandome's 0–5 whole-number anchors. `influenced_by` is intentionally empty in all four rows: named covers, guest musicians and sound-alikes do not establish formative influences for the particular album. The artist-wide documented influences appear in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`.

## 1. The Lion and the Cobra (1987) — `sinead-thelionandthecobra`

**Core credits and evidence:** Sinéad O’Connor produced; Kevin Moloney is the credited **co-producer** (not merely engineer). The original issue's MusicBrainz relationship credits list both guitar types, bass, live drums, drum machines, keyboard and synth. Mike Clowes arranged strings for **Troy**; the strings are track-limited, not a claim that the entire record is orchestral. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release/a8a8a43a-392e-3939-b7b5-854623da426c, https://chrysalis-records.com/release/437615-sinad-oconnor-the-lion-and-the-cobra.

**Instruments:** electricguitar|acousticguitar|bass|drums|drummachine|keyboards|synth|strings. `drummachine` and `drumprogramming`: original sleeve credits associate John Reynolds with machines/programming, rather than inferred 1980s studio technology. `stringarrangements` is explicit for Troy. `overdubbing` and `heavyreverb` are evidenced by Pitchfork's analysis of doubled, reverberant vocals; the technique is a documented song texture, not album-wide uniformity. Source: https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/sinead-oconnor-the-lion-and-the-cobra/.

**Scales:** energy **4** (volatile electric-guitar songs alternating with quiet cuts); electronic **2** (drum machines/synthesizer alongside the guitar band); mood **1** (persistent anger, sorrow and menace); theatrical **4** (the build in Troy and immense vocal dynamic); experimental **3** (hip-hop/new-wave/rhythm textures alongside ballads); polish **3** (studio layering but not pristine commercial gloss); tempo **3** (mid-range variety including brisk Mandinka); groove **2** (I Want Your is strongly rhythmic, but much material is not dance-facing). Textural variation documented by Chrysalis, Pitchfork and MusicBrainz; numbers are an editorial synthesis rather than source-reported measurements.

**Genres and disagreements:** AllMusic assigns album-level adult/alternative/college styles. Pitchfork describes experimental pop and new-wave **on Mandinka**; Chrysalis highlights hip-hop **on I Want Your (Hands on Me)**. None of these track-specific styles replaces the central alternative rock identity. Similarities to Kate Bush/Peter Gabriel/Enya arise in Pitchfork's comparative critical framing and therefore appear only under `sounds_like`. The presence of Enya's guest recitation does not put her in `influenced_by`. No model-number synthesizer claims.

## 2. I Do Not Want What I Haven’t Got (1990) — `sinead-idonotwantwhatihaventgot`

**Credits:** The label copy of the expanded edition **explicitly separates** the original ten-song album from unrelated bonus tracks. Its exact wording: Sinéad O’Connor produces the album; *Nothing Compares 2 U* is produced by Sinéad with **Nellee Hooper**. Chris Birkett engineered, and Seán Devitt engineered Three Babies; neither engineer is promoted to producer from ambiguous retailer aggregation. Nellee Hooper is not credited as producer of the entire ten songs. Source: https://sinead-oconnor.com/home/index.php/news/276-re-release-of-qi-do-not-wantq-special-edition. Alternative all-credits aggregators conflate original and expanded-release credits, so the track-specific label copy takes precedence.

**Instruments:** acoustic and electric guitar, bass, drum kit, piano, keyboards, percussion, fiddle/violin, strings. Original notes identify Andy Rourke, Jah Wobble, John Reynolds, Dave Munday, Marco Pirroni, Sinéad and Steve Wickham. `sampling` = James Brown *Funky Drummer* recording used in I Am Stretched on Your Grave, corroborated by AllMusic (this is **not** treated as a James Brown creative influence). `drumprogramming` from Sinéad credit on Jump in the River; `stringarrangements` from Nick Ingman's explicitly credited string work on Feel So Different and Three Babies.

**Scales:** energy **3** (driving Emperor's New Clothes amid spare laments); electronic **1** (sample/drum programming are notable but isolated); mood **1** (introspective, bereaved and angry); theatrical **3** (big dramatic peaks but many minimal arrangements); experimental **3** (unexpected beat/folk fusion and long unaccompanied vocal); polish **4** (well-defined studio/orchestral arrangements); tempo **2** (frequent slow and mid-tempo material); groove **1** (only a minority track has a notable beat). Judgments follow AllMusic and the original credit booklet.

**Genres and disagreement:** AllMusic characterises rock, protest folk, orchestral ballads and one hip-hop-sampled piece. Classify `hiphop` as **some tracks** only. The title track is unaccompanied; don't infer acoustic guitar. The cover of Prince's Nothing Compares 2 U **is not evidence** of Prince shaping the album musically. `sounds_like` is an editorial likeness to singer-songwriters, not a statement of borrowed production methods.

## 3. Am I Not Your Girl? (1992) — `sinead-aminotyourgirl`

**Status and producers:** **Studio** album of newly recorded cover versions, not `compilation` and not an all-swing album. 1992 contemporary Los Angeles Times interview explicitly rebuts the common 'swing album' oversimplification. Original release credits consistently list **Phil Ramone and Sinéad O’Connor** as producers. Torrie Zito, Rob Mounsey, Patrick Williams, Doug Katsaros and Sid Ramin are **arrangers/conductors, not producers**. Sources: https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-08-02-ca-5420-story.html, https://musicbrainz.org/release/43f03d0a-06ae-3605-83ec-ff712ee5ee7d, https://www.qobuz.com/se-en/album/am-i-not-your-girl-sinead-oconnor/a0pdmz2v0bdhc.

**Instruments and production:** MusicBrainz and AllMusic list orchestra, strings, brass, woodwinds, sax, trumpet, trombone, piano, bass, drums, flute, clarinet, cello, violin, harp, guitar and synth. Uilleann pipes and tin whistle are specifically listed for **Scarlet Ribbons**. `hornarrangements` and `stringarrangements` reflect the credited orchestral score/big-band arrangements; no vintage equipment or reverb settings asserted. Source: https://musicbrainz.org/release/43f03d0a-06ae-3605-83ec-ff712ee5ee7d.

**Scales:** energy **2** (mainly measured standards, a few more energetic big-band moments); electronic **0** (acoustic orchestra overwhelmingly determines the sound, despite a credit for synth); mood **2** (love/heartbreak and sadness); theatrical **4** (dramatic orchestral vocal arrangements); experimental **1** (established songbook structures, although unusual relative to her rock discography); polish **5** (large, meticulously arranged studio orchestra); tempo **2** (predominantly measured ballads); groove **2** (some swinging and rhythmic tracks, no sustained dance focus). Sources: Chrysalis, Los Angeles Times, MusicBrainz, AllMusic.

**Genres and disagreements:** Big band/vocal standards are record-wide; Album of the Year explicitly tags Big Band, Vocal Jazz, Standards, Adult Contemporary and Traditional Pop, https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/9330-sinead-oconnor-am-i-not-your-girl.php. The contemporary Harvard Crimson review provides specific jazz evidence, while Qobuz provides broad Pop metadata. `musicaltheatre` applies to **Don't Cry for Me Argentina**, not all tracks. `easylistening` is specifically listed in Musicbase metadata but is lower-strength discography tagging; all other canonical tags come from an album-specific source. **Do not tag `swing` as the album's defining style**: contemporaneous report denies that classification. Critic Erlewine finds the arrangements occasionally bury the voice, which contrasts with the official release's approving language. All tracks being covers does not establish the covered performers as `influenced_by`. `sounds_like` vocal analogies are editorial only. Some original releases add a spoken 'personal message'; `spoken` is a minority delivery, not the principal voice.

## 4. Universal Mother (1994) — `sinead-universalmother`

**Credits and texture:** Original-sleeve transcriptions identify **Sinéad O’Connor**, **John Reynolds**, **Phil Coulter**, **Tim Simenon** as credited producers across different tracks. Simenon co-produced Famine / Thank You for Hearing Me and contributes programming. Phil Coulter co-produced several piano/strings songs. In contrast, All Apologies is produced by O’Connor alone. Do **not** credit Nirvana/Kurt Cobain as producers or influences merely because she covers the song. Sources: https://www.thankyouforhearingme.com/songs/famine.html, https://thankyouforhearingme.com/songs/thank_you_for_hearing_me.html, https://www.thankyouforhearingme.com/songs/my_darling_child.html, https://www.thankyouforhearingme.com/songs/all_apologies.html.

**Instruments:** piano (Coulter), keyboards (Dave Clayton, Reynolds), bass (Claire Kenny/Nicky Scott), drums, guitar (Pirroni/Ivan Gilliland), Irish Chamber Orchestra strings and John O'Kane cello. `programming` is explicit in Famine and Thank You for Hearing Me, not inferred from the word electronic. Album-wide machine models, microphones, software, sampling and tape effects are **unverified** and therefore omitted. On The Voice Squad track In This Heart, backing group is documented, but `choral` is omitted from `voice` because Sinéad's lead voice remains central.

**Scales:** energy **2** (quiet piano and vocal statements dominate with hard-edged rhythmic exceptions); electronic **2** (programmed songs amid acoustic piano/string songs); mood **1** (motherhood, grief and childhood violence); theatrical **2** (mostly close and spare despite episodic confrontation); experimental **3** (spoken political history, programmed pop, quasi-rap and stark ballads coexist); polish **3** (varied and often intentionally austere studio finish); tempo **2** (mostly slow to medium); groove **1** (only few tracks foreground rhythm). Label account and contemporary 1994 review support the contrast.

**Genres and disagreements:** AllMusic's album-wide alternative style tags are supplemented by a secondary genre aggregator's `folkrock|songwriter|artpop|triphop`: mark those lower-confidence and interpret trip-hop as a texture on some tracks. AllMusic biography specifically notes electronic-based meditations. The contemporary Rip It Up review hears **mostly** piano, violin and horns, but regards Fire on Babylon as an electronic exception. AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine finds the album inconsistent, while the 1994 reviewer responds to its emotional unity. Famine's lyric briefly quotes Beatles lyrics; this does **not** qualify Beatles as `influenced_by`, nor does it document audio sampling. Influences remain blank, reflecting insufficient album-specific declaration.

## Cross-album cautions

- The 1990 reissue contains bonus material not in the original album; do not import these into 1990 descriptors.
- Original 1992 release is an album of new interpretations, **not** a compilation.
- The 1994 opener includes spoken contributions from Germaine Greer and a child appearance by Jake Reynolds; neither makes the whole LP a co-billed album.
- `sounds_like` is a disclosed editorial analogy and must never be interpreted as verified personal influences.
- Scale values are evidence-informed judgments, not externally published statistics. Evidence strength for broad 'genre' labels varies by outlet; per-row confidence is intentionally reduced where the evidence is mixed.
