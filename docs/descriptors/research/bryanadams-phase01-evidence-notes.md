# Bryan Adams — Phase 01 evidence and editorial decisions

Research date: 2026-10-28. One row per ORIGINAL studio album; only standard-track editions considered. All 0–5 scale ratings, `sounds_like` and short descriptions are independent editorial judgments; source evidence establishes the factual foundation, not an objective score.

## Bryan Adams (1980) — `bryanadams-bryanadams`

- **Release and producers:** Bryan Adams's official discography and first-person account by co-producer Jim Vallance confirm 1980, production by Vallance and Adams. Vallance dates much recording at Manta (Toronto) to October–November 1979 and later work at Pinewood and Sunset Sound. The original recording, not any rerelease bonus material, is the unit of description.
- **Genres:** AOTY indexes AOR, Pop Rock and Power Pop; the original album is also categorised Rock. These are overlapping but separately used source labels. Vallance calls it an uneven mixture of styles, which limits confidence in fine subdivisions.
- **Instruments:** Adams (guitar, piano), Vallance (drums, guitar, bass, keyboards) and credited guests including Jeff Baxter (guitar), David Hungate/Jim Clench (bass), Gene Meros (sax) are documented in A&M label and Sessiondays credit transcriptions. Instrument ordering follows the weight of their roles, not every guest track. No electronic instrument/brand is asserted.
- **Production:** Blank, because documented studio routing and a Neve console photograph do not by themselves establish an allowed recording-technique token for the whole released album. Do not transfer equipment from Vallance's demos to album masters. `electronic=1` is a cautious editorial judgment, not equipment evidence.
- **Voice, lyrics, scales:** Raspy baritone/tenor lead with clear and emphatic phrases; largely love, relational frustration and reflection. Moderate energy, low experimentation, comparatively modest polish relative to later Clearmountain work. `sounds_like` are editorial parallels, not documented influences.
- **Disagreement:** Vallance describes a weak/inconsistent record, whereas modern genre indexes classify it as power pop/AOR. These are perspectives on different aspects, not competing original credits.

## You Want It You Got It (1981) — `bryanadams-youwantityougotit`

- **Release / producers:** 1981 original, produced by Bryan Adams and Bob Clearmountain as credited on A&M pressings. Album title is styled *You Want It You Got It* on Adams's own site; some original labels and platforms insert a comma. Stored title follows the artist site.
- **Genres:** Pop Rock and AOR are whole-album AOTY labels; Rock and Hard Rock occur as release classifications. Qobuz/TiVo hears a *new-wave flavour* specifically in the title cut, so `newwave` comes LAST, scoped to some tracks. The same review explicitly calls 'Coming Home' and 'No One Makes It Right' power ballads; this is not a claim that the album is principally a ballad album.
- **Instruments:** Artist/label credits name Adams on electric guitar, an unspecified 12-string guitar, and piano, Tommy Mandel on keyboards, Brian Stanley bass, Mickey Curry drums, and guests on guitar, sax and percussion (Jamie Glaser, G. E. Smith, Jonathan Gerber, Jimmy Maelen). No synthesizer model is evidenced.
- **Production:** Source supports Clearmountain's radio-friendly clarity and Power Station/Le Studio recording but no exact token-specific processing or named machines, so blank.
- **Scales / vocals:** Faster, more consistent four-square groove than debut; instrumentally rooted in amplified rock. `raspy|powerful|clear` describes the lead's dominant vocal modes; the only guest vocal credit (Cindy Bullens) is documented, not a distinct lead voice. Romantic songs alternate agitation and warmth.
- **Editorial similarity:** Bruce Springsteen is specifically identified as a sonic comparison by the Qobuz/TiVo review; the other comparisons are editorial suggestions. No influence is inferred from resemblance.

## Cuts Like a Knife (1983) — `bryanadams-cutslikeaknife`

- **Release / producers:** Bryan Adams and Bob Clearmountain are credited producers on original 1983 album. Jim Vallance is a major songwriter and percussion contributor, not automatically a producer. Later bonus-track 'Christmas Time' on the official website is NOT part of this base row.
- **Genres:** AOTY indexes Pop Rock, AOR and Hard Rock. Apple describes stripped-back heartland rock across the album and a power-pop-tinged **title-track chorus** only. The genre evidence file intentionally marks that narrow scope. Broad Rock also supported by Apple Music record category.
- **Instruments:** Guitar, bass, drums, keyboards, piano and Jim Vallance's percussion from A&M pressing; MusicBrainz first-track credits show Tommy Mandel on Hammond organ AND synthesizer. Specific electronic instruments are supported; unidentified keyboard models are not.
- **Production:** Blank as neither instrument credit nor general studio producer attribution establishes a confirmed Fandome production token. Original mixing engineering by Clearmountain is recorded in sources but has no direct vocabulary item.
- **Lyrics and dynamics:** 'Straight from the Heart' and 'Cuts Like a Knife' motivate romance/heartbreak alongside ambition; 'The Best Was Yet to Come' supports memory/nostalgia (editorial, not author testimony). Distinguish bright choruses from wounded lyric subjects: middle mood rating.
- **Conflicting reactions:** AOTY relays contemporary Rolling Stone criticism of repetitiveness, while Apple describes the accessibility and heartland-rock character; source disagreements affect value judgments, not traceable personnel or styles.

## Reckless (1984) — `bryanadams-reckless`

- **Release / production credits:** Original 10-song 1984 release. Bryan Adams and Bob Clearmountain: original official-site producers. A&M's secondary credits database lists Jim Vallance as 'co-producer'; MusicBrainz detailed original-track annotations distinguish *associate producer*. Therefore Jim Vallance stays OUT of the producers column, pending reconciliation against photographs of original sleeve. His songwriting and percussion are separate.
- **Genres:** AOTY indexes Pop Rock, AOR, Heartland Rock, Hard Rock. MusicBrainz tags Arena Rock and album metadata recognises broad Rock. Overlapping labels represent commercial scale, writing style and amplified sonic weight.
- **Instruments:** Adams and Keith Scott guitars, Dave Taylor bass, Mickey Curry and occasional other credited drummers, Tommy Mandel/Rob Sabino keyboards, Adams piano, Vallance percussion, and handclaps on specified tracks. Tina Turner supplies a featured vocal on 'It's Only Love'; no uncredited female-singing category is invented. **Do not map A&M 'harp' credit to harmonica or orchestral harp without disambiguation.**
- **Production:** Adams recalls cutting/editing multiple drum performances and a one-take core for *Run to You*. His account of archive master-mix tapes, plus contemporary sessions, justifies `analogtape` and **track-scoped** `onetake`; Classic Rock interviews explicitly document extra vocal overdubs at Power Station. `onetake` applies to the song, NOT the whole album. Vallance photographed an Ampex tape machine and Urei 1176 at HIS studio in 1984; this is not sufficient to label the released record `ua1176` or a specific console.
- **Lyrics, mood, scale:** Big-room melodic anthems, unambiguous romantic material (*Heaven*, *Run to You*) and nostalgic youth in *Summer of '69*. High energy and polish are editorial scale judgments, not claims about mastering or dynamic range.
- **Edition caution:** Modern deluxe editions include tracks recorded earlier or later and alternate versions; exclude them from baseline descriptions. The track-specific MusicBrainz entry may show bonus tracks; evidence for `onetake` and tape applies to the original album only.

## Influences rule

Bryan Adams identifies influences from the Beatles, the Everly Brothers, Buddy Holly and the Crickets, Simon & Garfunkel, Led Zeppelin, Bowie and Elton John in published interviews. These names appear ONLY in the ARTIST INFLUENCES list of `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`, because the sources do not say that they shaped one particular album in this phase. Jim Vallance separately identifies Beatles as his own major influence; this does not justify adding Beatles to album-specific `influenced_by`. All four row values remain empty.

## Validation notes

The source `genre` classification is a recommendation feature rather than mutually exclusive academic taxonomy. No claim is made that generic pop music is the same as pop rock, that all ballads are power ballads, or that cross-album analogies establish causation. All rows remain `draft` for independent review.
