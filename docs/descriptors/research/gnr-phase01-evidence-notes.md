# Guns N’ Roses — Phase 01 evidence notes

**Method:** ratings 0–5 are album-wide editorial assessments guided by Fandome anchors, not measured metrics. Claims about credits, technique, named styles and musical influences are referenced below. All 1991 music/credits refer to the **original studio albums**, never 2022 deluxe live tracks or the later live-orchestra revision of “November Rain”. Song-level texture is not automatically generalized to an entire LP.

## gnr-appetitefordestruction — *Appetite for Destruction* (1987)

**Genres (in listed order):** `hardrock|heavymetal|albumrock` appear as AllMusic’s *album* styles. The GRAMMY retrospective calls attention to raw *punk* energy (texture, not a punk LP). The Jakarta Post explicitly describes Mr. Brownstone as blues-rock; low-priority `bluesrock` is therefore *track-scoped*, not an assertion that the whole album is blues rock. Glam metal is excluded: the GRAMMY feature positions the album **against** much of that scene’s mannered sound despite the contemporary visual context.

**Scales:** energy **5** (sustained speed and attack on Welcome to the Jungle, Nightrain, Paradise City and Rocket Queen); electronic **1** (almost all guitars/bass/drums; original issue credit mentions some Axl synthesizer, so not a fully acoustic recording); mood **2** (mostly menacing and volatile, balanced by romantic passages); theatrical **2** (frontman drama, yet band-scale arrangements); experimental **1** (conventional hard rock structures with a few long shapes); polish **3** (studio-clean but raw-feeling); tempo **4** (many fast tracks, balanced by Sweet Child o’ Mine); groove **3** (Adler’s swinging rock rhythm, not dance-focused).

**Voice / lyric topics:** Axl moves between rasp and explosive high-register singing, with an unusually melodic soft lead on Sweet Child o’ Mine. Lyrics discuss nightlife, sexual appetite, precarious street life, tenderness and personal alienation; these are specific songs, not a claim that all 12 concern the same subject.

**Instruments:** release-level MusicBrainz credits list Slash and Izzy electric guitars, Duff electric bass, Adler acoustic drum kit, Izzy percussion, Slash acoustic guitar and Axl synthesizer. Synth is minor and appears low priority. **Production:** Rose’s 1987 taped interview and Slash/Izzy retrospective explicitly describe rhythm beds tracked live by musicians in one room, later overdubbed guitars and multi-part vocal harmonies. `liverecording` means **in-studio live playing**, not concert recording. `overdubbing|layeredvocals` document later studio layers. Producer Mike Clink only; Barbiero/Thompson mixed, and Niven’s production oversight does not justify listing them as producers.

**Influence:** Slash identifies *Aerosmith — Rocks* as pivotal to his guitar identity. The album-specific `influenced_by` is limited to that verifiable artist-record link. Stones/AC/DC/New York Dolls in `sounds_like` are editorial similarities, not formal causal claims. **Critical tension:** GRAMMY describes a dangerous alternative to glam-metal convention, whereas AllMusic still indexes it in Heavy Metal and Album Rock. These categories coexist rather than forcing a glam-metal tag.

Sources: [GRAMMY](https://live.grammy.com/news/guns-n-roses-appetite-destruction-record), [The Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2018/09/28/album-review-appetite-destruction-gun-n-roses), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/appetite-for-destruction-mw0000192878), [MusicBrainz 1987 issue](https://musicbrainz.org/release/617908f2-7a0b-3aad-ae02-5a5c125fa1a7), [Axl 1987 tape](https://www.thetapesarchive.com/axl-rose/), [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/slash-izzy-stradlin-guns-n-roses-appetite-destruction), [Slash / Kerrang](https://www.kerrang.com/features/slash-guns-n-roses-were-a-gang-that-walked-into-a-room-like-you-dont-want-to-f-ck-with-us/).

## gnr-gnrlies — *G N’ R Lies* (1988)

**Edition/type:** one 8-track commercially issued album, but **not** eight new 1988 songs. Tracks 1–4 reproduce 1986 *Live ?!*@ Like a Suicide*, recorded in a studio and given sourced crowd noise. Tracks 5–8 are newly released stripped-down studio numbers. Rock Hall archive confirms the combination. Classify `studio` within the album chronology, not `live`; do not create a duplicate EP descriptor. The band is credited on the first half; Mike Clink on the 1988 acoustic half. Other archival databases may collapse credit to Mike Clink for the whole LP; this row preserves the distinction, pending sleeve verification.

**Genres:** AllMusic explicitly supplies hard rock, heavy metal and album rock. The heavy styles largely apply to the first half; the back half foregrounds acoustic guitar, not a whole-album metal arrangement. Do not invent acoustic-rock as a named genre solely from acoustic instrumentation.

**Scales:** energy **3** (fast electric EP against quiet, patient acoustic half); electronic **0** (no album electronics verified, although electrified guitars are present); mood **2** (romantic acoustic lead offset by darker and hostile material); theatrical **1** (stripped, informal acoustic sessions despite confrontational loud half); experimental **1** (straightforward songs and a two-part format); polish **2** (deliberately loose, bar-band quality and basic acoustic sessions); tempo **3** (fast and slow halves); groove **2** (rock boogie rather than dance music).

**Instruments:** MusicBrainz original recording and 1988 sessions document electric guitars in the EP half, acoustic guitars, bass, drums and percussion in the newly recorded half. Brass for Move to the City appears in a retrospective; lower confidence because archive credits are uneven. **Production:** `overdubbing|sampling` applies specifically to the bogus live presentation of the 1986 EP material: festival audience sound added to studio cuts. Do not use `concertrecording` or `liverecording` as if the band performed them live. No machine model attested. Mike Clink is producer of the newly recorded side, band is credited as producer on original 1986 material.

**Lyrics and criticism:** Patience is a patient relationship song; Used to Love Her is darkly ironic. One in a Million contains racist and homophobic language that attracted justified criticism: do **not** romanticize this as merely social commentary or endorse it. AllMusic’s harsh reassessment contrasts with some listeners’ affectionate view of the acoustic side. **Influences:** deliberately blank: the Aerosmith / Rose Tattoo covers are covers, not proof those acts inspired the new sessions.

Sources: [Rock Hall archive](https://catalog.rockhall.com/rrhof-ais/Details/fullCatalogue/200004240), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/g-n-r-lies-mw0000244545), [MusicBrainz early tracks](https://musicbrainz.org/release/c71c2f41-4eac-4d1e-a596-40e2b218940b), [MusicBrainz acoustic tracks](https://musicbrainz.org/release/38c8da0f-5063-46e8-97e9-0b43cff19856), [Louder EP recording history](https://www.loudersound.com/features/false-starts-and-a-whole-lotta-cash-how-appetite-for-destruction-was-made).

## gnr-useyourillusioni — *Use Your Illusion I* (1991)

**Genres:** AllMusic lists Hard Rock, Heavy Metal and Album Rock. Ric Albano’s album review explicitly mentions blues, country and progressive-rock elements, weighted below the main three and confined to tracks. `prog` is Fandome’s progressive-rock token. `punk` is only for the punk-influenced Garden of Eden, not an LP-wide style. *November Rain* is a power ballad, but adding a separate powerballad genre was avoided because only one track fits it clearly.

**Scales:** energy **4** (vigorous rockers against several much slower compositions); electronic **2** (still guitar band but synthesizer programming on Live and Let Die and November Rain); mood **2** (loss, fury, vulnerability coexist); theatrical **4** (sweeping piano ballads and 10-minute Coma); experimental **3** (wide structures and genre changes); polish **4** (detailed overdubs and carefully shaped large productions); tempo **3** (fast punkish cuts, midtempo rock, extended ballads); groove **2** (rhythm-driven rock but not primarily dance music).

**Instruments:** Slash/Izzy electric guitars; Duff bass; Matt Sorum drums; Axl and Dizzy piano/keyboards; Axl synthesizer work; acoustic guitars; Michael Monroe harmonica and saxophone on Bad Obsession; brass/horns and organ on documented song credits; six-string bass/dobro by Slash in release personnel. No acoustic-string **orchestra** token: the **1991** November Rain “strings” were synthesized, not the 2022 orchestral rerecording. **Production:** the band tracked live rhythm takes followed by extended overdubs; Axl and Johann Langlie credited for synth programming; Slash credited for talk box (“voice box”) on Dust N’ Bones. `talkbox` here is a guitar effect, not necessarily lead-vocal treatment. Mixing Bill Price is excluded from the producer field.

**Influence:** MusicRadar documents Elton John’s “Funeral for a Friend/Love Lies Bleeding” from *Goodbye Yellow Brick Road* as a specific inspiration to the composition of November Rain; it is a legitimate track-scope influence, so `Elton John (Goodbye Yellow Brick Road)` appears. Covers (Paul McCartney/Wings) and Alice Cooper/Hanoi Rocks vocalist Michael Monroe as **guests** do not alone create influence entries. **Critical disagreements:** modern Pitchfork reads operatic ambition as emotionally serious, whereas Ric Albano prefers I over II; neither view is encoded as a universal fact.

Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/use-your-illusion-i-mw0000598424), [MusicBrainz original release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/1a36c453-c728-4962-833f-be619542eda0), [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2011/07/1991-gnr-illusions/), [uDiscover / Slash and Clink](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/guns-n-roses-use-your-illusion-review/), [MusicRadar / Elton](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/how-axl-rose-took-inspiration-from-elton-john-to-create-a-classic-guns-n-roses-ballad-that-was-years-in-the-making), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/guns-n-roses-use-your-illusion-i-use-your-illusion-ii/).

## gnr-useyourillusionii — *Use Your Illusion II* (1991)

**Genres:** AllMusic again identifies Hard Rock, Heavy Metal, Album Rock. The first three describe the main LP, whereas `funkmetal` describes the Locomotive groove in Tim Peacock’s UMG retrospective. This is a **new genre token** not yet part of Fandome’s scenes.ts, parents or validator. Industrial and electronic describe the **brief closing track My World**, not 75 minutes of industrial rock. `funkmetal|industrial|electronic` intentionally rank last. Do not blindly map funk-metal to funkrock, or industrial to metal.

**Scales:** energy **4** (Get in the Ring / You Could Be Mine vs mid-tempo and ballad sections); electronic **2** (piano/keyboards across album, synth and electronic effect in My World); mood **1** (bitter anger, antiwar critique, loss and introspection predominate); theatrical **4** (Civil War and Estranged dramatic development); experimental **3** (extended songs, string-like devices and industrial finale); polish **4** (large studio sessions and post-tracking overdubs); tempo **3** (broad speed range); groove **2** (hard-rock rhythm and the Locomotive funk-metal groove).

**Instruments:** Axl/Dizzy piano/keys; Slash electric/acoustic guitar and **banjo on Breakdown**; Izzy **Coral sitar on Pretty Tied Up**. That is the dedicated `coralsitar` instrument already supported by Fandome, **not** a generic sitar token. Matt Sorum majority of drums, Steven Adler specifically Civil War; organ on 14 Years and other songs; Duff bass; Axl electronic/synth and drum-machine sounds specifically My World (indicated by secondary personnel and consistent with detailed MusicBrainz keys/effects/drums credit; cautious). **Production:** layered overdubs across the larger project; `sampling` is concretely tied to Civil War’s dialogue excerpts and thunder effects. Do not turn that into generalized hip-hop sampling or `fieldrecordings`. Do not invent workstation/synth model names.

**Lyrics:** antiwar/political Civil War, frustration toward institutions and media, Estranged’s separation and identity, Yesterday’s nostalgia. Some media interpreted the whole album as excessive, while Pitchfork stresses sincerity and intensity. **Influence:** `influenced_by` empty; the Bob Dylan cover Knockin’ on Heaven’s Door is not an influence citation, nor is an audible funk-metal resemblance to Faith No More.

Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/use-your-illusion-ii-mw0000035625), [MusicBrainz original](https://musicbrainz.org/release/48fdc9d4-dcfc-4e6a-acc9-c4e3d0b19fa9), [MusicBrainz details](https://musicbrainz.org/release/8f83c752-5a1e-48ac-9457-b3659cc5cefd), [Louder Civil War](https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-50-greatest-guns-n-roses-songs/5), [uDiscover Music](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/guns-n-roses-use-your-illusion-review/), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/guns-n-roses-use-your-illusion-i-use-your-illusion-ii/).
