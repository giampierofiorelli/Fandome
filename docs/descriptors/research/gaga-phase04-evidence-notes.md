# Lady Gaga — Phase 04 evidence notes (as of 2026-10-24)

## Research and import rules

This phase is strictly incremental. It describes only `g-mayhem` (March 7 2025, original 14-song studio edition) and `gaga-applemusiclivemayhemrequiem` (May 14 2026, standalone live project). Do not republish or recreate the eight Gaga rows on GitHub when checked (`g-fame`, `gaga-thefamemonster`, `g-btw`, `gaga-artpop`, `gaga-cheektocheek`, `gaga-joanne`, `gaga-astarisborn`, `g-chromatica`). Album-level numerical values are the researcher's **ordinal editorial judgments** following the repository's fixed 0–5 anchors, not critic scores or tempo/BPM measurements. Missing data remain empty. The genre evidence file explicitly distinguishes album from track, texture and context. No multi-value limit is applied.

## Mayhem (2025) — `g-mayhem`

**Edition:** March 7 2025, original fourteen tracks ending in *Die With A Smile*. This is neither the later expanded digital version featuring *The Dead Dance* nor versions with extra *Can't Stop the High* and *Kill for Love* [M_Q, M_ABC, M_OFF]. The song had been released in 2024, but the *album* is 2025. Keep Fandome's **existing** ID `g-mayhem` from `seed.ts`, not `gaga-mayhem`.

**Album-level styles:** critics concur on a large, hook-forward dance-pop record containing strong synthesizer production and classic Gaga theatricality [M_PF, M_GU, M_NME, M_Q]. Slant specifically finds much of the middle section more conventional eighties synth-pop and argues the advertised industrial dance is concentrated in *Disease* and *Abracadabra* [M_SL]. The Guardian emphasizes electro-funk, disco, house, boogie, new-wave/techstep passages and AOR/ballads as *differently weighted* elements [M_GU]. The Qobuz album taxonomy says pop, while AOTY records dance-pop, electropop, pop-rock, nu-disco and synthpop [M_Q, M_AOTY]. New tokens from **verbatim genres** not in current `scenes.ts` include `industrialdance`, `electrogrunge`, `techstep`, `boogie`, `aor`, `powerballad`, `hairmetal` (the last is explicitly a guitar-solo texture, not dominant genre). These are *scoped* in genre-evidence rather than presented as 28 whole-album identities. For editorial meaning, `genres` lists every cited genre, but a future algorithm should respect the scope in genre evidence.

**Scores (whole 14-track record):**
- `energy=4`: repeated forceful club tracks and vocal intensity, lowered from 5 by introspective slow closing songs and dynamically different middle tracks [M_GU, M_SL, M_Q].
- `electronic=4`: Gaga says synthesis/programming **and** live guitars, bass, piano and full instrumental recording co-exist, unlike an almost wholly synthetic Kraftwerk record [M_VO, M_Q].
- `mood=3`: aggressive anxieties about celebrity and selfhood counterbalanced by party pleasure and affectionate ballads; neither uniformly dark nor euphoric [M_PF, M_PA, M_GU].
- `theatrical=4`: oversized singing, heightened drama, persona/alter-ego struggles and show-song dramatic architecture, below the guide's operatic 5 anchor [M_PF, M_VO, M_NME].
- `experimental=3`: unusual industrial/rock/funk collisions and section switches within mostly intelligible verse/chorus mainstream pop [M_PF, M_GU, M_SL].
- `polish=5`: layered programming, detailed analog/electric sources, intentionally maximalist major-label studio production; qualitative gloss, **not** budget inferred merely from fame [M_VO, M_Q, M_PF].
- `tempo=4`: strong dance pulses dominate a sizeable part of the record; no album-average BPM sourced, so the judgment is about reviewer-described pace, not metronomic measurement [M_GU, M_PF].
- `groove=4`: disco, house, funk and pop dance grooves pervade the record, but its ballad-heavy conclusion prevents 5 [M_GU, M_Q, M_SL].

**Lead voice:** `powerful|clear|soft|raspy` covers forceful chest/belting, clear sung pop lines, vulnerable/soft ballads, growled or roughened passages, rather than treating every track as having one register [M_Q, M_PF, M_SL]. **Lyrics:** `identity|introspection|love|sensuality|party|heartbreak`. Gaga describes self versus public persona in *Perfect Celebrity* and identity tensions throughout, while reviews separately identify fleeting sex, club encounters and relationship/hurt material [M_PA, M_VO, M_PF, M_GU]. `language=en` describes lead vocals; scattered ad-libs and sampled fragments should not be elevated to other album languages.

**Producer evidence:** original album's Qobuz per-track `Producer` role unambiguously identifies **Lady Gaga, Andrew Watt, Cirkut, Gesaffelstein, Bruno Mars and Dernst “D'Mile” Emile II** (track-dependent); keep D'Mile common-stage form [M_Q]. Andrew Watt and Cirkut recur through many songs; Gesaffelstein works only on selected tracks; Bruno Mars and D'Mile are on *Die With A Smile*. **Michael Polansky is a songwriter and executive producer, not a credited track producer in those credits**; neither mixing engineer Serban Ghenea nor recording engineer Paul LaMalfa should be added. This distinction overrides casual references to an executive producer as a producer [M_Q, M_VA, M_PF].

**Documented influences:** Gaga cites Bowie, Prince, Earth, Wind & Fire, Nine Inch Nails and Radiohead as album inspirations [M_ST, M_EL]. Gaga explicitly connects *Perfect Celebrity* to the Cure's *Never Enough* [M_MR]. These six entries are `influenced_by`, not the production partners, not automatically source names used by critics for resemblance. For `sounds_like`, critic analogies include Chic, Daft Punk, Bowie, Prince, Nine Inch Nails and several specific comparisons such as Nelly Furtado, Gwen Stefani, Taylor Swift, Siouxsie and the Banshees, KMFDM and Michael Jackson [M_PF, M_GU, M_Q, M_VA, M_SL]. These are subjective sound analogies only. `landmark=1` is a substantial artist-era studio statement and extensively reviewed record [M_PF, M_VO, M_GU]. `confidence=high` reflects cross-source agreement on primary content, not agreement on quality.

**Contradictions:** Pitchfork finds stylistic collage expressive and coherent, while Slant calls most arrangements predictable and the industrial promise overstated [M_PF, M_SL]. The Guardian sees well-developed musical turns, while Variety treats many conspicuous influences as deliberate pastiche [M_GU, M_VA]. Scores do not average critic enthusiasm.

## Apple Music Live: MAYHEM Requiem (2026) — `gaga-applemusiclivemayhemrequiem`

**Release and uniqueness:** Apple Music labels a May 14 2026 separate record *Apple Music Live: MAYHEM Requiem*. Pitchfork's release report identifies a Wiltern Theatre January 2026 performance, 21-part track sequence and a corresponding concert film [R_AP, R_PF, R_FAN]. Apple's 42-assets count is a platform presentation quirk, likely combining audio and audiovisual assets, whereas Pitchfork and the specialist tracklist describe a 21-section live programme. Do **not** create two releases from the number of visible assets. Site's official discography files the record under *Live Albums* [R_OFF].

**Selection:** This is a distinct arranged *live* rendering rather than the same Mayhem master or a mechanical remaster: *Abracadabra* opens as a solo-piano piece, *How Bad Do U Want Me* becomes a ballad and *Die With A Smile* ends in electro-funk/electronic march [R_PF, R_AP, R_VA]. The named live release is included because the arrangements materially diverge from the album, independently reviewed and marketed as the period's closing performance. It does **not** qualify as one of Gaga's defining studio works (`landmark=0`). Its inclusion is an editorial qualifying exception for significant standalone live recordings.

**Genre differentiation:** `pop` (platform tag), `electronic` (droning synths/programming), `industrial` (specific harsher songs), `newwave` and `gothpop` (Variety's 80s/goth sound characterization), `electrofunk` (specific transformed finale), `rock` (full electric band and industrial guitar) [R_AP, R_PF, R_VA]. Album-wide claims are intentionally much narrower than the original Mayhem list; original tracks' disco/house/synthpop labels are **not inherited** merely because setlist titles recur. No added genre token is needed for this record.

**Scales (whole live programme):**
- `energy=3`: energetic industrial performance balanced by minimal piano/drone passages; less consistently driving than studio record [R_VA, R_PF].
- `electronic=3`: electronic synths and manipulations coexist with live electric band and considerable acoustic piano [R_VA, R_AP].
- `mood=1`: consistently shadowy, grave, requiem-like sonic framing rather than club euphoria; some warmer moments [R_AP, R_VA].
- `theatrical=4`: operatic concept, veils, gothic staging and a deliberate dramatic arc even though stage furniture/dancers are restrained [R_AP, R_VA].
- `experimental=3`: substantial genre and tempo rearrangements of accessible core material, not avant-garde composition throughout [R_PF, R_VA].
- `polish=4`: purposefully arranged, produced and released major-label live capture; no assumption that all individual tracks sound studio-glossy [R_AP, R_VA].
- `tempo` **empty**: substantial tempo changes and slowdowns make one reliable album-typical speed unsupported by any found whole-programme analysis. Do not copy 2025's 4.
- `groove=2`: selected funk-industrial propulsion but many intentionally transformed ballads and drones; not a continuous dance-floor recording [R_AP, R_PF, R_VA].

**Voice:** varied intimate and full-voiced Gaga interpretations (`powerful|soft|clear`), no independent evidence for large choral presence. **Lyrics** repeat a very similar 2025 song sequence (identity, introspection, love, desire, club encounters, heartbreak), but these are *underlying composition themes*, not proof the live arrangements newly foreground all of them [R_PF, M_PA, M_GU]. **Language:** predominantly English. **Producer** deliberately blank: source credits for the original 2025 masters cannot be transferred to who produced the *live recordings*; the available credits do not securely give a new live-recording producer or co-producer [R_AP, R_PF, R_VA]. **influenced_by** left blank for this performance: Variety's Nine Inch Nails comparison is sonic, not a documented causal re-arrangement influence; place it only in `sounds_like` [R_VA]. `confidence=medium` because album-wide descriptive consensus is narrower and producer credits lacking.

**Critical qualification:** Apple highlights a larger gothic-opera concept; Variety describes the live show as musically focused and significantly less spectacle-driven than the arena tour [R_AP, R_VA]. The score theatrical 4 concerns **musical/staging dramaturgy**, not number of props. The 2026 presentation does not automatically replace the original studio album in the recommendations.
