# Falco — Phase 01 album-level evidence notes

Evidence checked 28 October 2026. Classification and credits are tied to the **original LPs**, not later territory-specific discs, remasters, re-recordings or bonus tracks. All scores are calibrated editorial readings under the Fandome 0–5 anchors. They are **not objective audio measurements**. Primary original-lineup credits mainly from Falco-Compendium, corroborated with AllMusic, record-pressing metadata and contemporary criticism. Some sonic comparisons are explicitly editorial and remain separate from documented influences.

## `falco-einzelhaft` — *Einzelhaft* (1982)

**Format and identity.** Ten-track Vienna-centered debut, released June 1982 on GiG. Robert Ponger and Falco are co-credited as producers in contemporary issue credits. Falco's roles as co-writer and performer do not automatically establish him as producer on later records. [Falco-Compendium](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/einzelhaft/); [A&M issue credits](https://www.onamrecords.com/artists/falco/discography/britain/a-m-records/amlh-68550/einzelhaft).

**Genres.** AllMusic labels the album `newwave` and `dancepop`. The MusicBrainz release group tags `synthpop`, `electronic` and Neue Deutsche Welle, preserved as proposed token `neuedeutschewelle`. Trouser Press characterises selected tracks as rock or funk-influenced. `hiphop` is **track-level** through German rap delivery on 'Der Kommissar', as AllMusic's biography describes; do not imply the entire LP is conventional hip-hop. Criticisms differ sharply: Trouser Press dismisses the early funk/rock songs, while Falco-Compendium regards the album as his most personal and consistent. Neither view is a production fact.

**Instruments / production.** The reproduced 1982 album credits explicitly attribute **all keyboards** to Robert Ponger, **all guitars** to Peter Vieweger, saxophones to Andy Kolbe and Harry Sokal, roto-toms and cymbals to Thomas Böröczs. Falco-Compendium specifies **all basic tracks were made with a Linn drum computer**; `linndrum` and `drumprogramming` therefore have primary evidence here. Trouser Press independently describes drum-machine rhythm with live roto-tom/cymbal overdubs: `overdubbing` records those layers. Do *not* assume LinnDrum for any other Falco LP.

**0–5 scales.** `energy=3` and `tempo=3`: frequent propulsive mid/up-tempo pop with static/darker material ('Einzelhaft') balancing 'Der Kommissar'. `electronic=4`: Linn drum basis plus keyboards, but guitars and sax remain substantial. `mood=2`: urban isolation, surveillance and social decay in title track, mixed with upbeat dance surfaces. `theatrical=3`: declamatory partly rapped and spoken performance; less grand than the later Bolland productions. `experimental=3`: German rap/rock/New Wave collision and pointed text structure, but recognizable songs. `polish=3`: deliberate and rhythmic studio construction rather than an orchestral or hyper-glossy production. `groove=4`: rhythmic machine-led songs and funk-informed single(s) even though the album is not uniformly dance material. [Falco-Compendium title-song analysis](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/einzelhaft/einzelhaft/); [Trouser Press](https://trouserpress.com/reviews/falco/).

**Voice, lyrics and influence.** Spoken/rapped phrasing and melodic clear singing; themes of consumer society, metropolitan isolation, social surveillance and ironic persona. Primarily German with English fragments; language code `de|en` marks both, not an English-language edition. Early Bowie is well documented: Falco recalled *Heroes* as a major inspiration (Die Presse); contemporary critical writing *compares* the title track to Joy Division, Kraftwerk and Bowie's *Low*. Only the directly cited Bowie influence is entered into `influenced_by`; the rest are `sounds_like`.

**Landmark.** 1: debut with 'Der Kommissar' and a distinct Falco template. High confidence in album chronology, credits and broader styles; numeric judgments remain interpretive.

## `falco-jungeroemer` — *Junge Roemer* (1984)

**Format / production.** Second original album, nine tracks, May 1984 according to the Austrian discography; some digital stores state 1 June and German Wikipedia says April. The **year 1984** is consistent and is the only field required. Falco-Compendium's reproduced 1984 album credits explicitly say **produced by Robert Ponger**, with Ponger writing and arranging music. Some secondary infoboxes add Falco as producer: when the original credits and later infobox differ, this draft records Ponger alone and flags the disagreement. Recorded at Stereo West, **overdubs** at Musicland in Munich, mixed at Arco. [Falco-Compendium](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/junge-roemer/).

**Genres.** Original album tags from AllMusic (`newwave`, `dancepop`), English Wikipedia (`synthpop`, `pop`, `newwave`) and German Wikipedia (`pop`, `rock`, `funk`). Trouser Press describes *Philly soul in tone*, not the album's geographical scene: `phillysoul` **texture only** and ordered last. [Frankfurter Rundschau retrospective](https://www.fr.de/kultur/musik/falcos-junge-roemer-ist-40-der-phaenomenale-flop-92988533.html) describes synthesizer pop plus funk brass arrangements. Genre ranking reflects recurring album sound, not a single trumpet or string passage.

**Instruments / technique.** Reproduced original credits: Ponger keyboards and Digital Key Terminal; Mats Björklund guitars/Gitronics II; Curt Cress drums and percussion; Falco Bass Board; Hans Peter Ströer extra bass; Benny Gebauer sax; Felice Civitareale trumpet; strings from Munich Philharmonic; plus Billy Lang guitar. Synths also corroborated by Frankfurter Rundschau's account. `overdubbing` is **explicitly** in the recording credits. The Digital Key Terminal is **not** assumed to be a Fairlight or other specific sampler, and neither `sampling` nor a machine model is invented. No live album performance is conflated with the original studio session.

**0–5 scales.** `energy=3` and `tempo=3`: largely medium-paced structured funk/pop. `electronic=3`: plenty of keyboard/synth work with clearly documented live rhythm, guitar, brass and orchestral string sections. `mood=3`: cool worldly confidence and irony mixed with unease. `theatrical=3`: formal, deliberately stylized but less maximal than *Falco 3*. `experimental=3`: multi-language, conspicuous arranged textures and some formal ambition. `polish=4`: extensive Munich overdubbing and professional full ensemble. `groove=3`: funky and syncopated, yet less continuous club drive than the singles-led records. [Falco-Compendium](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/junge-roemer/); [laut.de 40th-anniversary feature](https://laut.de/News/Best-Of-1984-40-Jahre%2C-40-Alben-12-04-2024-20350/Seite-29).

**Voice / lyrics / comparisons.** Falco's cool melodic/spoken singing, sociological irony and relationships. [Album essay](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/junge-roemer/) explicitly confirms German and English plus French and Italian passages on this album; `de|en|fr|it` does not claim equal amounts. `sounds_like=ABC|Heaven 17` is **editorial production/genre proximity only**; no evidence that either influenced recording. `influenced_by` left empty. Landmark 1 for ambitious distinctive career record; retrospectively admired though initially commercially underperformed. Overall confidence high for facts and several sources, not a guarantee on comparisons.

## `falco-falco3` — *Falco 3* (1985)

**Year and version discipline.** Original GiG issue **October 1985** and **ten tracks**. AllMusic lists 1986 because it reflects an American market listing; use original-year **1985**. The song 'Rock Me Amadeus' appeared in several mixes on international editions; do not treat a remix as another album. [Falco-Compendium](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/falco-3/); [1985 German issue](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/falco/falco-3-album_frpehu.html).

**Genre evidence.** 1985 pressing styles are `poprock` and `synthpop`, AllMusic labels `dancepop` and `newwave`, and laut.de labels `pop` and `rock`. `germanrap` is separately named by the album's secondary genre metadata (NEW token; some tracks, not a claim of a pure rap album). The Los Angeles Times describes 'It's All Over Now, Baby Blue' as acoustic **country-blues**: `countryblues` is song-specific and last. This is a multi-genre pop record, not a classical album merely because 'Rock Me Amadeus' invokes Mozart. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/falco-3-mw0000189829); [LA Times 1986](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-04-06-ca-24741-story.html).

**Producers.** Original album credits: Rob and Ferdi Bolland produced and arranged the bulk. **Song 8 ('Nothin’ Sweeter Than Arabia') and song 10 (Dylan's 'It's All Over Now, Baby Blue') credit Falco as producer.** Curt Cress and Mats Björklund are listed as executive producers only for song 8, **not** album producers. *Falco 3* therefore has `Rob Bolland|Ferdi Bolland|Falco` without Cress/Björklund. Source directly distinguishes them: [Falco-Compendium credits](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/falco-3/).

**Instruments / production.** Reproduced LP credits identify the Bolland brothers, Ton Op't Hof, Lex Bolderdijk, Jan and Hans Hollestelle, Gerbrand Westveen, the Bollokk horns/strings, plus musicians for special tracks; 1985 issue metadata identifies pop/synth-pop production, electric guitar, bass, drums, sax, strings and brass. Saxophonist Benny Gebauer is documented on song 10. Original credit specifies remixes for the album's two non-Bolland sessions (song 8 at Arco Sound; song 10 at Red Rooster) so only `remixing` is entered in production. **No specific Fairlight, Linn or other synthesizer/drum-machine model is claimed for 1985.** `synth` is supported by LP synth-pop style and contemporary sound discussion, but synthesis technique/model is not inferred. The Dylan cover is described as acoustic country-blues, but this does not establish a particular guitar type; the row therefore uses generic `guitar`. [Original label/track credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/falco/falco-3-album_frpehu.html); [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-04-06-ca-24741-story.html).

**0–5 scales.** `energy=4`, `tempo=4`: beat-forward singles; longer narrative/cover slow some pacing. `electronic=4`: synth-based dance production shares space with guitars, bass and drums. `mood=3`: buoyant, comic 'Rock Me Amadeus' and 'Vienna Calling' contrasted with 'Jeanny'. `theatrical=4`: pronounced staged persona and acting/near-spoken narrative. `experimental=2`: sophisticated cross-over of languages, mostly accessible verse-chorus pop. `polish=5`: studio-directed international pop with major-label gloss. `groove=4`: dance grooves on major singles, balanced by ballad and country-blues. These are **album** balances, not individual hit scores.

**Lyrics/influence discipline.** German-English delivery, storytelling ('Jeanny'), cultural satire ('America', 'Macho Macho') and relationship tension. Contemporary LA Times reviewer explicitly *compares* Falco to Lou Reed, Bowie, and The Cars. They are entered in `sounds_like`, not asserted as production influences. Covering Dylan and reworking The Cars *does not* make them documented `influenced_by`. `influenced_by` intentionally blank for this album despite career-wide Bowie influence; an album-specific statement is not established. Landmark 1, his defining international breakthrough.

## `falco-emotional` — *Emotional* (1986)

**Album and song-level caveats.** Fourth studio LP, originally 1986, nine tracks. Some original Austrian documentation says October 1986, US platforms use 1 November: year is undisputed. The nine-track LP must remain separate from 2021 deluxe bonus mixes, English re-recordings and live versions. [Falco-Compendium](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/emotional/); [Apple Music listing](https://music.apple.com/us/album/emotional/355932017).

**Genre breakdown, intentionally scoped.** `pop` for the whole record (Apple Music), `newwave` and `dancepop` (AllMusic), `synthpop` and `poprock` (1986 pressing metadata). A specialist song-by-song assessment identifies `soul` and `blues` on 'Emotional'/'The Star of Moon and Sun'; `swing` and `jazz` on 'Crime Time'; `funk` on 'The Sound Of Musik'; `electronic` on 'Kamikaze Cappa'/'Cowboyz And Indianz'. These are **track-level** values ordered behind album-wide classifications. [Specialist analysis](https://rockmefalco.wordpress.com/en/translations/album-en/emotional-en/); [Crime Time essay](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/emotional/crime-time/).

**Original instrumentation / technique.** Rob and Ferdi Bolland: *all synths, digital keyboard samplers, mouthorgan, drum and percussion computer programming* and male backing vocals; Lex Bolderdijk electric and acoustic guitars; Jan Hollestelle electric and acoustic bass; Ton Op't Hof acoustic/live and **Simmons** drums; Gerbrand Westveen saxophones; Harry Mooten accordion; Lisa Boray female backing vocals; Jurre Haanstra big-band arrangement. Instruments `sampler`, `electronicdrums`, `harmonica` therefore refer to actual album credits, not generic synth-pop guesswork; mouthorgan is interpreted in the conventional harmonica sense, not a digital synthesizer. `drumprogramming` is explicit; `hornarrangements` is supported by credited big-band arranging on 'Crime Time' (track-specific). Avoid unsupported studio software and specific sampler models. [Full credit transcription](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/emotional/); [1986 record issue](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/falco/emotional-album_frpcru.html).

**0–5 scales.** `energy=3` and `tempo=3` because driving dance/electronic tracks are tempered by balladry and swing. `electronic=4`: programmed rhythm and samplers are fundamental but there are substantial acoustic rhythm players, sax and accordion. `mood=3`: exuberant showmanship and darker relationship narratives alternate. `theatrical=4`: genre pastiche, long 'The Kiss of Kathleen Turner' and big-band 'Crime Time'. `experimental=3`: stylistic jumps unlike a uniform chart-pop project, but still accessible. `polish=5`: complex professional Dutch multistudio production. `groove=3`: some strong funk/synth dance while slower soul and dramatic pieces reduce album-wide dance density.

**Lyrics/comparison/reception.** Love and heartbreak on the title song; narrative sequel 'Coming Home' to Jeanny; social irony elsewhere. English and German are both heard: French track title *Les Nouveaux Riches* is **not** proof that French is sung extensively, so language field stays `de|en`. `sounds_like=David Bowie|ABC` is editorial and not evidence of Falco citing them as album influences. `influenced_by` left empty. AllMusic gives mixed praise: quirky/fun but less essential than *Falco 3*; specialist appraisal is warmer. Landmark 0 for the career hierarchy, not a claim the record is artistically worthless. [AllMusic review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/emotional-mr0000282307); [specialist album essay](https://falco-compendium.at/musik/emotional/).
