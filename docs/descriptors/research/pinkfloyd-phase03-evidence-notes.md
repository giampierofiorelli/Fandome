# Pink Floyd — Phase 03: evidence notes

**Research convention:** source links in `pinkfloyd_phase03_sources.csv` identify independently available official pages, liner notes, MusicBrainz instrument credits, professional reviews and artist/producer interviews. Genre evidence is a separate `genre_evidence.csv` with a `scope` column. Album-level numeric ratings are Fandome editorial judgments based on those sources, not quoted source metrics. No track-specific technique is silently generalized to every song.

## Wish You Were Here (1975) — pinkfloyd-wishyouwerehere

- **Genres:** AllMusic assigns Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Album Rock and broad Pop/Rock; Acclaimed Music identifies art/progressive rock and parent rock. The blues-inflected guitar work and the funky `Have a Cigar` are textures rather than unsupported album-level genre additions.
- **energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 1:** Extended reflective pieces and synthesizer introductions dominate more than danceable material; `Have a Cigar` provides limited rhythmic contrast.
- **electronic 3:** documented EMS VCS 3, EMS Synthi AKS, Minimoog, ARP String Ensemble; contrasted with guitars, saxophone and acoustic title track (MusicBrainz original release).
- **mood 2 / theatrical 3:** grief, disillusionment and tenderness with dramatic extended instrumental passages; not an exuberant or stage-musical presentation.
- **experimental 3 / polish 4:** machine-like repeating synthesizer, radio/tape framing and multipart suite, but a comparatively coherent and carefully recorded mainstream studio album.
- **Voice:** clear and soft dominate; occasionally powerful sustained rock vocals. `instrumental` is omitted because singing is prominent across the full album.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz documents lap steel guitar (`steelguitar`), Dick Parry's tenor and baritone sax (`saxophone`), Wright's Wurlitzer (`electricpiano`), clavinet and Moog family synthesizers, plus **glass harp** (`glassharp`, new token). Instruments are aggregated from specific credited tracks, not all tracks.
- **Production:** EMS VCS3/AKS, Minimoog, ARP String Ensemble as documented model tokens; tape/radio treatments supported by musicological description of the title-song transitions. No claim that the unreleased household-object album was used: the official site says that attempt was abandoned.
- **Themes:** `nostalgia` and `introspection` for absence/Syd Barrett; `social|identity` for music-business critique; `love` covers the title song's broader friendship/affection per official account. `influenced_by` blank: a song *about* Syd Barrett is not evidence that he musically influenced this specific album.
- **Producers:** Pink Floyd, **not Brian Humphries**, who is credited as recording engineer.

## Animals (1977) — pinkfloyd-animals

- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly assigns Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Album Rock and broad Pop/Rock; its review identifies **blues-rock** guitar and **space-rock** textures. Classic Rock Review identifies a hard-rock-oriented album. Broader rock applies across the record.
- **energy 4 / tempo 3:** urgent drums, aggressive extended guitar sections, contrasting with brief acoustic bookends.
- **electronic 2:** recorded organs, synthesizers, ARP string synth and effects support moderate electronic content, but guitars, bass and drums dominate. **Not** a fully electronic record.
- **mood 0 / theatrical 3:** unrelentingly cynical class/power satire and ominous textures; mid-high drama in long animal allegories.
- **experimental 3 / polish 3:** elaborate suites, manipulated vocals and gear, but intentionally harder and more direct than 1975. Recorded in the band's own Britannia Row studio.
- **groove 2:** rhythmically strong but not dance-floor music.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz specifies `fretlessbass` (new token) played by Gilmour on `Pigs (Three Different Ones)`; clavinet, Rhodes, Hammond, VCS3 and Minimoog are track-credited. `vocoder` is a sound source in addition to a production technique.
- **Production:** `talkbox` for Gilmour's pig-like guitar processing, distinct from **vocoder** in `Pigs` and `Sheep`; music-credit entries also identify tape manipulation. Not all vocal effects should be described as talkbox. No undocumented automated percussion.
- **Themes:** political/social power groups through Orwell-derived pigs, dogs and sheep, with the short `Pigs on the Wing` love passages.
- **Producers:** Pink Floyd; engineer Brian Humphries is not a credited producer.

## The Wall (1979) — pinkfloyd-thewall

- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly assigns Prog-Rock, Art Rock, Album Rock, Hard Rock and broad Pop/Rock. Album of the Year also names Symphonic Rock and Rock Opera. The interconnected Pink storyline justifies `rockopera` as an editorial genre classification, supported by the official concept page and music-theatre documentation. **Disagreement:** Roger Waters himself says “rock opera” is a term critics apply, while Album of the Year and music discographies use it descriptively. `disco` applies to specific tracks—most notably `Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2`, with some beat adaptation in `Run Like Hell`—not the double album as a whole.
- **energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 2:** alternates hard rock, ballads, dialogue/ambience and selected driving dance-oriented passages; no all-album disco-score inflation.
- **electronic 2:** synth and organ overdubs documented in surviving session sheets, but conventional band playing, orchestra and choir remain central.
- **mood 1 / theatrical 5:** personal isolation, trauma and authoritarian fantasy, enacted as a multi-character rock opera with recitation, children chorus, orchestra and dialogue.
- **experimental 3 / polish 5:** studio-engineered concept narrative, tape edits, sound design and extensive session arranging; highly polished rather than uniformly avant-garde.
- **Voice:** powerful and soft leads plus spoken/sung theatre and a clearly attested children's choir (`choral`). Vocal typing includes album total, not a claim Waters personally uses every type.
- **Production:** session logs support `overdubbing`; Ezrin's explanation describes **analog tape copying** of a verse/chorus to extend `Another Brick` and subsequent children's chorus, supporting `analogtape|layeredvocals`; Michael Kamen/Bob Ezrin arranged orchestral music (`stringarrangements`). Tape effects and repeated dialogue/sound effects corroborated by notes and logs. `onetake` applies only to Gilmour's **guitar solo** on `Another Brick Part II` (Ezrin, Tape Op), not the complete album.
- **Producers:** Bob Ezrin, David Gilmour and Roger Waters as producer; **James Guthrie as explicitly credited co-producer** and recording engineer (official site, liner notes). Michael Kamen is an arranger, **not** a producer here.
- **influenced_by:** specific **recorded** influence on `Another Brick Part II`: Ezrin reports hearing a groove from **Chic** and deliberately transplanting that rhythmic idea. `Alice Cooper` is narrowly linked to Ezrin's established school-children chorus precedent, not a claim that the two full discographies sound alike. Both should be interpreted at song/production-technique scope only.

## The Final Cut (1983) — pinkfloyd-thefinalcut

- **Genres:** AllMusic classifies Album Rock, Art Rock, Prog-Rock and broad Pop/Rock; broad Rock included. Frequent orchestra does **not** prove `orchestralpop` or `classical` as genres.
- **energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 0:** mainly sparse, slow, mournful songs and orchestral passages with occasional rock climaxes, e.g. `Not Now John`.
- **electronic 1 / experimental 2 / polish 4:** mostly real strings, piano, guitar and drums; the Holophonics positional sound effects and tape effects increase production complexity, not electronic instrumentation to a dominant level.
- **mood 0 / theatrical 5:** bleak anti-war and anti-authoritarian lyrics and loud/quiet contrasts, orchestral builds and character-driven delivery.
- **Voice:** Waters alternates intimate, spoken-feeling phrases and powerful dramatic peaks; Gilmour has an additional lead on `Not Now John`.
- **Instruments:** Kamen's estate confirms piano/harmonium, conductor and arranger role; MusicBrainz credits National Philharmonic Orchestra strings, Hammond organ, tenor saxophone from Raphael Ravenscroft and electric piano. On `Two Suns in the Sunset`, the drummer is **Andy Newmark** (not automatically Nick Mason).
- **Production:** `holophonics` is an externally documented binaural sound-effects approach (new token), not proof that all the album instruments were recorded with a dummy head. `tapeeffects` and `stringarrangements` are documented in song credits. Avoid introducing a specific synthesizer model without direct album source.
- **Themes:** Falklands/other wars, nuclear annihilation, Waters's father, loss, disillusion and political leadership.
- **Producers:** Roger Waters **and** James Guthrie **and** Michael Kamen. Kamen's official estate independently corroborates his co-production; he is not only an arranger. `influenced_by` blank because topical/political inspiration is not an artist-to-artist musical influence.
- **Edition discipline:** Base row describes original **1983** release, excluding the later-added `When the Tigers Broke Free` bonus track when assessing dominant genre/instrumentation.

## Cross-album cautions

- `glassharp` and `fretlessbass` are **new instrument tokens**, with credited album-specific source support.
- `minimoog`, `arpstringensemble`, `holophonics` are **new production tokens**; others used are already in the `descriptors` branch production vocabulary.
- All known project producers are retained; mixing, engineering, orchestral arranging alone does not grant a producer credit.
- `symphonicrock` is a new genre token sourced to Album of the Year’s explicit genre list for The Wall. No genre or scale is extrapolated from the band's general Wikipedia biography or another album.
- The current GitHub snapshot contained no `The Final Cut` album; create that catalog item with the expected slug and avoid replacing existing three.
