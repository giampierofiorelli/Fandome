# Evidence notes — Pino Daniele, phase 04

**Read-only source control:** `descriptors`, 30 October 2026. Existing `docs/descriptors/pinodaniele.csv` has four Phase-01 rows only. This file documents exactly the four Phase-04 additions. Phase-02 and Phase-03 ZIPs have been delivered separately and are deliberately not copied. Producer credits are from the artist's album-specific official page, not secondary infoboxes. All scales are Fandome judgments (0–5), album-level, not numbers from reviewers.

## Che Dio ti benedica (1993) — `pinodaniele-chediotibenedica`

- **Style and genres:** the AllMusic style is Italian Pop; the Mediaset retrospective explicitly identifies a pop-oriented 1993 sound. OndaRock focuses on the guitar-led acoustic songcraft, while Gab's DeBaser track-by-track review distinguishes the blues numbers from the Chick Corea jazz track *Sicily*, the short instrumental duet *Two Pisces in alto mare*, and songs in Neapolitan. The long tail `jazz|neapolitansong` is intentionally track-scoped. `songwriter` reflects the critic's treatment of Daniele's own song repertoire; the classification is less formally stated than pop/blues. The albums' `genres` are not inferred merely from the musician's historical reputation.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 2` (reflective acoustic numbers outweigh the stronger blues), `electronic 1` (live players dominant, with keys/synth/programming credited), `mood 3` (romantic songs, irony, and melancholic or social lyrics), `theatrical 1` (intimate voice/guitar over stage-like drama), `experimental 2` (jazz guest pieces and style-switching within conventional songs), `polish 3` (conventional professional multi-studio production), `tempo 2` (predominantly laid-back and ballad-like), `groove 2` (some rhythmic blues but few dance-oriented passages). Based on the official original track list plus OndaRock and DeBaser's album-specific discussion.
- **Instruments:** official credits document Daniele's guitars and vocals, Ralph Towner's *acoustic guitar*, Jimmy Earl's *electric bass*, Melotti's drums, Annona's keyboards, Corea's acoustic piano/keys, De Filippi's harmonica, and Steele/Jermano percussion. AllMusic lists Daniele's acoustic and electric guitars and synth, hence separate `acousticguitar|electricguitar|synth` tokens. No specific synthesizer model was identified.
- **Production:** `programming` follows the artist site explicitly crediting Daniele and Annona as computer programmers. Recording at Studio Zero, Suono di Ripetta and Logic Studio does **not** mean the software Logic was used; do not add `logic` token. No machine model or tape format sourced.
- **Producers:** **Pino Daniele and Chick Corea**, with Corea **only** on *Sicily* and *Soleado up and down* per official credit. A widely repeated secondary attribution to Sergio De Angelis conflicts with original album credit, which explicitly calls De Angelis **executive producer**; excluded. Writers Massimo Troisi and Ralph Towner are not added as producers or influences.
- **Lyrics/language:** Italian and Neapolitan predominance, with a short English passage in *Soleado up and down*. Love, loss, personal reflection and humorous/social references are documented in official lyrics and DeBaser. No album-specific artist-to-artist sonic comparisons or influences were substantiated. Leave `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` empty. Voice type blank rather than guessing raspiness.
- **Landmark 0:** critics admire parts of the album and note its sound direction, but an album-of-the-artist endorsement or success is not, on its own, an independent claim of a defining transformation.

## Non calpestare i fiori nel deserto (1995) — `pinodaniele-noncalpestareifiorineldeserto`

- **Genres:** *Italian pop* (AllMusic); *pop* and *world music* permeate the record (DeBaser, OndaRock); pop-rock wording applies to this period in the estate biography; blues and jazz colour the pop tunes. Rap is audible and discussed on two Jovanotti songs (*Un deserto di parole*, *Stress*); funk is explicitly used about *Stress* alone, so those tokens are last. Arabic/North African colouring is documented, but **not** replaced with a formal `arabicmusic` tag just on a timbral description.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 3` (some dance-oriented rap/funk against languid ballads), `electronic 2` (sampled percussion and programming combined with an acoustic/electric band), `mood 3` (mixed hope/love with social criticism), `theatrical 2` (mostly direct song performances with guests), `experimental 2` (world/rap hybrid within conventional pop structure), `polish 4` (carefully arranged studio pop, mix and mastering), `tempo 3` (mixed ballads and mid/up-tempo material), `groove 3` (programmed/sampled percussion and syncopated funk tracks but not a dance album).
- **Instruments:** artist credits list guitar, bass (Jimmy Earl, Alfredo Paixão on *Bambina*), drums (Manu Katché and Lele Melotti on different cuts), piano/keys (Rita Marcotulli; Baldassari on two tracks), Mike Mainieri on **vibraphone**, mapped to `mallets`, and sampled percussion (`percussion`). `strings` from Rita Marcotulli's credited arrangements, not inferred from mainstream-pop genre. No specific drum machine or sampling computer model was sourced.
- **Production:** the source literally says *sample percussion* (`sampling`), names computer programmers (`programming`), and explicitly credits Rita Marcotulli's string arrangements (`stringarrangements`). The guests are not producers. Producer **Pino Daniele** only.
- **Influenced_by:** **Jovanotti** is included because Daniele explicitly said his friendship with Lorenzo influenced his **artistic choices** in making this record, *and* Lorenzo co-wrote two songs. He is not added merely because he guests on two tracks. The statement is broader than the two duets, hence no invented [2/13] fraction. No sampled/covered songs are counted as influences.
- **Lyrics/language:** mostly Italian with substantial Neapolitan on several songs; love, social issues, cultural identity, criticism, irony. `voice` blank: guest and backing singers are documented, but primary vocal type is not established. `sounds_like` blank: artists Daniele performed with are not sonic comparisons.
- **Landmark 1:** **TRAKS and OndaRock independently describe the album as a decisive career transition/commercial consecration**, to a broadly accessible pop, world and North African sound. This meets the 'changed the artist's whole career' criterion, separately from sales, awards or the estate's praise. Critics disagree about whether that shift improved his music; OndaRock is especially skeptical, DeBaser's reactions split.

## Dimmi cosa succede sulla terra (1997) — `pinodaniele-dimmicosasuccedesullaterra`

- **Genre disagreements:** Pressing-derived data explicitly gives `poprock|bluesrock` and Pop/Blues; AllMusic also applies the broad styles `adultalternativepoprock|alternativeindierock`, but these feel generic beside the detailed track-level reviews and so come **last**. The independent OndaRock study describes recurring world tones, funky-jazz on *Canto do Mar*, new-age/Arab hues on two songs. DeBaser describes the prominent rap beat on lead single *Che male c'è* and blues-rock on *The Desert in My Head*. `newage` is a late texture, not a whole-album label. Do not add broad `rock` just because a database categorizes the release under Rock alongside pop.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 3` (gentle pop coupled with punchier blues/rap), `electronic 2` (computer-programmed additions to an organic band), `mood 3` (sweet love singles vs conflict, justice, hardship), `theatrical 2` (generally conversational and song-based, with a brief spoken introduction), `experimental 2` (some off-centre world/rap material in radio structures), `polish 4` (radio-focused studio arrangements and tight mixing), `tempo 3` (moderate mix, newspaper calls album danceable), `groove 3` (rap groove in lead single and funk in Canto do Mar without being a dance release). Contemporary l’Unità critic/interview confirms deliberate greater simplicity, not an inference from bestseller status.
- **Instruments:** official album credit specifies Pino guitar, Jimmy Earl bass, Manu Katché drums, Hossam Ramzy percussion, Deron Johnson's Hammond (`organ`) and keyboards, Rita Marcotulli keys on three tracks, orchestra/strings on *Amici come prima*. A DeBaser critic explicitly describes audible piano/acoustic guitar on *Questo immenso* and vibraphone in *Dubbi non ho* and *Canto do Mar*: `piano|acousticguitar|mallets` supported **sonically**, but the reviewer speculates Mike Mainieri played vibes; we **do not** name that player. No synthesizer or electric guitar model specified.
- **Production:** explicit computer programming by Fabio Massimo Colasanti (`programming`) and Gianluca Podio's arrangement of track 7 (`stringarrangements`). The track-level critic hears a *doubled* lead for counterpoint (`layeredvocals`) and sea/wave sounds at the end of a track (`soundeffects`). We **do not** assume this was a true field recording instead of stock/sample audio, so `fieldrecordings` is absent.
- **Sounds_like:** DeBaser compares the acoustic guitar in *Dubbi non ho* to Extreme's *More Than Words*. This is a **one-track sonic comparison** (`Extreme (Extreme II: Pornograffitti) [1/13]`), not musical influence. Verified the Extreme album title separately. Critics' generic observations about Afro-Arab atmosphere do not automatically create an influence link. The guests Giorgia, Noa and Almamegretta are omitted from both relation columns. Voice type blank; a spoken intro is not a representative lead-vocal style.
- **Lyrics/language:** mostly Italian, one extended English Noa duet and Neapolitan rap/singing in *Canto do Mar*. Official lyrics document romantic and personal, ecological/social, historical/identity and afterlife/spiritual themes.
- **Landmark 0:** its record sales and radio hits exceeded the 1995 release, but independent commentary generally treats it as **continuation** of the already established pop/world direction. A contemporary headline about changing musical approach does not by itself establish a new career-defining landmark on this album.

## Come un gelato all’equatore (1999) — `pinodaniele-comeungelatoallequatore`

- **Genres/new genre:** In a **18 March 1999 La Repubblica interview**, Daniele explicitly calls the album's hybrid `afroblues`, a genre **not** in the current token list; propose `afroblues` exactly. Official comment and contemporary reporting support the world/Mediterranean dimension, while AllMusic classifies the record as Italian Pop. OndaRock independently specifies blues on parts, jazz on *Da soli no*, the title track and *Samba In My Mind*, reggae on *Ladro d'amore*, and prominent electronic arrangements in multiple tracks. DeBaser hears rap from Raiss on a couple of songs. No `samba` tag is inferred from the name *Samba In My Mind*: reviewers concentrate on its jazz singing. Never label it trip-hop because a critic writes 'trippy blues'.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 3` (dynamic riffs and bass but many languid love songs), `electronic 3` (contemporary journalist says roughly half is acoustic-electronic experimentation, supported by credited programming and sampled beats), `mood 3` (love/affirmation contrasted with war and anxiety), `theatrical 2` (small-scale love songs and guest vocals), `experimental 3` (about half are acoustically/electronically exploratory, artist's own characterization), `polish 4` (studio-constructed, careful pop arrangements), `tempo 3` (varied speeds, mostly mid-tempo), `groove 3` (reggae and sample-driven percussion, not a disco album).
- **Instruments:** artist credits explicitly give Daniele guitar, Jimmy Earl/Pino Palladino bass, Katché/Aron Ahmman drums, Mino Cinelu percussion, Dary/Gianluca Podio piano and keyboards, Fabio Massimo Colasanti's **Ztar** (an electronic performance controller, not generically equated with `guitarsynth`), and Podio's horn arrangements (`brass`). DeBaser describes electric guitars, including distortion and a Stratocaster solo; exact guitar *model* is not a production-machine token. The new instrument token `ztar` is needed. Horn players are not separately named; `brass` is lower-confidence than the other credited instruments.
- **Production:** original credits specify `programming` (Colasanti), `hornarrangements` (Podio), and female backing chorus (`layeredvocals`); independent DeBaser review explicitly hears sampled drums (`sampling`). Daniele says two songs were sung without breaks in a live-like studio manner; **no** `onetake` or `liverecording` token: the quote does not establish the complete recordings were captured in one take. No named synthesizer model is reliably credited.
- **Influenced_by:** Daniele **personally** described *Samba In My Mind* as his musical tribute to **Gilberto Gil and Toninho Horta** in La Repubblica; therefore each has a **[1/12]** song fraction. This is supported by his direct *interview*, not just their names in the lyric. The same interview mentions that he enjoys listening to Salif Keita, Khaled, Manu Dibango, Noa, Tupac Shakur, Massive Attack and Craig Armstrong, **without stating they shaped this specific album**; they are intentionally excluded from album-level influences. No sonic critic comparison is supported for `sounds_like`; leave empty.
- **Lyrics/language:** Italian dominates; Neapolitan phrases feature on *Ladro d'amore* and *I buoni e i cattivi*, English passages in *Cosa penserai di me* and other tracks. Love/heartbreak, social justice, cultural identity, religious images and regret coexist. Guest singers are not lead-vocal type evidence; `voice` stays empty.
- **Landmark 0:** the artist describes refinement of his Mediterranean approach, but independent sources do not claim the album created/changed a whole genre, his defining sound or career. Divergent critical judgments are documented: the contemporaneous journalist is more curious about the acoustic/electronic split; OndaRock is sharply critical, while DeBaser's writer is favourable.

## Global exclusions and methodology

- Do **not** reproduce any phase-01/02/03 descriptor rows in the import; preserve their existing album IDs and data.
- All four are already present in `src/data/catalog.ts`; catalogue additions are **none**. `seed.ts` has no separate Pino rows.
- Producer is distinct from executive producer, arranger, engineer, programmer, mixer and guest. Only original credited producing individuals are entered.
- `scene` remains Neapolitan Power as continuity of the musical movement; it is not a genre and does not derive individual instrument or scale values.
- Song-level genre evidence is intentionally late in each list. Broad database genres and listener opinions are identified as such. Sources avoid Wikipedia.
- Confidence reflects competing criticism and source certainty, not personal enjoyment.
