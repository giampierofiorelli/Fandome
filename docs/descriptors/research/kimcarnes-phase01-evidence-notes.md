# Kim Carnes — Fandome Phase 01 research notes

**Research status:** Draft, not second-person reviewed. Values are interpretive 0–5 descriptors anchored to Fandome's guide; ambiguous evidence is flagged rather than presented as established.

## Rest on Me (1971) — `kimcarnes-restonme`

- **Date:** December 1971 is documented for the original Amos release; AllMusic lists 1972, plausibly from catalogue metadata. Use 1971 as the original issue, record discrepancy for Claude. Do not use the subsequently slowed/remixed budget CDs as sonic evidence; early-1990s versions are altered.
- **Producers:** Jimmy Bowen (original album). John Guess was engineer, Larry Muhoberac arranger; they are not producers.
- **Genres:** `poprock` is the album classification; `softrock`, `folkrock` and `songwriter` describe this acoustic/session style and are interpretive. `soul` means vocal and instrumental texture, not the entire album. Supporting sources include the original player credits, the artist's bio, and the original-release album overview.
- **Instruments:** Sessiondays transcribes original personnel: bass Bill Perry/Reinie Press, drums Ed Greene/Dennis St. John, guitar Larry Carlton/Mike Deasy/James Burton/Gil Rogers, organ and piano Larry Muhoberac, piano Glen Hardin. Nothing supports named synthesizers or electronic drum models.
- **Production:** left empty: ordinary studio recording does not automatically prove a precise analogue format or effect chain.
- **Scales:** 3 energy, 0 electronic, 2 mood, 2 theatrical, 1 experimental, 2 polish, 2 tempo, 2 groove. The largely acoustic band and songwriter songs support moderate energy and low electronic character; the emotional colour is mixed.
- **Voice:** `raspy|soft|powerful`; the raspy quality is established for Carnes as a vocalist, but its intensity on these early recordings is an editorial evaluation.
- **Comparisons:** Billboard contemporaneously likened her approach to Carole King, Carly Simon and Gayle McCormick; similarities only. No album-specific artists documented as influences by Carnes or producer.

## Kim Carnes (1975) — `kimcarnes-kimcarnes`

- **Release:** A&M LP SP-4548, November 1975. A later combined Australian two-LP issue with *Sailin'* is not a separate Fandome album.
- **Producer:** Mentor Williams. David Briggs and Stephen Dorff arranged strings but are not album producers; David Foster played keys.
- **Genres:** `country` is a distributor/original metadata category, while AllMusic uses contemporary pop/rock and soft rock. `adultcontemporary`, `countryrock`, `folkpop` are editorial secondary classifications based on arrangements and material; see genre evidence for scopes.
- **Instruments:** Original A&M credits document Jim Keltner drums, Leland Sklar bass, Dean Parks guitar, David Foster piano/Clavinet/Rhodes, Michael Utley organ/Moog, Steve Forman percussion, plus orchestral strings. No fictional specific drum machine.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` documented; `moog` supported by credited Moog synth played by Utley. Synth timbres exist but this is still overwhelmingly played instrumental pop, so electronic=1.
- **Scales:** 2 energy, 1 electronic, 2 mood, 2 theatrical, 1 experimental, 3 polish, 2 tempo, 2 groove. Harmony-rich midtempo arrangements, restrained rhythmic drive.
- **Notes:** The voice blends a gritty upper register with gentler phrases. The late-1970s Gene Cotton *duet* of “You're a Part of Me” is a separate recording; it must not be counted as this 1975 solo album track.

## Sailin' (1976) — `kimcarnes-sailin`

- **Release and recording:** A&M 1976; part made at Muscle Shoals with its rhythm section. Later digital reissues and region-dependent A&M catalogue numbers do not change original identity.
- **Producers:** Jerry Wexler and Barry Beckett; both are explicitly credited.
- **Genres:** Original label Apple Music files it under country; AllMusic says pop/rock and soft rock. `blueeyedsoul` and `soul` are justified as localized Muscle Shoals texture, *not* a blanket claim. `folkrock` applies to selected acoustic songs. The country classification reflects mandolin, dobro, banjo and writing as well as label shelving, not just the birthplace of players.
- **Instruments:** Roger Hawkins drums, David Hood bass, Jimmy Johnson/Pete Carr guitars, Barry Beckett keyboards, Bill Cuomo piano, Dave Ellingson banjo, David Grisman mandolin, Pete Carr dobro, harmonica, flute, horns, percussion and arranged strings are present in album credits.
- **Production:** `hornarrangements` and `stringarrangements` explicitly credited (multiple arrangers); no machine models or precise tape effects documented.
- **Scales:** 2 energy, 0 electronic, 2 mood, 2 theatrical, 1 experimental, 3 polish, 2 tempo, 2 groove. Mostly warm organic roots-country and soul crossover.
- **Influences:** Van Morrison is the author of a covered song, *not* proof that Carnes named him as an influence on the album.

## St. Vincent's Court (1979) — `kimcarnes-stvincentscourt`

- **Original release:** February 1979, EMI America SW-17004. The 2003 combined *St. Vincent's Court/Romance Dance* CD is a two-album reissue, not a separate studio album.
- **Producers:** Kim Carnes and Dave Ellingson, plus Daniel Moore for track 9, “Take Me Home to Where My Heart Is”. Preserve the track-level caveat instead of implying Moore produced all songs.
- **Genres:** Original pressing filed pop/rock; contemporary newspaper reaction discusses country touches, rhythm-and-blues and pop ballads. AllMusic’s two-disc reissue descriptors apply to the *two-disc edition* and support a broad context only. `funk` is a limited arrangement inference driven by clavinet/percussion tracks, not a named critic genre; `soul` is a vocal/rhythmic texture.
- **Instruments:** Sessiondays and original LP lists support guitars, bass, drums, Bill Cuomo piano/Rhodes/Clavinet/synth strings, melodica, mandolin, banjo, dobro, organ, saxophone, flute, harmonica, recorder, vibraphone, congas and trumpet. Instruments occur on *some* songs, not every track.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` credited to Jim Ed Norman, with Bill Cuomo on a track; the source describes sound effects on “Jamaica Sunday Morning” but no specific synth model or sampler, so production has no invented device tokens.
- **Scales:** 3 energy, 1 electronic, 2 mood, 3 theatrical, 2 experimental, 3 polish, 3 tempo, 3 groove. Wider pop and syncopated textures than the preceding two records; most timbres are acoustic/electric.
- **Comparisons:** Linda Ronstadt, Rita Coolidge, Carole King, Little Feat are editorial analogies, not documented sonic influences. The title “Paris Without You (St. Vincent’s Court)” is not a standalone album.

## Evidence boundaries and source quality

- `sounds_like` = editorial sonic resemblance. None of those artist names is used as a documented influence.
- `influenced_by` intentionally empty for all four; no qualified direct evidence of specific named influences on these recordings was found. Covering a composition, meeting another musician, or sharing a recording session is insufficient.
- Artist-wide influence research: Myspace profile metadata lists Janis Joplin and Rod Stewart under “Influenced By,” but this is **not** a verified interview or signed first-person statement; do not import into `influenced_by` or present as confirmed personal declaration. See https://myspace.com/kimcarnessongs.
