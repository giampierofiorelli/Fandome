# Kylie Minogue — Fandome Phase 05 evidence notes

**Scope:** original **2020 12-track DISCO**, **2023 11-track Tension**, and **2024 13-track Tension II**. Excludes deluxe tracks, extended mixes, streaming bonus tracks and later re-recordings. All eight scales are holistic editorial estimates anchored to the current Fandome guide, not borrowed critic scores. All 27-column rows have `status=draft`. Original credits are prioritised above aggregated shorthand.

## DISCO (2020) — `k-disco`

- **Identity, scope and genres:** The original [Kylie store track list](https://music.kylie.com/product/disco) has 12 tracks. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/disco-mw0003410698) directly names dance-pop, *Neo-Disco*, club/dance and left-field pop, with separate weights assigned in the row. [The Music review](https://themusic.com.au/reviews/kylie-minogue-disco-guido-farnell/lPiMhomIi4o/06-11-20) calls out synth-pop, disco house and electro disco. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-disco-review/) identifies filter-disco on **Miss a Thing / I Love It** specifically: a new *filterdisco* token is necessary rather than silently replacing it with filterhouse. Genres with just track-level support go last.
- **Scales:** energy 4 and tempo 4 for generally kinetic dance tracks with brief midtempo starts; electronic 4 because drum machines and synths are central but the credited record also has guitars, bass, keyboards, live drums and strings; mood 5 for escapist optimism, despite lyrical isolation; theatrical 3 for disco melodrama without rock-opera staging; experimental 1 for revivalist familiarity; polish 5 for layered production and carefully assembled remote sessions; groove 5 because dancing is the album's explicit purpose. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/nov/07/kylie-minogue-disco-review) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/disco-mw0003410698) support overall sound.
- **Instruments directly credited:** [original 2020 MusicBrainz release personnel](https://musicbrainz.org/release/8c0542ec-0859-4fee-93d4-6281c316ea7e) lists **drum machines** (Magic, Miss a Thing, Real Groove), guitars/keyboards (Magic), **bass/strings** (Miss a Thing and Real Groove), **synth/drums/guitar** (Monday Blues, Supernova, Last Chance), **clavinet and Rhodes electric piano** (Unstoppable), **synth bass** (I Love It), and vocoder (Supernova). The reviews additionally describe disco horn stabs. No speculative brand-name drum machine is inserted.
- **Production:** [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/8c0542ec-0859-4fee-93d4-6281c316ea7e) names drum programming, string arrangements, vocoder on Supernova, and *talkbox* on Dance Floor Darling; Slant directly names digital editing. In a [2020 artist interview](https://www.elle.com/culture/music/a34631960/kylie-minogue-disco-interview/) Kylie describes using **GarageBand** initially, then **Logic** for home vocal recording and remote collaboration. Both are specific to making this album. `garageband` is a **new production token**.
- **Producer reconciliation:** MusicBrainz lists PhD on Magic, a duo explicitly consisting of Daniel Davidsen and Peter Wallevik; both members are shown, not a duplicate `PhD`. The other directly credited producers across the original twelve are Sky Adams, Teemu Brunila, Nico Stadi, Biff/Richard Stannard, Jon Green, Duck Blackwell (on I Love It, though only an *additional producer* on Say Something), Kiris Houston and Troy Miller. **Ash Howes** is credited for programming on Say Something, not as main producer there. Kylie’s vocal-engineer credit is **not** a full album producer credit.
- **Documented album influences:** [Kylie's own Apple Music commentary](https://music.apple.com/us/album/disco-deluxe/1539031326) explicitly says **Last Chance** was inspired by **ABBA and the Bee Gees**; she also names Donna Summer and Chic among direct, enduring disco inspirations in [her 2020 interview](https://www.augsburger-allgemeine.de/kultur/gesellschaft/Interview-Kylie-Minogue-Meine-Liebesgeschichte-mit-Disco-begann-frueh-id58525256.html). These count as legitimate 2020 album influences, unlike contemporaries Dua Lipa and Jessie Ware, who recorded disco albums independently. Sounds-like is an editorial sonic comparison only.
- **Critical disagreement:** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/nov/07/kylie-minogue-disco-review) and [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/disco-mw0003410698) celebrate joyful cohesion; [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/kylie-minogue-disco/) calls the disco reconstruction thin. This criticism supports low *experimental*, not reducing evidence confidence.

## Tension (2023) — `k-tension`

- **Identity:** [BMG's contemporary announcement](https://www.bmg.com/news/Kylie-Minogue-album-Tension) gives the exact original eleven tracks. Do not score deluxe bonus **Love Train**, **Just Imagine**, **Somebody to Love** or extension remixes.
- **Genre boundaries:** [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/kylie-minogue-tension/) identifies synth-pop, Euro house, EDM and pounding piano-house title track; [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/kylie-minogue-tension-review-lyrics-tracklist-3501195) and [The Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/tension-kylie-minogue-joyous-return-to-the-dancefloor) identify full-album dance-pop / electropop. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/sep/22/kylie-tension-review-padam-was-just-for-starters) singles out R&B/disco on Hands and dance-house on the title track. [The Quietus](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/kylie-minogue-tension-review/) describes Hi-NRG intensity, a French Touch nod in One More Time, and an unusually pop-punk *You Still Get Me High*; those are **different scopes**. [Slant single review](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-tension-single-review/) identifies the future-house producer context of 10 Out of 10. Album voice `rap` is included for the **Kylie-performed** rap on *Hands*, described explicitly by the Line of Best Fit; it is a small component, not the main lead delivery.
- **Scales:** energy 4 / tempo 4 from club-heavy but not uniform arrangements; electronic 5 due to predominance of synth production; mood 4 as exultant music includes melancholic lyrical highs; theatrical 3 for knowing camp; experimental 2 for the more surprising Padam and title track, with conventional song forms elsewhere; polish 5 and groove 5 from tight contemporary club production and frequent four-on-the-floor momentum. BMG itself emphasises sensual club release alongside melancholic mood.
- **Instruments:** [MusicBrainz original album credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/7c88cc10-33b8-4737-b64c-4da9d24c19d5) credits keys on **Padam Padam**, a gospel choir on **Hold On to Now**, and explicitly lists **bass, brass, drums, handclaps, keyboards, percussion, vocoder** on **10 Out of 10**. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-tension-album-review/) identifies the Hold On guitar solo and **saxophone** in Green Light; [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/kylie-minogue-tension/) describes piano house. `synth` is directly described in contemporary reviews, and these were selected without assuming instruments from genre labels.
- **Production:** MusicBrainz identifies programming and explicit *vocoder* on 10 Out of 10. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-tension-single-review/) calls the title track's vocal hook stuttering and processed, supporting **vocalchops** (without calling it Auto-Tune). [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/kylie-minogue-tension-vocal-production) documents mobile vocal production but does not establish one DAW for every original song, so `logic` is omitted from this album's production column.
- **Producers only:** [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/7c88cc10-33b8-4737-b64c-4da9d24c19d5) establishes full-producer Biff Stannard, Duck Blackwell, Jon Green, Lostboy (Peter Rycroft), Cutfather, Daniel Davidsen, KayAndMusic, PhD (expanded to Daniel Davidsen and Peter Wallevik), Oliver Heldens and Jackson Foote. **Ryan Ashley** is credited vocal producer only, so omitted. **Martin Bijelić** is *additional producer*, not fully credited. **Kamille** is co-writer of the title song, not producer. **Oliver Heldens** has a main producer credit even though 10 Out of 10 was issued as his single.
- **Album influences:** `influenced_by` stays empty: references to French touch, Robyn or 1980s motifs are critical comparisons or genre lineage and do not identify an independently substantiated artist-to-album influence. *Hold On to Now* sounds Robyn-like in Pitchfork, hence sounds_like; this is not a claim she influenced the making of the track.
- **Critic disagreement:** [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/kylie-minogue-tension/) praises Kylie’s comfortable looseness but finds *Vegas High* / *10 Out of 10* weak; [The Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/tension-kylie-minogue-joyous-return-to-the-dancefloor) praises the dance focus. Both are recorded without changing descriptive scales to reproduce critic scores.

## Tension II (2024) — `kylie-tensionii`

- **Release vs reused songs:** [Kylie/BMG official product](https://music.kylie.com/product/tension-ii?cur=GBP) gives **nine brand-new Kylie studio tracks** (1–9) followed by four **already issued 2024 collaborative singles** (10–13) with The Blessed Madonna, Bebe Rexha/Tove Lo, Orville Peck/Diplo, and Sia. An official 13-track **studio album** (not a live album or greatest-hits compilation) nonetheless contains these reused singles. Album-scale and genre assessments give primary weight to the nine new songs, especially to avoid duplicate recommendation evidence.
- **Genres:** [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-review-3804266) and [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-album-review/) describe a stronger **electropop** concentration than the 2023 record; [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/1078862-kylie-minogue-tension-ii.php) lists **house, electro house, nu-disco and slap house**, with lower confidence for this aggregate source. [The Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-confirms-world) explicitly describes **Eurodisco** in *Taboo / Kiss Bang Bang*, **Balearic house** in *Good As Gone*, and **synth-pop** in *Hello*. [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/music/review/2024/10/18/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-review-one-sexy-banger-after-another/) identifies **Eurodance** on *Lights Camera Action*. These names remain distinct; **slaphouse** and **balearichouse** are new tokens, and `nudisco` is not converted to the separately listed `neodisco`. Country pop exists only in *Midnight Ride* and is placed last.
- **Scales:** energy 4, electronic 5, polish 5, groove 5 and tempo 4 due to compressed, heavy club focus; mood 3 allows for the deflated romantic and breakup lines in *Someone for Me*, *Good as Gone* and *Shoulda Left Ya*; theatrical 3 for cheeky spectacle; experimental 2 as occasional odd vocal effects emerge amid conventional four-on-the-floor structures. Relative to 2023 the package is more consistently electronic, but emotionally less unconditionally euphoric.
- **Voices:** lead clear / soft; NME explicitly identifies Kylie **spoken/speak-singing** on *Diamonds*, so `spoken` last. Features are different singers, not separate main voice taxonomy for Kylie.
- **Instruments and production:** [MusicBrainz original credited recordings](https://musicbrainz.org/release/74b01c55-398f-453e-83f7-0eda86f2ff33) explicitly names **bass, drums and synthesizer** for *Lights Camera Action*, keyboards and drums on *Taboo*, string ensemble (Wired Strings) on *Good as Gone*, and guitar on *My Oh My*. The [original Edge of Saturday Night song credits](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1759350298) also document **piano, electric piano, synthesizer and drums**. [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/tension-ii-kylie-minogue/r54g2uubmg4zc) credits programming on *Lights Camera Action*, drum programming on *Someone For Me* and string arrangement on *Good As Gone*. [Apple Music track-level personnel for Midnight Ride](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1778205555) name **baritone guitar, Mellotron, Rhodes electric piano, bass, drums and percussion**; these belong to **one already released guest track** and therefore appear lower in the album instrument hierarchy. [Renowned for Sound](https://renownedforsound.com/album-review-kylie-minogue-tension-ii/) explicitly calls out vocoder on *Dance To The Music*. No uncredited drum-machine brand or Auto-Tune plug-in is asserted.
- **Only credited producers:** [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/74b01c55-398f-453e-83f7-0eda86f2ff33) and [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/tension-ii-kylie-minogue/r54g2uubmg4zc) substantiate full producing roles on songs 1–9: Lewis Thompson, Steve Mac, Lostboy, Zhone, TMS, German, Neave Applebaum, Jennifer Decilveo, Vaughn Oliver, Punctual, Biff Stannard and Duck Blackwell. The Apple Music *Midnight Ride* track credits explicitly list Orville Peck, Diplo, Christopher Stracey, Picard Brothers as producers; [Apple Music original Dance Alone song credits](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1752435540) name Jesse Shatkin and Jim-E Stack; [Apple Music Edge Of Saturday Night credits](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1759350298) name The Blessed Madonna and Pat Alvarez as full producers. **Dance System**, Joy Anonymous and Jay Reynolds receive only additional-production roles and are excluded. **Adrià García** is an additional producer on *Lights Camera Action*, and is excluded. On *Good As Gone*, Qobuz labels **Tommy D** recording engineer while MusicBrainz labels **Tommy Danvers** producer: disputed and omitted, with the well-supported **TMS** left. The word *producer* next to an artist in a review alone was not treated as a production credit.
- **Credits / album billing:** Features do not make Tension II a co-billed album: the album is credited to Kylie; four songs carry varying collaborative single billings. No `coBilled.ts` entry. The album has four previously issued singles, so avoid misreporting it as 13 newly recorded songs.
- **Critics' disagreement:** [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-review-3804266) and [Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-confirms-world) celebrate the album's focused rhythm and fresh hooks; [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2024/oct/19/kylie-minogue-tension-ii-review-more-of-the-same-is-much-much-less) argues it repeats Padam's tricks with diminishing returns. Both agree it is an electronic dance-pop record.

## Reproducibility and caveats

1. The new token entries below must be added by Claude **before** running the validator; the three descriptor rows are strictly new phase-05 rows.
2. No producers from deluxe-only songs were imported. Kylie recording/editing her own vocals does not mean she has an album-wide producer credit.
3. `sounds_like` represents editorial timbral comparison, not documentable influence. `influenced_by` is deliberately empty for both Tension releases.
4. Genre phrases in prose (*disco house*, *filter disco*, *slap house*, *Balearic house*) are assigned their own exact tokens, rather than silently collapsing distinct source terms.
