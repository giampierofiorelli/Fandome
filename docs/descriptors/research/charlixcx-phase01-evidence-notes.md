# Fandome / Charli xcx — Phase 01: evidence and reasoning

Research date: 2026-10-24. **These are editorial drafts, not numerical measurements**. All 25 fields follow the current GitHub `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`. The CSV covers only three works released in 2013, 2014 and 2016. Track-specific genres have been retained as instructed, but the first genres represent the overall project and track-only scope is marked in `genre_evidence.csv`.

## 1. True Romance (2013) — `charlixcx-trueromance`

**Identity / genre.** Apple Music lists 13 songs, 12 April 2013. Marc Hogan (Pitchfork, 25 April 2013) describes a mix of pop hooks and moody 1980s synth-pop; Rebecca Nicholson (Guardian, 11 April 2013) describes inventive sample use and electronic production. Lucy Jones (NME, 19 April 2013) uses a goth-pop label; her disco description is about *Take My Hand*, and darkwave is about *Nuclear Seasons*. These last two tags are placed last so they do not define the entire album. AllMusic discusses the rap/R&B and neon-goth setting around this recording period; that is weaker evidence for album-wide `rnb` than the synth-pop evidence. This uncertainty is local to that tag, despite the row-level confidence reflecting agreement about the dominant sound. **New genre tokens:** `gothpop`, `darkwave`.

**Scores (editorial synthesis, not claims made numerically by reviewers).** `energy=3`: midtempo vs driving dance tracks, not uniform maximum intensity (Pitchfork + Apple). `electronic=4`: repeated synthesizer and programmed beat descriptions (Pitchfork + Apple). `mood=2`: brooding love/heartbreak mood is recurrent, with notable brighter dance songs (AllMusic + Pitchfork). `theatrical=3`: theatrical posture not as pronounced as a rock opera; strong pop performances and character changes (NME + Pitchfork). `experimental=3`: sampling surprises and odd arrangements within melodic structures (Guardian + Pitchfork). `polish=4`: layered produced sound and deliberate sequencing (Pitchfork + Guardian). `tempo=3`: fast dance tracks coexist with slower swoons (AllMusic + Apple). `groove=3`: definite dance elements but not a continuous club project (Apple + AllMusic).

**Voice / lyrics.** `raspy` is the closest available vocabulary entry to Apple Music's description of a husky timbre; this is a taxonomy approximation, not a clinical vocal evaluation. `love|heartbreak` from Pitchfork's album-level account. English-language lead vocals.

**Production.** Ariel Rechtshaid and Patrik Berger are both documented in NME and in the tenth-anniversary coverage; Ariel is the most recurrent name. This CSV names the two best-confirmed principal producers, not every track-level co-producer. A complete track-by-track credit audit is recommended if adding additional names.

**Relations.** `sounds_like=Grimes|Marina and the Diamonds|The Knife` is editorial sonic comparison explicitly connected to this era's album textures in Guardian/NME writing; it does **not** establish historically documented influence. `influenced_by` remains empty: comparisons, fandom and co-production are insufficient proof of influence on this specific album. `landmark=1` denotes a defining early work; debut status and anniversary retrospectives support its significance.

## 2. Sucker (2014) — `charlixcx-sucker`

**Identity / genre.** Pitchfork (Jamieson Cox, 12 December 2014) identifies power-pop as the main style, with punk and new wave elements. Alexis Petridis (Guardian, 12 February 2015) describes guitar power chords combined with synthesizers, and disputes treating the whole record as genuinely radical punk. Kitty Empire (Guardian, 15 February 2015) identifies pop-punk on *London Queen*, an EDM build on *Break the Rules*, and grunge guitar motifs also on *Break the Rules*. These are **local elements**, not the dominant album identity. **New genre tokens:** `poppunk`, `newwave`, `grunge`.

**Scores.** `energy=4`: consistently brisk, forceful shout-and-chant hooks (Pitchfork + Guardian). `electronic=2`: guitars/drums and rock arrangements are more foregrounded than in True Romance, despite synths and an EDM section (Petridis + Empire). `mood=4`: defiant and upbeat overall; not exclusively joyous because of dismissive and ironic lyrics (Cox + Empire). `theatrical=3`: expressive bravado and vocal layering but compact songs (Cox). `experimental=2`: pop songwriting uses established new-wave/punk pop vocabulary, with Petridis expressly questioning the record's radicalism. `polish=4`: radio-directed arrangements with multiple experienced producers (Cox + Billboard). `tempo=4`: brisk pulse with occasional midtempo tracks (Cox). `groove=3`: some dance-ready songs, but guitar-led movement is not the same as continuous club grooves (Guardian).

**Voice / lyrics.** `raspy` approximates the sneering, brash lead delivery emphasized by critics; `party|irony` reflects the party lyrics and taunting wit, but romance exists too. English vocals.

**Producers.** Patrik Berger is prominent in Pitchfork; Billboard names additional credited producer-songwriter contributions from Greg Kurstin, Benny Blanco, Ariel Rechtshaid and Stargate. Names are producers, not the artists who influenced the record.

**Relations.** Sonic comparisons One Direction, Kelly Clarkson and Gwen Stefani are anchored in Pitchfork/Guardian discussion. `influenced_by=The Strokes|Weezer` is a **critic-attributed connection** (Pitchfork says the production/songwriting borrows their devices); it is not a first-hand statement by Charli. NME carries an artist statement about a separate Swedish-punk listening/writing period. Do not conflate an unreleased punk record with an officially released version of *Sucker*. `landmark=1` because of documented commercial breakthrough context.

## 3. Vroom Vroom (2016) — `charlixcx-vroomvroom`

**Identity.** This is **an EP**, not a full studio album or mixtape. Apple Music lists the original 26 February 2016 four-track version. The storefront suffix “- EP” is not part of the work title in the Fandome `title` field. The release is **not currently in `catalog.ts`**, so Claude should add `Vroom Vroom`/2016 under the existing `charlixcx` row. `charlixcx-vroomvroom` is the expected slug.

**Genre / sound.** Andrew Ryce (Resident Advisor, 1 March 2016) documents SOPHIE's album-spanning production, experimental pop, a `gothtrap` element co-produced with Jodie Harsh on *Secret (Shh)*, and Eurodance on *Paradise*. Laura Snapes (Pitchfork, 23 March 2016) calls *Paradise* Eurotrance, *Trophy* glitchy dancehall and *Secret (Shh)* grime-influenced. Adam P. Newton (Bearded Gentlemen Music, 23 March 2016) reports broader EP elements from EDM, footwork, hip-hop and garage. Apple Music describes neo-rave on *Trophy*. The FADER (Grace Byron, 24 February 2026) retrospectively characterizes an industrial electronic feel; retrospective accounts link the work to the *later* hyperpop movement. Hyperpop is a **later classification**, not the term universally used in 2016. Source-specific genre evidence and scope are preserved in `genre_evidence.csv`. **New genre tokens:** `experimentalpop`, `industrial`, `eurotrance`, `grime`, `footwork`, `garage`, `neorave`, `gothtrap`. `garage` is retained verbatim rather than silently converted to `ukgarage`, because the source does not specify that narrower form.

**Scores.** `energy=5`: sonic intensity and dance-driven peaks reported by RA, Pitchfork and FADER. `electronic=5`: SOPHIE's highly synthetic drum/bass timbres throughout (Apple + RA). `mood=2`: abrasive, menacing tracks, with brighter *Paradise*; intensity is **not** cheerfulness (RA + Pitchfork). `theatrical=4`: exaggerated vocal personas and confrontational, hyperbolic presentation (RA). `experimental=5`: fragmentary, radical sonic contrasts relative to standard pop structures (RA + 2016 Pitchfork + 2026 FADER). `polish=4`: meticulous sound-design production despite deliberate abrasion; not lo-fi (RA + Bearded Gentlemen). `tempo=4`: predominantly club energy, but timbral changes and drop sections rather than uniformly fast BPM (RA). `groove=4`: designed for clubs though unusual rhythm changes disturb conventional dance flow (FADER + RA).

**Voice / lyrics.** `voice` intentionally left blank: the 4 tracks alternate rap-like, spoken, pitched-up and melodic singing; the allowed single-label enum obscures this. `party|identity` reflects hedonism and self-assertion described by reviewers; vocals predominantly English.

**Producers.** SOPHIE is clearly album-wide; RA specifically reports Jodie Harsh co-production on one track. Both are shown with this distinction. Do **not** list SOPHIE as `influenced_by`: she directly made the EP. `sounds_like=M.I.A.|Britney Spears|Uffie` reflects editorial comparisons (Apple; Pitchfork; The FADER 2016). No independent documentary evidence for **artist-to-artist influence** is asserted, so `influenced_by` is blank.

**Interpretive disagreement.** The 2016 Pitchfork review was strongly negative, while later Pitchfork/FADER retrospectives regard the sound as prescient. Those opinions do **not** make the production attributes incompatible; `confidence=high` describes converging evidence on sound and credits, not unanimous quality appraisal.

## Source / rights policy

- Only bibliographic metadata and short **original paraphrases** are stored here; no downloaded article bodies or long copied review passages.
- Genre descriptors are sourced per album and per track; absence of a reference is not proof of an absent genre.
- A release-type field is not a recording-genre field. These titles include one EP in a distinct release category.
- `status=draft` for all three. A second reviewer must check the source links and conflicting claims before changing to `reviewed`.
- Audit release versions before extending credits or extracting song-level tags. The full discography is scheduled across multiple future batches rather than guessing remaining descriptors now.
