# Queen — Phase 02 evidence notes

**Evidence standard:** Original 1976–1980 albums, not 2011 remixes, deluxe bonus tracks, singles versions or concert recordings. `R`, `N`, `J`, `G` source IDs match `queen_phase02_sources.csv`; all genre tokens individually linked in the genre-evidence CSV. Fandome’s 0–5 numeric descriptors are **editorial album-level assessments** anchored to `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md`, not quoted measurements. A source describing one song is not presented as proof of an entire album characteristic.

## `queen-adayattheraces` — A Day at the Races (1976)

**Production and credits.** Queen are the **sole credited producers**, and Mike Stone is the engineer rather than co-producer [R1,R3,R4]. Original UK LP says **“No Synths!”** [R4]. Main credits and track credits establish Freddie’s piano, Brian’s guitars, harmonium, piano, slide guitar and Roger’s drum kit/percussion plus John’s acoustic guitar [R3,R4]. `tambourine` is credited to Roger on *Teo Torriatte* [R9]. `timpani` is supported by track-level original personnel compiled in the Queen discographic research; the instrument appears on *Drowse*, but is a minor inclusion rather than a dominant characteristic [R9]. “Plastic piano” is grouped under `piano`, because it is not independently proven to be a distinct keyboard technology. The “orchestra” Brian directs in sleeve terminology is **overdubbed electric guitars**, not a hired string orchestra: do **not** populate `orchestra` or `strings` [R3,R4].

**Vocal production.** `layeredvocals` and `overdubbing` are sustained by the recording of *Somebody to Love*: Brian, Freddie and Roger constructed a mass gospel choir through successive vocal harmonies [R5,R6]. `choral` describes a vocal type, not actual outside gospel singers. The ensemble explicitly recreates the choir itself. No exact tape machine or plug-in is claimed. `influenced_by=Aretha Franklin` is **album-track specific**: Freddie Mercury and Brian May explicitly describe Aretha’s effect on *Somebody to Love* [R6]. No source found strong enough to tag further album-wide named musical inspirations. `sounds_like` consists of **audited editorial sonic comparisons**, not influence claims.

**Language and lyrics.** English is the main language; the originally released final track *Teo Torriatte* contains **two Japanese-language choruses**, so `en|ja` is justified, despite the small proportion of Japanese [R7]. `love`, `heartbreak` and `spiritual` primarily reflect *You Take My Breath Away*, *Somebody to Love* and *Teo Torriatte*; `social` comes from *White Man* and `storytelling` from character songs [R1,R2]. These are album-level thematic aggregations based on the originals, not literal quotations.

**Scale notes.** `energy=4`: driving *Tie Your Mother Down* and *White Man* counterbalance slower vocal ballads [R1,R2]. `electronic=1`: guitars use layered amplification but the sleeve denies synthesizers [R4]. `mood=3`: yearning gospel/ballad material alongside buoyant Lover Boy [R1,R2]. `theatrical=4`: operatic choir, theatrical phrasing, music-hall changes, just below the guide’s *A Night at the Opera* 5 anchor [R2,R6]. `experimental=3`: inventive styles, less episodic than the 1975 predecessor [R2]. `polish=4`: refined studio arrangements and dense overdubs, but less lavish than the guide’s 1975 5 benchmark [R2]. `tempo=3`: mostly brisk rock with substantial slow and midtempo songs [R1,R2]. `groove=2`: propulsive rock pulses, with few rhythm-first dance tracks [R2]. **Critical nuance:** AllMusic calls it tighter and less eccentric than *A Night at the Opera*, not artistically empty; avoid extrapolating broad gospel or hard metal from an isolated track [R2].

## `queen-newsoftheworld` — News of the World (1977)

**Credits controversy.** The original 1977 sleeve says **“Produced by Queen, assisted by Mike Stone; engineered by Mike Stone”** [N3,N4]. A QueenOnline 2017 writer loosely calls it “co-produced” with Stone; MusicBrainz sometimes models the same person as assistant and co-producer [N6]. The strongest **contemporary credit** is assisted producer, so per Fandome’s rule `producers=Queen` and **do not include Mike Stone as producer**. The choice is explicit rather than silently resolving contradictory retrospective credit taxonomies.

**Instrumentation.** A drum-and-stomp recording underlies *We Will Rock You*; MusicBrainz identifies *footstomps* and *handclaps* separately, so `footstomps` requires a new token [N6]. John Deacon, Brian May, Roger Taylor and Freddie Mercury are credited with the stomps and claps; recording was not built from a synthesized drum machine [N6]. Freddie’s piano, Taylor drums/percussion, Deacon bass, May guitar, plus John and Brian acoustic guitars, Brian’s maracas and Freddie’s cowbell on *Who Needs You* are credibly documented [N3,N4,N9]. `maracas` and `cowbell` require new tokens. No *trumpet* is played on *Sleeping on the Sidewalk*: the song’s lyrics mention one; do not infer an instrument from words. Nor is a synth credited on *Get Down, Make Love*: its alien electronic effects arise from guitar, vocal effects and a **harmonizer** described in band interviews [N5]. The exact harmonizer model is not validated for this LP; don't tag `eventideh910`.

**Recording and production.** `harmonizer` is directly confirmed by May and Taylor [N5]. `onetake` applies *only to the basic track* of *Sleeping on the Sidewalk*; May later described editing/chopping it, so the finished album or even the finished song is not untouched one-take audio [N5,N8]. `analogtape` is supported by Brian’s reflections on the vintage master tape, saturation and fader moves [N5]. `overdubbing` persists, particularly *Who Needs You* vocal stacking; `layeredvocals` remains important on *We Are the Champions*, though the album is much less saturated with layers than the preceding two albums [N1,N9].

**Genres / lyrical themes.** The *Sheer Heart Attack* track is Queen’s explicit punk-rock salute per AllMusic, not a punk-rock album [N7]. *Sleeping on the Sidewalk* has blues shuffle, *Fight from the Inside* a neo-disco beat, *Get Down, Make Love* mechanical funk, *My Melancholy Blues* cod-jazz phrasing, *Who Needs You* Latin colours [N2,N9,N10]. `sensuality` anchored to *Get Down, Make Love*; `introspection` to *All Dead, All Dead*, whose cat-related genesis Brian has documented [N8]. No specific named musical artist has sufficient evidence to enter `influenced_by` for the complete album: stylistic dialogue with punk does not license guessing “Sex Pistols” as an influence.

**Scales.** `energy=4`: stadium percussion, muscular riffs, one near-punk speed track, balanced by jazz-tinged material [N2]. `electronic=1`: instrument-driven guitars and drums; harmonizer in a particular track does not make it a synth-pop album [N5]. `mood=2`: anthems alongside grief, cold eroticism and disquiet; AllMusic calls the record chilly despite stylistic diversity [N2]. `theatrical=4`: stadium chants and dramatic Mercury vocals, less studio operatic than 1975 [N2]. `experimental=2`: deliberately leaner compositions with one conspicuous effects episode [N1,N5]. `polish=3`: restrained compared with the luxurious 1975/76 LPs, not badly engineered [N1]. `tempo=3`: dynamic slow-and-fast spread [N2]. `groove=3`: rhythmic clap/stomp and funk/disco cuts increase bodily drive compared with 1976 [N2].

## `queen-jazz` — Jazz (1978)

**Credits and production.** Queen and Roy Thomas Baker are both **full credited producers**, while Geoff Workman and John Etchells were engineers, not co-producers [J1,J3,J10]. Original sessions were in Switzerland and France, unlike earlier UK-based albums [J1]. *Jazz* is **not primarily jazz music** despite the title; do not populate the genre token `jazz` from title alone [J2,J7]. The bulk is layered hard rock and melodic rock; *Dreamer’s Ball* has an old-fashioned ballad arrangement but no sufficiently strong source to add a separate blues genre token [J2]. `pianorock` comes from genre metadata [J8], not an inference from the presence of a piano.

**Instrument evidence.** Freddie piano/vocals, May electric and acoustic guitar, Deacon bass plus acoustic guitar, Taylor drum kit/percussion are directly credited [J3,J10]. An audible ensemble of **bicycle bells** is specifically credited to all four musicians on *Bicycle Race* [J4]; `bicyclebell` is a new instrument token. Do not label actual sampled cycling noises or an unsourced machine. Roger is credited with performing bass/rhythm guitar for some of his songs [J3,J10], not a different outside bassist. Studio reconstruction of *Fun It* specifies **a close-miked acoustic drum kit** and voice/groove effects; it is a disco/funk approach but **not a documented drum-machine part** [J5].

**Vocal techniques.** `overdubbing` and `layeredvocals`: Stephen Thomas Erlewine explicitly discusses May/Mercury multitracking [J2], and a peer-reviewed audio production analysis of Queen’s sound describes *Leaving Home Ain’t Easy* harmony layering (supplement: https://www.arpjournal.com/asarpwp/the-development-of-the-epic-queen-sound/). `pitchedvocals`, `vocalfilter` and `distortedvocals` are **limited to Brian May’s modified vocal part on Leaving Home Ain’t Easy**, per detailed studio-style listening analysis [J6]; no particular harmonizer or synth model is named. Those per-track production tokens are retained but must not be used to present the entire LP as vocal-electronic. *More of That Jazz* inserts short excerpts from the same album [J9], so those snippets must **not** be reclassified as outside sampled influences, remixes or a separate album.

**Lyrical themes / contrasts.** *Bicycle Race*, *Fat Bottomed Girls*, *Let Me Entertain You* and *Don't Stop Me Now* justify irreverent, sensual and exuberant lyrical tags [J2,J7]. The original *Mustapha* has stylised non-English/Arabic-sounding syllables, but coherent Arabic-dominant singing for the album cannot be demonstrated; `language=en` is intentionally conservative. The 1978 Rolling Stone review was openly hostile to the album, whereas later AllMusic/Guardian reviews value its energy and excess [J2,J7]. We register the disagreement rather than averaging away perspectives.

**Scales.** `energy=4`: high-velocity *Dead on Time* and *Don't Stop Me Now* against softer tracks [J2]. `electronic=1`: amplified guitar with active multitrack studio work; recorded drum kit in *Fun It* and no verified album-specific synthesizer [J3,J5]. `mood=4`: notably humorous and buoyant, with select wistful pieces [J2]. `theatrical=4`: Mercury caricature, layered choruses, emphatic arrangements [J2]. `experimental=3`: eclectic songs and vocal transformation on one track, without 1975-style suites [J2,J6]. `polish=4`: renewed Baker-assisted density and more guitar overdubs than *News of the World* [J1,J2]. `tempo=4`: many fast, busy or brisk tracks, others slow [J2]. `groove=3`: *Fun It* and buoyant rock add danceability without continuous club structure [J2,J5].

## `queen-thegame` — The Game (1980)

**Credit and model verification.** The actual 1980 sleeve distinguishes **“Produced by Queen”** and **“Engineered and Co-Produced by Mack”** [G4]. Because Mack is genuinely a credited co-producer, Fandome’s `producers=Queen|Mack` is warranted [G1,G4]. The first Queen synthesizer is **Oberheim OB-X**, prominently used at *Play the Game*’s opening, confirmed by sleeve and MusicBrainz [G3,G4]. `oberheimobx` is a NEW machine token: do **not** substitute `oberheimob8`, `oberheim` or invent DX7. Freddie, May, Taylor and Mack are identified with synth tasks in detailed secondary original-track credits, but not all play the OB-X on all songs [G9]. `synth` is therefore behind the traditional rhythm section in instruments.

**Disco groove / tape production.** Mack’s first-person interview says he **created a drum loop and backwards piano-note sound** on *Another One Bites the Dust* and used the **Publison Infernal Machine** for a mid-song processed percussion effect [G5,G6]. The special equipment `publisoninfernal` needs a new token, while `drumloop` denotes the constructed loop, rather than a programmed drum machine. Brian May’s Eventide *harmonizer* is also documented on that track, but the model is NOT specified; thus add `harmonizer`, not `eventideh910` [G7]. Mack explicitly described reversing tape, supporting `backmasking` and `analogtape` [G5]. Track also has electric guitar, Deacon bass, piano, handclaps and Taylor’s natural acoustic kit; the loop does not imply TR-808, LinnDrum or any particular sampler [G7,G9]. Other tracks retain the band’s recognizable overdubbed vocals and guitars [G2,G3].

**Influences carefully separated.** Official Queen history and Mack/Nile Rodgers accounts directly document the bass style of **Chic / Good Times** inspiring Deacon’s *Another One Bites the Dust* [G1,G5,G7,G11]. Brian May explicitly calls *Crazy Little Thing Called Love* Freddie’s tribute to **Elvis Presley** [G8]. `influenced_by=Chic|Elvis Presley` refers to distinct album tracks and is NOT based on a guest performance, copyright sample or sonic similarity alone. `sounds_like` is a separate editorial field: e.g. The Cars/Bee Gees as comparisons have not been asserted as Queen's inspirations.

**Genre reconciliation.** Album is a deliberate pivot to polished pop-rock and multiple funk/disco grooves, but more than half of its songs involve guitars, ballads or rock-and-roll [G1,G2,G10]. `rockabilly` belongs principally to one key track; `hardrock`, `britishmetal`, `heavymetal` are lower-priority AllMusic genre labels for still-present rock material, not the album’s defining sound [G2]. Contemporary listeners disagreed about the disco shift; the band’s history notes both fans gained and those alienated [G1]. `lyrics` tags draw from song narratives of relationships, suicide and danger; avoid making an album-wide political statement.

**Scales.** `energy=3`: rhythmic singles and rock songs balanced by reflective ballads [G2]. `electronic=2`: first synth plus treated effects, while most instruments remain guitars/bass/live drums [G1,G3,G7]. `mood=4`: buoyant pop, playful retro rock, with sadder *Save Me* [G2]. `theatrical=3`: still bold Mercury presence but less epic or operatic [G2]. `experimental=2`: bold stylistic reset and loop editing with mostly simple song structures [G5,G2]. `polish=4`: Munich studio clarity and early modern pop technique [G1,G2]. `tempo=3`: middling mix of brisk dance-rock and slower ballads [G2]. `groove=3`: the strongest tracks are dance-driven, but the album is not a continuous dance record [G1,G2].

## Cross-album safeguards

- All four original albums are **studio records**. The chronologically released 1979 *Live Killers* is deliberately excluded: live recordings recycle earlier compositions and do not materially describe novel studio sound.
- 1976, 1977 and 1978 albums were recorded **without a confirmed synthesizer**. Do not confuse Brian May guitar-orchestration or 1977 harmonizer experiments with synth instrumentation. The first album with a documented Oberheim OB-X is *The Game* [R4,N5,G4].
- Genres such as AllMusic’s `contemporarypoprock`, `britishmetal`, `arenarock` are included because sources **explicitly assign those taxonomies**. Their generic applicability is labelled low/medium where appropriate. No new genre is proposed by Phase 02; `arenarock` and `britishmetal` were already requested as new in Phase 01, so the importer must add them if Phase 01 has not yet been imported.
- All noted `sounds_like` artists are **editorial resemblance candidates** meant for curation, never disguised causal influences.
- Production fields contain only credits or documented track-level processes. Unknown machine models are omitted, including any attempt to back-fill synthesizers onto *Jazz*.
