# Troye Sivan — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes (2014–2020)

Research checkpoint: 25 October 2026. Four **incremental** descriptor rows only; Phase 02 covers the 2023 and 2026 studio albums. The 2015 WILD EP is audited but intentionally not added because the entire six-song EP appears on the 2015 Blue Neighbourhood deluxe; standard release basis is set separately. This avoids duplicate sonic descriptions. The pre-major early EPs are omitted as non-defining. No live records. All scores are independent editorial judgments inferred from detailed critical descriptions, **not measured audio features**. Source identifiers appear in the sources and genre-evidence CSVs.

## Scale and source discipline
- Each 0–5 scale covers the original whole release. `mood` is emotional color, **not** tempo or danceability; lyrics alone do not determine sound. `voice=soft|clear` reflects documented restrained/lilting vocal presentation; no `powerful` just because some choruses swell. `theatrical` means demonstrable dramatic delivery, not queer subject matter, artist image, or videos.
- Producer columns include only documented producer/co-producer/additional producer roles, **not** mixing engineers, featured singers, mere writers or session musicians.
- `sounds_like` is a sourced editorial sonic comparison; `influenced_by` stays empty here. Influences (causal) must not be inferred from styles, queer lineage, sampled recordings, visual homages, collaborators or critics comparing tracks. Reviewers hear Frank Ocean in the *Animal* bridge and Dean Blunt in one 2020 track, but this does not justify a whole-release influence automatically.
- A genre row can be justified at **track-level**, but its scope remains visible. Genre list length has no preset limit, yet evidence must still justify inclusion. No new genre tokens proposed in this phase.

## 2014 — TRXYE (`troye-trxye`, landmark EP)
- First major-label EP (15 August 2014, five original tracks), not a full studio LP; Universal Music Italia independently provides all per-track production credits; AllMusic and Apple Music supply critical style context (T14_UM, T14_AM, T14_AP, T14_MB).
- The sound is electronic pop with relaxed lead singing, keyboard layers, some R&B-grooved darker tracks and brighter dance-pop hooks; `genres=pop|electronic|electropop|dancepop|leftfieldpop|rnb`. R&B is **track-level**, not dominant. AllMusic's James Blake comparison belongs to `sounds_like` only.
- `energy=2, tempo=2, groove=2`: moderate, often held-back beat and resonant intimate vocals despite some more urgent hooks. `electronic=4`: heavily electronic but not a purely synthetic/instrumental album. `mood=2`: brooding/uneasy textures plus occasional lightness. `theatrical=2`: emotive without operatic scale. `experimental=2`: layered electronics but recognizably accessible structures. `polish=4`: carefully studio-produced major-label pop. `voice=soft|clear`: relaxed/controlled voice (not rap or powerful belting). Lyrics introspection/relationships supported by release summaries.
- Producer roster from label: Alex JL Hiew, SLUMS, Tat Tong, Matt Gio, Lindsay Rimes, Dan Heath, no unverified additions. `landmark=1` based on first major-label crossover, not implied critic consensus about quality. `confidence=high` for broad identity, medium for precise scores.

## 2015 — Blue Neighbourhood (`troye-blueneighbourhood`, studio)
- Original **2015 ten-song standard edition**. AllMusic original listing, Guardian contemporary reviews, MusicBrainz original recording credits, and Sivan's interview T15_INT supply distinction; WILD preview EP six songs fully appear on deluxe, **not** a separate extra album.
- Main styles pop, electropop, programmed/electronic pop and some dance-pop, left-field pop cataloguing. EDM is a **texture** explicitly discussed by Guardian, not a dominant festival-house identity. The emotional arc is youth, nostalgia, relationship anxiety and isolation (T15_GU2, T15_INT).
- `energy=3, electronic=4, tempo=3, groove=3`: heavier processed rhythms than TRXYE yet many slow/anthemic emotional tracks; no claim of actual BPM mean. `mood=2`: even upbeat 'Wild'/'Youth' called melancholy. `theatrical=3`: larger choruses but intimate storytelling. `experimental=2` accessible pop with production detail; `polish=4` multilayered, finished. `voice=soft|clear`, controlled singing emphasized over power belting. Album-wide values, no remix contamination.
- Producers from AllMusic standard credits: Alex Hope, Alex JL Hiew, SLUMS, Bram Inscore, Pip Norman, Caleb Nott, Emile Haynie; no musicians/mixers conflated. `sounds_like=Lorde|Sam Smith`: generic pop benchmark/critical contemporary likeness, **not** proof of direct influences. `landmark=1` as landmark studio debut.

## 2018 — Bloom (`troye-bloom`, studio)
- Original ten songs, 31 August 2018. AllMusic genre/production credits; Pitchfork, Guardian, NME and artist first-person Apple Music explanation (T18_AP).
- Song-led pop, dance-pop and electropop; R&B only secondary Pitchfork taxonomy; quieter guitar ballads are important but **not** evidence that album is primarily folk. `genres=pop|dancepop|electropop|leftfieldpop|rnb|electronic` with scoping in evidence CSV.
- `energy=3, electronic=4, tempo=3, groove=3`: bigger shiny dance-pop singles with reflective slower half. `mood=4`: **artist states** this was more upbeat/happier and romantic, unlike earlier melancholy, though critics emphasize delicate vulnerable moments. `theatrical=3`, `experimental=2`: polished conventional pop not experimental merely due to sexual subject matter. `polish=4` well-produced mainstream studio set. `voice=soft|clear`; intentionally **not powerful** from arena-style singles.
- Producers: Oscar Görres, Alex Hope, Bram Inscore, Ariel Rechtshaid, Jam City, Oscar Holter, Buddy Ross, Bobby Krlic (additional producer); music and mixing staff differentiated. Explicit track-level sonic reference to Frank Ocean's *Blonde* on 'Animal' from Pitchfork stays **out** of album-level `influenced_by`; similar caution with Lorde, retained only in `sounds_like`. NME's enthusiasm vs Pitchfork's concern over sonic homogeneity documented, not hidden.

## 2020 — In a Dream (`troye-inadream`, landmark EP)
- Six original songs released August 21, 2020. Some later streams/listings show seven with 'Take Yourself Home (Acoustic)'; **exclude acoustic bonus from original analysis**. Reviewed separately as an EP, not a third studio album.
- Pitchfork calls the six-song project adventurous and indie-pop adjacent with warped indie-rock and tech-house; Slant hears the same synthetic dance energy as Sivan's earlier pop with limited real departure. **Substantive critical disagreement**: high experimentation relative to prior output vs embellished synth-pop continuity. Proposed `experimental=4` is provisional editorial synthesis; Claude may reasonably lower to 3.
- `energy=3, electronic=4, tempo=3, groove=3`: synth-driven dance-pop with at least one distinct guitar interlude, stronger 'Stud' club passages; the entire EP is not tech-house. `mood=2`: sadness, longing and unstable self-discovery under colorful/occasionally euphoric beats; this is not a 'happy dance album'. `theatrical=3` expression/abstract effects; `polish=4` intentional studio sonic detail.
- `voice=soft|clear`: softly sung voice despite heavy Auto-Tune on some tracks; processed vocals are **not a separate allowed voice type**. `lyrics=heartbreak|introspection|identity|sensuality`. Verified producer roles Oscar Görres and Teo Halm (track credits T20_MB and T20_AM); do **not** include Leland/Tayla Parx just because writing and background vocals.
- `sounds_like=Negative Gemini|Vegyn|Rostam` comes from Pitchfork's **critical sonics comparison**, not artist self-report or proven influences. `influenced_by` blank. `landmark=1`: major aesthetic shift and standalone EP, supported by Pitchfork's judgement; 2020 EP is not a live album.
