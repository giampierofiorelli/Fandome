# Janet Jackson Phase 03 — album-specific evidence notes (draft)

Research cutoff **2026-10-26**. All 0–5 scores below are calibrated editorial ratings, **not claims that a source assigned the number**. They use cited reviews about the named album only. Where evidence does not specify a device, the row does not invent one.

## `janet-20yo` — *20 Y.O.* (2006)

### Genre and mood

Album of the Year names **Contemporary R&B** as primary and **Pop Rap**, **Dance-Pop** secondary; AllMusic includes R&B and adult contemporary R&B. Classic Pop identifies hip-hop beats, 1980s synthesizer sounds and sampling on the first half. Sal Cinquemani’s later *Discipline* review explicitly describes “Get It Out Me” as 1980s freestyle, a **one-track** label, not a whole-album tag. Production engineer report (*Mix*, Gallagher) explicitly describes contemporary dance-oriented beats and a pop/R&B production lineage, blending Jam/Lewis' layered arrangements and Dupri's more aggressive hip-hop beats. Genre evidence file records separate scope for each. 

`energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: strong club pulse on “So Excited”, “Get It Out Me” and others balanced by an extended run of slow love songs. `electronic=3`: documented MPC and digital tracking, synthesizers and samples coexist with actual guitar, strings, percussion. `mood=4`: album primarily playful and romantic rather than severe or socially urgent (*AllMusic*, *The New Yorker*). `theatrical=2` and `experimental=1`: generally direct, conventional pop/R&B songwriting with transitions and prominent production. `polish=5`: multilayer vocal, studio-engineered orchestration, Pro Tools workflow.

### Verified instruments and production

*Mix* engineer testimony: Jimmy Jam/Jermaine Dupri record sessions with separate rooms on **Pro Tools HD3 / HD2**, exchanging project files; Dupri uses the **Akai MPC60II**, sampling synth patches; Jimmy Jam states Janet often records 30+ background vocal tracks; keyboard/programmer Avila brothers and guitarist Paul Jackson Jr. involved. Label / AllMusic and MusicBrainz credits independently give **keyboards, drums, percussion, bass, guitar, violin, viola, cello**, and Suzie Katayama **string arrangements**. These support `protools|sampling|drumprogramming|layeredvocals|stringarrangements|mpc60ii`. No unsupported specific synth keyboard models are placed in the row (Mix lists machines in Dupri's wider kit without establishing that each appears on a released Janet track). The `synth` instrument stems from *Classic Pop* specific album analysis of 80s synths and *Mix* synth-patch discussion.

### Producers / influence discipline

Janet Jackson, Jimmy Jam, Terry Lewis, Jermaine Dupri, LRoc, Manuel Seal, Bobby Ross Avila, Issiah Avila and No I.D. credited as producer/co-producer (AllMusic; Qobuz; track metadata). Do not include the named guest singers, arranger Suzie Katayama, assistant engineers, or Luny Tunes' standalone remix. The project aimed to recapture sonics of *Control*; that's **self-referential branding, not proof another artist influenced the music**, so `influenced_by` blank. `sounds_like` is a clearly editorial sonic-neighbour comparison.

## `janet-discipline` — *Discipline* (2008)

### Genre and mood

AOTY genre list: **Electropop, Contemporary R&B, Dance-Pop, Nu-Disco, Electro House**. AllMusic R&B / Adult Contemporary R&B. *Classic Pop* explicitly calls “Rock With U” house and describes the rest of the upbeat set as electro-inflected. Slant directly calls the same song **Euro-disco** and notes Daft Punk's sample on “So Much Betta”. *Washington Post* hears **French house** in “Rock With U”, explicitly **not** a claim that this album is produced in France. Associated Press calls it **pop-house**. The Washington Post identifies *The 1* as old-school **funk**. These narrower subgenres occupy single-track scopes.

`energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4`: fast, forceful opener and dance-club run through “2Nite”, tempered by mid-tempo ballads and many interludes. `electronic=4`: squelchy synth/keyboard and vocoder arrangements dominate, with credited guitar and horns. `mood=3`: ebullient dance tracks against yearning and intimate material. `theatrical=3`: concept DJ/interludes with highly stylised processed voice but no overarching narrative. `experimental=2`: some futuristic vocal processing, more conventional hooks and grooves. `polish=5`: multi-producer, high-gloss modern studio album (Slant, AllMusic, Washington Post). `voice=soft|clear|spoken`: Janet's breathy lines plus speaking/interludes, without misclassifying Missy Elliott's guest rap as Janet's lead vocal type.

### Verified instruments and production

*Washington Post*: synth and keyboards, synthesized string swells and horn blasts; specifically describes “Rock With U” as **vocoder-swathed**. Slant: robotic synthetic voice on “Feedback”, and **Daft Punk-sampling** on “So Much Betta”. Original Qobuz track credits for “Discipline”: electric guitar by Robert Taylor; Apple Music independently identifies guitarist. Herb Music personnel names several guitars on specific tracks. Original producer/engineer credits include editing work, warranting `digitalediting`; unlike *Unbreakable* no identified Ensoniq Mirage. Synth strings are **not** counted as real violin/orchestra.

### Producers / disputed credits

No Jimmy Jam/Terry Lewis. Producers and co-producers compiled from original 22-track Qobuz and AllMusic roles: Rodney Jerkins, D’Mile, Jermaine Dupri, Janet Jackson (credited only on certain recordings), Stargate, Alex Greggs, Eric Stamile (co), David Gough, Terius Nash, Tricky Stewart, Shea Taylor, Manuel Seal (co), Ne-Yo (co), No I.D. (co). Songwriter-only names (Johntá Austin, LaShawn Daniels) excluded. Ian Cross frequently appears on a broad Qobuz `Producer` field alongside `Additional Producer`, while other sources chiefly say engineer/vocal producer: excluded pending liner confirmation. Chuck Harmony is a producer on the **deluxe bonus track** “Let Me Know” and thus excluded from original album row. Source credits disagree in granularity, not the album's central sound; `confidence=high` reflects strong original discography and multiple expert reviews, with this credit qualification explicitly preserved.

## `janet-unbreakable` — *Unbreakable* (2015)

### Genre and mood

AOTY names **Dance-Pop, Contemporary R&B**, plus **Electropop** and **Pop Soul**. AllMusic also files under R&B / Adult Contemporary R&B, describes **classic Philly soul** and **Minneapolis funk**, while noting many lyrical themes. *Classic Pop* explicitly labels “The Great Forever” **synth-pop**, “Dammn Baby” **hip-pop**, and “Well Traveled” a **power ballad**. Slant labels “Night” **electro-disco**, “Shoulda Known Better” **trance**, “Broken Hearts Heal” *Off the Wall*-era **disco**; Apple Music calls “Night” **house**-tinged. *The Observer* calls “Lessons Learned” a **country**-leaning guitar ballad. The Edge explicitly discusses **neo-soul**, **jazz** chord progressions and **hip-hop** on Dream Maker/Euphoria. The *Los Angeles Times* identifies **EDM** styling on Shoulda Known Better, **electro-rock** on Take Me Away, and **soul**-vocal sampling on the title track. The Feminist Wire calls out some **rock and roll** and **Brazilian jazz**, both conservatively classified as *texture*. All narrow tags are scoped *some tracks* and **phillysoul** is also *texture*, not album-wide.

`energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: “BURNITUP!” and “Dammn Baby” demand movement, but the album leans toward measured R&B, reflective piano and guitar ballads (Guardian, Pitchfork, Slant). `electronic=3`: explicit synths and Ensoniq sample are balanced by session organ, drums, bass, guitars and horns. `mood=4`: largely hopeful, affectionate, warmly reflective, with serious social criticism. `theatrical=2`: lower-key intimate vocals and personal expression. `experimental=2`: blends contemporary styles with classic form without radical structure. `polish=5`: clear sophisticated layering and precise production. `voice=soft|clear|spoken|powerful`: delicate harmonies and alto ballads; more forceful sung passages in anthems, **not** Missy Elliott or J. Cole's guest rapping.

### Specific instrumentation and real production details

AllMusic personnel: John Jackson **bass, drums, organ**, Paul Jackson Jr **guitar**, three players labelled horn; Apple Music “Night” supplies Paul Jackson Jr and vocalist credits. *Pitchfork* notes a **synthesizer** on “BURNITUP!” and **piano** on “After You Fall”; *Guardian* independently calls the latter skeletal piano ballad. Dem Jointz is credited **keyboards** on “BURNITUP!” on a transcription of the original physical edition. These warrant `synth|keyboards|piano|bass|drums|organ|guitar|brass`. `brass` is a pragmatic family for untyped label `horn` credits; if importing system requires a specific horn instrument, leave `brass` out rather than invent trumpet/sax.

Jimmy Jam's firsthand Guitar Center interview explicitly identifies an **Ensoniq Mirage** wind patch resurrected on **BURNITUP!**; do **not** substitute `emulator` / `emulatorii`, and do not project Jam's old TR-808 onto this album simply because he mentions owning one. Tommy Parker's REVOLT interview says samples on “Unbreakable” and “Dream Maker/Euphoria” originate in his music sped up to sound old-school; valid album-specific **sampling** proof. Pitchfork, AllMusic and vocalist credits support multiple recorded **layered vocals**. No DAW model is independently documented for this actual record so the row omits one.

### Producers / influence / dissent

Core credit Janet Jackson + Jimmy Jam + Terry Lewis; Dem Jointz has full producing credits for some songs, only additional production on others; Missy Elliott is formally a producer on “BURNITUP!”, beyond guest artist. Tommy McClendon credited as co-producer, Thomas Lumpkins listed separately as producer in AllMusic and media metadata. AllMusic labels Jermaine Cole a producer but other sources identify only a guest part on “No Sleeep”; **verify this before import**. If primary liner shows no producer duty, remove only `J. Cole` from producer column, leaving guest credits out. Slant explicitly calls “Broken Hearts Heal” an *Off the Wall* musical salute to Michael Jackson: reasonable album-specific `influenced_by=Michael Jackson` on this qualified critical account, **not** because a cover or sample would automatically qualify. *Pitchfork* and *The Guardian* differ on whether the record's nostalgia produces cohesion or some weaker stretches, but agree on musical range and personal themes. This comeback is `landmark=1`, versus the two earlier records `0`.
