# David Bowie — Phase 02 evidence notes

**Scope:** 1972–1974 original studio albums only. Descriptors are editorial evaluations based on album-specific sources. Sources and genre scope appear in their own CSVs. No Phase 01 rows included.

## Ziggy Stardust (1972)

- **Identity:** Existing catalogue slug is `bowie-theriseandfallofziggystardustandthespidersfrommars`. Original 11-track issue and official Bowie/Ken Scott co-producer credits (official discography).
- **Genre weighting:** Glam/art rock leads. AllMusic further names hard rock, experimental rock, proto-punk, contemporary pop/rock, album rock and singer-songwriter; latter terms are less discriminating or track-specific. Source does not warrant electronic or synth-pop labels.
- **Scales:** energy 4 and tempo 3 represent a mixture of urgent rock songs and reflective ballads; theatrical 5 from persona/storytelling and staged dynamics; electronic 1 because Scott describes a principally live guitar, bass and drum setup; mood 2 reflects apocalyptic subject matter; groove 2 because not primarily dance music; experimental 2 accounts for the theatrical concept within accessible songwriting; polish 4 for multitracking and string overdubs. All values are editorial synthesis, not measurements.
- **Instrumentation:** electric guitar (Mick Ronson), acoustic guitar, bass, drums, piano, strings from Ken Scott's Sound on Sound interview and AllMusic. MusicBrainz original track credits document Bowie's saxophone on *Soul Love* and *Moonage Daydream*, plus Ronson synthesizer on *Starman*. These secondary parts are not continuous across the record.
- **Production:** Scott documents live rhythm tracks with subsequent overdubs on a 16-track tape machine and first-take vocals; `onetake|liverecording|overdubbing|analogtape` refer to these 1971–72 recordings, not the modern Atmos remix. No particular named synthesiser model or studio software has been confirmed here: a MusicBrainz credit names an ARP synthesizer but not its precise model.
- **Voices and lyrics:** variable intimate delivery and increasingly forceful raspy passages. The character, future disaster and identity motifs support storytelling/identity/social/introspection; these are broader album readings.
- **Influences:** AllMusic connects Marc Bolan/T. Rex; Bowie's own Velvet Underground reference and accounts of Iggy Pop, Lou Reed and Vince Taylor concern the **Ziggy character and concept**, not necessarily individual sounds. This distinction is important to Claude.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic calls it loosely structured rather than narratively coherent. Theatrical 5 does not imply a watertight plot.

## Aladdin Sane (1973)

- **Identity:** New catalogue item; use 1973 April release and exact title. Produced by Bowie and Scott (official album page).
- **Genre weighting:** Broad glam rock first. AllMusic names art/hard/experimental/proto-punk/album rock; Pitchfork describes explicit blues-rock material. Mike Garson names avant-garde jazz and Brechtian cabaret in particular songs. `avantgardejazz` is a new token. `doowop` relates to *Drive-In Saturday*, not the entire record. Broad `jazz` appears late and is clearly track-scoped in genre evidence.
- **Scales:** energy 4 for abrasive rock tracks; mood 2 for anxiety/alienation underneath flashy performances; theatrical 5 for cabaret, character vocals and spectacle; experimental 3 for dissonant jazz passages; polish 4; electronic 1; tempo 3 and groove 2 reflect contrasting rock and ballad pieces. Editorial synthesis.
- **Instruments:** Ronson guitar, Garson piano, Trevor Bolder bass, Woody Woodmansey drums and Ken Fordham saxophone/flute from the original RCA credits transcription; official 50th anniversary article supports brass and woodwinds on the album. No specific synth/drum machine added.
- **Production:** Scott's interviews describe live basic tracking, overdubs, one-take vocal method, and period multitrack tape. The methods are sourced to his Bowie albums collectively, and direct Aladdin-specific instrument credits are stronger than claims for any individual processor.
- **Voices and lyrics:** snarled/glam-rock and theatrical crooning; US tour, alienation, hedonism and anxiety from AllMusic and Pitchfork.
- **Influence caution:** Sonic resemblance to Rolling Stones is critical analogy; their covered composition is not on its own proof that Stones directly shaped the entire album. Hence `influenced_by` deliberately empty.
- **Critical disagreement:** AllMusic hears a diverse but less cohesive sequel to Ziggy; Garson stresses jazz/cabaret breakthroughs. Both views retained.

## Pin Ups (1973)

- **Identity:** October 19 **1973** confirmed by Bowie's 2018 and 2023 anniversary news. The Bowie album landing page incorrectly says 1976. Flag this, do not propagate error. This is a full new *studio* recording of twelve cover songs (not a greatest-hits compilation).
- **Genre weighting:** Glam-rock performance leads. AllMusic also names art/hard/experimental/proto-punk and rock categories, while identifying rock & roll and R&B material. Do not read the styles of source recordings as uniformly describing all rearrangements.
- **Scales:** energetic electric live-band songs (energy 4, tempo 4), moderately lively emotional colour (mood 3), visibly theatrical vocals (4), largely faithful song structures with some adventurous reinterpretations (experimental 2), production polish 4, limited electronics 1 despite credited Moog, groove 3. Editorial judgments.
- **Instruments:** Detailed *official* Bowie 2018 anniversary personnel: guitars, bass, drums, saxophones, piano, organ, harpsichord, electric piano, harmonica and Moog synthesiser. `synth` is a broad instrument token, `moog` the confirmed machine token. Moog appears last as it is not the dominant album texture.
- **Production:** Scott and Bowie co-producers; first takes documented by Scott for the four co-produced Bowie albums. Analog recording / overdubbing is a contextual technique for his 1970s recording approach, not a claim of particular hardware on each *Pin Ups* track. Avoid modern remaster gear, which only applies to the 2023 reissue.
- **Influences:** Unlike other albums, Bowie personally lists the 1964–67 London club acts on the *original sleeve*: Pretty Things, Them, Yardbirds, Pink Floyd, Mojos, Who, Easybeats, Merseys and Kinks. These are directly verified via the 2023 official anniversary text. `sounds_like` is editorial resemblance from those arrangements.
- **Lyrics:** left empty as a unified lyrical narrative is unwarranted for a covers project. Nostalgia is a curatorial impulse, not necessarily a lyric theme.
- **Critical disagreement:** Bowie’s official site praises the album as glam-rock classic; AllMusic and Pitchfork see a less coherent stopgap. Landmark 0 means less defining than originals, not unimportant.

## Diamond Dogs (1974)

- **Identity:** Bowie is the sole officially credited producer. Keith Harwood was an engineer/mixer and Tony Visconti a mixer and string collaborator, **not co-producer** in original official credits. Not a co-billed album.
- **Genre weighting:** Core glam/art/rock with experimental and proto-punk elements. Pitchfork specifically describes funk/soul and rock-opera elements, notably on *1984*; AllMusic hears disco in *Candidate*. Funk, soul and disco are track-scoped textures, not predominant genres. `albumrock` belongs to AllMusic generic styles.
- **Scales:** energy 4 for forceful rhythm and riffs; electronic 2 for Moog/Mellotron within an otherwise band-led record; mood 1 for sustained paranoia and dystopia; theatrical 5 for album scenarios and suite; experimental 3 for fractured writing; polish 3 for abrasive, densely constructed production; tempo 3 and groove 3 since some funk/soul patterns coexist with less danceable suite. Editorial assessments.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz recording relationships and Garson's own account support Bowie/Alan Parker guitars, Garson piano/Moog/Mellotron/harpsichord, Herbie Flowers bass, Aynsley Dunbar/Tony Newman drums, Bowie sax/percussion. Strings for *1984* are supported by Pitchfork’s description of Visconti’s Barry White-inspired string contribution. Instruments represent credited appearances, not full-album uniformity.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` supported for *1984*. No `sampling`, named digital sequencer or `tapeeffects` assigned because available descriptions of sonic loops do not independently establish exact recording machinery. This is why production has only one token.
- **Influence caveat:** Barry White is listed *only* because Pitchfork reports Bowie asked Visconti for Barry White-style strings; George Orwell and William Burroughs are essential **literary** influences, so belong in evidence notes, not `influenced_by` (artist-only field).
- **Critical disagreement:** Pitchfork values the cohesive unease; AllMusic sees a transitional and uneven record, and Bowie himself reportedly later said it was a 'collection of things' rather than a rigorous concept album. Preserve the difference rather than asserting unqualified total narrative coherence.

## Vocabulary, format and import safeguards

New genre tokens: `albumrock`, `avantgardejazz`. These must be added to the Genre type/genre families and any hardcoded validator list **before** validating. No new instrument or production token is required by these four rows (the existing `moog`, `mellotron`, etc. suffice). `Pin Ups` is neither a compilation of existing masters nor a live album.
