# Sia — phase 02 evidence notes (English, 25 October 2026)

Research scope: **original standard studio albums**, not expanded/deluxe track mixes. Evidence is attributed to identified tracks; `genres` includes minority genres whose evidence is not album-wide. `scope=texture` means the reviewer described a sonic quality, **not** that the entire composition belongs to that genre. Numbers on the eight scales are reasoned editorial assessments (0–5), not algorithmic averages or published measurements. No undocumented production software or synth model is assigned.

## We Are Born (2010) — `sia-weareborn`

**Source hierarchy:** MusicBrainz original-CD credits (track-by-track player and producer roles) + contemporaneous reviews by Jillian Mapes (Pitchfork, June 18 2010) and Jonathan Keefe (Slant, June 15 2010).

**Original track evidence (13 standard tracks):**

1. **The Fight** — electric guitar Nick Valensi; bass Samuel Dixon, drums Felix Bloxsom, keys Greg Kurstin; group backing vocals. Pop-rock under a sing-along pop approach.
2. **Clap Your Hands** — live band roles in MusicBrainz (guitar, bass, drums, keys), a disco-inflected dance-pop single; **do not substitute Diplo's remix** for the original album recording.
3. **Stop Trying** — live drums Aaron Redfield; guitars and keys; kids' vocals recorded alongside Sia. Upbeat guitar-pop with choral interjections (kids' vocals do not make album `voice=choral`).
4. **You've Changed** — funk rhythm section documented by Slant; original Lauren Flax dance version is NOT the album recording. No pure-electronic instrumentation assumed for rework.
5. **Be Good to Me** — Pitchfork describes waltzing arrangement, Slant R&B/soul timbre; string credit Oliver Kraus. Retain both waltz and R&B cues, neither album-wide.
6. **Bring Night** — Kurstin/Valensi guitar and band rhythm; kid backing voices. Energetic pop.
7. **Hurting Me Now** — Slant reports unusual metallic percussion; do not name a specific metallic instrument without a recording credit.
8. **Never Gonna Leave Me** — keyboard and bass credited to Kurstin plus electric guitar and drums.
9. **Cloud** — conventional studio band arrangement from detailed personnel.
10. **I'm in Here** — Kurstin piano and xylophone, plus guitar and drums; a subdued song, not identical to the dance tracks. Piano-vocal alternate is a bonus version, not an additional original composition.
11. **The Co-Dependent** — ensemble bass, guitar, drums, keyboards from credits.
12. **Big Girl Little Girl** — Pitchfork calls its guitar riff angular and rock-oriented; additional Inara George backing vocals.
13. **Oh Father** — Madonna cover (Madonna/Patrick Leonard as *writers*); reviewer describes soft drum/keyboard treatment. Madonna **not** added as `influenced_by`, because a cover is not itself a documented sonic influence on the album.

**Genres/scales:** The critics call it disco/funk-influenced pop and compare guitar-centered construction to earlier Sia downtempo work. `energy 4`, `tempo 4` and `groove 4` describe the dominant momentum but not all tracks; `electronic 1` acknowledges keys and programming but credits mainly acoustic/electric band players. `mood 4` is bright in sound, notwithstanding lyrical conflict; `theatrical 3` reflects extroverted vocal turns without full dramatic concept; `experimental 1` because familiar radio structures dominate; `polish 4` from studio-produced, polished arrangements. Voice raspy/clear/powerful justified by Slant’s description of a ragged but agile vocal.

**Credits/production:** `Greg Kurstin` is credited **producer**; Samuel Dixon, Valensi, engineers and mixers aren't mistakenly listed as producers. `production` blank: backing vocals, guitar-based arrangements and generic programming are not equivalent to specific effects/machines. `instruments=mallets` derives from xylophone on I'm in Here. Do not infer Prophet, Pro Tools, autotune.

## 1000 Forms of Fear (2014) — `sia-1000formsoffear`

**Source hierarchy:** original recording credits indexed by MusicBrainz; TIME, Slant and The Guardian; genre index Album of the Year less authoritative for individual tracks. Original 12-song sequence only.

**Track-specific variation:**

1. **Chandelier** — co-produced Greg Kurstin/Jesse Shatkin; credited guitars, piano, Mellotron, drums, keyboard; high-intensity vocal. Lyrical partying reads as self-destruction, not simply celebration.
2. **Big Girls Cry** — **Chris Braide and Greg Kurstin** jointly credited producers. Piano, organ, guitars, percussion, bass, Mellotron from music credits. Slow/midtempo fragile pop.
3. **Burn the Pages** — Kurstin piano, bass, guitar, keyboards, drums; more active pop framework.
4. **Eye of the Needle** — restrained piano ballad with other credited band instruments, avoid describing as a literal solo piano piece.
5. **Hostage** — guitars Nick Valensi and Kurstin with organ, drums and bass: rocky / new-wave-like pulse, different from Chandelier. `newwave` carries LOW evidence because primary credits prove guitar but do not themselves assign new wave.
6. **Straight for the Knife** — serious piano/guitar-led song with bass and Mellotron.
7. **Fair Game** — piano, xylophone, Mellotron and drums; the specific percussion is credited rather than inferred from genre.
8. **Elastic Heart** — Diplo producer, Kurstin co-producer; percussion/drum programming by Diplo and Andrew Swanson. **Do not** count Andrew Swanson as a credited producer from programming alone.
9. **Free the Animal** — bass/guitar/piano and xylophone plus percussion, tense arrangement.
10. **Fire Meet Gasoline** — widescreen ballad with piano, guitar and other instruments.
11. **Cellophane** — keyboard-led pop beside guitars/bass/drums; no invented software model.
12. **Dressed in Black** — credits include *celesta*, *Chamberlin*, *Mellotron* and xylophone alongside drums, bass and guitars: these require individual tokens; not all generic `synth`.

**Scales:** `energy 4` from crashing choruses countered by quieter verses; `electronic 3` from pop-electronic arrangement with organic playing; `mood 1` from darkness, vulnerability and destructive imagery; `theatrical 4` and `polish 5` from the dramatic forward voice and designed pop dynamics; `experimental 2` from timbral and vocal idiosyncrasies inside conventional song structures; `tempo 3`, `groove 3` reflect many ballads and some rhythmic cuts. TIME explicitly discusses *walls of sound* at crescendos (`production=wallofsound` qualified as review description, not verified technical Spector method); reviews explicitly describe belting.

**Disagreement:** Slant values emotional interplay, Guardian questions how enduring its slick contemporary sound will be, TIME notes a structurally standardized power-pop template. No automatic causal influence from Beyoncé: Guardian's *Halo* comparison is sonic analogy, so `sounds_like` only. Existing source includes new-wave qualitative tag at low evidence, and genre evidence indicates scope.

## This Is Acting (2016) — `sia-thisisacting`

**Scope**: January 2016 ORIGINAL 12-track edition. Do not contaminate with the October 2016 deluxe *The Greatest*, *Confetti*, *Jesus Wept* or *Move Your Body* Alan Walker remix.

**Track-level original tracks and personnel:**

1. **Bird Set Free** — Greg Kurstin producer; piano, Mellotron, drums, bass and keys; originally intended for another act. Dramatic ballad.
2. **Alive** — producer Jesse Shatkin, with explicit drum-machine and string programming, synth, piano, guitar, acoustic drums; Adele co-writer, **not** producer. Vocal belting/military percussion reviewers.
3. **One Million Bullets** — Jesse Shatkin producer, guitar, piano, bass, drums and keys. AllMusic says this was written for Sia rather than another artist; contrasts more intimate ballad with loud tracks.
4. **Move Your Body** — Kurstin producer, drums, bass, keys, higher dance momentum.
5. **Unstoppable** — Shatkin producer/programming, piano, bass, drums, strings programming; programming is **not automatically actual acoustic strings**.
6. **Cheap Thrills** — Kurstin producer; bass, guitar, keys, drums; AllMusic/Pitchfork explicitly describe reggae-tinged rhythm. Sean Paul guest remix is NOT part of original standard album.
7. **Reaper** — producers per MusicBrainz **Dom $olo, 88-Keys, Noah Goldstein, Jesse Shatkin, Kanye West, Jake Sinclair**; Shatkin drum-machine/synth/bass programming. Writer credit not conflated with producer credit.
8. **House on Fire** — Jack Antonoff and Jesse Shatkin producers; Jake Sinclair *additional producer*; drums, synth, piano, guitar and tambourine in credits.
9. **Footprints** — T-Minus producer; Jesse Shatkin, Josh Valle, Nikhil Seetharam credited *additional producers*; keyboard, bass, piano, guitar, drums.
10. **Sweet Design** — Kurstin producer; original recording **samples** *Girl in the Window* (The Mist), *Remind Me* (Schiller Street Gang), and *Thong Song* (Sisqó). R&B track; `sampling` specifically defensible, no sample inference for rest of record.
11. **Broken Glass** — Shatkin producer, with drum-machine programming, drums, synth and bass. Slant/Pitchfork discuss soaring belted choruses.
12. **Space Between** — Christopher Braide and Cameron Deyell both explicitly producer credited (including vocals); Deyell bass/guitar/keys, vulnerable slow closing song.

**Genres:** Pop is album-wide; Metacritic labels broad pop-rock, left-field, and adult-alt-pop-rock. AllMusic and Pitchfork specifically attribute reggae to *Cheap Thrills* and R&B to *Sweet Design*. Album is NOT uniform reggae or R&B. Comparison with Adele, Rihanna, Celine Dion and Major Lazer is by reviewers/sonic editorial interpretation; **their being pitch recipients does not establish `influenced_by`**. No directly documented shaping artist was found, so `influenced_by` blank.

**Scales and production:** `energy 4`, `electronic 3`, `mood 3` mixed emotional palette, `theatrical 5` dramatic register and anthemic dynamics, `experimental 1` conventional pop songwriting, `polish 5`, `tempo 3`, `groove 3` upbeat cuts mixed with long ballads. `production=belting|sampling` from explicit reviews and credits. **No autotune or named workstation claimed.** Multiple named producers are actual track producers or extra producers; listed after the two primary anchors.

## Everyday Is Christmas (2017) — `sia-everydayischristmas`

**Scope:** November 2017 standard ten-song album, all ORIGINAL Christmas material co-written by Sia/Greg Kurstin, all produced by Greg Kurstin. Exclude 2018 cover *Round and Round*, 2021–22 holiday bonuses and subsequent speed edits from this original descriptor.

**Track-by-track variation:**

1. **Santa's Coming for Us** — Time Out specifically hears barroom **piano boogie**, **calypso-like melody**, brass/horns and guitars; original holiday pop, not homogeneous Christmas ballad.
2. **Candy Cane Lane** — Time Out hears Spector-style arrangement and sleigh bells; historical resemblance goes in `sounds_like` or track annotation, **not** `influenced_by` and **not** assumed technical `production=wallofsound` without concrete engineering account.
3. **Snowman** — Apple Music describes as *bluesy*; ballad with personified melting lover in lyric (Guardian).
4. **Snowflake** — more vulnerable lyrical theme, not a second cheerful dance single; Guardian questions duplication with Snowman.
5. **Ho Ho Ho** — comedic and boisterous holiday song; AllMusic notes exaggerated party references.
6. **Puppies Are Forever** — child-oriented sing-along novelty song, not the sonic profile of the entire LP.
7. **Sunshine** — uplifting, consolatory Christmas tune, festive instrumentation and bright delivery.
8. **Underneath the Mistletoe** — bittersweet love ballad; review explicitly distinguishes it from dance-oriented cuts.
9. **Everyday Is Christmas** — Apple Music performing credits include Kurstin guitar, bass, drums, piano and Mellotron; title track is an emotional, expansive love song.
10. **Underneath the Christmas Lights** — slower dramatic seasonal pop ballad; Guardian and Time Out see familiar Christmas themes in the writing.

**Instruments:** Sia 2017 original liner-derived album personnel (as reproduced in album credit summaries) include Kurstin: bass, drums, keyboards, piano, guitar, organ, percussion, vibraphone, pump organ, Mellotron, celeste, baritone guitar, glockenspiel; David Ralicke: trumpet, trombone and multiple saxes and other horns. Time Out independently confirms piano boogie, horns, guitar, bells. The new specialized tokens `sleighbells`, `pumporgan`, `baritoneguitar`, `celesta`, `mellotron` retain instruments instead of normalizing all to generic keys. Their **presence is not equal to album-wide dominance**.

**Scales:** `energy 3` since dance-pop and children's songs coexist with ballads, `electronic 1` because original credits and reviews emphasize played retro arrangements, `mood 4` festive but with melancholy cuts, `theatrical 4` from exaggerated holiday performance, `experimental 1` familiar holiday forms, `polish 4` controlled production; `tempo 3`, `groove 3` mix boogie/dance with slower ballads. `production` blank because Spector-esque sound is a critic's resemblance, not a verified recording technique. `lyrics=love|party|storytelling|nostalgia` is a high-level editorial mapping of sourced Christmas tales, not uniform themes.

**Disagreements:** Time Out celebrates the genre range, Guardian dismisses its repeated imagery and grandiose ballads; both agree the tracklist mixes levity and melancholy. No retrospective 2022 sped-up remix style has been entered into the 2017 album.
