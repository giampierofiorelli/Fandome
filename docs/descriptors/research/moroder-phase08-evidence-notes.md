Research and GitHub audit: **25 October 2026**, branch `descriptors`. Four previously undescribed titles, 1992–2018, all available as released original commercial records. Scales are **our anchored album-wide interpretations**, not source ratings. `source_ids` refer to the source CSV; weaker track-only genre evidence is expressly labelled. ## 1. `moroder-foreverdancing` — *Forever Dancing* (1992)

**Original identity / discographic dispute.** Official artist credits list a 1992 album [F-OFF]. AllMusic lists the 1992 album under dance-pop and disco with an original 19-track running order and record personnel [F-AM]. Virgin 1992 original issue [F-ORIG] includes a newly released Giorgio Moroder and Donna Summer collaboration, **Carry On**, plus mostly short re-worked disco-era standards such as *That's the Way (I Like It)*, *Don't Leave Me This Way*, *Born to Be Alive* and *I Feel Love*. One collectors' listing calls it *Best-Of* [F-ALT]; do not mistake reinterpretations/re-recordings for a mere 1970s master compilation. `type=studio` provisional, label/discography-supported.

**Producer / voices.** Original-edition credits list *producer Giorgio Moroder*. Marcello Zini is a *programmer*, Max di Carlo an *arranger*, Steve Bates engineer/mixer, none to be added as producers [F-LP, F-AM]. Vocalists include Donna Summer (the new *Carry On*), Marietta Waters, Christina Nichols, Melanie Taylor, Joey Diggs, Larry Lee; the original sleeve also credits a rap performance [F-LP]. `voice=powerful|rap` uses the contemporaneous description of Summer's soaring singing [F-BILL] and the explicitly credited rap. It **does not** assume all 19 cuts have the same vocal quality. `lyrics` left empty: song titles and familiarity with source hits do not establish the distribution of complete lyrics for this newly arranged set. Mostly English; a Spanish-looking title is not proof of a Spanish lead vocal.

**Genres / scoring.** `disco` and `dancepop` are documented album-level styles [F-AM, F-ORIG]; original catalog metadata also names house, Hi-NRG, synth-pop and electronic [F-LP], all given lower-weight scopes because original-song critical analysis is sparse. `energy 4`, `tempo 4`, `groove 5` reflect 19 generally brisk, short, dance-focused segments and album/label genre descriptions rather than one famous single. `electronic 4` derives from explicitly credited programming plus synthetic dance idioms; **not** 5, since no source proves every sound is generated electronically. `mood 4` takes the broad bright disco/dance set list and contemporary joyous duet context, with genre context insufficient to rate 5. `experimental 1` recognizes deliberate reinterpretations of known pop/disco standards. `polish 4` from professional 1992 studio production, mixing and arrangement credits; `theatrical` left blank. **Low specificity** of track-by-track commentary, so whole row medium.

**Relationships and landmark.** `sounds_like` names original performing artists of several deliberate reinterpretations and is an editorial music-neighbour comparison, **not** a claim of documented external production influence. `influenced_by` empty because covering a song does not establish the record's production inspirations. `landmark 0`: *Carry On* notable, but not sufficient to elevate the whole album to a career-defining recording. The late-1990s remixes/Grammy relate to the **song**, not new 1992 album tracks [F-DANCE, F-BILL].

## 2. `moroder-dejavu` — *Déjà Vu* (2015)

**Edition / identification.** This is an existing Fandome catalog album with `moroder-dejavu`, keep exact ID. Original standard issue consists of **12** tracks [D-OFF, D-APPLE]. Different outlets show 12, 15 or 16 June for the initial digital issue; retain 2015 as year, use original 12 songs, and do not count deluxe *Magnificent*, *City Lights*, *Timeless* or a *74 Is the New 24* remix in its album scoring [D-CHART].

**Producers (careful).** The official page generically lists Giorgio as producer [D-OFF], but Qobuz gives **track-specific production**: Giorgio Moroder on all base album tracks; Joakim Åhlund (co-production on *Diamonds*), Roman Lüth (*Right Here, Right Now* and *74 Is the New 24*), Raney Shockne, Dan Book, Jeeve and Patrick Jordan-Patrikios (*Tempted*); Patrick Jordan-Patrikios also explicitly produced *Tom's Diner* [D-QOB]. `Michael 'Smidi' Smith` is **executive producer** (not added), as he himself describes [D-TECH]. **Emily Wright** is credited as engineer, not producer, on standard *Tom's Diner* Qobuz record. `Oskar Sikow` is credited as composer only in the original standard-issue *I Do This for You* Qobuz entry, not producer. Do not copy from undifferentiated database album producer rosters or confuse writers and mixers with producers.

**Genres / disagreement.** AllMusic names dance-pop, disco, pop, EDM, club/dance and left-field pop [D-AM]; Resident Advisor critic Andrew Ryce independently explicitly names pop/disco and describes failed EDM crossover [D-RA]. `electro` and `synthpop` are original release database styles [D-CHART]; `techno` is weak **Qobuz store category**, not claimed album-wide techno form [D-QOB]; `nudisco` is a specific description of the Kylie single, not an all-album style [D-VAR]. The least established styles therefore remain in the row **only because the project requires every sourced genre**, with scope in the evidence CSV. AudioTechnology's producer interview is more useful technically than marketing names: some programmed drums, reFX Nexus soft synth, Boomstar hardware, and real Rhodes/live drums on a substantial part of the record [D-TECH]. Hence `electronic 4`, **not 5**. An artist quote calls the goal EDM with proper verse-chorus melody and some acoustic instruments [D-VAR].

**Scales and lead voices.** `energy 4` and `groove 4` (many club singles, not an uninterrupted DJ suite), `tempo 4` (mostly uptempo with some slower moments), `mood 4` (bright, glossy choruses yet romantic tension), `theatrical 3` (large hooks but mainstream pop structure), `experimental 2` (some odd vocoder/spoken passages; reviews say conventional pop crossover), `polish 5` (elaborate studio/guest production, highly finished modern mix) [D-TECH, D-RA, D-GUARD]. `voice=clear|powerful|raspy|spoken|instrumental`: multiple guest timbres Sia, Kylie, Charli XCX, Foxes, Kelis; Moroder spoken bridge on *Tom's Diner*, instrumental/synth-led cuts; no rap as bridge is spoken, not credited rap [D-RA,D-GUARD]. `lyrics=love|party|sensuality` at broad *themes expressed by songs*, not universal lyrical topics; lower priority party and sensuality reflect primarily club/romantic vocal singles; should be reviewed against complete licensed lyric annotations. `language=en` for sung English; unintelligible French-like synthetic whisper on *La Disco* is not evidence to add fr [D-GUARD].

**Documented influences.** Pitchfork explicitly states *Daft Punk*'s influence hangs over the album, detecting *Get Lucky*-reminiscent guitar across several tracks [D-PITCH]. So they qualify for `influenced_by` **via a named specialist critic**, not merely a coincidence or the artists' 2013 collaboration. `sounds_like` is a separate editorial resemblance and includes Kylie/Charli/Daft Punk/Avicii, whose parallels were discussed by critics [D-RA,D-PITCH]. `landmark 1` because it marks Moroder's consequential 2015 return after ~23 years without a solo studio album, **not** because reviews were laudatory. Critics sharply disagree with promotional language: Pitchfork, RA and the Guardian found it uneven; producer interview establishes genuine musical participation, but no guarantee of aesthetic success [D-PITCH,D-RA,D-GUARD,D-TECH].

## 3. `moroder-tronrunroriginalsoundtrack` — *TRON RUN/r (Original Soundtrack)* (2016)

**Original album / significance.** Disney announced official separate commercial 14-cut soundtrack for the video game in 2016, released May 31 via Sumthing Else [T-D23,T-PITCH,T-SPOT]. AllMusic displays June 17 2016, probably a particular retailer release rather than initial [T-ALL]. Original cues credited jointly to Moroder and Raney Shockne alternate with newly commissioned remixes by Bibio, Darkstar, patten, Nicholls, Autechre, Plaid and Rusko. This is a **full released collaborative soundtrack** and a meaningful later original scoring partnership, qualifying for inclusion [T-STEAM,T-MOBY]. `type=soundtrack`; don't miscode as `remix` just because the issue contains commissioned remixes.

**Credits.** Primary game staff documentation credits Giorgio and Raney as original **composers** [T-MOBY]. Disney's official Steam listing explicitly calls Raney Shockne *co-composer/producer* [T-STEAM], so only Raney is recorded in `producers` with strong project-specific evidence. Giorgio is undeniably co-composer and named an iconic producer **by profession**, but surviving accessible original records do not separately give him an explicit *record producer* credit for this issue; do not turn professional identity into an individual production credit. The artist names in brackets on remix tracks are **remixers**, not evidence of album co-production credits. Original in-game credits include further remixes absent from this 14-track commercial album; those do not enter evaluation [T-MOBY,T-PITCH].

**Genres / scales.** AllMusic explicitly calls it an *electronic* video-game soundtrack [T-ALL]. Disney calls the remixer selection EDM [T-D23], but Autechre, Bibio, Rusko, etc. have distinct sounds; `edm` thus scoped **to part of the remix material** rather than claimed whole-album identity. `videogamemusic` is new Fandome genre token derived from AllMusic's named **Video Game Music** style; parent should be `soundtrack` and the genre evidence stores full issue scope. `energy 4` and `tempo 4` from game's speed theme, short action score motifs and many energetic remixes [T-STEAM,T-D23]; `electronic 5` electronic digital score and remix toolkit; `mood 2` cool/urgent synthetic action tone, not mainly euphoric; `theatrical 3` game-action scoring; `experimental 3` due art-electronic remix reworks yet short direct cues; `polish 4` professional contemporary digital sound; `groove 3` mixed score and beat-heavy remixes. These are interpretive **medium-confidence estimates**, explicitly not measured BPM or proof of every note. `voice=instrumental` because album 14-track listing is predominantly instrumental electronic cues/remixes; don't project game speech-credit **Ana Free** onto released OST recording or invent lyric/language.

**Analogies.** `sounds_like=Daft Punk|The Glitch Mob` is an editorial comparison of polished digital synthetic action scoring, **not** a claim of influenced-by, and **not** collaborators on this original. `influenced_by` blank. `landmark 0` (documented collaboration and a substantial album, but not established as a defining artistic milestone). Initial criticism sparse, overall row medium.

## 4. `moroder-queenofthesouthoriginalseriessoundtrack` — *Queen of the South (Original Series Soundtrack)* (2018)

**Original album / attribution.** Lakeshore artist-operated Bandcamp page and contemporary label notice affirm commercial 28-track release **13 July 2018**, authored and publicly billed to Giorgio Moroder **and** Raney Shockne [Q-BAND,Q-LABEL,Q-APPLE]. Qobuz lists both as composers track-by-track, confirming much more than a one-song guest contribution [Q-QOB]. Thus the full album properly belongs in Moroder's artist catalogue. Do not confuse the songwriter/composer with the production credit. Raney's professional bio describes himself as a producer, but **no reliable source accessed for the particular 2018 album confirms a producer credit by name**, so `producers` is deliberately blank; neither label executive producers of the TV series nor music composers belong in that column [Q-QOB,Q-SHOCK].

**Genres and disputed metadata.** *Soundtrack* and *electronic* apply to the original full score [Q-BAND,Q-LABEL]. Contemporary Stereogum independently calls the single *Moyocoyotzin* an **electro-noir** blend, and the label calls the partnership's approach the same [Q-SG,Q-BAND]. Token `electronoir` is newly proposed, **labeled coined term / production aesthetic**, rather than a proven established movement. Label's original Bandcamp tags include **synthwave** and **retrowave**; `retrowave` is new, kept at medium. Classical and Latin are documented **elements**, particularly one prominent cue, rather than asserting the 70-minute score is chiefly a classical or Latin record [Q-SG,Q-LABEL].

**Album-wide scoring.** `electronic 4` because vintage synthesizers are central yet strings/classical instrumentation explicitly present; `energy 3` because action passages coexist with restrained tense score cues; `mood 1` because press and score concept focus on violence, suspense, and threat; `theatrical 4` because TV drama underscore is often emphatically cinematic; `experimental 3` because retro synth scoring fuses Latin/classical elements; `polish 4` from professionally made Lakeshore studio OST; `groove 2` because many cues do not serve dancefloor, although rhythmic electronic passages exist [Q-SG,Q-BAND,Q-LABEL]. **Tempo blank:** without dependable album-average pacing/BPM assessment of all 28 cues, one promoted fast excerpt cannot establish 0-5 typical speed.

**Voice, lyrics, languages.** `voice=instrumental|clear`: 26-plus cues mainly instrumental, with two labeled guest vocal renditions of *Everybody Wants to Rule the World* (Luz Elena Mendoza; artist Bandcamp misspells "Moendoza") and *One Way or Another* (Chloe Chaidez), so clear applies only to minority vocal cuts [Q-BAND,Q-APPLE]. These are English songs, so `language=en`, but not predominantly sung material. `lyrics` left blank since album primarily score and no original album-wide words/theme distribution documented. `sounds_like=Cliff Martinez|John Carpenter|Tangerine Dream` is an editorial cinematic-electronic sound match, **not** a sampled/interpolated credit or sourced influence. `influenced_by` blank: label says they developed Moroder's *own* established synth scoring approach [Q-BAND]. `landmark 0` because it is a substantial late score but not established career-defining. Review material sparse; medium confidence is appropriate.

## Cross-package and schema cautions

- Fandome genre list at GitHub audit has `synthwave`, `leftfieldpop`, `techno`, etc.; **three new tokens proposed**: `videogamemusic` (AllMusic game score style), `electronoir` (artist/critic label, not settled genre), `retrowave` (original label Bandcamp tag). Add only if Claude retains each after review; then update `Genre`, parents, display map, validator, tests. Suggested parents: `videogamemusic → soundtrack`, `electronoir → electronic + soundtrack`, `retrowave → synthwave`.
- Keep `producers` strict: **executive producer and composer are not record producer**. *Déjà Vu*: producer Smith listed only as executive. *Queen of the South*: no album producer specific evidence found. *TRON RUN/r*: Raney explicitly co-composer/producer, Moroder composers only verified.
- Relative scores describe entire original issues; local review of raw audio could further strengthen or alter medium-score values. Original live/deluxe/2020s remixes are not carried back into original album descriptors.
- `sounds_like` is editorial judgement, **not documentary influence**, and should not establish Fandome connection edges without separate review. Daft Punk in *Déjà Vu* `influenced_by` is an exception supported by a named Pitchfork writer.
