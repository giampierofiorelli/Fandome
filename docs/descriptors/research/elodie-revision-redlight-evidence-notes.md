## Red Light (2023) — re-evaluation

- **Album identity:** seven continuous tracks, a *clubtape*; Elodie expressly rejects album/EP labels. Existing Fandome `mixtape` type remains correct (R02, R06, R07). Distinct from the earlier studio album *OK. Respira*.
- **Genre hierarchy:** `house`, `dancepop`, `dance`, `electronic`, `rnb`, `pop` are album-level descriptions (R01, R03, R05, R06). The trailing `trance`/`edm` tags apply chiefly to **Ascendente** (R09); `afrobeat` is a critic-described stylistic seasoning (R05); `techno` is a producer-identified influence on **Ascendente** (R08), not whole-album classification. These minor styles are preserved solely under the current rule requesting all named genres; if Fandome cannot distinguish scope operationally, downstream recommendation must down-weight them.
- **Scores:** `energy=4`, `electronic=5`, `groove=5`, `tempo=4` capture the relentless electronic four-on-the-floor dance sequence and strong bass (R01, R02, R05). `polish=4` is source-informed studio/editorial impression. `theatrical=3` reflects linked visuals and declarative presentation, with weaker album-audio evidence; second reviewer may abstain. `experimental=2`: the continuous mix and aesthetic concept are unusual, but the melodies and house structures are accessible.
- **Mood revised `4 → 3`:** R01 describes a glittering euphoric club but explicitly mentions melancholy, fleeting loves. R02/R04/R05/R06 document anger, queer liberation and satire; closing **A fari spenti** is vulnerable. A 5/5 groove cannot automatically imply bright/euphoric emotional colour. **Mixed mood is supported, not clinically measured**. A critic focused only on instrumental euphoria could argue 4; our whole-project score includes song and vocal affect.
- **Voice:** `raspy` is a *provisional* fixed-vocabulary approximation based on the label's description of Elodie's raspy/deep vocal timbre (R11), broadly compatible with R01's R&B/electro persona. R11 predates these recordings; there is **no authenticated whole-album voice taxonomy**. The second reviewer should listen across all seven tracks and may prefer **blank** if this voice tag is not stable. Do not infer `rap`, `spoken`, `powerful`, or `choral` from genre or visual styling.
- **Lyrics:** `sensuality|identity|social|political|party|irony|love`. `Elle` explicitly addresses discrimination and LGBTQ rights, `Glamour` satirizes elite fashion, other tracks describe nightlife and brief relationships (R01, R02, R04, R05, R06). `political` is narrow but artist-confirmed at **track level**, not an entire political album.
- **Producers:** per-track producer roles Qobuz: B-CROMA (Red Light/Euphoria), Simon Says! (Glamour/Elle), Dardust (Ascendente), Lvnar (Lontano Da Qui), Marz + Zef (A Fari Spenti). **Dardust also supervised the whole project**, but did **not** single-handedly produce all seven; never conflate creative supervisor with credited producer. R07 and R06.
- **Influence — Madonna CONFIRMED, with crucial specificity.** Elodie explicitly recalls the **Confessions Tour** as inspiring what a powerful woman could do in dance music (R04), and says she listens to **Hung Up** (R02). Rolling Stone journalist calls the **dancefloor Madonna** an influence on this seven-song set (R03); this supports `influenced_by=Madonna` as a direct album-level attribution. Qualified critic R05 also identifies strong Duke Dumont and Fred again.. traces and Beyoncé *Renaissance* transitions. A SentireAscoltare article (R09) calls *Glamour* and *Elle* a Myss Keta homage: **two tracks only, excerpt not full text**. We list the names in ranked order but mark certainty and scope here. Elodie's general remarks that she listens to Miley Cyrus, Rosalía and Tame Impala (R04) do **not** demonstrate their influence on this specific release, so they are not added.
- **Erotica / Sex?** We searched contemporary independent reports, artist press-conference interviews, Manara commentary and review articles. None explicitly connects *Red Light* to Madonna's **1992 album *Erotica* or 1992 book *Sex***. Artist mentions Confessions Tour and Hung Up instead. Manara created the cover from Elodie's nude photograph (R02, R04, R12), while Elodie describes the nude as artistic self-expression and a statement about bodily freedom. An **editorial parallel** with Madonna's 1992 projects is valid as a question to investigate, but **not a documented causal reference** and never a separate token in `influenced_by`. Note the distinction: Manara is the confirmed visual artist; he is not a musical producer/influence.
- **Sonic resemblance:** Madonna, Beyoncé, Romy and Icona Pop are album-level critic comparisons (R01, R05). A sonic parallel doesn't establish causal influence. Specific visual comparisons to other artists were omitted.
- **Uncertainty:** `techno` is sound lineage of one song and `afrobeat` just texture. `raspy` inferred from singer career description. Editorial quality divides critics, so high row confidence applies to release and core genres, not every optional tag.

## Mi Ami Mi Odi (2025) — re-evaluation

- **Edition:** canonical **12-track CD** released 2 May 2025 (M04), ending with Raro. A 13-track digital release exists with extra **Yakuza** (M03); OndaRock references Yakuza as additional single, but the album row remains standard 12 tracks. Do not add producer Rvssian just because of bonus song. Keep existing `elodie-miamimiodi`.
- **Genre hierarchy:** OndaRock **explicitly lists electropop, house, pop and R&B at album level** (M01). Rockol independently describes pop, R&B, electronic and urban; urban is a scene not a token (M02). Dance-pop is secondary (M06); `poprap` applies to Muah/In grado, and `contemporaryrnb` specifically to Feeling's 2000s-sounding production. Do not assign techno or trance: the OndaRock reviewer merely imagines a build-up that might lead to them, then says it doesn't.
- **Scores:** `energy=3`, `electronic=3`, `groove=3` reflect two contrasting halves: assertive club opening, quieter ballads, strings, and early-2000s R&B ending; not the same as *Red Light*. `experimental=2`, `polish=4` reflect mainstream structures with varied high-quality studio production, rather than abstract sound experimentation. `theatrical` and `tempo` remain empty, not asserted by the available album-level evidence.
- **Mood remains 3:** sexual confidence and celebratory self-expression in opening 4–5 tracks, later grief/relationship tensions and soft introspection in Cuore nero and Dimenticarsi alle 7 (M01, M02). The contrast warrants **mixed**, not automatically happy because early tracks dance or automatically dark because the lyrics mention hurt.
- **Voice:** provisional `raspy|soft|rap`: Universal's general raspy timbre description (M07), Rockol says the opening of **1 ora** is whispered (M02), and OndaRock explicitly identifies rap parts in **Thaurus** and rap/pop-rap on tracks (M01). Rap applies only to some tracks and may be a guest/interleaved section. Avoid mistaking vocal filtering / studio processing for a new innate voice category. Human audit can remove `raspy` if contradicted by this album.
- **Lyrics:** love, sensuality, identity/self-empowerment, heartbreak and introspection, all tied to album-specific artist statements or track reviews (M01, M02). None of the Madonna visual motifs on cover is evidence for musical influence on this record.
- **Producers:** 11 credited per-track producers on **standard** version (M03). Do not include album-release producers `Elodie` and `Davide Simonetta` from MusicBrainz M04 without independent track-level verification; source metadata disagree on credit scope. Both remain noted for Claude to audit. `Rvssian` works on bonus Yakuza but **not on the standard 12-song descriptor**.
- **Landmark=1:** editorial choice as Elodie's current mature mainstream era, not objective critical acclaim (Rockol 6/10, OndaRock 5/10). Second reviewer may choose 0. Do not turn critics' reviews into Fandome's numerical attributes.
- **Influenced_by / sounds_like:** remain blank. The critic compares **Feeling alone** to Mary J. Blige / early-2000s R&B; does not establish an album-wide influence. Rockol's references to Madonna pertain to *cover art/show imagery*, not this album's music.
