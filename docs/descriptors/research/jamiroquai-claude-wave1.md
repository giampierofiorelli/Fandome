## jamiroquai-emergencyonplanetearth

Sources:
- T1: Baltimore Sun, J.D. Considine, 20 Aug 1993. https://www.baltimoresun.com/1993/08/20/taylor-polishes-his-golden-oldies-to-a-new-high-finish/
- T2: Trouser Press, Scott Frampton. https://trouserpress.com/?p=699
- T3: Melody Maker, Christopher Dawes, 20 Mar 1993. http://www.pushstuff.co.uk/mmfeatures/jamiroquai200393.html
- T4: Interview, Jill Selsman, Jun 1993. https://www.interviewmagazine.com/music/new-again-jamiroquai
- T5: BBC Music, David O'Donnell, 2008. https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/n5mq/
- T6: Guitar World, Mike Brooks, 18 Mar 2023. https://www.guitarworld.com/features/the-lowlist-jamiroquais-emergency-on-planet-earth-was-a-solid-platinum-gem-for-all-bass-players
- T7: Glide Magazine, Ryan Dillon, 14 Jun 2023. https://glidemagazine.com/292904/30-years-later-revisiting-jamioquais-funky-acid-jazz-debut-emergency-on-planet-earth/
- T8: Sony Music US bio, Dec 1996 (label speaking for the artist). https://funkin.com/the_band/biographies/sony/sony_199612.php
- T9: Official Charts. https://officialcharts.com/albums/jamiroquai-emergency-on-planet-earth

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - acidjazz: T5, "laid the foundations for an acid-jazz sound". T7, "a fusion of acid jazz and funk that borrows from the golden age of soul".
  - funk: T7; T5, "funk mezze".
  - soul: T2, "solid set of soul grooves"; T1, "full of '70s soul overtones".
  - disco: T4, on the opener and single "When You Gonna Learn": "disco-jazz grooves". One track, so 4th.
  - latin, salsa: T6, Music of the Mind "chorus has a more Latin/salsa feel". One track.
- **energy 3, groove 4:** groove-based funk (T1, T7 "invigorating"), dance roots (T3). Not a high-energy record.
- **electronic 1:** live band with horns, strings and didgeridoo (T1, T5). Synths are only a bass synth on Revolution 1993 (T6) and turntables (credits).
- **mood 3:** anti-war and ecological anger (T3: "'Too Young To Die' is an anti-war song"), but "lots of hope in there too" (T3).
- **theatrical 3:** "dense, funk-with-strings arrangement" (T1); "sweeping arrangements" (T7).
- **experimental 2:** "adventurous multi-culturalism ... anything-goes attitude" (T1), yet retro soul (T2).
- **polish 3, tempo 3:** normal studio record; no strong source either way.
- **voice:** left empty. Sources compare the voice to Stevie Wonder and Aaron Neville but give no type.
- **instruments:**
  - Credits (T-Discogs): bass, didgeridoo ("The Yiddaki"), drums, guitar, keyboards, strings (Reggae Philharmonic Strings), trumpet, sax and flute, trombone, percussion, turntables.
  - electricguitar: "wah-wah guitar" (T1).
  - synthbass: "bass synth in places" (T6).
- **production:**
  - stringarrangements: strings credited as arranged by Jono, Toby Smith and Jay Kay.
  - wahwah: T1.
  - compression, envelopefilter: T6, Blow Your Mind's bass is "heavily compressed ... an envelope filter or auto-wah effect".
- **lyrics:** political, social, spiritual. T3: anti-war, environment, "what we need is a spiritual leader", karma. T4: "takes on everything from racism to corporate greed".
- **producers:** Toby Smith (producer, tracks 2 and 8) and Mike Nielsen (co-producer). Jay Kay's own credit dropped. Stuart Zender is not credited as producer on this release.
- **sounds_like:**
  - Stevie Wonder: T1, "singer Jason Kay evokes the jazzy exuberance of Stevie Wonder"; T2, "a knack for replicating the sound of Stevie Wonder"; T4, "Little Stevie Wonder-like vocals".
  - Aaron Neville: T3, "Stevie Wonder and Aaron Neville were the instant reference points".
- **influenced_by:** Donald Byrd and Roy Ayers. T8 (label bio): "In love with American soul and mid 70's jazz-soul fusion greats like Donald Byrd and Roy Ayers, Jay set about forming a band". This is about the band's founding, so it fits the debut; medium strength.
  - Not used: Flora Purim ("Moon Dreams" as the model for Music of the Mind). It comes from Kay's 2013 reissue liner notes, cited only by Wikipedia; I could not open it.
- **landmark 1:** T5, "became an instant classic ... laid the foundations".
- **Reception:**
  - importance 5: UK #1 debut, three weeks at #1 (T9); Brit nomination (T6).
  - acclaim 4: BBC "instant classic", Baltimore Sun positive, Glide "timeless status". Q's "contender for best British soul album of the '90s" was seen only via Wikipedia and is not counted.
  - mainstream 3: band-written, self-produced, came out of Acid Jazz Records; T3, Kay: "Jamiroquai isn't a record company product".
- **Legacy:** none. BBC's "foundations" refers to the band's own later work.

## jamiroquai-travellingwithoutmoving

Sources:
- S1: Salon, Aidin Viziri, 17 Feb 1997. https://www.salon.com/1997/02/17/music_28/
- S2: Albumism, Justin Chadwick, 28 Aug 2026. https://albumism.com/features/tribute-celebrating-25-years-of-jamiroquai-travelling-without-moving

- S4: Daily Bruin (student paper), Michael Nazarinia, 15 Jan 1997. https://wp.dailybruin.com/1997/01/14/soundbites157
- S5: Official Charts. https://officialcharts.com/albums/jamiroquai-travelling-without-moving

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - funk, soul, electro: S1, "elements of straight soul, modern funk and electro"; "upholds the lustrous funk tradition of the 1970s".
  - disco: S1, "nobody said disco bands were meant to innovate"; S3, "disco flavoured title track"; S2, "disco-tinged 'Cosmic Girl'".
  - dance: S4, "hip club scene danceability", "club music".
  - raregroove: S3, "rare groove rhythms".
  - reggae: S1, "a stab at reggae rhythms" on Drifting Along; S2 agrees.
  - latin: S4, "reggae, Latin and jungle beats".
  - jungle: S3, "the hit jungle collaboration with M Beat".
  - drumandbass: S2, "drum-and-bass imbued M-Beat collaboration".
  - acidjazz is not used: no opened source names it for this album (the Inquirer's Carter is quoted only via Wikipedia). Scene is kept as "British acid jazz" for consistency with the debut.
- **mood 4:** S3, Kay: "I wanted this to be an up album"; "an admittedly happier record". S1, "happy-go-lucky fun".
- **polish 4:** S2, "more mature and polished sound".
- **experimental 1:** S1, "does sound retro at times ... nobody said disco bands were meant to innovate".
- **electronic 2:** live band plus "synth and string-blessed rhythms" (S2). Tom Moon's "vintage analog instrument" remark could not be opened.
- **groove 4:** S4, danceability; S2, "high-energy vibe" with "a handful of downtempo numbers".
- **energy 3, tempo 3, theatrical 3:** mixed uptempo and downtempo (S2).
- **voice:** clear. S1, "his clear and sweet voice"; S4, "the sweetest soul voice since Stevie Wonder".
- **instruments:**
  - Line-up (S1): keyboards Toby Smith, bass Zender, drums McKenzie, guitar Katz, didgeridoo Wallis.
  - Credits: electric guitar, sax, trumpet, strings (Simon Hale).
  - piano: S2, Virtual Insanity "piano-driven".
  - synth: S2.
  - percussion: S2, McKenzie's percussion on Didjerama.
- **production:** stringarrangements (Simon Hale credited for string score and conducting); wahwah (S1, "horns and wah-wah guitar kick in on 'You Are My Love'").
- **lyrics:**
  - love, sensuality: S1, "exploring lust in 'Alright'"; S2, love songs Alright and Spend a Lifetime.
  - social: Virtual Insanity on technology and genetic engineering (S1, S3).
  - party: S1, "keeps the party alive".
  - introspection: S3, "playful yet introspective"; drugs on High Times.
- **producers:** Al Stone (co-producer with Jay Kay; Kay dropped).
- **sounds_like:**
  - Earth, Wind & Fire and Roy Ayers: S1, "the lustrous funk tradition of the 1970s, one embodied by ... Earth, Wind & Fire and Roy Ayers".
  - Stevie Wonder: S4, voice comparison. Student press, so weaker; EW's Diehl review, which also compares him to Wonder, was unreachable.
- **influenced_by:** empty. Nothing album-specific was found.
- **landmark 1:** S2, international and US breakthrough, multi-million seller, "Jamiroquai's most consistently gratifying song suite".
- **Reception:**
  - importance 5: S5, UK #2 and 72 weeks on chart; S2, breakthrough, platinum in the US, GRAMMY for Virtual Insanity.
  - acclaim 3: positive (S1, S2) against "derision ... by more than a handful of music snobs" (S2). EW, the Guardian and NME were critical according to Wikipedia; not opened.
  - mainstream 3: S3, Kay wanted "memorable choruses and stronger hooks", but the album is band-written and artist-produced.

## brunomars-24kmagic

Sources:
- B1: Rolling Stone, Christopher R. Weingarten, 18 Nov 2016. https://www.rollingstone.com/music/albumreviews/review-bruno-mars-24k-magic-w451090
- B2: Consequence, Karen Gwee, 22 Nov 2016. https://consequence.net/2016/11/album-review-bruno-mars-24k-magic/
- B3: Irish Times, Jim Carroll, 15 Nov 2016. https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/music/album-reviews/bruno-mars-24k-magic-album-review-once-more-around-funky-planet-of-sound-1.2867499
- B4: MusicCritic aggregator, with AllMusic, Pitchfork, Guardian, Observer, Slant, NYT and HipHopDX excerpts. https://musiccritic.com/bruno-mars/24k-magic
- B5: Golden 1 Center, quoting the Rolling Stone 2016 cover interview. https://www.golden1center.com/news/detail/bruno-mars-talks-album-influences-beyonce-and-more/
- B6: Revolt, Grammy speech, 2018. https://revolt.tv/2018/1/29/20824168/grammys-2018-bruno-mars-credits-teddy-riley-and-more-for-24k-magic

Cells:
- **genres:**
  - rnb, soul, funk: B2, "a devoted resurrection of the soul, funk, and R&B of '80s and '90s"; B3, genre "R&B / Soul"; B4 (AllMusic), "goes all-out R&B".
  - newjackswing: B1, "hearkens back to the new jack swing".
  - contemporaryrnb, retrosoul, pop: AllMusic genre tags shown in B4.
  - quietstorm: B1, "Ashford & Simpson–esque quiet-storm balladeer".
  - trap: B1, That's What I Like brings 12 Play vibes "into the boom of modern trap". One track.
  - hiphop: B1, "Perm" is "a future-shocked James Brown hip-hop hybrid". One track.
- **polish 5:** B1, "arrangements throughout are simply outstanding ... overdubs, subplots"; B2, "the most lush and layered record of Mars' career".
- **theatrical 4:** B1, "go well beyond 'tribute' into the realm of 'IMAX reboot'"; B2, "swagger and schmaltz".
- **experimental 1:** B1, "America's greatest pastiche pop star".
- **electronic 3:** "various synths (some pillowy, some buzzy)" (B2), talkbox and drum programming (credits), with live drums and horns.
- **mood 4:** B6, Mars: "written with nothing but joy".
- **groove 4:** B6, people dancing; B5, school-dance R&B. Ballads are present (Versace, Too Good).
- **energy 3, tempo 3:** half uptempo, half slow jams.
- **voice:** clear, powerful. B2, "Mars' clear, unapologetic tenor ... He's got the pipes"; B1, "soaring voice".
- **instruments:**
  - From credits: guitar (Dave Foreman), keyboards (Greg Phillinganes' solo), live drums (Eric Hernandez, Homer Steinweiss), horns ("Perm").
  - synth, piano: B2, "dramatic piano plunks".
  - percussion: B2, "snappy percussion of 'Finesse'".
- **production:**
  - talkbox: credited to Mars and Byron "Mr. Talkbox" Chambers.
  - drumprogramming: Stereotypes, additional drum programming.
  - overdubbing: B1.
  - layeredvocals: B2, "lush and layered", "enthusiastic chorus of backing vocals".
  - falsetto: B2, "melodious falsetto".
- **lyrics:** party, sensuality, love. B2 on designer clothes, fame and seduction; B4 (Slant), "the era's themes of money and sex".
- **producers:** Shampoo Press & Curl (Mars, Philip Lawrence, Brody Brown; the team credit is kept), Stereotypes, Emile Haynie, Jeff Bhasker.
- **sounds_like:**
  - Boyz II Men, Bell Biv DeVoe, Bobby Brown: B1, "turn-of-the-Nineties charts ruled by Boyz II Men, Bell Biv DeVoe and Bobby Brown".
  - Ashford & Simpson [2/9]: the ballads Versace on the Floor and Too Good to Say Goodbye.
  - James Brown [1/9] and Son of Bazerk [1/9]: "Perm".
  - R. Kelly (12 Play) [1/9]: That's What I Like.
  - Marvin Gaye (Midnight Love) [1/9]: B1, Versace is "the umpteenth tribute to Marvin Gaye's 'Sexual Healing'".
  - Prince [1/9]: B3, Versace "full of Prince-like flourishes".
- **influenced_by:**
  - B5 (Rolling Stone interview, quoted): he "wanted to re-create the feeling of the R&B he fell in love with as a kid ... Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, New Edition and Bobby Brown, Jodeci, Boyz II Men, Teddy Riley, Babyface".
  - B6, Grammy speech: "This album wouldn't exist if it wasn't for these guys" (Babyface, Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, Teddy Riley).
  - Babyface is excluded: he co-wrote and sang backing vocals on "Too Good to Say Goodbye", which makes him a collaborator.
  - Zane Lowe's West Coast list (Dr. Dre, DJ Quik) could not be opened.
- **landmark 1:** B6, Album of the Year; "lauded pop masterpiece".
- **Reception:**
  - importance 5: Grammy AOTY sweep.
  - acclaim 3: B4 aggregate 67, with RS, Guardian, Observer and Slant 3/5, Pitchfork 6.2, CoS B-, AllMusic 4/5, HipHopDX 86. Metacritic 70 seen only via Wikipedia.
  - mainstream 4: retro formula crafted for radio; AllMusic, "the full-length sequel to 'Uptown Funk'"; RS, "pastiche". It is co-produced by Mars, so not 5.
