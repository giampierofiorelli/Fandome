## Like Someone in Love (1957) — `ella-likesomeoneinlove`

- **Original edition:** Verve MGV-4004, **15 tracks**, recorded October 15 and 28, 1957. Reissues add cuts first issued on *Hello Love*; ignore those as evidence for the original programme. [like_orig, like_jazzimages, like_jazzjournal]
- **Sound:** intimate, predominately slower vocal-jazz standards, lush but restrained studio orchestra arranged/conducted by **Frank DeVol**. Saxophone by **Stan Getz** is audible on four tracks according to Scott Yanow; do not extrapolate that he plays on the entire disc. Some reissue session databases assign him more songs, but there is a genuine discrepancy. [like_allmusic, like_jazzjournal]
- **Instruments:** `orchestra|saxophone` only. The exact original DeVol orchestra roster/instrumentation is incomplete, and one reissue itself says unknown: do not infer strings, instruments, rhythm players from unrelated bonus tracks. [like_jazzimages, like_musicbrainz]
- **Numbers:** energy 2, tempo 2, groove 2 because mostly restrained ballads; mood 2 because melancholy/reconciliation predominates; theatrical 2 and experimental 1 reflect intimate conventional standards; electronic 0 acoustic; polish 4 sophisticated orchestral studio sound. `voice` clear/soft/powerful from critic-led accounts of nuanced dynamic expressive singing. [like_jazzjournal, like_allmusic]
- **Genre evidence:** see per-tag file; traditionalpop is editor-inferred from explicit period-pop orchestral standards rather than a verbatim AllMusic album style, and should be reviewed under strict source-named genre policy. `sounds_like` is Fandome editorial comparison, not direct cited influence; composer covers never imply influence.

## Porgy and Bess (1958; conflicting dates) — `ella-porgyandbess`

- **Original:** Ella Fitzgerald **and Louis Armstrong** perform Gershwin selections with a large studio orchestra arranged/conducted by **Russell (Russ) Garcia**, produced by **Norman Granz**. Sessions August 18/19 and October 1957. *Not* the opera's original-cast recording, a live album or a film soundtrack. `type=studio`, not `soundtrack`, despite the source being a staged opera. [porgy_allmusic, porgy_qobuz, porgy_jazzdisco]
- **Original-release year conflict:** AllMusic and Verve/UMG-Qobuz give **1958**; specialist jazzdisco identifies mono MGV-4011-2 as 1958 and 1959 stereo; official artist discography groups under **1957** (recording year); some modern vinyl/reissue descriptions claim initial 1959 release. **This is unresolved to the day: use 1958 as best-supported Fandome working year, flag as disputed**. No fabricated exact date. [porgy_allmusic, porgy_qobuz, porgy_jazzdisco, ella_official, porgy_2013_notes]
- **Style:** substantial theatrical Gershwin narrative repertoire, wide scale, bright brass and reed tuttis with some lush strings, romantic duets and Armstrong's rasp against Ella's clean vocals. Instrument choices `orchestra|trumpet|trombone|saxophone|frenchhorn|woodwinds|strings|piano|guitar|doublebass|drums` supported by reissue personnel and Garcia's ensemble. `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` describe explicitly documented Garcia orchestral arrangements; not invented equipment. [porgy_allmusic, porgy_2013_notes]
- **Scores:** energy 3, tempo 3 across contrasting overture, theatrical numbers and ballads; mood 2 for tragic drama, love and loss despite joyful moments; theatrical 4, experimental 2 (re-casting opera in studio jazz treatment), polish 4; groove 2 due to many narrative orchestral passages, electronic 0. Lyrics storytelling, love, heartbreak, social community, spiritual all based on Gershwin/DuBose Heyward scenario. [porgy_qobuz, porgy_allmusic]
- **Credits:** Granz PRODUCER, Garcia ARRANGER/CONDUCTOR, Gershwin COMPOSER, Armstrong co-billed VOCAL/TRUMPET; collaborators and composers are excluded from influenced_by. **Import crosslink:** `CO_BILLED['ella-porgyandbess'] = {with:['armstrong'], credit:'Ella Fitzgerald & Louis Armstrong'}`. Reuse this globally keyed ID on Armstrong's page, no `armstrong-porgyandbess` duplicate.

## Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Irving Berlin Song Book (1958) — `ella-ellafitzgeraldsingstheirvingberlinsongbook`

- **Original:** 1958 Verve **32-song two-LP project** recorded March 13–19, 1958, produced **Norman Granz**, arranged/conducted **Paul Weston**, engineer **Val Valentin**. Subsequent Volume 1/2 CD reissues are NOT two new album entries. [berlin_qobuz, berlin_musicbrainz]
- **Genres and musical detail:** AllMusic names Vocal Jazz, Jazz, Standards, Traditional Pop, Swing, American Popular Song, Bop, Early Jazz Vocals and Blues (the last 3 are lower-weight source category tags, not a claim the whole album is bebop or blues); cross-check by All About Jazz's assessment of supple Ella swing and Weston's largely discreet arrangements. [berlin_allmusic, berlin_allaboutjazz]
- **Instruments:** full Weston orchestra plus pianist Paul Smith, guitar Barney Kessel, bass Joe Mondragon, drums Alvin Stoller, brass/reeds solos by Harry Edison, Matty Matlock, Juan Tizol. Hence orchestra, piano, guitar, double bass, drums, saxophone, trumpet, trombone, clarinet. Avoid claiming specific synthesizers or modern production. [berlin_allaboutjazz, berlin_musicbrainz]
- **Numbers:** energy/tempo/groove 3 mixed upbeat swing and slower ballads; mood 4 buoyant and romantic with occasional sadness; theatrical 3 for witty show-tune interpretations; experimental 2 familiar material shaped with subtle phrasing; polish 4 refined studio orchestra, electronic 0 acoustic. Horn arrangements by Weston, not production effects. [berlin_allmusic, berlin_songbookmuseum]
- **Names:** Berlin is songwriter/subject, *not* a documented musical influence on this session; all `influenced_by` deliberately blank. `sounds_like` Sarah Vaughan/Doris Day/Frank Sinatra = Fandome editorial sonic association.

## Ella Swings Lightly (1959 working year, recorded November 1958) — `ella-ellaswingslightly`

- **Source-date conflict:** Jazzdisco's complete original MGV-4021 discography and AllMusic album page give **1959 original issue**, recording **22–23 November 1958**. Some first-pressing databases show 1958 original mono copies, and AllMusic release variants include 1958 dated digital metadata. **1959 is a conservative working year and requires human validation against first issue sleeves and trade announcements**. 1958 would be defensible if a contemporaneous first pressing is demonstrated. This ambiguity is not a second album. [lightly_jazzdisco, lightly_allmusic, lightly_jazzcodex]
- **Ensemble:** original **Marty Paich Dek-tette** (not a conventional 17-piece big band) with Don Fagerquist and Al Porcino trumpet; Bob Enevoldsen valve trombone/tenor sax; Vincent DeRosa French horn; John Kitzmiller tuba; Bud Shank alto sax, Bill Holman tenor sax, Med Flory baritone sax; Lou Levy piano, Joe Mondragon bass, Mel Lewis drums. Granz is producer, Paich arranger/conductor, not producer. [lightly_jazzdisco, lightly_musicbrainz]
- **Sound:** agile lightly orchestrated Swing Era standards, moderate small-ensemble drive, occasional scat ("Little Jazz"), affectionate and sometimes teasing vocal delivery. Horn arrangements supported; no blanket digital effects or modern production devices. [lightly_jazzlibrary, lightly_allmusic]
- **Numbers:** energy 3, mood 4, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 3, groove 4, electronic 0; based on album's mostly swinging midtempo groove and occasional ballads, original band configuration and 1958 recording methods. `landmark=1` marks a distinctive well-known Paich collaboration; `confidence=medium` because issue year is disputed.

## Genre vocabulary / collisions

New (or still awaiting update after Phase 01-02): `americanpopularsong`, `earlyjazzvocals`, `bop`. These are exact AllMusic style labels but Fandome's present `src/data/scenes.ts` and validator did not include them when checked. Especially `americanpopularsong` is a repertoire classification, and should be approved as a genre or stored separately; **no silent relabeling**. All other genre/instrument/production tokens used here have been verified against current Fandome guide. The 1958/59 year for Ella Swings Lightly must be resolved before a final reviewed import.

## What is *not* an album-specific influence

No named influenced_by entries: no Ella or producer interview in the gathered materials specifically links these individual records to the inspiration of named artists. Songwriters Gershwin/Berlin, arranger Weston/Garcia/Paich, accompanist Louis Armstrong, Getz or any covered composition do not qualify under the project's owner rules. Editorial `sounds_like` comparisons must stay in their own field. `status=draft` on every line; Claude should resolve date/style concerns before reviewed status.
