# Donatella Rettore — Phase 02: evidence notes (English)
Research date: 2026-10-25. Original studio LPs only, chronological 1981–1985; 38 distinct original tracks inspected across sources. Track inventories are **metadata and published criticism review**, not a claim of first-hand listening. Distinguish a documented genre from a plausible stylistic reading or an instrument credit. Even one reliably documented minor style remains in `genres`, flagged by `scope` in genre-evidence. Unknown is blank or described as unverified. Review sources explicitly distinguish album-level versus individual-track judgments.
## Evidence hierarchy and scale discipline
Original sleeve/personnel transcriptions and official artist/label discographies establish original editions, instruments, writers and who received producer credit. Critical reviews support styles and analysis. Broad vendor genres (especially the original pressing `Funk/Soul` family) are kept with weaker evidence. `electronic` scores are about whole-album source balance; synth use on one track is insufficient to label an entire album 5/5. Scores are editorial judgments with uncertainty: instrumentation is better documented than per-track tempo, mix and lyrics. No recorded influence on a specific LP means `influenced_by` stays empty.
## Estasi clamorosa (1981) — `rettore-estasiclamorosa`
**Original object / catalogue:** Ariston AR/LP 12387. Already in the Fandome catalog, no creation or renaming.
**Whole-row values:** Genres `pop|poprock|synthpop|disco|ska|rock|electronic|funk|soul`; instrument tokens `electricguitar|bass|drums|keyboards|percussion|piano|saxophone|organ|mallets|kazoo`; lyrics `identity|irony|party|love`. Scores energy=3, electronic=2, mood=3, theatrical=4, experimental=2, polish=4, tempo=3, groove=3. These numerical values are qualified album-level editorial inferences; no audio-analysis measurements were available.
**Genre interpretation:** Discographic style tags Pop Rock, Synth-pop, Disco refer to the LP; OndaRock explicitly tags `Donatella` ska. The pressing broad category `Funk/Soul` is weak and *does not* identify particular tracks as soul performances. Avoid applying ska to every track. The instrumental credit for Gotta Go notes vibraphone, Clamoroso kazoo and sax, Donatella sax. Main non-listed instruments `keyboards` and `kazoo` require tokens added by Claude.
**Personnel and production:** Original credits name Roberto Danè producer and Claudio Rego as realization collaborator; only Danè placed in `producers` pending confirmation whether the collaborator credit means co-production. Pinuccio Pirazzoli arranger, not producer. Rego, Pirazzoli, Leandro and various session players handle documented instruments. `electric` style genre should not be taken as proof of one specific synthesizer on every track.
**Critical disagreement and uncertainty:** Davide Bassi/OndaRock considers this a weaker LP than its predecessors, while it contains the iconic ska hit. A blanket high-energy ska score would be biased. Voice `clear|powerful` is a broad observational descriptor, not precise track-by-track transcription. English-language Gotta Go and Remember establish `it|en`. `landmark=1` refers to album containing signature hit; album itself not critically canonized.
**Original-track evidence inventory (specificity and abstentions):**
- **Diva:** Electric guitar/bass/drums/keyboards; LP-level pop-rock context only.
- **Curiosa:** Electric Hammond organ and guitars/drums documented; exact genre unconfirmed.
- **Mamma:** Guitar, bass, drums, percussion, keyboards; no secure separate subgenre.
- **Estasi:** Multiple guitars and Tullio De Piscopo drums; album-level Pop Rock.
- **Donatella:** Ska explicitly named by OndaRock; saxophone Claudio Pascoli, electric guitars, grand piano.
- **Gotta go (Gola giù-liva):** English lyric; Rego vibraphone/drums/percussion; Kim & Trutz backing vocals.
- **Meteora:** Guitar/drum/bass plus grand piano and marimba; no verified specific genre beyond album context.
- **Il filo della notte:** Electric guitar, bass, drums/percussion and keyboards.
- **Clamoroso:** Baritone sax Sergio Rigon and kazoo Claudio Rego; novelty texture, no fixed genre asserted.
- **Verrò:** Electric guitars, bass and drums; no confirmed standalone subgenre.
- **Remember:** Elton John/Bernie Taupin original for Rettore; piano/keyboards and rhythm section, English.

## Kamikaze Rock 'n' Roll Suicide (1982) — `rettore-kamikazerocknrollsuicide`
**Original object / catalogue:** Ariston AR/LP 12398, nine-track original 1982 LP. New Fandome album, slug as title.
**Whole-row values:** Genres `newwave|synthpop|pop|electronic|rock|punk|ballad|march`; instrument tokens `synth|electricguitar|bass|drums|keyboards|percussion|piano`; lyrics `social|political|irony|storytelling|identity|heartbreak|sensuality`. Scores energy=4, electronic=3, mood=2, theatrical=5, experimental=3, polish=4, tempo=4, groove=4. These numerical values are qualified album-level editorial inferences; no audio-analysis measurements were available.
**Genres:** OndaRock (Claudio Fabretti 2022) calls the *album* new wave, hears synth-wave/pop influence and names punk-wave on Garage, danceable new wave on Lamette, a march-like Sangue and a very sparse piano Giulietta. Thus all those *minority* genres appear instead of discarding a single song. The album title alone is not proof of rock-and-roll. Distinguish a sonic `punk` track from punk cultural imagery and a `march` rhythmic texture from a military recording.
**Instrumentation/production:** Original 1982 Ariston sleeve credits Lele Melotti and Claudio Rego drums, Cappellotto/Steffan bass, Pirazzoli/Steffan electric guitar, Leandro keyboards and Oberheim/Prophet/ARP 2600 programming, Maurizio Preti percussion. OndaRock documents piano in Oblio and piano-led Giulietta. `synth` is certain; `sequencer` and `drummachine` are left out because programming alone does not identify those devices. Roberto Danè is producer, Rego production collaborator; Pirazzoli arranger. No assertion that each synth appears in each track.
**Artist-specified influences:** Rettore’s quoted memoir on her official biography lists Siouxsie and the Banshees, Joy Division, Soft Cell, Talking Heads, Devo and Blondie while she was preparing the album. These are included in `influenced_by`; OndaRock separately makes sonic comparisons to Blondie, Devo, OMD and Ultravox. No claim all are direct inspirations for each track. Emotional scores account for intense satirical tracks *and* tender Giulietta, so low mood does not mean low energy.
**Original-track evidence inventory (specificity and abstentions):**
- **Kamikaze Rock ’n’ Roll Suicide:** Opening concept track; darker/lower tempo than Lamette per OndaRock.
- **Karakiri:** OndaRock: unsettled keyboards in dark Japanese-themed narrative.
- **Oblio:** Storm-effect opening and tragic piano described by OndaRock.
- **Sayonara:** Synthesizer riff, effects resembling explosions, and rock guitar explicitly described.
- **Lamette:** Fast forceful danceable new wave; irony surrounding self-harm imagery.
- **Garage:** Punk-wave hammering pulse, gendered workplace harassment narrative.
- **Sangue del mio sangue:** Critic calls out playful march cadence and sensual dark surreal imagery.
- **Canta sempre:** Present on original LP; track-specific arrangement not established.
- **Giulietta:** Piano-led spare tragic ballad (Roméo and Juliet imagery).

## Far West (1983) — `rettore-farwest`
**Original object / catalogue:** CGD 20384, nine-track 1983 original LP. New Fandome album.
**Whole-row values:** Genres `pop|poprock|rock|electronic`; instrument tokens `electricguitar|bass|drums|synth|keyboards|drummachine|saxophone|piano`; lyrics `storytelling|identity|love|sensuality`. Scores energy=3, electronic=3, mood=3, theatrical=5, experimental=3, polish=4, tempo=3, groove=3. These numerical values are qualified album-level editorial inferences; no audio-analysis measurements were available.
**Album sound:** Original LP is broadly Pop/Rock and Pop Rock in original-edition genre inventories; Rolling Stone retrospective highlights experimental electronics, corroborated by keyboards/synths/drum machine credits. It was conceptually planned as soundtrack for an *unrealized* Western-themed stage musical: do not label as actual soundtrack or proven country music. Western clothing, ranch names, cowboy imagery and a song titled Can Can are not musical-genre evidence.
**Instrument credits/production:** Musicbase transcription of 1983 LP documents guitars/bass by Paolo Steffan/Pinuccio Pirazzoli, Lele Melotti drums, Gaetano Leandro keyboards/synth, Pirazzoli programming and piano, drum machines and saxophone by Gabriele Comeglio. `Can can` has sax noted specifically by the album entry; other instruments not mapped to exact tracks. Credit explicitly calls Rego a co-producer, so `producers=Roberto Danè|Claudio Rego`. Pirazzoli arranger, not album producer.
**Lyrics and uncertainty:** Vera concerns identity/authenticity, the Western songs use narrative staging; Can can is described as sensual. The Elton John/Gary Osborne composition Sweetheart on parade is *written* for the project, not proof Elton John plays, produces or influences the album sound. Rolling Stone calls this record adventurous, contrasting with its commercially disappointing reception. Do not assign `country`, `musicaltheatre` or `cancan` on concept/title alone.
**Original-track evidence inventory (specificity and abstentions):**
- **Rodeo:** Western-themed framing; not evidence of musical country genre.
- **Far West:** Title track; studio synths and drum machine documented album-wide, track allocation unknown.
- **La cantante del saloon:** Stage/songwriting saloon persona; not evidence of country instrumental arrangement.
- **Ranch (Lola Pink):** Narrative Western stage imagery; 1983 single B-side.
- **Can can:** Review specifically calls out driving saxophone; cancan song title is not proof of classical can-can rhythm.
- **Il ponte dei sospiri:** Promoted song; song-level genre unconfirmed.
- **Io ho te:** Major 1983 single; no automatic dance genre from chart success.
- **Vera:** Authenticity/persona lyric documented; genre unresolved beyond LP.
- **Sweetheart on parade:** English song newly written by Elton John/Gary Osborne, no Elton production or performance credit.

## Danceteria (1985) — `rettore-danceteria`
**Original object / catalogue:** CGD 20467, nine-track 1985 original LP; not the 2012 combined reissue nor the 2025 reissue. New Fandome album.
**Whole-row values:** Genres `synthpop|dance|pop|electronic|rock`; instrument tokens `synth|keyboards|electricguitar|bass|drums|percussion|acousticguitar|saxophone|flute|accordion|mallets`; lyrics `love|heartbreak|nostalgia|identity|irony`. Scores energy=3, electronic=4, mood=3, theatrical=4, experimental=2, polish=4, tempo=3, groove=3. These numerical values are qualified album-level editorial inferences; no audio-analysis measurements were available.
**Genres and contrasting tracks:** Saifam 40th-anniversary label notes synth-pop and electronic textures. Encyclopedia catalogs album as Dance/Rock/Pop; because these are not detailed per-track genre annotations, keep them as broad styles, with exact per-track rock classification unresolved. Rettore describes Femme fatale as melancholic and specifically identifies Giù dal nero ciel and Il porco romantico as sensitive/romantic songs. Therefore `mood` and `groove` reflect both club/synth pop and softer tracks.
**Instrumentation:** personnel credits synth/bass/electric and acoustic guitars (Peter Woelpl), Curt Cress drums and percussion (explicitly Notte vana), keyboards (Laszlo Bencker, Walter Zweifel), accordion (Armin Rusch), sax and flute (Giuseppe Solera), marimba (Arnold Riedhammer), percussion Pit Troja, backing voices Paradise Birds. `keyboards` is a newly required instrument token; marimba maps to allowed `mallets`. Avoid asserting all instruments play on every song.
**Production credit conflict:** Hit Parade Italia assigns production to Roberto Danè, Peter Leudemann and Claudio Rego; Qobuz labels Roberto Danè *producer* and the other two *executive producer* on certain tracks. This file’s `producers` includes the credited principal producer Danè only, while recognizing Leudemann/Rego roles separately; Claude may revise after inspecting original jacket, but never treat arranging credits as production. German recording sessions were at Paradise Studio Munich. No direct documented named musical influences for this album.
**Edition caution:** original 1985 album not a remix of the 2012 double-CD Far West/Danceteria; do not transfer retrospective compilation `New Wave` or `Europop` blindly to an individual 1985 LP. The two English tracks are Diamonds clubs and spades and I.O.U. (a cover, not a new Elton John song); remaining tracks Italian.
**Original-track evidence inventory (specificity and abstentions):**
- **Femme fatale:** Retrospective 1930s–40s film-diva conceit, melancholy rather than uniform club euphoria.
- **Diamonds clubs and spades:** English song, TV sports-program theme; style within synth-pop album.
- **Il porco romantico:** Official biography explicitly calls it sensitive/romantic, minority emotional counterweight.
- **I.O.U.:** English-language cover of earlier US song; production differs from original, no automatic country tag.
- **Finché si è giovani:** Original single B-side, softer theme not enough to declare an instrument-specific sound.
- **Stringimi più forte:** Original music video and television theme; album context synth-pop.
- **Notte vana:** Curt Cress explicitly played drums/percussion on this track.
- **Giù dal nero ciel:** Officially described as emotionally intense; singled out by Rolling Stone; no fixed subgenre.
- **Chi sarà chi nel 1985:** Original track; title alone cannot establish futuristic genre.

## Known conflicts / boundaries
- **26 vs 27 columns:** User-required exchange contract is 26 columns and *does not* include `production`. But GitHub branch `descriptors` guide / validator as checked on 2026-10-25 now expects **27 columns**, with `production` after `instruments`. Importer must insert an empty `production` column *before* `lyrics` when translating this deliverable to the repository. Do not shift 26-field values into 27-field positions, and do not invent production values. Any additional production extraction would require a separately sourced edit by Claude.
- **New instrument tokens:** `keyboards`, `kazoo` are specifically credited but absent from the restricted list. Insert them in `src/data/instruments.ts` before validation or, if project policy requires a different mapping, discuss and map with evidence; do not silently delete them.
- **No new genre tokens** required in current `scenes.ts` (new wave, punk, ballad, march and ska already exist).
- **Genre granularity:** album-level tags are not proof every track conforms; vendor Funk/Soul is particularly underspecified. Source column in genre evidence holds a specific link and scope. **Instruments** are sleeve-based, track scope identified where possible.
