# Tiffany — Fandome phase 01 — evidence notes

Research checked: 29 October 2026. **All four entries are draft editorial proposals, not human-reviewed.** No files were changed in GitHub.

## Scope and original editions
The American singer Tiffany Renee Darwish is the subject, not artists with the same first name. The four original studio albums: *Tiffany* (1987, MCA original 10 tracks); *Hold an Old Friend’s Hand* (1988, MCA original 11 tracks including the instrumental Overture); *New Inside* (1990, original MCA audio CD has 10 indexed tracks even though printed booklet numbers 11); *Dreams Never Die* (1993, originally 12 songs issued in Asian countries only). Do **not** add the 2005 *Dreams Never Die* six bonus tracks to the 1993 album's evidence. These versions are chronologically released studio albums; no live albums, compilations or retrospective reissues are included.

The repository `descriptors` branch was checked: neither `catalog.ts` nor `seed.ts` has a Tiffany entry, and `docs/descriptors/tiffany.csv` does not exist. Proposed artist ID `tiffany`; it is not currently assigned. Every album must be added to `src/data/catalog.ts` before import. The catalogue includes neither the artist nor these four titles.

## Tiffany (1987)
Genres from AllMusic: Teen Pop, Dance-Pop, Adult Contemporary, Contemporary Pop/Rock. In the written review Bryan Buss identifies a country-pop approach beyond the dance singles, so `countrypop` is not inferred from her childhood country background. `dancepop` remains high because the album's best-known single is a programmed dance-pop cover. Track-by-track session credits from Sessiondays specify John Duarte synthesizers and drum programming on most tracks, synth bass on “Johnny’s Got the Inside Moves,” guitars from Dann Huff, Chuck Yamek et al., Richard Elliot saxophone, Steve Rucker piano and Willie Ornelas live drums on “Could’ve Been.” Hence guitar is intentionally the generic `guitar`, since the available transcription does not specify electric for every part, and `drummachine` is omitted because no hardware model was documented. No production-model tag has been invented. The voice's rasp and strength are described in retrospective reviews and her later interviews; numeric values judge the album's balance of catchy pop and melancholy ballads, **not** isolated single remixes. `sounds_like=Stevie Nicks` is a song-level subjective comparison in Tiffany.org, **not** an album-specific documented influence. Lyrics are broad editorial themes, especially romance, disappointed affection and teenage identity. The record peaked #1, therefore landmark 1.

## Hold an Old Friend’s Hand (1988)
AllMusic album styles are adult contemporary, dance-pop, teen pop, contemporary pop/rock; *original 1988 MCA LP* is labeled pop rock. `synthpop` comes only from a secondary catalog and is deliberately placed last. The **original LP** explicitly credits John Duarte on keyboards and drum programming, Michael Thompson guitar, Richard Elliot saxophone and George Tobin production. Tiffany.org explicitly identifies Grant Geissman's acoustic-guitar-only Overture, making `acousticguitar` an album-specific track tail. LP sleeve notes say the recordings **and mixes were digital** in August–October 1988 at Roxx Studios, North Hollywood, on a Trident DI-AN console. This supports `digitalrecording`; it does *not* support a tape machine, sampler, or a named synth. The duet “Hearts Never Lie” is a guest performance with Chris Farren, not a co-billed album and not an artistic influence. The material spans uptempo “Radio Romance” and yearning songs “All This Time” / the title track; `nostalgia` and `heartbreak` are grounded in the reviewed lyrics. Landmark 1 covers the singer’s first major follow-up with noted singles; this is an editorial choice.

## New Inside (1990)
The 1990 album's attempted pivot away from the early teen sound is documented by AllMusic and contemporary reviews: urban contemporary/R&B and new jack swing. AllMusic additionally supplies Dance-Pop, Adult Contemporary and Teen Pop styles, the latter a **catalogue legacy category** and kept at low weight. More credible album-level core is `rnb|newjackswing`; their order is editorial, based on review emphasis. Maurice Starr produced *Tiff’s Back*; Phillip Damien produced the lead single and other tracks, so producer list includes both. **Dick Scott is credited only as executive producer** and must not be listed in producers. The title-song rap credited to Dennis Cheese is Donnie Wahlberg. `rap` in voice reflects this one track (not Tiffany’s lead voice) and should be considered a low-weight editorial choice; **Tiffany's own vocal delivery is raspy and strong**. The electronic production is documented in critics' synthesizer discussion; `synth` and `percussion` are supported by the review, but there is no securely established drum-machine brand or documented studio treatment, so `production` is intentionally empty. `sounds_like=New Kids on the Block` is an editorial comparison arising from the period’s Maurice Starr urban-pop sound, not an album producer being treated as an influence. Score 4 electronic/groove and tempo reflects frequent high-energy cuts, while “Here in My Heart” and “There Could Never” are slower. **Release-day disagreement:** AllMusic/MCA catalog and Tiffany.org have October 2, 1990; some reference works list September 25. Only the year is stored, do not assert exact date until resolved. The CD cover lists 11 titles because a very short “A Moment to Rest” interlude is merged with “Tenderly” in its track indexing. Do not invent an additional independent release.

## Dreams Never Die (1993)
Use the **1993 Japanese/Asian MCA album**, not Headliner’s 2005 expansion; AllMusic's 78-minute listing corresponds to the latter reissue and its Dance-Pop style may span expanded material, so `dancepop` has lowest genre weight. Contemporary/historical release notes classify the original pop, pop-rock and country-pop; the latter also has **steel guitar credited on the album** (Doug Livingston). Album booklet personnel, transcribed on the discography page, name John Duarte keyboards, Bret Zwier drums, guitarists Grant Geissman, Kevin Dukes, Keith Howland, Monty Byrom, Drew Nichols acoustic guitar and backing singers. No named production machines are documented; production intentionally blank. The original 12-track list is independently confirmed by Tiffany.org and AudioDB. AllMusic's original-era review describes a mellow adult contemporary return and compares her voice with Stevie Nicks and Mariah Carey; these two go under `sounds_like`, never `influenced_by`. `countrypop` and `poprock` have weaker source quality than the album style; flag for human review, not an invented claim of sound alone. **Production attribution caveat:** 1993 material describes **George Tobin as producer, in association with John Duarte**; some aggregated credits list both as producers. Because “in association with” is not unambiguously an explicit co-producer credit, the CSV lists only George Tobin. Claude should check the original Japanese booklet for John Duarte's precise role before promoting him to a producer. The proposed harder-rock US version never appeared; no genre of that projected version is assigned here.
