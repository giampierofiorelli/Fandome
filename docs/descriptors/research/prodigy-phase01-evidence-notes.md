# The Prodigy — Phase 01: evidence and judgement notes

Research date: 25 October 2026. Four phase-only records. Rows are draft editorial descriptors, not audio-signal measurements. Citations below are URLs also present in the sources CSV. Higher confidence in release/credit claims than exact placement of a borderline subgenre.

## 1992 — *Experience* (`prodigy-experience`)

- **Genre order:** AllMusic explicitly characterises the album as rave/hardcore techno with rapid breakbeat techno and records it under electronic/club-dance. Album of the Year further classifies it as breakbeat hardcore and retroactively as jungle/ragga jungle. Jungle terms are secondary, particularly associated with `Ruff in the Jungle Bizness`; do not present the 1992 album as an established drum-and-bass record. The Max Romeo reggae vocal sample is specific to `Out of Space`, not a reggae-album claim.
- **Instrument and production:** In his GAFFA interview Liam Howlett expressly names Roland W-30 for all sequencing and the Akai S1000 added while recording *Experience*; Mackie's interview describes a CR1604 mixer on early records including this album. MusicBrainz identifies Howlett's keyboards, Simone's vocals and Maxim's MC presence. A general 1996 Sound On Sound studio inventory must *not* be used to backfill later Korg/Emu/Cubase devices to 1992. `sampler|sequencer|keyboards|synth|drummachine|piano` includes functions of the W-30 and the raving piano parts; not a claim of each part being individually credited on every track. `pitchedvocals` supported by chipmunk/speed-shifted sampled voices described by AllMusic and discography.
- **Scales:** energy 5 / tempo 5 / groove 5 match the review's frantic rave tempos and club orientation. electronic 5 describes dominant synthesised/sampled sources, *not* proof every sound was synthetically generated. mood 4 is jubilant but some passages feel manic; theatrical 2 because there is more sonic spectacle than conventional staged vocal drama. experimental 2 recognises cut-and-paste hooks while retaining accessible club structures. polish 3 recognises resourceful home recording rather than a glossy expensive studio finish.
- **Voices/lyrics:** predominantly instrumental/sample-led, with Maxim rap/MC parts and Simone vocals. `party` describes the dance/celebratory focus; `social` is minor, associated with the public-service source on `Charly`. Lyrics are fragmentary and not thematic throughout.
- **Producers:** Liam Howlett alone. Sampled performers, session vocalists and songwriting-credit holders are not producers. `influenced_by` intentionally blank: sampling Max Romeo or Ultramagnetic MCs proves sample provenance, not necessarily that they inspired the *whole album*.
- **Disagreement/caveat:** AllMusic prints an October 1992 release listing while original XL UK releases are catalogued in September 1992. Year, not month/day, is stored. Reviews of “kiddie rave” image differ from modern landmark assessment.

## 1994 — *Music for the Jilted Generation* (`prodigy-musicforthejiltedgeneration`)

- **Genre order:** AllMusic identifies grittier UK breakbeat techno/hardcore, still rooted in rave and approaching drum-and-bass. Contemporary dance/techno is the foundation, with big beat a **retrospective** label. Rock guitars occur on identifiable tracks; they do not replace the electronic core. `acidbreaks` is track-scoped as Album of the Year names the substyle; AllMusic explicitly says the band moved *away* from its earlier acid-house association, so `acidhouse` is rightly omitted.
- **Instruments:** XL release sleeve documents Lance Riddler playing live guitar on `Voodoo People`, Phil Bent live flute on `3 Kilos`. Producer-engineer Neil McLellan names the W-30 sequencer/sampler and Roland TR-909 in the setup; synth and keyboard roles also supported in AllMusic credits. `electricguitar` is a cautious Fandome normalization of a credited live guitar part, amplified on a heavy electronic/rock track; original sleeve says `live guitar`, not the electric qualifier.
- **Production:** Roland W-30 had limited sample memory and drove patterns; Neil describes a TR-909 connected to the sequencer, and Howlett's Mackie 8-Bus mixing was used in the Jilted-era setup. Sampling and distortion are audible and discussed by critics/interviews. Do not invent a single brand of hardware for all drum or synth sounds.
- **Producers:** XL sleeve/MusicBrainz establish Liam Howlett for all tracks, Neil McLellan co-production on the subset `Full Throttle`, `Voodoo People`, `The Heat (The Energy)`, `No Good (Start the Dance)` and `One Love` (track numbers 4,5,7,9,10 on CD). Note that the Howlett/McLellan split needs care: not all album tracks have both credited.
- **Scales:** energy 5 but tempo 4 due to instrumental midtempo intervals in the Narcotic Suite; groove 5 from substantial dancefloor cuts. electronic 4 and polish 4 reflect sampled/electronic cores plus some live instrumentation and professionally refined mixes. mood 2 dark/intense, theatrical 3, experimental 3 thanks to extended suites and hybrid structures.
- **Political interpretation dispute:** Critics place *Their Law* in opposition to the UK Criminal Justice and Public Order Act, but Dazed republishes Howlett rejecting “political band” as a whole-group identity. `political|social` applies chiefly to the title/framing and this track; party culture spans others. Avoid attributing explicit manifestos to all songs.
- **No documented named album-wide influences:** `influenced_by` empty, to prevent converting a musical resemblance, sample or collaborator into a historical influence claim.

References: https://www.allmusic.com/album/music-for-the-jilted-generation-mw0000622674 ; https://theprodigy.info/discography/official/music-for-the-jilted-generation/cd-xl-recordings-xlcd-114/ ; https://musictech.com/features/prodigy-engineer-neil-mclellan-jilted-generation/ ; https://theprodigy.info/articles/england/a-soundtrack-for-the-jilted-generation.html

## 1997 — *The Fat of the Land* (`prodigy-thefatoftheland`)

- **Genres:** AllMusic foregrounds big beat, funky breaks, electronica and club/dance. Hip-hop sample/rhythm influence is specific on `Diesel Power` and other tracks; rock/punk textures are strongest on Flint-fronted cuts and the L7 cover `Fuel My Fire`. Keep `punkrock` towards the tail; do not imply the whole release is punk. `techno` is a lower-weight broad historical connection rather than central as in 1992.
- **Production and instrumentation:** The original XL sleeve reproduces *Smack My Bitch Up*'s Ultramagnetic MCs and *Firestarter*'s Breeders/Art of Noise sources. MusicBrainz credits Jim Davies as guitarist and Liam as producer/mixer. Sound On Sound's 1996 interview gives an *era-specific* equipment inventory including Korg Prophecy, Akai S3200, E-mu SP1200 and Cubase. MusicTech relates the Korg Prophecy riff to `Smack My Bitch Up` and the SP1200 to `Firestarter`. Liam specifically says he built/mixed *The Fat of the Land* on his Mackie 8-Bus. `sampling|distortion` are justified by both primary and technical sources. `sequencer` is a functional unit in that equipment setup, not a claim of the Roland W-30 being exclusively used for every song.
- **Vocals:** Keith Flint snarls on `Firestarter` and `Breathe`; Maxim raps/shouts and Kool Keith guests on `Diesel Power`, while Saffron/Crispian Mills/Shahin Badar diversify texture. Do not infer the `vocalfilter` token from raspy vocal timbre. `language=en` is the main language although vocal snippets and non-English-adjacent melismatic delivery exist.
- **Scales:** energy 5, groove 4, electronic 4, theatrical 4 (the aggressive frontman performance), polish 4 (forceful sonic craft, deliberate abrasive saturation) and mood 2 (menacing with moments of exhilaration). Experimental 2 recognises manipulative sampling within forceful repeated-hook songwriting. Tempo 4, not 5, given slower/headier tracks such as `Climbatize`.
- **Producer credit:** Liam Howlett only. Neil McLellan is credited as engineer on recordings here, not automatically an album co-producer. The sampled authors are also not producers of the new album.
- **Caveat:** 30 June 1997 original UK release shown by XL archival sleeve versus 1 July AllMusic market listing. Correct year is 1997. MusicTech's SP1200 and Korg-track attribution are secondary technical retrospective supported by contemporaneous Sound On Sound inventory; if Claude disputes the track-level attribution, downgrade those tokens rather than extrapolate.

References: https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-fat-of-the-land-mw0000054699 ; https://theprodigy.info/discography/official/the-fat-of-the-land/cd-xl-recordings-xlcd-121/ ; https://www.soundonsound.com/people/liam-howlett-prodigy-firestarter ; https://musictech.com/guides/essential-guide/landmarks-prodigy-fat-of-the-land/

## 1999 — *Prodigy Present: The Dirtchamber Sessions Volume One* (`prodigy-prodigypresentthedirtchambersessionsvolumeone`)

- **Type/credit:** Historically listed as a **compilation**; sonically a continuous DJ mix performed by Liam Howlett, released under `Prodigy Present`, which has been filed under The Prodigy in discographies. Do NOT present it as newly composed, produced Prodigy music. The album `producers` field is empty since Howlett's documented role here is DJ/selector/mixer, **not** producer of every sampled original track.
- **Genre map:** XL and AllMusic describe an intentional pan-genre mix of hip-hop, funk, big beat, techno and rock/punk records. Album styles describe the *contents of the DJ collage*, not each original song or a shared recording-session production aesthetic. Genre entries are deliberately some-tracks scoped where appropriate. `hiphop` and `funk` receive first priority because Howlett expressly calls this his foundation.
- **Instruments/production:** Howlett told Alternative Press that the master-mix used just two turntables and an ADAT recorder. This supports `turntables` and the proposed new production token `adatrecording`. Do not insert all guitars, bass, drum machines, saxophones etc. appearing across borrowed commercial records into Fandome's own equipment list: such instruments are not credited as newly performed on this DJ mix. `sampling` is deliberately absent as digital sample playback/editing is not established by the source as core production method, although the mix contains sourced excerpts and scratches.
- **Influence:** The original booklet expressly names Double Dee and Steinski, Coldcut, Big Apple Productions, Grandmaster Flash `...Wheels of Steel`, DJ Cheese, Cash Money, Cutmaster Swift, Mixmaster Mike and the Scratch Piklz as influences. These are documented influences for the *mix*, not automatically separately confirmed for each earlier Prodigy album. The influences field lists normalized performer/group names.
- **Scales:** energetic mixed-source dance listening (energy 4, groove 4, tempo 4), electronic 3 because significant analogue funk, hip-hop and punk originals coexist with electronics, experimental 4 for intense cross-genre cutups, polish 2 for intentionally rough, non-seamless live turntable mixing, mood 3 mixed and theatrical 2. These reflect the resulting audio collage rather than style of any individual constituent recording.
- **Critical disagreement:** John Bush (AllMusic) praises the old-school DJ flow; Caroline Sullivan's Guardian review (quoted in discography) questions its purpose beyond turntable virtuosity. Review disagreement retained rather than collapsed into an imagined consensus. First issued 22 Feb 1999 UK; AllMusic date 6 Apr 1999 reflects another market edition.
