# KC and the Sunshine Band — research evidence and decisions, phase 02

Research date: 2026-10-28. Fandome branch `descriptors` read-only. Four original album rows; all are `draft`, subject to second-person review. Fandome 27-column guide governs values.

## Who Do Ya (Love) (1978)

- **Identity:** Original 1978 TK studio album, sometimes styled *Who Do Ya Love* without parentheses. Its id normalizes punctuation to `kcandthesunshineband-whodoyalove`. The original is eight tracks; bonus tracks, later edits and compilations are excluded. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/cd633d54-d1e4-47f1-885d-64b05feeeff2), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/who-do-ya-love-mw0000355305).
- **Production/personnel:** Harry Wayne Casey and Richard Finch produced. Casey keyboards and lead vocals; Jerome Smith guitar; Finch bass, and drums only on "It's the Same Old Song" and "I Will Love You Tomorrow"; Robert Johnson drums elsewhere, Fermin Goytisolo percussion, trumpets Faulk/Tanno, saxes Lewis/Sidener. [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/), [original credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Do_Ya_%28Love%29), [official track metadata](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/original-album-series-kc-the-sunshine-band/0825646944149). No specific synthesizer or drum machine is documented: electronic **0**, `production` blank rather than guessing analog-tape techniques.
- **Genres:** [Alex Henderson, AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/who-do-ya-love-mw0000355305) specifically uses disco-funk **and** disco-soul and notes Latin/Caribbean details. `discofunk|discosoul|disco|dancepop|rnb` are supported: both exact hybrid genre tokens exist in Fandome's source union. Do **not** add `latin` or `reggae` as whole-album genres based only on a track's colour.
- **Scales:** energetic and strongly groove-based but less dramatic than *Part 3*: `energy=4`, `groove=5`, `theatrical=2`, `polish=3` editorial assessments. 1978 commercial reach declined versus the earlier 1975–76 records, but that is not in itself evidence of a distinct sound. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/who-do-ya-love-mw0000355305), [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?name=KC+and+the+Sunshine+Band). `landmark=0` reflects album significance versus prior period.
- **Influences:** the Four Tops' *It's the Same Old Song* is a **cover**, not evidence they influenced the making of the entire album. Leave `influenced_by` empty. No other album-specific declared influences or rigorous sounds-like comparisons identified.

## Do You Wanna Go Party (1979)

- **Identity and production:** Original June 1979 TK studio LP, seven tracks. Casey/Finch share production. Not to be confused with later hits collections. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/do-you-wanna-go-party--mw0000838986), [MusicBrainz album chronology](https://musicbrainz.org/artist/76a5a114-ed6a-4eb6-8a81-ad5768d4b41c), [original personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_You_Wanna_Go_Party).
- **Two album poles:** [AllMusic album review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/do-you-wanna-go-party--mw0000838986) documents the heavier funk and extended party sound of the title track, contrasted with the slow, romantic *Please Don't Go*. The [AllMusic song analysis](https://www.allmusic.com/song/do-you-wanna-go-party-mt0054785803) describes the lengthy minimal vocal chants and bass/horn patterns of the title track. This mixture explains moderate `tempo=3`, not `5`, despite strong dance material.
- **Instruments:** Casey keyboards, Finch bass/drums/percussion, Jerome Smith guitar, French horns Jerry Peel/Joe Singer, trumpets Faulk/Tanno, saxes Lewis/Sidener, harp Valerie Vonpechy and string ensemble. Multiple backing voices. [Original session personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_You_Wanna_Go_Party), [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/), [Qobuz official credits](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/very-best-of-kc-the-sunshine-band/0724382918257). The `hornarrangements` / `stringarrangements` tags signal the documented arrangements, not any specific electronic processing.
- **Genres:** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/do-you-wanna-go-party--mw0000838986) explicitly supplies disco, funk, Dance-Pop and broad R&B. Latin accents on *Hooked on Your Love* are **track texture**, not blanket `latin` album classification. [AllMusic song review](https://www.allmusic.com/song/do-you-wanna-go-party-mt0054785803) compares the title track's bass figure to James Brown **as sonic analogy**; it is not a verified album influence. Keep `influenced_by` empty.
- **Significance:** `landmark=1` is justified by this album containing *Please Don't Go*, the band's final US No.1, and *Do You Wanna Go Party*, another substantial single. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/do-you-wanna-go-party--mw0000838986), [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/). Distinguish chart relevance from the 0–5 **polish** rating, which is based on arrangement and recording quality.

## The Painter (1981)

- **Original release:** September 1981, Epic, eleven-track studio album. [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/38d904c6-0296-438c-a1b4-164d8cd360c7) distinguishes original LP from 2015 14-track remaster; the 2015 bonuses are **excluded**. Casey/Finch again credited as producers; Milan Bogdan engineer/mixer. [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-painter-mw0000840270), [original notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Painter_%28KC_and_the_Sunshine_Band_album%29).
- **Documented stylistic transition:** Geoffrey Himes's [contemporary Washington Post review, Feb 11 1982](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1982/02/11/up-from-disco/7464ea83-7c0a-4d1a-991b-18408dab4979/) explicitly hears movement from four-to-the-floor disco toward classic soul's off-beat snare rhythm, Casey's warm tenor on Van McCoy's *Baby I'm Yours* and falsetto on *Love Me*. The review also mentions electronic accents and string writing. This is strong album-specific evidence for `soul` first, `falsetto` production, lower `groove=3`, and `electronic=1` rather than assuming synthpop solely from keyboards.
- **Credits:** Anthony Battaglia guitars/arrangements, Blue Weaver and Victor Paliuca synthesizers, bass by Finch and Emmanuel Taylor, drums Harold Seay, saxophones and brass, real strings. Paul Harris and Battaglia string arrangements. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-painter-mw0000840270), [original personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Painter_%28KC_and_the_Sunshine_Band_album%29), [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/). Use `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` as documented; synths have no verified specific maker/model.
- **Genre evidence:** `soul` from the Washington Post, `rnb`, `disco`, `dancepop` from AllMusic, `pop` from contemporary release classification, `funk` from [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/38d904c6-0296-438c-a1b4-164d8cd360c7). `funk` is a subordinate genre. The original album contains pop ballads and covers but **covers are not influences**; Donna Summer, Van McCoy, Bugatti-Musker are songwriters of included tracks, not documented formative models. `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` remain empty.
- **Judgments:** `energy=3`, `mood=3`, `experimental=2`, `polish=4`; `landmark=0`. A 1981 shift of direction is real, but it did not create an equally defining catalog album. Avoid reusing scores from the earlier 1979 LP.

## All in a Night's Work (1982)

- **Original album:** 10-track 1982 Epic studio LP recorded Miami and Los Angeles. Expanded CD editions include remixes and outtakes and are not the object rated. [Sessiondays liner notes](https://sessiondays.com/2024/03/09/1982-kc-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work/), [1982 vinyl original record](https://highfidelityla.com/release/9069193/kc-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/all-in-a-nights-work-mw0002845107).
- **Producer:** Harry Wayne Casey **and Richard Finch** for this original album, even though their working relationship declined after its completion. Production is not to be re-attributed to 1983 *KC Ten* collaborators. [Liner notes](https://sessiondays.com/2024/03/09/1982-kc-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work/), [Epic/Legacy recording metadata](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/all-in-a-nights-work-expanded-version-kc-the-sunshine-band/0886445248104).
- **Sound/instrumentation:** *Give It Up*, *(You Said) You'd Gimme Some More*, dance grooves, electric bass, drums, horns, synthesizer, guitar, percussion and backing chorus. [Pop Rescue track analysis](https://poprescue.com/2022/06/24/review-all-in-a-nights-work-by-kc-the-sunshine-band-vinyl-1982/) explicitly describes synths, brass, occasional huskiness in Casey's lead and a contrasting duet with Teri DeSario on *Don't Run*. [Personnel](https://sessiondays.com/2024/03/09/1982-kc-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work/) confirms horns by Jerry Hey, Larry Hall and band colleagues, plus harp Stacey Berkley and limited-track woodwinds. `strings` is **omitted** from instruments because while Mike Lewis is credited for string arrangement (track 10), source instruments explicitly list orchestral woodwinds and harp; string presence is not directly established in the verified personnel. `stringarrangements` is retained because arrangement credit is explicit. No unsupported DX7, Fairlight, LinnDrum or TR-808 claims.
- **Genres:** `disco|funk|rnb|synthpop|dancepop`: [discography summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_in_a_Night%27s_Work) gives disco, funk, R&B and synth-pop; [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/all-in-a-nights-work-mw0002845107) independently lists R&B; [Pop Rescue](https://poprescue.com/2022/06/24/review-all-in-a-nights-work-by-kc-the-sunshine-band-vinyl-1982/) documents the retro disco/early synth-pop palette and dance-pop pieces. Genre evidence file specifies `dancepop` as our editorial translation of the review's *disco pop* rather than an exact copied label. Do not imply the album is entirely electronic; score `electronic=2`.
- **Remix production:** original [liner notes](https://sessiondays.com/2024/03/09/1982-kc-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work/) credit John Luongo remixes to original tracks 1–3, so `remixing` is valid at album level, distinct from later **bonus remix versions**, which are excluded from the musical judgments. `hornarrangements` explicitly credited to Hey/Hall, `stringarrangements` to Mike Lewis on track 10.
- **Reception and landmark:** *Give It Up* was a major UK No.1 in 1983. [JazzRockSoul](https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/kc-and-the-sunshine-band/) and [retrospective](https://poprescue.com/2022/06/24/review-all-in-a-nights-work-by-kc-the-sunshine-band-vinyl-1982/) highlight it as defining the album. `landmark=1` reflects this career hit, not a declaration every track was acclaimed. [Pop Rescue](https://poprescue.com/2022/06/24/review-all-in-a-nights-work-by-kc-the-sunshine-band-vinyl-1982/) has mixed views of the second side; [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/348861-kc-and-the-sunshine-band-all-in-a-nights-work.php) contains sparse **user** reviews, not a reliable critic consensus.

## Across all four albums

- All `influenced_by` rows remain empty: no primary testimony found assigning specific **artists** as direct influences to these four albums. Harry Wayne Casey has named Stevie Wonder, Marvin Gaye, the Supremes, Temptations, Gladys Knight, Joe Cocker, James Brown, Chicago and Blood, Sweat & Tears in an [interview about his general influences](https://musicfeeds.com.au/features/kc-sunshine-band-dangers-fame-exciting-new-music/); this is **ARTIST-WIDE ONLY**, not evidence for all four original records. Do not convert Motown or Junkanoo traditions to an artist.
- `sounds_like` is also empty: covers, duet guests, labels, credits and inspiration analogies must not automatically create sonic-similarity edges. The AllMusic James Brown analogy is tied to one track and insufficient for a whole album relation.
- Numeric values are anchored editorial evaluations (0–5), not claimed research statistics. Specific devices in `production` only appear where explicit credits or accounts support them. Unknown methods stay empty.
- Source links are original, independently checkable URLs. All four rows carry `status=draft`, not a false claim of peer review. All 27 tokens follow the guide, with `discofunk` and `discosoul` already in the 2026-10-28 `Genre` union.
