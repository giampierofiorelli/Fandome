# Research and evidence notes — Laura Pausini, Fandome phase 01

Date checked: 29 October 2026. All rows are original Italian studio editions; they are draft research judgments, not audio-feature measurements. They contain **27 columns**, and every populated instrument/production cell is based on album credits or original-label documentation. A descriptor is not evidence that every track contains the feature.

## 1. Laura Pausini (1993) — `pausini-laurapausini`

- **Edition:** original CGD eight-song LP/CD, *not* later streaming compilations or the distinct Spanish 1994 album also called *Laura Pausini*. The 1993 first-edition debut features “La solitudine”, “Non c’è” and the duet “Mi rubi l’anima” (Raf). The duet is a track feature, not album co-billing. Qobuz's 2007-distributed 8-track label issue and the transcribed 1993 original European CD confirm the basic repertoire. Other Qobuz pages mix bonus tracks and English renditions, so do not calculate language from those.
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly classifies the album Euro-Pop, Italian Pop and Latin Pop; a 1993 pressing description supplies Soft Rock and Ballad. Adult Contemporary is a less direct album-level classification in the biographical album entry. `latinpop` is last because AllMusic's market-facing Latin designation does *not* demonstrate Latin rhythms in the Italian originals.
- **Instrumentation:** the original European CD credits Riccardo Galardini (acoustic guitar), Gianni Salvatori (electric guitar), Stefano Allegra (bass), Massimo Pacciani (drums, percussion) and Eric Buffat (piano, computer programming). Do not add saxophones or particular synth brands from the much wider catalogue.
- **Production:** `programming` is specifically credited to Buffat. The credit does not say drum machine, so `drumprogramming` is **not** asserted. No reliable exact model of an electronic instrument, tape medium or vocal processing was found.
- **Producer ambiguity:** 1993 original CD database transcribes “Producer – Angelo Valsiglio, Marco Marati”, whereas later Warner/Qobuz distinguishes Valsiglio as producer and Marati as *executive producer* on several individual tracks. Both names are retained **provisionally** because of that original physical CD credit; verify the sleeve before final import, particularly Marati's role.
- **Voice and themes:** a prominent full-range lead with restrained verses and high-intensity refrains justifies `powerful|clear`. Youthful separation and loss dominate; the 1997 direct interview confirms the debut's melancholic cast. `energy=2`, `mood=1`, `groove=1`, `polish=4` are comparative editorial ratings, not measured properties. `sounds_like` references are cautious editorial proximity to similarly arranged ballad repertoire; not artist-stated influence.
- Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/laura-pausini-italian-mw0000171948), [1993 CD personnel](https://clubdiva.ee/Laura-Pausini/35810), [Warner/Qobuz 1993 issue](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/laura-pausini-laura-pausini/0745099238568), [Folha artist interview, 1997](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/folhatee/fm190505.htm).

## 2. Laura (1994) — `pausini-laura`

- **Edition:** 1994 Italian ten-track studio album, including “Strani amori”, “Gente” and “Lettera”. The Spanish-language album *Laura Pausini* released later in 1994 contains new Spanish recordings of songs from the first two Italian records and should not silently generate a duplicate descriptor. Some AllMusic international CD pages date the same recording *1995*; the [original 1994 CD](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/45099557324509955732), [label edition](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/laura-laura-pausini/0745099557362) and [original label charts](https://swedishcharts.com/showitem.asp?cat=a&interpret=Laura+Pausini&titel=Laura) establish 1994.
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly lists Euro-Pop, Italian Pop and Latin Pop. 45cat labels the original release Pop and Easy Listening. Adult Contemporary is used as a contextual style label for the restrained ballad arrangements rather than a quote from the main AllMusic style tags; review before final use if strict explicit genre evidence is required. `latinpop` follows music reference classification, not an assertion of Spanish words in the Italian recording.
- **Instruments:** full original liner credits: Massimo Pacciani drums/percussion; Cesare Chiodo and Stefano Allegra bass; Gianni Salvatori and Riccardo Galardini electric/acoustic guitar; Simone Papi piano and computer programming; Luca Signorini sax. No branded sequencer or drum machine credited.
- **Producer:** Angelo Valsiglio is *artistic producer* on the original 1994 sleeve; Marco Marati is **executive producer/management**, not main sonic producer. `producers=Angelo Valsiglio` alone. Gianni Salvatori is an arranger, not producer.
- **Scales, voice, themes:** tuneful mid-tempo and big ballad choruses with a polished studio band, but little experimental material. Love, friendship and personal reflection coexist. Voice `powerful|clear`; `programming` is explicit. Similarity to Céline Dion/Giorgia is an editor's comparison, not evidence Pausini took influences from either.
- Sources: [45cat original sleeve](https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/45099557324509955732), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/laura-mw0000644050), [Qobuz label metadata](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/laura-laura-pausini/0745099557362).

## 3. Le cose che vivi (1996) — `pausini-lecosechevivi`

- **Edition / languages:** use the 12-track Italian CGD edition (1996). A **Brazilian 15-track** edition adds three Portuguese recordings, which must not make this *Italian original* `it|pt`. The parallel Spanish album *Las cosas que vives* is a separate language edition. Neither is a fresh original repertoire album in the present phase; keep as linked versions for a future variant-specific pass. Original 12-song list independently shown by [CGD Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/1jr2QzSLfXx4tgCNPaueSB) and [specialist discography](https://www.laurapausinidiscografia.it/1996---le-cose-che-vivi.html).
- **Genres:** AllMusic classifies Euro-Pop, Italian Pop and Latin Pop; the original CD pressing transcript supplies Soft Rock and Qobuz supplies Pop. The title single has a documented Pop Rock classification, deliberately *last*, because a single does not establish an entire album genre. The track-specific classifications are weaker than AllMusic's album-level styles.
- **Musicians:** [AllMusic original album credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/le-cose-che-vivi-mw0000079611) document Eric Buffat (piano, Hammond organ, keyboards, programming), Dado Parisini (keyboards), Gianni Salvatori / Riccardo Galardini (guitars), Andrea Braido (electric guitar), Cesare Chiodo / Nathan East (bass), Steve Ferrone / Massimo Pacciani (drums), and **London Symphony Orchestra**, led/arranged by Geoff Westley, on the orchestral closing track. The orchestra is not claimed to appear throughout.
- **Production:** `programming` is named for Buffat. `stringarrangements` is **not** assigned because the source names an orchestra plus arranger/conductor but no separate string-arrangement credit. Do not infer exact equipment models or multi-tracking.
- **Producers:** Warner/Qobuz 1996 original-track metadata repeatedly names **Laura Pausini, Alfredo Cerruti and Dado Parisini** as producers; AllMusic album table foregrounds Cerruti and Parisini. Include all three with role-variance caveat. Do not include engineers, arrangers or the Meringa executive-management company.
- **Themes & scales:** optimism and affection alongside family reflection (“Mi dispiace”) and a social-hope outlook (“Il mondo che vorrei”). Her 1997 interview contrasts the melancholy debut with this more optimistic album. An orchestrated song and dense harmonic choruses warrant theatrical=4, polish=5, but experimental=1. Editorial `sounds_like` identifies international ballad-production neighbours only.
- Sources: [AllMusic personnel](https://www.allmusic.com/album/le-cose-che-vivi-mw0000079611), [Qobuz 1996 track labels](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/le-cose-che-vivi-laura-pausini/0706301572764), [specialist collector release chronology](https://www.laurapausinidiscografia.it/1996---le-cose-che-vivi.html), [Folha 1997 interview](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/folhatee/fm190505.htm).

## 4. La mia risposta (1998) — `pausini-lamiarisposta`

- **Edition / language:** Italian original with 13 tracks, of which “Looking for an Angel” is **sung in English** and **written by Phil Collins**. Thus `it|en`. Do not imply that Collins is a guest vocalist or that an English song proves he influenced the original album's overall production. Spanish *Mi respuesta* is the parallel-language album, not this row; the English closer occurs on the Italian edition.
- **Genres:** AllMusic identifies Euro-Pop, Italian Pop, Latin Pop; the 1998 pressing style transcript names Soft Rock and Ballad. [Rolling Stone Italy](https://www.rollingstone.it/artista/laura-pausini/) describes **soul elements**, marked *texture/some tracks* and last in the genre list, not an invented full-album soul style. Pausini told La Repubblica (quoted in [Rockol, 1999](https://www.rockol.it/news-5266/intervista-a-laura-pausini)) she intentionally blended Italian melody and South American textures and American-rooted vocal approaches.
- **Instruments:** [1998 European CD original pressing](https://readdork.com/album/laura-pausini-la-mia-risposta/pressing/407584) gives Nathan East bass, Dado Parisini synth bass, John Robinson drums, Michael Landau/Ray Fuller/Riccardo Galardini electric guitars, Landau/Galardini acoustic guitars, Alex Richbourg drum programming. [AllMusic's shared Spanish counterpart](https://www.allmusic.com/album/mi-respuesta-mw0000046048/credits) also lists keyboards and piano for Parisini and percussion for John Robinson. The Italian original [CD transcript](https://musicbase.net/la-mia-risposta-042164s) specifically credits **Michael Landau with sitar on tracks 3 and 8**, correcting a conflicting aggregate attribution to Parisini in some reference credit tables.
- **Production:** `drumprogramming` is direct track-credit evidence on songs 3 and 10; `programming` also explicitly credited to Eric Buffat and Claudio Guidetti. No specific hardware or autotune credited; no `autotune` by inference. A number of backing singers are credited, but that alone is insufficient proof for `layeredvocals` technology: keep absent.
- **Producer hierarchy:** track-specific original [Warner recording metadata](https://www.qobuz.com/dk-en/album/lo-mejor-de-laura-pausini-volvere-junto-a-ti-laura-pausini/0809274107060) explicitly credits Laura Pausini, Alfredo Cerruti, Dado Parisini; some physical sleeve lists Cerruti as executive producer as well, and Parisini as realised/arranged. AllMusic also has Eric Buffat as producer on the Spanish counterpart; **do not automatically transfer his track-level role to the Italian original**.
- **Theme / scoring:** mature love, introspection and the social topic of “Anna dimmi sì”. Energy=3 and theatrical=3 reflect more driving songs amid strong ballads. Soul shades and prominent bass lines do not make groove a dance record (groove=2). An adult contemporary arrangement with Phil Collins songwriting credit does not prove Collins as direct influence; leave `influenced_by` blank.
- Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/la-mia-risposta-mw0000515602), [1998 pressing](https://readdork.com/album/laura-pausini-la-mia-risposta/pressing/407584), [1999 interview report](https://www.rockol.it/news-5266/intervista-a-laura-pausini), [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.it/artista/laura-pausini/).

## Artist-wide influences ≠ album-specific influences

The direct interview evidence is unusually good: [Folha, 1997](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/folhatee/fm190505.htm) names Aretha Franklin and Whitney Houston *as influences*; Tom Jobim is called a musical idol, while Ornella Vanoni and Eros Ramazzotti are musicians she likes. [Messenger of Saint Anthony, 2009](https://www.messengersaintanthony.com/content/god-i-laura-pausini) has her explicitly say she learned from Whitney Houston, George Michael, Sade, Phil Collins, Lucio Battisti, Claudio Baglioni, Ornella Vanoni and Mina. [Grammy interview, 2022](https://www.grammy.com/news/laura-pausini-flute-interview-video-jethro-tull-singer-songwriter-album-winner-latin-grammys-it-goes-to-11/) states Jethro Tull inspired her to learn flute and helped her find her own vocal style. These are **artist-wide**, not particular 1993–98 album sessions; do not duplicate as `influenced_by` without an album-level statement.

## Cross-album constraints and audit

- All `sounds_like` are tentative *editorial* sonic comparisons, **not** inferred direct influence. No source verifies a particular album was modeled on those artists.
- The 1993 original CD + 1994 original CD have different documented producer credits and must **not** be normalised to identical producer fields merely because some databases merge producer and executive producer metadata.
- The singer's 1994 Spanish album is a *new Spanish recording and reordered selection* of material already covered in two Italian albums; 1996 *Las cosas que vives* and 1998 *Mi respuesta* likewise are language counterparts. These are excluded from this pass by the same strategy applied to Eros Ramazzotti; create separate albums only on explicit request.
- **Cover studio albums are not compilations.** *Io canto* (2006), *Laura Xmas* (2016), and *Io canto 2* (2026, released 6 February) belong to later phases. They are newly recorded studio performances, even though the repertoire is composed of songs written by others. The 1995 international *Laura Pausini* assemblage, 2001 *The Best of...*, 2013 *20 - The Greatest Hits*, deluxe reissues, and live concerts are excluded.
- The album-catalogue audit is the **full original-studio plan** as of 29 October 2026; its `ADD` future rows are audit-only and must not be imported as this phase's descriptors. Scores 0–5 are original editorial estimates, not musicological measurements.
