# Bonnie Tyler — Fandome Phase 01: evidence notes

Method: period album reviews, original-pressing credits, recording-level MusicBrainz relationships, and Bonnie Tyler’s official archive. Album scores and `sounds_like` are our editorial judgments, independent of published rating scores. Only verifiable contributors are entered in the instrument and production fields. Album-wide genres come before narrow/track-specific styles.

## The World Starts Tonight (1977)

- **Style and genres:** Tomas Mureika’s AllMusic review places the record between country and pop; the database classifies it Pop/Rock. Those are the primary categories (`countrypop`, `poprock`, `country`, `pop`). Gentle songs support `softrock` as an editorial secondary tag, and the cover of "Piece of My Heart" gives the rock dimension modest weight rather than defining the full album. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-world-starts-tonight-mw0000842694
- **Scales:** energy 3 / electronic 0 / mood 2 / theatrical 3 / experimental 1 / polish 3 / tempo 3 / groove 2. These reflect a mostly acoustic studio band, romantic and melancholy repertoire, distinct but not yet maximal theatrical delivery. Mood refers to emotional character, not critical score.
- **Voice:** `clear|raspy|powerful` reflects expressive vocal colour of the debut, with less extreme rasp than later recordings. AllMusic's artist biography places the most prominent husky change after the debut in 1977.
- **Instruments:** original sleeve and AllMusic album credits cite guitarists Terry Britten, Dave Christopher, Steve Wolfe and Alan Tarney, bassists Alan Tarney and Mo Foster, Simon Phillips on drums, Barry Guard on percussion, and David Mackay's string arrangements. `strings` has a direct arrangement credit; no unverified keyboards or tape machinery are added. Sources: https://sessiondays.com/2017/01/28/1977-bonnie-tyler-world-starts-tonight/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-world-starts-tonight-mw0000842694
- **Production:** Ronnie Scott, Steve Wolfe and David Mackay are credited as producers. `stringarrangements` documented; no other technology token is justified. Original LP has 10 tracks; avoid retrofitting expanded-CD bonus songs.
- **Comparisons:** Linda Ronstadt/Olivia Newton-John/Rod Stewart refer to country-pop crossover and vocal delivery, not direct recording influence. `influenced_by` blank. Covers are never automatic proof of influence.

## Natural Force (1978)

- **Edition and review:** European RCA title *Natural Force*, also issued as *It's a Heartache* in the United States. AllMusic’s review under *It's a Heartache* singles out the strongly country-oriented pop-rock groove. Album not to be imported as two different records. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/its-a-heartache-mw0000376169
- **Genres and scales:** `countryrock|countrypop|poprock|softrock|country|rock|pop`. energy 3, electronic 0, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 1, polish 3, tempo 3, groove 3: rock pulse in a primarily live guitar/bass/drums production, with contrasting romantic ballads. `softrock` applies to selected ballads rather than the entire record.
- **Voice:** The distinctive raspy and powerful sound is much more prominent after the 1977 vocal change, documented in the official biography and AllMusic biography: https://bonnietyler.com/bio/
- **Instruments and credits:** MusicBrainz’s original recording relationships credit Steve Wolfe on acoustic guitar and backing vocals, Kevin Dunne on bass, Micky Gibbins on drums and percussion; other sleeve sources identify Taff Williams on guitars and Roger Bara on keyboards. Drum-player names differ across secondary liner transcriptions, so the CSV records the drum instrument but deliberately does not settle the session-person dispute. Source: https://musicbrainz.org/release/b35539ca-c423-47b4-be0b-99725cead634 ; https://www.lpdiscography.com/?album=22722&page=album
- **Production:** David Mackay, Ronnie Scott and Steve Wolfe have production credits. No named drum machine, synthesizer or recording technology established: `production` left empty. `sounds_like` solely an editorial judgment; no claim about direct influence.

## Diamond Cut (1979)

- **Genre dispute:** AllMusic argues that the group moved towards pop and away from the country flavour of the first two albums. Its album category remains Pop/Rock; country and country-rock labels remain in the long tail because of the steel/slide/dobro guitars and selected songs. Soul-pop is a *selected-tracks editorial gloss* on the review's explicitly identified soulful ballads, not a whole-album metadata tag. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/diamond-cut-mw0000840913
- **Scales:** energy 3 / electronic 1 / mood 2 / theatrical 3 / experimental 2 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 3. `electronic 1` reflects a credited Moog colour against overwhelmingly acoustic/electric band instrumentation; not a synthpop claim.
- **Instrumentation:** AllMusic and Drummerszone list Martin Jenner, Hugh Burns, Steve Wolfe and multiple other guitars, bassists, drums, acoustic and slide/steel guitars, mandolin, dobro, keyboards, harmonica, tenor saxophone, bells/cowbell and Moog. The token order prioritises common instruments; rare elements placed later. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/diamond-cut-mw0000840913 ; https://drummerszone.com/music/release/16710/diamond-cut
- **Production and arrangements:** Producers Robin Geoffrey Cable, Ronnie Scott, Steve Wolfe. Original credits also identify Mike McNaught’s arrangements for strings, oboe and French horn; therefore `stringarrangements` and `hornarrangements`. The generic `moog` production token is supported by AllMusic's explicit Moog synthesizer credit; no unverified model number. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/diamond-cut-mw0000840913
- **Comparisons and themes:** Linda Ronstadt, Rod Stewart, Kim Carnes are editorial sonic proximity only. `influenced_by` blank. The record has expressive romantic/heartbreak storytelling; no claim of an artist directly shaping *Diamond Cut* is sourced.

## Goodbye to the Island (1981)

- **Re-recording and styles:** The official Bonnie Tyler archive says sessions started in 1979 and much of the album was re-recorded in 1980, producing a stronger blues/rock direction. `bluesrock` and `blues` indicate the bluesy opening "I’m Just a Woman", not a blanket blues genre. It also describes the Nashville-oriented approach aimed for on *Diamond Cut*, whereas AllMusic describes that earlier record as more pop-facing. Both are recorded, not artificially reconciled. Source: https://bonnietyler.com/news/celebrating-40-years-of-goodbye-to-the-island/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/diamond-cut-mw0000840913
- **Scales:** energy 3 / electronic 1 / mood 2 / theatrical 4 / experimental 2 / polish 4 / tempo 3 / groove 3. Higher theatrical relates to raspy, rock-inflected delivery, not Steinman-era stadium-scale sound.
- **Instrumentation:** The official album archival feature names Garth Watt-Roy on guitar solos; Steve Lipson, Gary Taylor, Robert Ahwai on rhythm guitar; Kevin Dunne/Felix Kirsch on bass; Jeff Allen/Liam Genockey on drums; Betsy Cook/Mike McNaught on keys; and Raphael Ravenscroft on sax. `guitar|bass|drums|keyboards|saxophone` are each directly supported. Source: https://bonnietyler.com/news/celebrating-40-years-of-goodbye-to-the-island/
- **Production:** Hugh Murphy produced certain tracks; others credited Hugh Murphy jointly with Ronnie Scott and Steve Wolfe (also confirmed in MusicBrainz recording relations). All credited producers appear in the row, but their involvement is not assumed across every track. No specific synth or effects technology meets evidence threshold: `production` blank. Source: https://musicbrainz.org/release/81a5a229-f4d9-417d-8392-7ed7aff25f27
- **Songwriting and influence caveat:** Procol Harum, Dan Hill and Chris Rea are present as sources for covered/written compositions, not recorded proof of direct musical influence. Tina Turner and Janis Joplin remain in ARTIST INFLUENCES in CLAUDE_IMPORT; the official archive’s description of the bluesy song is not a direct quote identifying the album’s inspiration. `influenced_by` blank.

## Disagreements and uncertainties

1. **1978 title:** *It's a Heartache* is a regional title of *Natural Force*, not an extra studio album.
2. **Natural Force drummers:** original-recording MusicBrainz credits Micky Gibbins; another secondary discography assigns Simon Phillips. Do not turn that conflict into an invented definitive personnel list. The instrument drum remains certain.
3. **Diamond Cut identity:** AllMusic emphasizes pop direction whereas the official retrospective emphasizes attempts to reach Nashville audiences. The sonic descriptor retains both pop priority and low-weight country tags.
4. **Studio equipment:** 1970s does not automatically mean documented analog tape, drum machines or identifiable synthesizers. Only the credited Moog on *Diamond Cut* has an explicit machine-family mention; no model number is assumed.
5. **Critical reviews and URLs:** Sources are listed with canonical source pages. Reviews may include reissued tracks or bonus-CD personnel; original LP material takes precedence. All `sounds_like` values are editor judgments.
