# Gloria Gaynor — Phase 02: source review and descriptor decisions

Research date: 28 October 2026. Only original studio albums; no live records, compilations, expanded remasters or bonus mixes. Every number from 0 to 5 is an independent Fandome editorial description, not a quantitative result from a source. The accompanying `genre_evidence.csv` distinguishes genre evidence at whole-album and selected-track level. All four `influenced_by` cells are deliberately empty: there is no verified contemporary testimony linking a named musical influence to the production of a particular one of these albums. Artist-level vocal influences are already provided in Phase 01.

## Gloria Gaynor's Park Avenue Sound (1978)

- **Original issue / content:** Polydor PD-1-6139, 1978, seven tracks. The March date appears in discographical sources, though some later databases abbreviate the sleeve title to *Park Avenue Sound*. Its entire programme, not reissues, underlies the assessment. AllMusic and JazzRockSoul describe recordings at Sigma Sound Studios in Philadelphia with Norman Harris and Ron Kersey associated with its Philadelphia soul arrangements. This is why `phillysoul` is added after the dominant `disco`, broad `soul` and `rnb`: it characterises much of the arrangement, not a new artist-level influence.
- **Production credits require track-level care:** 1978 contemporary label transcription for *This Love Affair* identifies **Al Stewart and Conell Johnson Jr. as co-producers of that track**, **TAN Productions** on several selections, and **Joel Diamond** on *You're All I Need to Get By* (and later sleeve transcriptions report Diamond also on *After the Lovin' / Sweet Sounds for My Baby*). AllMusic's album-level list also gives **Tan, Al Stewart and Conell Johnson Jr.** Thus Fandome includes the four original production credits **Tan|Joel Diamond|Al Stewart|Conell Johnson Jr.**, rather than treating session musicians as producers. `Tan` is a production credit/company name, not assumed to be an individual person. Source: https://musicbase.net/this-love-affair; https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000851999. Researcher's import reviewer should recheck whether Fandome's producers field supports a collective (and retain the album-level evidence regardless).
- **Instrumentation:** Period LP credits: Keith E. Benson drums, James Williams electric bass, Norman Harris and Bobby Eli guitar, Ron Kersey keyboards, Bruce Gray piano, James Walker/Larry Washington congas, backing voices. JazzRockSoul's track discussion identifies horns/strings and an electric sitar figure in *For the First Time in My Life*. Since instrument is a track-level texture, `electricsitar` is placed last. `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` is justified by the published music commentary and named 1978 arrangers, but not by a specific synth model. Both sources: https://musicbase.net/gloria-gaynors-park-avenue-sound ; https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/gloria-gaynor/.
- **Style and ratings:** Lively four-to-the-floor tracks interleaved with midtempo material explain `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=4`; polished session arrangements justify `polish=4`; conventional structures explain `experimental=1`. `electronic=0` means acoustic/electric live players dominate, not that no studio electronics were used. `sounds_like` is editorial comparison only, not a claimed historical influence. AllMusic is positive about some tracks but less enthusiastic about the album's consistency.

## Love Tracks (1978)

- **Original timing and edition:** Polydor PD-1-6184. The 23 December **1978** issue of *Cash Box* already reviewed the LP, while AllMusic's interface labels the album 1979 in its chronology because of its ensuing chart cycle. The original release date is November 1978 (release databases place it on 27 November), therefore the row correctly uses **1978**. The original eight-song LP includes *I Will Survive*, *Anybody Wanna Party?*, *Please, Be There*, *Goin' Out of My Head* and *Substitute*. No extended mixes from 2013 reissues count. Sources: https://retrocdn.net/images/a/a3/CashBox_US_1978-12-23.pdf ; https://musicbrainz.org/release/2716d091-9a43-46b8-ad05-78a786f0147c .
- **Credits conflict carefully handled:** The **1978 LP sleeve** says **Produced by Dino Fekaris in association with Freddie Perren**; session research calls Perren **associate producer**. Some Qobuz individual tracks and later reference pages instead present both as producers. Per Fandome instructions, associates alone are **not** included in `producers`; the row therefore holds only **Dino Fekaris**, leaving Perren's credited arrangement/associate role documented here. Original liner annotation: https://musicbrainz.org/release/2716d091-9a43-46b8-ad05-78a786f0147c . Qobuz: https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/love-tracks-gloria-gaynor/jq04x2rbe4p5a .
- **Instrumentation:** *Love Tracks* credits James Gadson on drums, Eddie Watkins Jr. and Scott Edwards on bass, Bob Bowles / Melvin Ragin / Robert White on guitar, Freddie Perren and Peter Robinson on keyboards, Peter Robinson on synthesizer, Paulinho da Costa / Bob Zimmitti on percussion; four credited horn-and-string arrangers provide orchestral accents. `stringarrangements|hornarrangements` uses these specific notes; no drummachine or synth model is proven. Source: https://sessiondays.com/2023/06/22/1978-gloria-gaynor-love-tracks/ .
- **Genre granularity:** Disco and soul are album-wide; broad R&B is AllMusic's category; Northern soul is a named historical root audible in *Substitute*; funk is assigned only for the explicitly described *Anybody Wanna Party?* arrangement. Not an all-funk album. *Please Be There* is a smooth soul ballad and offsets strong club grooves. The lyrics include romantic rupture and self-assertion in *I Will Survive*, explaining `identity` and `social`; no personal romantic-history inference is required. `landmark=1` for durable recognition of *I Will Survive* and prominence of the LP, not simply chart status. AllMusic and Qobuz support context.

## I Have a Right (1979)

- **Original scope and musical contrasts:** Eight Polydor tracks include *Let Me Know (I Have a Right)*, *Say Somethin'*, *Don't Stop Us*, *Tonight* (Bernstein/Sondheim song remade as disco), and *Midnight Rocker*. Alex Henderson at AllMusic explicitly names *Say Somethin'* as Northern soul and the uptempo titles as disco-soul. The album's title and songs justify the mix of love, self-assertion and social/identity themes; these are editorial tagging of lyrical content, not direct quotations. The cover **does not make this a soundtrack** or a showtunes album.
- **Producer hierarchy:** The first edition **explicitly credits Dino Fekaris as producer** and **Freddie Perren as associate producer, keyboard player and rhythm arranger**. Do **not** add Perren to `producers` solely from later aggregators that simplify producer/associate distinctions. Source: https://musicbase.net/cs/i-have-a-right ; https://recordsale.de/en/artists/gloria-gaynor/albums/i-have-a-right .
- **Instruments:** Original sleeve transcription: James Gadson drums, bass David Shields/Freddie Washington, Bob Bowles and Melvin Ragin guitars, Pete Robinson keys/synth, Paulinho da Costa congas, Bob Zimmitti percussion, **Katie Kirkpatrick harp** and **Doc Severinsen trumpet solos** on *Let Me Know* and *Tonight*. Arrangers Wade Marcus, David Blumberg and Pete Robinson support horn/string arrangements. No specific synthesizer or drum-machine model is identified. `electronic=1` acknowledges synth among mostly live players. This is a much more demonstrative orchestral disco recording than electronic dance music.
- **Ratings and disagreement:** `energy=4`, `theatrical=4` reflect extended high-drama club tracks and emphatic voice, but quieter Northern soul songs reduce album-wide groove relative to *Love Tracks*. Reviewer Henderson criticises the opening title track as an imitation of *I Will Survive* while praising the remainder. This critical resemblance is **not** an `influenced_by` entry; nor do the original writers Bernstein and Sondheim become documented influences through a cover.

## Stories (1980)

- **Original eight-song release:** The *Stories* 1980 album comprises *Ain't No Bigger Fool*, *I Let Love Slip Right Through My Hands*, *On a Diet of You*, *Lock Me Up*, *All My Life*, *Don't Read Me Wrong*, *The Luckiest Girl in the World* and *Make Me Yours*. AllMusic explicitly contrasts lively dance songs with slower **Northern soul**, recognises Gaynor's narrative focus and notes the lighter disco emphasis. The contemporaneous *Billboard* comment reportedly calls it classic pop-soul; this last source is secondarily reported, and `popsoul` is therefore low-confidence and listed last pending checking the original magazine. No new genre token is needed: `popsoul` already exists.
- **Documented musicians / production:** Sessiondays' sleeve transcription has Nathan East/Nathan Watts bass, James Gadson drums, Bob Bowles/David T. Walker guitars, Clarence McDonald/Freddie Perren/Pete Robinson keys, **Pete Robinson synthesizer**, **Ernie Watts flute and saxophone**, **Gayle Levant harp**, Bobbye Hall/Eddie Brown/Paulinho da Costa congas and orchestral arrangements by Pete Robinson, Gene Page and David Blumberg. **Dino Fekaris and Freddie Perren are both credited producers**, unlike the preceding two records' original associate-producer distinction. Source: https://sessiondays.com/2021/04/09/1980-gloria-gaynor-stories/ .
- **Sound and scales:** Eight discrete tracks, comparatively few extended disco medleys; lively *Make Me Yours* and *On a Diet of You* balance restrained *I Let Love Slip Right Through My Hands* and *The Luckiest Girl in the World*. `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`, `mood=3` characterise the whole record rather than only its fastest songs. `electronic=1` is only supported by the credited synthesizer amid a larger live band. No sampler, drum-machine model or digital processing is documented.

## General caveats for importer

- Production technology is deliberately conservative. *Recorded and remixed at* is a routine studio credit, not evidence of Fandome token `remixing` as a standalone album process; use horn/string arrangements only where specifically documented.
- All four `status` cells are `draft`. A second human/expert check is still needed to mark `reviewed`. The four rows are unique and strictly incremental after phase 01.
- Original title *Gloria Gaynor's Park Avenue Sound* is kept in the row even though databases index it as *Park Avenue Sound*. Original year *Love Tracks* is 1978 despite AllMusic's 1979 chronology.
- `sounds_like` are editorial analogues derived from repertoire and arrangements; they are **not** sourced testimonials by Gaynor or Fekaris. None of the cover-song performers, co-writers, session contributors or arrangers has been silently inserted as an album-specific influence.
- The current GitHub `descriptors` branch may not contain phase 01 updates already loaded into a private or offline branch. Before importing, Claude must check the **actual working branch** and reconcile phase 01 IDs and any later changes. No direct modifications to GitHub have been made here.
