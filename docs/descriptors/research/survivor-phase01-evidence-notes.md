# Survivor — Fandome Phase 01 research / evidence notes

Research scope: original albums **Survivor (1979)**, **Premonition (1981)**, **Eye of the Tiger (1982)** and **Caught in the Game (1983)**, original studio album editions only. 2026-10-28. Genre terms follow the Fandome `scenes.ts` catalogue. Scores are editorial 0–5 judgments, not review ratings. Source URLs appear here and in the two evidence CSVs. No compilations, live recordings or bonus/reissue tracks counted as original LP cuts.

## Survivor (1979) — `survivor-survivor`

### Date and sonic classification

The original debut is dated **21 December 1979** by the album overview and issued commercially/represented in some catalogues as **1980** (e.g. AllMusic February 1980, management biography). Fandome uses the original-release year **1979** rather than the 1980 wider-release or CD dates.

An explicitly sourced **AOR** selection in Louder's seventies list describes tightly constructed catchy hard rock, the original LP's textured guitar sound, and cowbell emphasis on `Somewhere in America`. AllMusic's distinct styles are `Hard Rock`, `Album Rock` and `Contemporary Pop/Rock`; broader `rock` comes from album metadata. Five genre terms have album-specific evidence and none are guessed from later Survivor hits.

Sources: https://www.loudersound.com/music/albums/70s-aor-10-essential-albums; https://www.allmusic.com/album/survivor-r50610; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_(Survivor_album); https://www.mm-group.org/talent/survivor/.

### Instruments, production, and personnel

Credited players: Dave Bickler (voice, keyboards), Frankie Sullivan (lead guitar), Jim Peterik (guitar and lead voice on `Love Has Got Me`), Dennis Keith Johnson (bass **and Moog bass pedals**), Gary Smith (drums/percussion). Accordingly `electricguitar`, `guitar`, `bass`, `drums`, `keyboards`, `percussion`, and `synthbass` have a specific album-personnel basis; `moog` is a **named device used for the bass parts**, not an assertion that a Minimoog or particular sampler was used. No speculative digital effects or drum machines in `production`.

**Producers:** Ron Nevison and Barry Mraz; Mike Clink was an assistant engineer, Bruce Fairbairn involved in engineering, not album producers. This was the **only album with the original bassist and drummer**, not the later Ellis/Droubay rhythm section. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor_(Survivor_album).

## Premonition (1981) — `survivor-premonition`

### Genres / arrangements

AllMusic explicitly tags `Hard Rock`, `Album Rock` and `Contemporary Pop/Rock`; Album of the Year and a specialist Westcoast/AOR discography explicitly classify it `AOR`. The album overview names broad `rock`. Louder's band interview distinguishes `Chevy Nights` (harder driving rock), `Poor Man's Son` (working-class anthem) and `Light of a Thousand Smiles` (melodic ballad, **critic compares to Journey**, not an influence declaration). Five supplied genres span both sides of its sound.

Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/premonition-mw0000691184; https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/177030-survivor-premonition.php; https://www.loudersound.com/features/survivor-interview-eye-of-the-tiger-sylvester-stallone; https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/07/survivor-premonition-1981.html.

### Instruments / producer attribution

Bickler is specifically credited on synthesizer; Peterik on keyboards, rhythm guitar and backing vocals; Sullivan on lead guitar/backing; Ellis on bass; Marc Droubay on drums; additional keyboard player Daryl Dragon. `electricguitar|guitar|synth|keyboards|bass|drums` is based on these credited instruments, not the album's AOR category. Unnamed synthesis/drum processing details **do not justify a specific `production` token**, so the field is empty.

Full-album primary producers are **Jim Peterik and Frankie Sullivan**. **Artie Ripp and Artie Kornfeld** are **credited co-producers on tracks 1 and 2 only** (`Chevy Nights` and `Summer Nights`); they are included *after* the main producers under the guide's rule to capture documented co-producers, and clearly annotated as track-limited. Other tracks list production assistance rather than co-production. Verify the original sleeve on import if disambiguation is needed. Source: https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/07/survivor-premonition-1981.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premonition_(Survivor_album).

The release month differs across databases: original US **October 1981** in the album reference, **August 1981** on Album of the Year. Fandome year remains **1981**; do not treat February 1982 overseas release as original.

## Eye of the Tiger (1982) — `survivor-eyeofthetiger`

### Album rather than movie soundtrack

**The 1982 Survivor album is a studio album (`type=studio`), not the Rocky III film soundtrack**. The song `Eye of the Tiger` also appears in the film, but the album includes eight other original band songs. The movie's use does not change the album's classification. AllMusic lists five precise rock styles: Hard Rock, Arena Rock, Album Rock, Contemporary Pop/Rock, and Rock & Roll. Its review specifically identifies `I'm Not That Man Anymore` as a **power ballad**: place `powerballad` **sixth**, as a track-level sound rather than overarching album style.

Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000191987; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_the_Tiger_(album).

### Instrumentation, production, voice

Album liner notes: Sullivan on lead/rhythm and **12-string acoustic** guitars; Peterik on **piano, Hammond B3 organ, electric guitar and 12-string acoustic guitar**; Ellis bass; Droubay drums; Daryl Dragon additional keyboards, synthesizers and **E-mu Emulator**; Fergie Frederiksen backing vocals. `sampler` + `production=emulator` derive from the credited **E-mu Emulator**, not a speculative Fairlight or Emulator II. The 1982 date supports avoiding later 1984-era machines. Both Peterik and Sullivan are explicit **album producers** in the original liner notes and MusicBrainz release-wide track credits (despite the single-producer summary in the album's infobox).

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_the_Tiger_(album); https://musicbrainz.org/release/21dc932c-5469-41d4-8e7c-b2136c8e220c.

### Influences are specific, not inferred from a soundtrack placement

Jim Peterik's first-person account describes Rocky III's early temp music as **Queen's `Another One Bites the Dust`**. He and Sullivan treated matching or surpassing that pulse/impact as part of the writing challenge. Therefore `influenced_by=Queen (The Game)` is supported **for the title track**, from which the original Queen song comes, not for the remaining songs. Guitar World testimony: https://www.guitarworld.com/features/interview-guitarist-jim-peterik-talks-tiger-and-new-pride-lions-album-immortal; compare https://parade.com/entertainment/1982-survivor-anthem-inspired-by-a-funky-1980s-queen-hit-that-nearly-appeared-in-rocky-iii. Film visuals and the punching rhythm are **non-musical inspirations**, not entries in `influenced_by`; Peterik describes writing the guitar pattern synchronised to boxing punches in https://musictap.com/2020/03/16/the-musictap-interview-jim-peterik-of-the-ides-of-march/.

AllMusic's Greg Prato compares the rock content to **Foreigner**, and `Hesitation Dance` to **Led Zeppelin**. Both therefore belong in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`. `Journey` is a broader editorial proximity to Survivor's period hard-rock ballads and can be removed by Claude if the album-specific similarity threshold is stricter.

## Caught in the Game (1983) — `survivor-caughtinthegame`

### Genre evidence

AllMusic explicitly lists Hard Rock, Album Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock, and critic Mike DeGagne describes a stronger, guitar-dominated **rock and roll** approach. Original album metadata additionally says **pop rock**, retaining the group's melodic writing despite tougher arrangements. No attempt is made to call the whole LP 1984 `Vital Signs`-style synth-pop; that is a different Survivor record.

Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/caught-in-the-game-mw0000312496; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caught_in_the_Game.

### Instruments and production credits

Survivor: Bickler lead voice, Peterik keyboards, Sullivan guitars, Ellis bass, Droubay drums. Guests include **Daryl Dragon keyboards, Dave Arellano synthesizer, Richard Page, Kevin Cronin and Tom Kelly backing vocals**. These are performing credits, not co-writers/production personnel; no guest is entered into `influenced_by`. Hence `electricguitar|keyboards|synth|bass|drums`. There is no defensible album-wide named technique or signature machine in sources; **leave `production` empty**, rather than guessing drum programming, LinnDrum, Prophet-5 or tape effects.

**Frankie Sullivan is the sole credited producer.** Peterik is credited as **assistant producer** only; he is excluded under the guide's express rule. Both original-pressing transcription and full-song MusicBrainz credits agree. The separate **title-track single** entry in an online encyclopedia lists both as producer, but that conflicts with album LP records; original album credits take precedence. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caught_in_the_Game; https://musicbrainz.org/release/39ba555b-66c3-4dcb-b787-19d1639724ed; https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/survivor/caught-in-the-game-album_ffvuuf.html.

More guitar weight than the previous hit LP, but multiple softer passages bring average energy below maximum. Anxious, troubled love and reputational conflict are present across titles such as `Slander`, `I Never Stopped Loving You` and `What Do You Really Think?`. `sounds_like` names are **editorial comparisons**, and **none is claimed to be a documented causal influence**.

### Cross-phase boundaries

`Survivor` is the **Chicago, Illinois** melodic hard-rock band, not the unrelated Louisiana 1970s act that appears in a separate AllMusic artist listing. All four records use Dave Bickler's lead voice (debut features a Peterik lead vocal on one track). Phase 02 covers **Vital Signs (1984), When Seconds Count (1986), Too Hot to Sleep (1988), and Reach (2006)**; no compilations, live recordings, remixes or singles.
