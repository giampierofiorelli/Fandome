# Mariah Carey — Phase 04 evidence & editorial score audit

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-10-25. **Release set:** four ORIGINAL studio albums, 2010–2025. This is not a review summary or a list of critic ratings; the eight 0–5 fields are independent source-informed **ordinal editorial judgments**. Each has potential uncertainty. Every genre in the descriptive CSV has one corresponding row in `genre_evidence.csv`, with dominant/significant/track-level scope. The system must NOT act as if a track-level genre permeates an entire album. `status=draft` pending Claude's independent review.

## 2010 — Merry Christmas II You (`mariah-merrychristmasiiyou`)

**Core sources:** M01 (AllMusic), M02 (Slant), M03 (AllMusic credits), M04 (physical release), M06 (production corroboration).

- **Taxonomy:** This is a **studio album** of newly recorded or reworked holiday songs, not a compilation, not the 1994 Merry Christmas album, and not a live album. Although the standard tracklist has an archival live performance of *O Holy Night*, that does **not** reclassify the release as a live album. The standard versus bonus track distinction matters: the Jump Smokers Edit of Oh Santa! is not used to evaluate the original set.
- **Genres:** `christmas` dominates repertoire; `pop`, `gospel`, `rnb`, `contemporaryrnb`, `adultcontemporary`, `traditionalpop`, `soul` recur or form identifiable substantial strands. `hiphop` is **track-level only** (Housetop Celebration repurposes a Kurtis Blow beat). `jazz` is **track-level** (Vince Guaraldi / Charlie Brown repertoire). The Qobuz/AllMusic categories do not make all songs Christmas jazz.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (upbeat originals interspersed with hymns and ballads); electronic **2** (largely choir/orchestra and conventional pop/R&B production, some programmed passages); mood **4** (predominant festive warmth and celebratory uplift, punctuated by solemn sacred music); theatrical **4** (large choral and symphonic gestures); experimental **1** (classic seasonal repertoire, predictable structures); polish **5** (orchestra/choirs and costly studio ensemble; quality judgment, not measured budget); tempo **3** (straddles quick pop and slow ballads); groove **2** (some bouncy R&B tracks, mostly not club music).
- **Vocal labels:** `powerful|soft|choral`: sustained belts, quieter verses, repeated choirs. Whistle register is not an allowed `voice` enum; describe it here rather than injecting new invalid vocal tags.
- **Lyrics:** festive romantic and spiritual themes; `party` is occasional rather than nightclub content. Some tracks are instrumentally seasonal or carry traditional religious lyrics.
- **Producers:** nine names have `Producer` credits on M03/M06; **Walter Afanasieff** may be associated with one re-recorded version, not a producer for every track. Note that `Marc Shaiman` is simultaneously arranger/orchestrator AND credited producer; not all arrangers count.
- **Mood dissensus:** AllMusic finds an engaging holiday tone; Slant calls the album mannered and excessively nostalgic. The positive `mood` describes the music's emotional timbre, not unanimous critical satisfaction.
- **No documented individual artist influence** established for this project. The presence of a Vince Guaraldi cover or a Kurtis Blow rhythmic quotation is evidence of repertoire and a sample, not proof that Carey was personally formed by either artist.

## 2014 — Me. I Am Mariah... The Elusive Chanteuse (`mariah-meiammariahtheelusivechanteuse`)

**Core sources:** C01 Pitchfork, C02 Guardian, C03 Slant, C04 Boston Globe, C05 Qobuz track credits and C06 AllMusic producers.

- **Genres:** the record is primarily `rnb|contemporaryrnb|pop`, with substantial classic `soul`, hip-hop sampling / hip-hop-soul beats, and gospel-like ballads. `disco` applies to *You Don't Know What to Do* and *Meteorite*; these are **not** representative of the entire tracklist. `quietstorm` is the Guardian critic's low-key vocal styling description; **secondary texture**, not uniformly a quiet-storm radio album. `dancepop` track-level from disco songs only.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (mostly slow–midtempo with a few genuinely energetic disco songs); electronic **3** (hitmaker loops and synths alongside piano, organ, choir and guitar); mood **3** (nostalgic warmth, romantic yearning and heartbreak, comedic bravado, church uplift mixed); theatrical **4** (towering belts and gospel ending, although often intimate verses); experimental **2** (updated production but mostly familiar R&B templates); polish **4** (high-end contemporary production; no raw/lo-fi claim); tempo **3** (midtempo dominant); groove **3** (disco excursions lift mixed mainly R&B record).
- **Vocal:** `soft|powerful|choral|rap`; featured rap guests supply **track-level** `rap` not the lead singer's default style; make this distinction in modelling. The album contrasts near-whisper softness with expansive choral belting.
- **Production:** Original standard 2014 edition includes all 12 attributed lead/co-producers in our rows. C05/C06 verify their actual credits, including `Darhyl Camper`, `Rey Reel`, `Mike WiLL Made-It`; producer contributions differ by track. Neither Nas nor Stevie Wonder is included just for being a featured performer, and George Michael is a composer of the *One More Try* cover rather than a producer.
- **Disagreement:** Pitchfork hears coherent reclamation of classic R&B; Slant and Boston Globe raise stylistic and editorial cohesion concerns. These opinions affect confidence in representativeness but do **not** alter source-backed producer/genre facts.
- **Influences:** explicit tribute to 1980s/1990s hip-hop and interpolations may be documented; these are NOT automatically evidence that the entire project was causally influenced by a specific featured guest or sampled musician. `influenced_by` left blank pending direct artist/producer album-scoped source.

## 2018 — Caution (`mariah-caution`)

**Core sources:** W01 Pitchfork, W02 Slant, W03 AllMusic; original 10-track production ledger W04 and W05 cross-check.

- **Genres:** dominant `rnb|contemporaryrnb` with pop-soul (`popsoul` explicitly in Pitchfork); hip-hop elements recur via sampled beats and guests but the album is not a rap record; `downtempo` and `quietstorm` identify pervasive low-key production while `adultcontemporary` concerns a few ballad sections. None of these genres implies a separate single-style album.
- **Scores:** energy **2** (muted overall drive); electronic **4** (synth-heavy R&B arrangements despite some guitar and piano); mood **3** (romance, irritation and independent wit balance melancholy); theatrical **2** (restrained core delivery, occasional large belt); experimental **3** (textural twists within accessible slow-jam structures, especially Giving Me Life); polish **5** (finely layered sleek studio production); tempo **2** (slow/midtempo overall); groove **3** (supple body-moving midtempo syncopation, not a 5/5 dance record).
- **Voice:** `soft|powerful|raspy|rap`; the lower-breathed singing dominates, belts are contrast moments, guest rap is limited to specific songs. Raspy is a secondary, debatable descriptor; second reviewer should check if `raspy` is justified album-wide or should be removed. No automatic claim that timbre is physically impaired.
- **Production:** W04 transcribes individual credits for the **ten original** tracks. The list captures actual *producer / co-/additional-producer* roles including less prominent Jordan Manswell, Federico Vindver and Larrance Dopson. It excludes label executive producers and collaborators credited solely as singers. Japan bonus *Runway* excluded. The original version of *A No No* contains Lil' Kim sampling, but the later Lil' Kim-feature remix is NOT on original Caution.
- **Sonic comparisons versus influences:** Pitchfork invokes contemporary peers; W04 includes a direct Carey recollection of her preferred Lil' Kim sample for A No No. Since sample use is not identical to an album-wide documented causal influence, `influenced_by` is left empty. Source can be revisited if project expands to track-scoped influence chains.
- **Landmark=1:** critical reassessment and a concise, distinct late-career creative peak, editorial importance judgment only; not a numerical rating of popularity.

## 2025 — Here for It All (`mariah-hereforitall`)

**Core sources:** H01 Pitchfork, H02 Slant, H03 NME, H04 Qobuz complete original credits, H05 label's original CD listing.

- **Genres:** central `rnb|contemporaryrnb|pop|soul`; significant retro soul and funk (especially *Play This Song*, *I Won't Allow It*), gospel *Jesus I Do* and title track. Other tags carefully preserved but scoped: **disco** (*I Won't Allow It*), **trap** (*Confetti & Champagne*), **dancehall** (*Sugar Sweet*), **doowop** (*In Your Feelings* comparison), **hiphopsoul** (*Type Dangerous*). Those are not album-wide genre assignments. `nusoul` reflects production described in Qobuz's album review, not an assertion that all songs belong to neo-soul.
- **Scores:** energy **3** (mid-tempo album with a couple dance-funk lifts); electronic **2** (live-band arrangements and piano prominence, electronic bass programmed in some pieces); mood **2** (romantic bitterness and argumentative breakup material dominate even when bright arrangements give contrast); theatrical **3** (occasional soaring gospel finale, otherwise understated); experimental **2** (well-worn R&B/soul formats with few unexpected production leaps); polish **4** (carefully constructed studio, though more exposed and grainy lead voice than past decades); tempo **3** (mostly midtempo and ballads); groove **3** (select disco/funk cuts, substantial balladry).
- **Vocal:** `raspy|soft|powerful|choral` supported by simultaneous descriptions in three major critics. Caution: **raspiness is a descriptive timbral observation about the recording**, not a diagnosis of Carey's health or a deduction of vocal pathology; do not medicalize it. The Clark Sisters' gospel voices are featured, not assumed throughout the album.
- **Production:** Qobuz H04 gives exact credited `Producer` roles for *all 11 original tracks*, source of all nine retained names: Mariah Carey, The Stereotypes, 9AM, Daniel Moore II, N.W.I., HARV, Anderson .Paak, Rogét Chahayed, Alissia Benveniste. Label owner listing corroborates key collaborators. **L.A. Reid is an *executive producer* only** in H07: exclude from this column. Several composers and featured vocalists are similarly *not* listed as producers.
- **Conflicting opinions:** Pitchfork sees careful, sometimes unusually candid songcraft; NME foregrounds uneven high and low moments; Slant sees an incoherent collection and criticizes vocal processing. The descriptor reports style and mood, **not consensus of quality**. The studio album receives `landmark=0` because defining-album status is not established by multiple independent critical sources as of this cutoff; this does not imply weak commercial performance.
- **Influence scope:** Pitchfork compares certain arrangements to the Chi-Lites and Delfonics, and Qobuz mentions Smokey Robinson for one track. These are critic **sonic comparisons**, not declarations by Mariah about the album's causation. `influenced_by` and `sounds_like` are blank until album-wide comparisons warrant a field-wide entry.

## Modelling safeguards

1. Repertoire/cover/explicit sample ≠ album-wide artistic influence. All blank `influenced_by` cells are intentional, not missing processing.
2. `mood` measures dominant musical/emotional colouring, not BPM, danceability, energy or critical acclaim. Holiday brightness **4** does not imply tempo **4**.
3. If the production/source tracking cannot assign some claimed co-producer robustly, remove that **individual person** only rather than dropping entire album; preserve exhaustive verified names.
4. Every output has `status=draft` until second-person review; never silently promote `high` to `reviewed`.
5. Track-level and texture styles appear in `genres` because current genre guide has no hard genre limit, but evidence-scope MUST accompany them for recommender weighting. A consumer ranking that disregards scope should withhold low-weight track-level tags.
