# The Notwist — Phase 01 / evidence notes

Sources below are keyed to `notwist_phase01_sources.csv`; `genre_evidence.csv` gives a row per genre and explicitly narrows track-only genres. Scale judgments follow `docs/album-descriptors-guide.md` anchors, and uncertain literary themes remain blank.

## `notwist-thenotwist` — The Notwist (1991 provisional)
- **Genre evidence:** the band's own official reissue description combines hardcore punk, metal and melodic indie; James Mason (AllMusic) calls it hard rock/metal with punk energy and classifies it alternative/indie rock, indie rock, post-hardcore. Thus `hardcorepunk` is the proposed new exact token; `posthardcore` and `alternativeindierock` are already canonical. **Do not carry over the Bandcamp site-wide 'electronic' tag:** nothing indicates album-wide synthesizers here. [band_debut; all_debut]
- **Energy 4 / tempo 4:** repeated aggressive riffing, fast drums, bellowing vocals and abrupt changes, rather than one steady moderate pace. Not 5 throughout given softer passage on Nothing Like You. [all_debut; pitch_debut]
- **Electronic 0:** trio-only performance credited with electric guitar, bass and drums, and Acher remembers conventional live-band tracking; no synth/sampler evidence. The 0 means no significant generated-electronic component, not unplugged acoustic guitars. [amoeba_debut; 15q]
- **Mood 2:** intensity, dissatisfaction and darker timbral color dominate, but hooks and singalongs keep it above a uniformly bleak 0–1. This is an editorial inference from critic descriptions, not a lyrical analysis. [all_debut]
- **Theatrical 2 / experimental 1:** gruff early vocals and occasionally emphatic delivery, otherwise familiar punk/metal song forms; critics contrast the conventional debut with later experimental work. [all_debut; pitch_debut]
- **Polish 2 / groove 1:** noisy, direct guitar-and-drum sound with none of the later studio ornamentation or dance-production emphasis. [band_debut; all_debut]
- **Voice `raspy|clear`:** critics note barked/shouted phrases but also restrained melodic singing; these are broad descriptors, not claims that Markus has a uniformly raspy tone. [band_debut; all_debut]
- **Instruments `electricguitar|bass|drums`:** attributable to named original lineup on original Subway CD. No modern Bandcamp lineup carryover. [amoeba_debut]
- **Production blank:** distorted guitar *sound* is mentioned but no verified concrete recording method, machine or processing credit ties to the album. [15q]
- **Lyrics blank:** the evidence does not support thematic coding beyond the music itself. Song titles are not a source.
- **Producers blank:** early engineers were said to record/mix the songs, but no confirmed credited producer. [15q]
- **Influenced by:** Acher explicitly links his band's founding to Dinosaur Jr.'s *You're Living All Over Me* as well as Neil Young, Jerry's Kids and Minor Threat. [pitch_interview]
- **Sounds like:** compositional analogy to Dinosaur Jr., Hüsker Dü, the Ruts and Minor Threat; only part of this list is historically documented as influence, and the rest is an editorial sonic comparison. [all_debut; pitch_interview]
- **Landmark 0 / high:** historically first release, but critics distinguish it from the band's defining later creative breakthroughs. High evidence does not imply high artistic rating. [pitch_debut]
- **Date conflict:** official Bandcamp and the Subway CD listing say 1991, but AllMusic and German radio report 1990. Original LP issue needs a final decision. [band_debut; amoeba_debut; all_debut]

## `notwist-nook` — Nook (1992)
- **Genre evidence:** artist's own description is metallic, hardcore-inflected indie rock; AllMusic adds post-hardcore, hard rock and alternative/indie rock. `hardcore` refers to influence audible in selected passages; don't equate the whole LP to later electronic indie. [band_nook; all_nook; norman_nook]
- **Energy 4 / tempo 3:** a feedback-laden, forceful guitar album with varied paces and songs longer than the self-titled debut's short hardcore blasts. [visions_nook; band_nook]
- **Electronic 0:** confirmed trio recording of guitar, bass, drums and trumpet, recorded April 1992 at Marquee. No album-specific synthesis/processing credited. [musicbase_nook; all_nook]
- **Mood 2 / theatrical 2:** fragile/near-collapsing voice over harsh guitars, sometimes dramatic crescendos without a theatrical vocal persona. The mood number is a production/emotional-color reading, not inferred from track names. [visions_nook]
- **Experimental 2:** unusual contrast between noisy guitars and vulnerable melody; band was starting to explore other textures, but no verified electronic processing. [band_nook; rockobrobje]
- **Polish 2 / groove 1:** contemporaneous review foregrounds saturated riffs/volume rather than studio gloss or dancefloor pulse. [visions_nook]
- **Voice `soft|clear`:** reviewers repeatedly stress the restrained, delicate singing amid heavily distorted guitar. [visions_nook; band_nook]
- **Instruments:** guitar (electric guitar sound), bass, drums, trumpet in an original-release credit transcription. Saxophone appears in later aggregations but isn't listed in the checked original CD note, so withheld pending independent original-credit support. [musicbase_nook]
- **Production blank:** feedback is an observed sonic outcome, but the original-credit transcriptions do not verify a specific studio effect model or technique; no inferred `analogtape` or `tapeeffects`. [musicbase_nook]
- **Producers and lyrics blank:** no proven producer credit or lyric/theme evidence. [15q]
- **Influenced by:** Acher describes discovering Slayer, Voivod, Fred Frith and Sonic Youth's orbit while making *Nook*; album-specific testimony, in a Slovenian-language live interview. [rockobrobje]
- **Sounds like:** Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr., Fugazi and Hüsker Dü are stylistic comparisons, not alleged guest appearances. [visions_nook; rockobrobje]
- **Landmark 0 / high:** crucial stylistic early evidence but not as widely defining as *12* or *Neon Golden*. Strong contemporaneous review and release credits. [visions_nook]

## `notwist-12` — 12 (1995)
- **Genre evidence:** AllMusic indexes indie rock, post-hardcore and alternative/indie rock, calls the electronic turn unusual dance-pop, and notes analogue synth; original-issue genre notes also tag noise rock and post-rock. Artist reissue cites techno *texture* only for Torture Day, and jazz improv in final track. Neither `techno` nor `jazz` represents the whole album. [all_12; credits12_alt; band_12]
- **Energy 3 / tempo 3 / groove 2:** contrasts vigorous guitar sections with measured, spacious passages and subtle programmed elements, rarely a continuous dance beat. [all_12; band_12; washpost_12]
- **Electronic 2:** band-plus-sampler/analogue synth crossover; loud guitars and organic rhythm section still dominate, unlike fully electronic follow-up records. [all_12; jpc_12]
- **Mood 1:** reviewers describe dark Torture Day and Noah and sombre, layered record atmosphere, tempered by rhythmic breaks. Emotional reading, not proof of lyrical themes. [band_12; washpost_12]
- **Theatrical 2 / experimental 3:** soft voice and dramatic dynamic switches, exploratory electronic collages and jazz phrases without abandoning song forms. [band_12]
- **Polish 3:** deliberately arranged and mixed in Uphon/Mohrmann, with careful studio collage; not a high-gloss pop record. [credits_12; 15q]
- **Voice `soft|clear`:** critics contrast Markus' thin/understated voice with the more spacious arrangements. [band_12]
- **Instruments:** electric guitar, bass, drums (original trio); **synth** directly named by AllMusic; **sampler** from Gretschmann's credited assistance; **bassclarinet** Rudi Mahall; **doublebass|cello** Henning Sleverts; **organ** Krlte. This is one of the few reliable original-release personnel listings. [credits_12; all_12]
- **New instrument `bassclarinet`:** an explicit credited instrument, not generic `clarinet`. Must be registered in the Fandome token list. [credits_12]
- **Production `sampling`:** original booklet explicitly credits Gretschmann and Mario Thaler with sampling assistance. No Akai machine ID is conclusively proven for *this* studio session. [credits_12]
- **Producer `O.L.A.F. Opal`:** Acher says Opal produced starting on their third LP. The original credits specify Mario Thaler and Opal *recorded* the sessions; recording alone is not enough to assign Thaler production. [15q; credits_12]
- **Influenced by:** Acher identifies the last two Talk Talk records *Spirit of Eden* and *Laughing Stock* as decisive immediately before the making of *12*. The influence is documented for this specific album. [rockobrobje]
- **Sounds like:** AllMusic draws analogies with Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr., Oval and Autechre; Adrien Begrand suggests Radiohead resemblance. **Do not** automatically promote Autechre or Radiohead to `influenced_by` based on resemblance. [all_12; band_12]
- **Lyrics blank:** reviews cover arrangements and affect but not sufficient lyric content. [band_12; washpost_12]
- **Landmark 1 / high:** retrospectively a major band transition toward electronic post-rock/indietronica, though critics disagree on how cohesive the result is. [band_12; jpc_12; all_12]
- **Edition caution:** original nine-track album only. Some US/reissue versions append *Nook*-derived remixes and extra discs; not separate Fandome albums. [pitch_special; credits_12]

## Critical disagreements
- *The Notwist:* some reviews characterize early LPs as straightforward hard rock with few signals of later innovation; the band's retrospective acknowledges a combination of melodic independence and heavy punk/hardcore. Model the full range, without applying today's global indie-electronic tags. [all_debut; band_debut]
- *Nook:* hard metal and aggressive guitar are audible while Visions especially values delicate melody and fragile singing. Both count, not a single generic 'punk' label. [all_nook; visions_nook]
- *12:* John Bush considers the record an unorthodox dance-pop blend; Adrien Begrand treats its scatter of sounds as transitional and not entirely consistent; the Washington Post emphasizes melodic noise-rock crescendos. Hence 2/5 electronic, 2/5 groove, 3/5 experimental and limited-scope genre evidence. [all_12; band_12; washpost_12]
