# Röyksopp — phase 02, evidence rationale (original studio albums only)

## Method and uncertainty

Based on qualified album reviews, the artists' original statements and documented release credits, not user-review popularity. The eight 0–5 columns are **transparent editorial calibrations**, not measured acoustic properties. Keep `status=draft` until independently reviewed. Source IDs refer to `royksopp_phase02_sources.csv`; every `genres` entry has its own row in `royksopp_phase02_genre_evidence.csv`. Songs/remixes outside original editions are excluded. An expert source's genre category can be broad; that does **not** imply every track embodies it. All percentages or studio gear would be unsupported and are omitted. The original file has exactly three **new** rows; previous phase is not bundled.

### Senior — 2010 (`royksopp-senior`)

**Edition and type:** original nine-track 13 September 2010 studio LP, recorded around the same time as *Junior*, but intentionally fully instrumental and conceptually distinct [S_OFF,S_AM,S_GAFFA]. `type=studio`, `landmark=0` does not express its artistic worth, only that the earlier pop-focused albums and later project better define the duo's public profile [S_PF,S_UTR].

**Genre evidence:** AllMusic explicitly categorizes the record as `downtempo`, `downbeat`, `ambientbreakbeat` (original descriptor: Ambient Breakbeat), `electronic` and Club/Dance [S_AM]. Pitchfork emphasizes languid downtempo and melancholy synth/guitar material [S_PF]. NME describes its ambient character and an element of Scandinavian cosmic disco [S_NME]. `disco` and `dance` are **minor contextual tags**, NOT blanket dancefloor descriptions; album has very little 4/4 drive [S_AM,S_PF]. The parent `breakbeat` is tied to AllMusic's explicit named subgenre plus glitch percussion discussion [S_AM,S_UTR].

**Metrics:** energy **1** (predominantly slow and meditative [S_PF,S_AM]); electronic **4** (synth/programmed textures with documented live guitar, strings and bass [S_AM]); mood **1** (critics repeatedly call introspective, dark and melancholic [S_OFF,S_PF]); theatrical **1** (understated, voice-less [S_AM,S_PF]); experimental **3** (long-form atmospheric instrumental sequence and occasional glitch, not extreme sound-design breakthrough [S_UTR,S_PF]); polish **4** (detailed glossy layers, not lo-fi [S_NME,S_AM]); tempo **1** and groove **1** (no overall dancefloor arc; even AllMusic notes paucity of four-square beats [S_AM]). **Voice:** `instrumental` only; some marginal background-vocal credits exist in AllMusic [S_AM], but qualified accounts characterize the album as wholly instrumental; do not reclassify based solely on the performer listing. **Lyrics/language:** blank, not `en` [S_OFF,S_PF]. **Producers:** Röyksopp credited explicitly as Producer [S_AM]; the group is Svein Berge + Torbjørn Brundtland; don't list the duo *and* both individually as three distinct producers. **sounds_like:** Air and Kraftwerk (NME); Zero 7 intentionally excluded because the review discusses broader career similarity rather than this album. Leave `influenced_by` blank because critics' resemblance comparisons do not establish causal influences [S_NME,S_PF]. Critical disagreement: AllMusic praises the full-length mood arc; Pitchfork finds the pace restrictive; NME judges it not sinister enough [S_AM,S_PF,S_NME]. These are editorial reactions, not evidence that the album is danceable.

### The Inevitable End — 2014 (`royksopp-theinevitableend`)

**Edition:** original 12-track album (10 Nov 2014); do not automatically include five tracks from the separate **Prologue** bonus disc [E_MB,E_AM]. The late-2014 claim of a farewell meant *last traditional-format album*, **not retirement or the group's final music** [E_OFF,E_AM,C_MB]. `landmark=1` because of its explicit career-era boundary and conceptual end motif, rather than universal critical acclaim.

**Genre evidence:** AllMusic names `downbeat`, `triphop`, `ambientbreakbeat`, `downtempo`, and Club/Dance [E_AM]. It explicitly describes the duo as electro-pop producers [E_AM]. Pitchfork describes mostly slow, glossy electronic-pop songs, filter-disco *Skulls*, and a handful of live-set/club-ready moments [E_PF]. Guardian identifies Scandinavian pop songs with strong electronic arrangements and highlights *Running to the Sea* as a dance-oriented exception [E_GU2]. House is omitted: no album-specific expert evidence unambiguously names it for the 2014 record. `disco` is explicitly one-track, not the dominant style [E_PF].

**Metrics:** energy **2** (isolated muscular beats amid reflective slow numbers [E_PF]); electronic **4** (digitally shaped music interspersed with strings, guitar, vocals [E_MB,E_AM]); mood **1** (goodbyes, loss and brooding affect [E_GU,E_OFF]); theatrical **3** (grand gestures on songs including Monument, but long understated passages [E_AM]); experimental **2** (recognizable electro-pop format despite long reflective arcs [E_PF]); polish **5** (band explicitly sought pristinely clean production as contrast to lyrics [E_OFF]); tempo **2** and groove **2** (not a dance album in artists' own words; `dance` applies to select tracks only [E_OFF,E_GU2]). **Voices:** `clear|soft|powerful|instrumental` reflects Susanne Sundfør, Ryan James, Jamie Irrepressible, Robyn and occasional instrumentals; it is *not* Robyn's solo discography [E_AM,E_MB]. **Lyrics:** heartbreak, love, introspection, nostalgia correspond to recurrent loss/ending narratives [E_OFF,E_GU]. **Producers:** Röyksopp throughout, confirmed track by track [E_MB]; guest vocalists/strings arrangers should not be copied into `producers`. **sounds_like:** Daft Punk (Skulls), a single-track comparison only; Disclosure is omitted because the review mentions a hypothetical singer fit, not a reliable album resemblance; `influenced_by` remains empty [E_PF]. The Guardian's two critics rate cohesion differently; preserve mixed assessment [E_GU,E_GU2].

### Profound Mysteries — 2022 (`royksopp-profoundmysteries`)

**Edition:** April 29 2022 **first ten tracks only** [P_AM,P_DORK]. Separate August and November sequels *Profound Mysteries II* and *III* are **not bonus tracks** and belong in a subsequent incremental package [P_NME,C_MB,C_BACON]. A review of the trilogy as a whole is NOT direct evidence about each volume's sound. The audiovisual films reflect an expanded artist-led concept, but are not additional musical tracks [P_INT,C_BACON]. `landmark=1` for return to multi-album conceptual work and multimodal rollout [P_AM,P_PF].

**Genre evidence:** AllMusic's explicit styles include `downbeat`, `triphop`, `ambientbreakbeat`, `downtempo`, `eurodance`, Club/Dance [P_AM]; Pitchfork identifies `electropop` (energetic vocal songs), `techno` (*This Time, This Place*), `synthpop` (*The Ladder*), and atmospheric instrumental episodes [P_PF]. `techno` and `synthpop` are track-specific. `ambient` references instrumental atmosphere, not ambient-only album [P_PF]. **Important:** *Profound Mysteries II* is not evidence for a 2022 first-volume genre [P_NME].

**Metrics:** energy **3** (juxtaposed quiet passages and forceful synth-beat tracks [P_PF,P_AM]); electronic **4** (synths dominate, but heard piano and guest singing [P_AM]); mood **2** (unearthly, wistful and occasionally euphoric [P_AM,P_PF]); theatrical **3** (cumulative audiovisual concept and soaring guest vocals [P_INT,P_PF]); experimental **3** (juxtaposed instrumentals/pop and multimedia context, not every song avant-garde [P_PF]); polish **5** (clean layered production [P_PF]); tempo **3**, groove **3** (split between club pulse and ambient-piano segments [P_PF]). **Voices:** `soft|clear|instrumental`, guest singers Goldfrapp, Beki Mari, Pixx, Astrid S and Sundfør, interspersed with wordless pieces [P_AM,P_PF]. **Lyrics:** `introspection|love|heartbreak` is a provisional thematic abstraction based on the existential project and ballad lyrics; judge entire record, not singles alone [P_INT,P_AM]. **Producers:** Röyksopp are principal producers [P_AM]; Dork's aggregate production field *also* lists **Madelene Eliasson**; include but flag because Dork does not explain precise track scope and duplicates band members' personal names [P_DORK]. *Do not separately duplicate Svein Berge and Torbjørn Brundtland alongside group credit*. **sounds_like:** Daft Punk comparison is **track-specific** to *Impossible*, not a claim the whole album sounds like Daft Punk [P_PF]. No `influenced_by`: a David Lynch creative-film framing discussed in an interview does not establish direct **musical** influence [P_INT]. AllMusic finds cohesive eerie atmosphere; Pitchfork emphasizes inconsistent pacing between peaks and quiet tracks [P_AM,P_PF].
