# Evidence notes — Pino Daniele / Phase 05 (2001–2007)

**Scope.** Four original studio records only. `status=draft`; every number is Fandome's album-level interpretation from the cited *album-specific* evidence, not a review score. `influenced_by` is deliberately empty: guests, quoted songs and covers are **not** proof of artistic influence. All sources are opened working pages, not Wikipedia. See `*_sources.csv` and `*_genre_evidence.csv` for exact evidence links.

## Medina (2001) — `pinodaniele-medina`

- **Genres and dispute:** independent criticism describes a world/pop record in Arabic/Mediterranean colours (OndaRock, QuestoTrentino, DeBaser), with the explicitly named **raï and flamenco** confined to *Galby* and funk/rap to *Evviva 'o rre*. The official album note says Pino pursued Arabic musicians and melody; this was *not* a genre inference from the guests' nationality. An unlisted Gesualdo da Venosa madrigal concludes the issue (OndaRock and artist note), prompting `madrigal` as a low-weight last genre. The 12 songs printed in the original track list plus one ghost piece total **13 performed songs**; other databases report 12 individually listed tracks. `rai` and `madrigal` are new literal genres, not reassigned to generic world music. The Arab musical instruments/parts are not a reason to force unrelated genres like flamenco across the whole album.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 3` (Evviva's pronounced beat and Galby's energy counter laid-back melodic songs); `electronic 2` (studio computer programming beside clearly credited acoustic instruments); `mood 3` (parental affection and romantic warmth contrasted with racism and political unease); `theatrical 2` (mostly direct songwriting, several dramatic guest sections); `experimental 3` (Arabic instrumentation, raï/flamenco, rap and Renaissance madrigal on a pop framework); `polish 3` (assembled multi-studio sound, polished yet mostly live instrumental sessions); `tempo 3` (mid-paced melodies with energetic world tracks); `groove 3` (funk/rap and percussion alongside slow songs). Critic OndaRock strongly dislikes some hybrids, whereas DeBaser user feedback is split; none constitutes a genre-changing landmark statement.
- **Instruments:** album's *own credits* name Pino guitars and keyboards; Bailey/Sullivan electric bass; Erskine/Melotti drums; Mainieri vibes and marimba (`mallets`), piano; Podio/Rachel Z piano, Rino Zurzolo double bass; Tekbilek baglama (specific lute: `baglama`, new), darbouka (`gobletdrum`), bendir (`framedrum`), nay (`ney` spelling variant), zurna (new `zurna`); Nabil Khlidi/Abdel Krim Halilu oud (`oud`), Khadija El Afrite canun (`qanun` spelling variant), Hamadi Ben Mabrouk Arabic riqq (`riqq`, new), violin and cello, and A.M.I.T. strings. The instrument tokens indicate actual stated performance, not assumed use by genre. `baglama` is kept distinct from the broader `saz` umbrella.
- **Production:** artist site specifically names Colasanti and Iuè as computer programmers (`programming`), and Podio's authored string arrangements (`stringarrangements`). No Fairlight, samplers, particular drum machine, DAW or multitrack medium evidenced. **Producers:** Pino Daniele for the album and **Mike Mainieri co-producer** on tracks 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12. Podio is arranger/conductor, not co-producer. The many performers are guests, not co-billed album artists.
- **Lyrics/languages:** primary printed lyrics explicitly include Italian, Neapolitan, English, Arabic and French (French/Arabic on *Galby*). Main songs express affection, separation, identity, political/social critique of racism, Naples nostalgia; song excerpts also support family and changing one's life. Lyrics do not justify a generic guest-as-influence link.
- **Sonic comparison:** OndaRock compares the ending of *Sara* to Sting's *Desert Rose*, whose first album is **Brand New Day** (1999), verified by Sting's official song page; `Sting (Brand New Day) [1/13]` is a **critic's limited sonic comparison**, not an artist-cited influence. Do not add 99 Posse/Faudel/Salif Keita/Lotfi Bushnaq as sounds-like or influences merely because they perform. `landmark 0`: ambition/critical appraisal alone does not equal independent evidence of having redefined a genre or whole career.

## Passi d’autore (2004) — `pinodaniele-passidautore`

- **Release credit / type:** official page heads the project **Pino Daniele Project – Passi d’autore**, but `catalog.ts` identifies the album as Pino Daniele under existing `pinodaniele-passidautore`. The project moniker is not an additional artist and `coBilled.ts` needs no entry. The record is a **studio** album (not live) although it uses an experienced jazz trio. Do not create a separate "Pino Daniele Project" artist ID. AllMusic original title matches.
- **Genres:** contemporary Rockol directly lists jazz, blues, Brazilian **bossa nova** and Latin music; AllMusic assigns Italian Pop. The official album description mentions **world music**. OndaRock's detailed review documents polyphonic **madrigal** passages, Gregorian chant and *sacred music* flavour, and **baroque** choir writing on several pieces, also explicitly calling them **a cappella**. These are hybrid texture and selective song genres, **not** a claim that the whole album is an actual sacred choral work. `songwriter` recognizes the many original songs, rather than the guest musicians' jazz reputations alone. The new `madrigal` token serves this record and the Medina hidden track.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 2` (relaxed acoustic songs outweigh the few rhythmic numbers); `electronic 1` (Ztar only track 1; computer editing/programming is credited, but the audible ensemble is mainly acoustic); `mood 3` (romantic sentiment and rebirth mix with loss and political weariness); `theatrical 3` (soprano/alto/tenor/baritone ensemble brings staged choral formality amid direct songs); `experimental 4` (unusual mix of early music, polyphony, Latin grooves, jazz trio and contemporary songs, called least direct by OndaRock); `polish 3` (carefully studio-produced but natural instrument-focused); `tempo 2` (laid-back rhythms, soft trio arrangements); `groove 2` (some bossa/Latin sections but not primarily dance music). OndaRock finds the result intriguing but uneven; DeBaser critic is notably harsher than Rockol/QuestoTrentino.
- **Instruments:** official credits Pino **guitars**, Alan Pasqua piano and keyboards, Dave Carpenter double bass, Erskine/Gatto drums plus Erskine percussion, A.M.I.T. strings, Colasanti **Ztar only on track 1**. `ztar` was already proposed in Phase-04; check the current instrument list at import time. Do not infer acoustics models from an album labelled jazz. Vocals described as **thin/soft** by contemporary Rockol, choral/classical ensemble by OndaRock (lead passages by the ensemble). `operatic` is *not* automatically added based solely on vocal range headings.
- **Production:** Pino produced the album (official twice); Gianluca Podio wrote/directed the **vocal ensemble and string arrangements** on tracks 1, 4, 5, 9, 13, but is *not credited producer*. `choirvocals|stringarrangements|programming|digitalediting` from explicit official credits, **not** generic assumptions about a jazz record.
- **Lyrics/languages:** overwhelmingly Italian; love, introspection and fading hopes, Che Guevara political story in *Isola Grande*, Maradona tribute in *Tango della buena suerte*, summer memories and longing. There is no evidence the performer identified a *musical* figure as an album **influence**: Django Reinhardt's piece *Nuages* is actually adapted/covered with Pino words, excluded from `influenced_by` and `sounds_like`. OndaRock independently likens the guitar sound on *Bella da vivere* to Dire Straits, so `Dire Straits [1/14]` is a critic comparison for **one** of 14 tracks. `landmark 0` despite superlatives about ambition: no independent claim of genre-defining or career-defining transformation.

## Iguana cafè – Latin blues e melodie (2005) — `pinodaniele-iguanacafelatinbluesemelodie`

- **Genres and dispute:** `latin` is central (the subtitle calls itself Latin blues); AllMusic confirms Italian Pop; OndaRock identifies **mambo** on *Narcisista in azione*, *Maria*, *Patricia*, **bossa nova** on *Pensando amor* and a Neapolitan serenade on *Serenata a fronn'e limone*. DeBaser says a couple of covers almost reach **liscio**; hence that token goes last as texture. Artist calls the album *Latin blues*, thus literal **new** `latinblues` (do not silently remap it to Latin, blues or bluesrock) but **both OndaRock and the contemporaneous DeBaser critic expressly dispute that blues actually appears in the sound**: `latinblues` and `blues` go later in the genre order, and confidence does not pretend that they are uncontested. A mere cover of a Pérez Prado mambo track is not proof of an influence. A Presley arrangement of *It's Now or Never* and *'O sole mio* is a cover, not an influenced_by link.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 2` (brief laid-back love songs and light mambo); `electronic 2` (keyboards, credited programming and Ztar interwoven with physical percussion/strings); `mood 3` (sunny/ironic songs balance breakup and nostalgia); `theatrical 2` (playful mambo and serenade, generally restrained); `experimental 1` (recognizable pop/Latin conventional structures, two familiar covers); `polish 4` (highly finished studio pop, critic explicitly remarks polished sound); `tempo 3` (mid tempo through faster light Latin dance); `groove 3` (mambo/bossa tracks are danceable while ballads and brief instrumentals lower whole-album weight). This score is based on distinct named tracks, not the word Latin alone.
- **Instruments/production:** official album personnel credits Pino on guitars and bass; Naná Vasconcelos percussion; Karl Potter congas and other percussion; Gianluca Podio piano/keyboards; AMIT strings; Fabio Massimo Colasanti Ztar and **computer programming**, hence `programming`. Source does not identify synthesizer model, drum-machine, multi-track medium or a specifically named string **arranger**, so do **not** fill `synth`, `drummachine`, `stringarrangements` or a named machine without a second source. Ztar is distinct from `guitarsynth` and `keytar`.
- **Producers, language, words:** Pino Daniele alone is credited producer. Podio is arranger, Salzano and Alex Daniele are **executive** and excluded. Lyrics printed by artist are mainly Italian, Neapolitan (the serenade and embedded *'O sole mio* passage) and English (the cover single). Themes love, lost relationships, self-analysis, irony and romance/physical attraction. Voice intentionally **blank**; no source gives reliable album-wide lead vocal taxonomy. `landmark 0`; no independent change-definition statement; mixed independent criticism.

## Il mio nome è Pino Daniele e vivo qui (2007) — `pinodaniele-ilmionomeepinodanieleevivoqui`

- **Styles:** SUONO calls it pop; AllMusic names Italian Pop, Adult Contemporary, Adult Alternative Pop/Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock. The latter generic database labels are deliberately at low weight because OndaRock and DeBaser emphasize much more distinctive **electronics mixed with live guitars/drums**, blues and Latin/Caribbean tracks. OndaRock specifically names swing on *L'africano* and almost chill-out on *Ischia isola nascente*, placing these late as single-song links. `electronic` as *genre* also has album-spanning corroboration by SUONO and DeBaser, not just the programmer credit. `neapolitansong` omitted without independently clear song-level style evidence. No `rock` plain category without a whole-record rock description.
- **Eight scales:** `energy 3` (fired-up Back Home, jaunty Mardi Gras, other relaxed ballads); `electronic 3` (DeBaser hears repeated keyboard sequences/electronic drums alongside real percussion, SUONO confirms); `mood 3` (upbeat returns with anguished romantic songs); `theatrical 2` (emotional duets but mainly direct band singing); `experimental 2` (programming/live interplay within mainstream composition); `polish 5` (two independent contemporary reviewers explicitly emphasize immaculate precision and exceptionally finished production); `tempo 3` (mixed mid-tempo, swing, ballads); `groove 3` (Latin percussive numbers balanced by ballads). `landmark 0`: "return to form" and strong recording quality are not genre- or career-definition evidence.
- **Instruments:** official credits Pino guitar, Alfredo Paixão bass, Peter Erskine/Mariano Barba drums, Dave Carpenter double bass, Tony Esposito percussion, Gianluca Podio piano, Bob Sheppard saxophone (one track), Corrado Ferrari **tammorra** (track 10; **new exact token**). DeBaser reviewer reports Cuban **brass** on *Rhum and Coca* and electronic **drums**/keyboards widely heard. The brass player is *uncredited* in the official shortened personnel list: name nobody; token describes heard sound, not the maker. `electronicdrums` denotes a sonic percussion source the review explicitly names, without assuming a drum-machine brand.
- **Production:** `programming` explicitly credited to Fabio Massimo Colasanti; `stringarrangements` explicitly credited to Podio (no strings performer names found, so no `strings` instrument). Pino says some album pieces were recorded in direct group takes: `liverecording` means **studio performance captured live**, not a concert or live album. No workstation or drum machine model identified; `choirvocals` omitted (Giorgia duet is not choir). Pino Daniele is the **only** producer; Giorgia, Podio, Tony Esposito et al are performers/arrangers, not co-producers.
- **Languages/lyrics:** artist official lyrics provide Italian, Neapolitan, English, Portuguese (Paixão co-writes/sings *Mardi Gras*), Spanish (*L'africano* and *Rhum and Coca*). Love, loss, nostalgia, roots, social/personal pressures and irony are supported. `voice` blank for the lead: critics describe expressive rather than named vocal categories; no default voice from the artist's career.
- **Sounds-like:** OndaRock and SUONO each **sonically** compare *Back Home* to Santana; OndaRock explicitly compares the *Mardi Gras* groove with Jorge Ben; DeBaser hears Pat Metheny-style guitar specifically on *Passo napoletano*. They are **single-track comparisons** `Santana [1/10]|Jorge Ben [1/10]|Pat Metheny [1/10]`, not influences, producer relationships or guests. OndaRock's mention of Bob Sheppard playing with Steely Dan/Joni Mitchell is mere personnel biography and therefore *not* a resemblance; neither is added.
