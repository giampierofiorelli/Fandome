# Janet Jackson — Phase 02 album evidence notes

Research date: **26 October 2026**. This file concerns **four new studio rows only**. Codes resolve to complete working URLs in `janet_phase02_sources.csv`. Genre evidence provides an independent row for each token, with scope. The eight 0–5 scales are **editorial assessment against the project guide**, not acoustic measurements; they are grounded in discussion of the specific LP. **`sounds_like` is explicitly editorial resemblance; `influenced_by` is blank** because none of the reviewed sources establishes an album-specific influence in the required sense. A sampled song, guest, composer, engineer or producer is not an album influence.

## `janet-janet` — *janet.* (1993)

**Genres.** AllMusic [jall] explicitly catalogs contemporary R&B, dance-pop, adult contemporary R&B and pop, under R&B. Slant [jslant] explicitly groups new jack swing, house, pop, rock, hip-hop, jazz and opera among this album's idioms. Album of the Year [jaoty] additionally tags pop soul, hip-hop soul, smooth soul and trip-hop as **secondary** genres. The contemporary Los Angeles Times preview [jla] independently names funk, hip-hop, rap, rock, pop, jazz and industrial (industrial is a **texture**, not the main genre). Classic Pop [jclassic] attributes house to **Throb**, alternative rock to **What'll I Do**, opera to **This Time**, and hip-hop inflections to *You Want This* and *Because of Love*. AllMusic [jall] explains that *Funky Big Band* samples Lionel Hampton; `bigband` accordingly means the recorded sample heard on that song, not a fresh big-band session on the entire record. Secondary styles remain late in the genre string. Do **not** treat Kathleen Battle's soprano vocal on *This Time* as proof that Janet's lead voice is operatic.

**Instruments.** Liner-derived personnel [jgate] include keyboards, organ, guitar, bass, orchestral string players, brass, woodwinds and percussion. A personnel listing for *This Time* [jthis] separately verifies violin, viola, trumpet, flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, double bass and an operatic **guest singer** (Kathleen Battle). Given their localized role, winds and orchestral instruments are listed **after** rhythm-section parts. `guitar`, not `electricguitar`, follows credits that often lack electric/acoustic precision. A classic 1990s R&B genre label does not justify adding a specific synthesizer model, sampler brand, drum-machine model or recording software. `sampling` is documented for Lionel Hampton; horn and string **arrangements** are supported by discography personnel. `layeredvocals` is supported by Janet's lead and backing parts [jgate], and constitutes the modest generic claim of arranged backing harmonies.

**Production.** Janet, Jimmy Jam, Terry Lewis are album-wide credited producers [jmb][jq], with **Jellybean Johnson** separately credited *producer* for *What'll I Do* [jmb]. Mick Jagger and Keith Richards are songwriters on that track, **not** producers. The *This Time* singer Kathleen Battle is **guest**, not producer. Neither the use of samples by Lionel Hampton nor references to Marvin Gaye, Prince or Mary J. Blige establishes a documented influence for the album. The latter names occur only in `sounds_like` as editorial audio comparisons.

**Themes and scales.** Central sensuality and liberation coexist with romance and politically aware *New Agenda* featuring Chuck D [jla][jclassic]. `energy=3` and `tempo=3` reflect midtempo bedroom grooves, dance songs and ballads; `electronic=3` acknowledges modern beats alongside a large live instrumental cast; `mood=4` romantic confidence with a few conflict tracks; `theatrical=3` interludes and *This Time*; `experimental=3` wide genre shifts and sampled interludes; `polish=5` blockbuster pop/R&B studio finish; `groove=4` propulsive house/new jack passages plus a substantial slow-jam section. Landmark: yes, central shift in Jackson's self-presentation [jslant][jclassic].

**Caveat.** Discographies report both *Janet* and stylized *janet.*; catalog's `Janet` determines the existing slug. Store official original album title `janet.` in row without changing ID. Avoid treating *Poetic Justice* songs and later *janet. Remixed* as separate studio albums.

## `janet-thevelvetrope` — *The Velvet Rope* (1997)

**Genres.** Contemporary review [vla] foregrounds R&B, pop melodies, plus jazz, folk and techno *nuances*. Classic Pop [vclassic] describes R&B, hip-hop, trip-hop, electro, dance and jazz. Bandwagon [vband] explicitly names techno, electronica, G-funk, trip-hop, hard rock and drum-and-bass elements. Albumism [valbumism] calls out neo-soul and original-track **house** (not dance remixes). Album of the Year [vaoty] adds **art pop and dance-pop** as secondary labels. Slant [vslant] calls *Empty* drum and bass, Pitchfork [vpitch] says it was described as jungle-inflected in 1997, and Pitchfork's 1990s roundup [v90s] calls *Empty* proto-**footwork** and *Anything* quiet storm. `footwork` is placed last as **modern retrospective texture**, not a claim of authentic 1997 Chicago-footwork stylistic affiliation. `gfunk`/`folk`/`jazz` are **local colors**, not core album identity. The 2022 *deluxe* review [vpitch] is used only for original 1997 album content; its remixes are **excluded**.

**Instruments and production.** Track-level original release credits [vmb][vmb2][vq] explicitly credit Alex Richbourg with drum-machine programming, supporting `drummachine|drumprogramming`; Dave Barry and Mike Scott with guitar; James “Big Jim” Wright with organ/keyboard; Vanessa-Mae violin; brass and reeds, and arranged strings on *Every Time*. The United Children's Choir appears on *Velvet Rope*, hence `choral` **secondary**. Generic synthesizer machine models are **not** asserted: no verified original-release make/model was found. `sampling` is unequivocal on *Got 'til It's Gone* (Joni Mitchell); *My Need* samples Diana Ross, and other tracks incorporate borrowed records [vpitch][vmb]. Background/lead vocal arrangements justify `layeredvocals`; orchestra arrangements support `stringarrangements`. Janet, Jam and Lewis are recording producers [vmb][vq]; René Elizondo Jr. is **writer/executive producer**, and *not* in the row producer column. Q-Tip and Vanessa-Mae are **guest performers**, not producers or musical influences solely by virtue of appearance. Later J Dilla/Timbaland/Teddy Riley remixes are **outside** the original-album data [vpitch].

**Lyrics and numeric judgments.** Intimacy, depression, social exclusion, sexual identity, domestic abuse, AIDS loss and grieving [vpitch][vclassic][vslant]. `energy=3` (frantic *Empty* and cathartic *What About* balanced by slow ballads); `electronic=3` (many programmed loops, organic strings/guitars); `mood=1` (substantial bleak/introspective lyrical content, despite euphoric *Together Again*); `theatrical=4` (narrated/interlinked concept and confessional staging); `experimental=4` (jungle rhythm and style collage); `polish=5`; `tempo=3`; `groove=3` (strong club songs amidst many reflective slow tracks). Landmark `1` supported by the critics' retrospective reappraisal [vpitch][v90s].

**Disagreement.** Critics' evaluations differ: [vslant] finds aspects of the sexuality affective/unstable; [vpitch] sees artistic coherence and important frankness. Avoid flattening the review debates into a supposed objective score. Rereleases vary in track list and bonus remixes; the base 1997 recording defines this descriptor.

## `janet-allforyou` — *All for You* (2001)

**Genres.** AllMusic [aall] lists dance-pop, contemporary R&B and adult contemporary. Pitchfork's July 2026 retrospective [apitch] directly lists pop-rock, electronica and house alongside pop/R&B. NME [anme] explicitly describes the title track and *You Ain't Right* as funk, and *Come On Get Up* as tribal house. Album of the Year [aaoty] adds secondary labels smooth soul, neo-soul, downtempo and art pop: these remain after the core musical terms. Washington Post [awpost] relates its dance material to disco. Classic Pop [aclassic] cites mellow drum'n'bass on *Better Days*, and rock/hip-hop crossover on *Trust a Try*. These are **song-scoped**. Do not describe *All for You* overall as drum-and-bass or rock because of one track; those tags go near the end.

**Credits, instruments, technology.** MusicBrainz's track-derived credit list [amb] contains Rockwilder as a **producer and drum programmer** beside Janet/Jam/Lewis on songs such as *You Ain't Right* and *Would You Mind*; also documents Alex Richbourg's drum programming, guitar by Mike Scott and Dave Barry, bass guitar, live drums, violins, violas and cellos. Qobuz [aq][aq2] corroborates producer credits, string arranging by Lee Blaske, drum programming and album collaborators. Music critic [aslant] documents guitar solo and strings on *Better Days*. [apitch] describes distorted electric-guitar material on *You Ain't Right*/*Trust a Try* and sampled source recordings from Change, America/Erik Satie and Carly Simon. The Carly Simon guest contribution includes a **new recording** of her chorus for *Son of a Gun*, not necessarily only a straight sample; don't conflate the two techniques. As Pitchfork describes a spoken segment recorded on DAT, `datrecording` was **intentionally not** assigned: an interlude captured on DAT is insufficient to assert the album's master recording medium. No DAW, drum-machine model or guitar pedal can be substantiated. `layeredvocals` intentionally omitted pending more explicit production testimony.

**Lyrical/scale judgments.** Emphasizes courtship, lust and post-separation optimism, alongside anger at perceived betrayal and emotional processing [apitch][aslant]. `energy=3`/`tempo=3` reflect alternating up-tempo dance material and slow bedroom tracks; `electronic=3` electronic programming plus documented band/strings; `mood=4` generally bright, tempered by bitter tracks; `theatrical=3` skits and hyperbolic rock sections; `experimental=2` mainstream forms with some unusual passages; `polish=5`; `groove=4` dance singles versus long ballad passages. Critics disagree [anme][aslant] vs sympathetic [apitch] on the length and artistic success. `landmark=1` as one of her defining commercial eras, **not** a claim of universal critical acclaim.

**Influences.** Pitchfork describes *Someone to Call My Lover* as built around a sample of America's *Ventura Highway* and Satie's *Gymnopédie No.1*. Those are **samples, not evidence that either artist influenced the entire album**. Slant says 1970s sources inspire production, but only vague decade-level attribution; no `influenced_by` entry is created. `sounds_like` is editorial only.

## `janet-damitajo` — *Damita Jo* (2004)

**Genre evidence.** Album of the Year [daoty] gives Contemporary R&B as principal genre, dance-pop, smooth soul, downtempo and hip-hop soul secondaries. Classic Pop [dclassic] explicitly describes hip-hop tracks (*Damita Jo*, *My Baby*), an 1980s R&B throwback (*R&B Junkie*), 1960s soul pastiche (*I Want You*), and new-wave-flavored *Just a Little While*. The Guardian [dguardian] confirms the early-eighties rock feel of the last song. Metacritic [dmeta] lists pop. These narrower styles appear late. **House and synthpop were considered and removed**: club-oriented does not necessarily establish house, nor does new wave prove synthpop. Pitchfork [dpitch] describes the center as breezy, mid-tempo R&B, undermining classification as an album-wide pure dance record.

**Instrumentation and studio process.** Original-credit transcription [dmusicbase] lists Bobby Ross Avila **bass/guitar**, Iz Avila **drums/percussion**, Jimmy Jam **keyboards**, while Qobuz [dq] additionally names *All Nite* programmers, Scott Storch keyboards and multiple programmed productions. Pitchfork [dpitch] specifically mentions a **horn line** on title track, backing layers on *Truly*, and varied soft vocal attacks. The instruments column includes `brass` because of the reported horns, not due to assuming a horn session exists album-wide. There is no verified machine model, analog tape, live recording medium or specific DAW; production is conservatively `programming|layeredvocals`.

**Producer audit (essential).** Track-level Qobuz [dq] separates writers, mixers, executive producers, vocalists and real recording producers. These are included because credited as **producer or co-producer** for at least one track: Janet Jackson, Jimmy Jam, Terry Lewis, Dallas Austin (*Sexhibition*, *Just a Little While*), Kanye West and credit entity Roc The World (*Strawberry Bounce*, *My Baby*, *I Want You*), Scott Storch (*Island Life*), Anders Bagge and Arnthor Birgisson (*All Nite*, *SloLove*), Babyface (*Thinkin' Bout My Ex*), Bobby Ross Avila and Iz Avila (specified as **co-producers** on *Damita Jo*, *Spending Time with You* and other songs). Kanye is **featured vocalist on My Baby** as well as credited producer; appearance alone would not suffice. **Jermaine Dupri is not included** (romantic partner; no verified album-track production credit in these sources). Nor is David Ritz (author) or Télèpopmusik (sample/track-writing origins) to be added merely through a songwriter line. Do not interpret 2004 press coverage of Kanye or Jermaine as evidence of specific mechanical/software technique. Qobuz has alternate editions with edited titles; cross-check the original explicit album variant [dq].

**Themes/scales/criticism.** Repeated love, sex, playful role/persona exploration and club freedom; identity appears but is less central than romance [dpitch][dslant]. `energy=3`, `tempo=3` (mid-tempo R&B dominates), `electronic=3` (programming and live bass/drums/guitars), `mood=4` (bright romance), `theatrical=2` (interlude personae but intimate delivery), `experimental=2` (minor genre detours in mainstream pop arrangements), `polish=5`, `groove=4` (clear club component mixed with slow jams). `landmark=0`: artistically notable retrospective reassessment [dpitch], but not as defining as Control, Rhythm Nation, janet. or The Velvet Rope; do not conflate this judgment with sales suppression during 2004 media controversy. Critics disagree, often strongly: 2004 critiques [dslant][dguardian] contrast with later recognition [dpitch].

**Release/edition notes.** Press/distributor release dates for different territories range across March 2004. Original year **2004** is stable. The title *Damita Jo* is absent from branch `catalog.ts` (audited 26 October 2026); Claude must add it before running the descriptor validator.
