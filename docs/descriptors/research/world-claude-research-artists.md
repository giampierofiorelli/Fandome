# World music: proposed artists

## King Sunny Adé
- id: `kingsunnyade`
- display name: King Sunny Adé (album credit: King Sunny Adé and His African Beats)
- country: NG
- language: yo (Yoruba)
- start: 1960s (percussionist, then guitarist in the 1960s; own band formed 1967 per Wikipedia, 1965 per RBMA)
- genre family: world (closest fit; jùjú has no family of its own)
- tagline: Nigeria's king of jùjú
- colour: #2f9e6e
- studio discography (international studio albums; see caveat):
  [Juju Music, 1982], [Synchro System, 1983], [Aura, 1984], [E Dide (Get Up), 1995], [Odù, 1998], [Seven Degrees North, 2000], [Bábá Mo Túndé, 2010]
- caveat: Adé has released well over a hundred LPs in Nigeria on his own labels (de.wikipedia; RBMA puts it at about 50 by 1982). No complete, reliable list is available from open sources (Discogs and AllMusic were blocked). MusicBrainz also lists Nigerian LPs such as Private Line (1978), Searching for My Love (1980), Check "E" (1981), Ajoo (1983), Vintage (1984), Sweet Banana (1986), Let Them Say (1987) and Ogun (1997). I suggest cataloguing only the international studio albums above for now. Live Live Juju (1988) and Live at the Hollywood Palace (1994) are live albums; The Return of the Juju King (1987) and the Classics series are compilations.
- artist-level influences: Tunde Nightingale. Adé played with him and borrowed elements of his "so wa mbe" jùjú style (Wikipedia, King Sunny Adé): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Sunny_Ad%C3%A9 . Adé himself credits traditional sources ("the ancestors", the kora, the African violin) for how he wrote his guitar and pedal steel parts (RBMA 2014): https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2014/07/king-sunny-ade-from-lagos-to-hollywood . These are not artists, so they are not added.

## Youssou N'Dour
- id: `youssoundour`
- display name: Youssou N'Dour (1980s albums often credited "Youssou N'Dour & Le Super Étoile de Dakar")
- country: SN
- language: wo (Wolof); some French and English on later international albums
- start: 1970s (Star Band 1976, Étoile de Dakar 1978)
- genre family: world (pop for some later international records)
- tagline: Senegal's voice of mbalax
- colour: #e0a23a
- studio discography (from the en.wikipedia list and MusicBrainz; years are first release):
  [Ndiadiane Ndiaye, 1982], [Diongoma, 1983], [Immigrés, 1984], [Nelson Mandela, 1985], [The Lion, 1989], [Set, 1990], [Eyes Open, 1992], [The Guide (Wommat), 1994], [Lii, 1996], [St. Louis, 1997], [Rewmi, 1999], [Joko: From Village to Town, 2000], [Ba Tay, 2001], [Nothing's in Vain (Coono du réér), 2002], [Egypt, 2004], [Alsaama Day, 2007], [Rokku Mi Rokka, 2007], [Dakar–Kingston, 2010], [Mbalakh Dafay Wakh, 2011], [Africa Rekk, 2016], [Seeni Valeurs, 2017], [History, 2019], [Mbalax, 2021], [Éclairer le monde – Light the World, 2025]
- caveats:
  - Dozens of Senegal-only cassettes (Vol. 1–15, 1982–88) are not listed.
  - Joko: The Link (2000) is the international edition of Joko.
  - Gainde (1995) is billed jointly with Yandé Codou Sène.
  - Djamil / Inédits 84–85 are collections of outtakes.
  - The Le Grand Bal and Bercy titles are live albums.
  - I Bring What I Love and Kirikou are soundtracks.
  - Wikipedia and MusicBrainz give different years for Rewmi (1999 vs 2000) and Ba Tay (2001 vs 2002).
  - "Respect" (2018) is on Wikipedia's list but I could not verify it.
- artist-level influences: none confirmed in his own words in the sources I could read. Trouser Press reports that seeing Touré Kunda live pushed him toward the international pop market, and that Étoile de Dakar started from Africanized Cuban music (https://trouserpress.com/reviews/youssou-ndour/). Low strength, so not added.

## Buena Vista Social Club
- id: `buenavistasocialclub`
- display name: Buena Vista Social Club
- country: CU
- language: es
- start: 1990s (assembled 1996)
- genre family: latin (second choice: world)
- tagline: Havana's veterans revive son
- colour: #c8643c
- studio discography: [Buena Vista Social Club, 1997]
- caveats: At Carnegie Hall (2008) is live. Lost and Found (2015) is a compilation of unreleased studio and live recordings. MusicBrainz lists "Carnegie 25" (2023) as an album; it appears to be a Carnegie Hall anniversary release (live), but I did not verify this. The members' own albums (Buena Vista Social Club Presents Ibrahim Ferrer, Introducing Rubén González, etc.) belong to those artists.
- artist-level influences: none documented as statements. The repertoire comes from earlier Cuban composers (Arcaño y sus Maravillas, Cachao, Sindo Garay, María Teresa Vera); these are covers, not influences.
