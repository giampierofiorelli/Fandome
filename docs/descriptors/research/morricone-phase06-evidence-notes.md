# Morricone — Phase 06: critical evidence notes (original LPs only)

**Audit date:** 26 October 2026. **GitHub branch:** `descriptors` (read-only), artist ID `morricone`. Four rows are strictly new; the checked branch currently contains the first sixteen catalogue albums through Phase 04 but the latest `morricone.csv` has sixteen previous rows (Phase 01–04). Phase 05 was supplied separately and is excluded here whether or not it has been imported. The phase's first-release years are dates for **records**, not merely for film premieres.

## How to read values

- The 0–5 scales are conservative editorial judgements about the album **supported by album-specific descriptions**, not computed signal measurements; unknown `polish` stays blank everywhere.
- Source support is stored in `morricone_phase06_sources.csv` and genre token-by-token in `morricone_phase06_genre_evidence.csv` (with `scope`). `soundtrack` describes purpose/format and appears **last** in `genres`.
- Instruments are credited or independently described for these *scores*. `production` is filled only for a clearly described vocal technique. Unverified microphones, synthesis models, tape machines, software, overdubs and producer roles are not inferred.
- `sounds_like` is a transparent *editorial comparison*, not influence or personnel evidence; leave all `influenced_by` empty because no album-specific artist influence statement was established. Note especially that quotation/parody of Wagner in *Nessuno* is **not** evidence that Wagner shaped this entire album.
- Original soundtrack LP repertoire is the basis of the descriptor. Sources describing later expanded CDs are used only for clearly applicable earlier programme or session facts.

## 1. Chi l’ha vista morire? — 1972

**Original record:** Gemelli GG.ST.10.017, Italian LP (October 1972), **ten tracks**, corroborated by SoundtrackCollector. Digitmovies recovered the *original stereo album master* and says little extra session material survived. This is unusually good original-programme evidence; do not mistake Cometa 1978 mixed-film album or CEM's 1979 pressing for the first LP.

**Genres and disagreement.** `childrensmusic` is a **limited track-level** token for the repeatedly sung **filastrocche** repertoire; the record is for adult thriller audiences, so this token is deliberately ranked behind classical, experimental and pop: Bietti identifies them, while Digitmovies identifies both traditional texts and four lyrics by **Maria Travia Morricone**. `classical` is an arrangement/texture judgement from the specialist review’s liturgical-style choral harmonies, Renaissance instruments and harpsichord, not a claim that the film is an orchestral classical work. `experimental` concerns the stranger choral harmonies (MainTitles); KQEK specifically describes a **contemporary pop** drum/bass underpinning for *La bela riposava*, so `pop` is a limited track-level genre. It would be wrong to classify the entire album as atonal experimental music: Digitmovies expressly differentiates it from conventional atonal giallo writing. Vital Weekly considers the score less successful than other admirers; that disagreement affects reception, not credits.

**Instruments / voice.** The archival label explicitly identifies **drums, electric guitar, keyboards** under children's choir; KQEK and MainTitles identify **harpsichord, electric bass, violin, woodwinds, strings** in specific cues. `bass` is used for electric bass (there is no separate `electricbass` token). The main singing presence is Coro di Voci Bianche di Paolo Lucci (directed by Nicola Samale), with **Edda Dell’Orso on track 8**; `choral|soft` is predominantly choir and gentle solo, not operatic. Language `vec|it`: MainTitles explicitly reports Venetian-language songs; other texts use Italian and local forms. `vec` is ISO 639-3 for Venetian. Not all songs use one language, and language follows the sung record, not dialogue in the film.

**Scales.** Energy 2 and tempo 2 reflect an album of mostly measured choral tunes with brief agitation; mood 1 acknowledges the darkening lullabies, theatrical 4 marks prominent arranged choir; experimental 3 captures occasional unsettling harmonies without making all ten songs avant-garde. Electronic 1 reflects electric instruments, groove 0 and unknown polish are deliberately conservative.

**Recording caveat.** Musicologist Guglielmo Bottin (2024) identifies **post-recording cuts and radical montage** in the **film's use** of score cues. It does not demonstrate that the published ten-track LP contains these edited layers; hence `tapeeffects`/`sonicmanipulation` are **not** assigned to album production. The producer credit is absent. These choices preserve the difference between an audiovisual film mix and the first LP.

Sources: https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/22928/Chi%2BL%27Ha%2BVista%2BMorire; https://shop.digitmovies.com/it/cd/59-CDDM059-8032628990595.html; https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/1079108; https://www.kqek.com/cd_lp_reviews/v2z/CD_0046_WhoSawHerDie.htm; https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/chi-l-ha-vista-morire/; https://www.bietti.it/riviste/inland-n-92019-aldo-lado/orizzonti-sperimentali-delle-colonne-sonore-le-musiche-di-ennio-morricone-per-il-cinema-di-aldo-lado/; https://www.vitalweekly.net/number-553/.

## 2. Revolver — 1973

**Original record:** Italian **General Music LP, 1973**, ten tracks. Quartet and Beat Records independently establish this. The modern twenty-plus-track editions include alternates, longer suites and versions not on the original LP. Do not credit original album with the **12-string guitar version** or **synth version** of *Un amico*: Quartet locates those after track ten, i.e. bonus repertoire.

**Musical shape and genres.** Quartet calls the main *Revolver / Inseguimento e fuga* theme a driving, repeated **piano-and-orchestra** staccato thriller motif; there is also gentle *Anna*. The GDM reissue describes **modern pop arrangements**, particularly around the *Un amico* song, so `pop` is directly supported; `classical` is a qualified *orchestral texture* judgement, not a genre literally supplied by Quartet. The score spans propulsion and tenderness; the high theatrical 4 refers to forceful orchestral pursuit, not a vocalist-driven spectacle. Electronic remains blank because original-LP instrumental or electronic-color evidence is not adequately documented; no synth model or original synth arrangement was verified. Groove 1 and experimental 2 recognise that the core is non-danceable film music with a pronounced rhythmic motif.

**Voice and technique.** *Un amico / Un ami* is a **French-language** song sung by Daniel Beretta (sometimes spelled **Berretta** in platform metadata), described in GDM archival commentary as **falsetto** on the main version. The suite is otherwise principally instrumental, hence `instrumental|soft`, `language=fr`, `production=falsetto`. Do not assign `falsetto` to Morricone himself; this is Beretta’s performance. Original soundtrack lyric-writing credits appear with variants across IMDb and Qobuz; they are not `producers`. Friendship is not in the permitted Fandome lyric-topic tokens, and there is no album-wide documented allowed lyric theme, so `lyrics` is blank. No album producer specifically credited in verified materials; do not enter Morricone solely as composer.

**Instruments.** `piano|orchestra` supported directly by the label. Note omitted 12-string guitar and synthesizer **variants are bonus tracks**. A title like *Quasi un Vivaldi* is neither a Vivaldi cover nor a documented influence. `sounds_like` points to rhythmic poliziesco idioms via an editorial comparison only.

Sources: https://quartetrecords.com/product/revolver-2xlp/; https://www.beatrecords.it/it/catalogo/3373/revolver; https://arksquare.net/detail.php?cdno=GDM03618; https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/revolver-original-motion-picture-soundtrack-ennio-morricone/5055478216687; https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0073626/soundtrack/.

## 3. La proprietà non è più un furto — 1973

**Original record:** RCA **OLS 19**, Italian LP **October 1973**, thirteen original tracks; Quartet splits these visibly from the extra film-version cues added later. This is the third film in Elio Petri's neurosis trilogy. The estate filmography lists this score under **1972** (work/composition chronology), but the record itself is **1973**. This is a legitimate distinction and not an album-date inconsistency.

**Musical character.** Quartet explicitly describes **avant-garde compositional technique**, electronic timbral experimentation and sonic evocations of an oppressive city. It also identifies a **grotesque march**. `avantgarde` is the dominant genre, `march` is music in some cues, `electronic` is texture rather than a fully electronic genre. Their importance is ordered accordingly; `soundtrack` last. The unsettling/diseased mood supports 0; grotesque institutional ritual supports theatrical 4; electronic 2 is cautious in view of the orchestral basis and unidentified electronics; experimental 4 reflects formal language. Tempo 2 and groove 1 reflect a non-dance score, not evidence of a BPM; unknown polish blank.

**Instruments, voice and production.** Goodfellas' account identifies **orchestration**; `orchestra` is the only safe listed instrument. Quartet refers to voices and electronically altered timbres but does **not** name a synthesizer, device, vocalist or specific recording technique for the original thirteen tracks. Thus `synth`, individual electronic machine names, `tapeeffects` and `sonicmanipulation` are not attributed; `voice` is left empty rather than inventing `choral` or `spoken`. `lyrics`, `language`, `producers` also blank. Sound-design resemblance to postwar avant-garde writing in `sounds_like` is **editorial**, not a sourced compositional debt.

**Crediting caveat.** Bruno Nicolai is explicitly documented as **conductor**, which is not a producer or co-producer. The DVD/film producer is not an album producer.

Sources: https://quartetrecords.com/product/la-proprieta-non-epiu-un-furto/; https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/7637/Propriet%C3%A0%2BNon%2B%C3%A8%2BPi%C3%B9%2BUn%2BFurto%2C%2BLa; https://b2b.forcedexposure.com/assets/weekly_updates/store_170220.htm; https://www.soundtrack.net/album/la-proprieta-non-e-piu-un-furto-remastered/.

## 4. Il mio nome è Nessuno — 1973

**Original record:** **ten-track** General Music soundtrack LP, documented in 1973 European pressings (including German gatefold release). In later digital/remastered editions there are **23 tracks**; the descriptor concerns the first ten. SoundtrackCollector, Discogs and album sleeve metadata confirm the original programme; AMS provides musical analysis distinguishing the film from straightforward 1960s Westerns.

**Genres and character.** The source itself places the score in the **spaghetti-western** tradition but notes overtly comic **playful** themes next to epic **symphonic** writing (`classical` scoped to some tracks). A secondary soundtrack entry explicitly lists **Easy Listening** (`easylistening`, texture only; weakest genre evidence). The music has alternating suspense, action and levity: mood 3, energy 3, theatrical 4, tempo 3. Synth is credited among largely acoustic/orchestral resources; electronic 1 and experimental 2 are proportionate, polish unknown. No club groove is documented.

**Instrument/session evidence.** Original-era label/sleeve credit transcriptions identify **guitars (including electric guitar), trumpet, synthesizer (Giorgio Carnini), harmonica, flute, piano, keyboards, percussion**, plus **whistling (Alessandro Alessandroni)**, ensemble/chorus and the vocalists **Edda Dell’Orso and Franco Cosacchi**. The expanded Screen Trax archival notes additionally identify **accordion (Wolmer Beltrami)** and recording at **Orthophonic, Rome, November 1973**, directed by Bruno Nicolai. As this evidence refers to the same score recording sessions, these basic instruments are retained; no uncredited individual machine model or instrument model is guessed.

**Voices/production.** Primarily instrumental/orchestral cues include wordless choir and vocal solos; the archival notes name **Franco Cosacchi as falsetto vocalist**, so `falsetto` is supported as technique. Lyrics and singing language are blank because the cited sources do not document sung words. Choir and whistles are credited musicianship, not proof of double-tracking or sampling.

**Quotation versus influence.** *Più delle valchirie* references Wagner's *Ride of the Valkyries*, an overt quotation/parody within the soundtrack. Do **not** add Richard Wagner to `influenced_by` (the user's rule expressly separates quotation/cover from a documented influence). The screen soundtrack’s composer/arranger is Ennio Morricone; **Bruno Nicolai (conductor), Giancarlo Iannucci (recording), Francesco Miracle (artistic direction), and Gianni Dell'Orso (executive producer on later CD)** are not verified album producers. Consequently `producers` is blank.

Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1025912-Ennio-Morricone-Mein-Name-Ist-Nobody-Il-Mio-Nome-E-Nessuno-Original-Film-Soundtrack; https://www.musik-sammler.de/release/ennio-morricone-mein-name-ist-nobody-lp-657050/; https://www.45cat.com/cdalbum/cd/cdst330; https://ams-music.it/wp/il-mio-nome-e-nessuno-3/; https://musicbrainz.org/work/7ddcd0cd-6978-45e5-a89b-5f4425804a41.

## Specialist disagreements / exclusions

1. The scholarly discussion of tape-cutting on *Chi l’ha vista morire?* examines the **film montage**, not a verified 1972 LP production credit; keep the album's `production` empty.
2. *Revolver* has a quoted and varied 12-minute cue on the 1973 LP; modern guitar/synth bonus versions are not assumed to be the 1973 LP's instrumentation.
3. For *La proprietà...*, 'electronically explored' does not establish the exact machine; `instruments` stops at orchestra.
4. *Nessuno*: soundtrack **quotes** a Wagner melody, which is not a source for album influence. Same caution for artist credits: composer, conductor, arranger, artistic director, recording engineer and executive producer are distinct roles.
5. KQEK's review appears to misidentify the director of *Chi l’ha vista morire?* as Antonio Bido; the film is Aldo Lado’s, as the Morricone Foundation and scholarly references confirm. KQEK is cited only for its detailed listening descriptions, not directorial credits.
6. Listening-based genre similarity values and 0–5 intensities are assessments derived from the cited descriptions rather than laboratory measurements. `confidence` describes the whole row, and individual genres carry scope and caveats.
