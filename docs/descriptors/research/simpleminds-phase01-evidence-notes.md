# Simple Minds — Phase 01: evidence notes

Research 28 October 2026. The CSV numbers (0–5) represent independent editorial judgments anchored to Fandome's guide, not numeric claims by sources. Confidence applies to the overall evidence base. Each genre is separately scoped in `genre_evidence.csv`; do not infer that each is equally prominent. All titles use the original UK album rather than bonus-track reissues. 

## Life in a Day (1979) — `simpleminds-lifeinaday`
- **Genres and sound:** AllMusic tags new wave, post-punk, dance-rock, punk/new wave and retrospective alternative/indie rock. Trouser Press adds artful pop, some psychedelic colouring and orchestration, with *Chelsea Girl* more conventional than the band's later work. `pop` and `psychedelia` therefore go last as partial textures, not flagship identities.
- **Instrumentation:** Official Simple Minds credits Burchill on guitar and violin, Forbes bass, McGee drums/percussion, MacNeil keyboards. MusicBrainz confirms separate strings on *Pleasantly Disturbed*, with Haydn Bendall string arranger. `stringarrangements` is justified by these specific credits; Bendall is **not** added as producer. The band's description and the Vassar essay support synth timbre on the title track, not a named model.
- **Producer:** John Leckie only, per official sleeves. Arrangement credit to band/Leckie is not an additional producer credit.
- **Vocals, scores, lyrics:** Early, somewhat nasal/scratchy Kerr singing; energetic but not yet stadium-scale. Emotional colour tends tense and introspective. Vassar reading of the lyrics supports alienation/identity and human connection; there is no generalized party theme.
- **Influences:** Roxy Music, Bowie and Magazine comparisons belong to `sounds_like`. Although Roxy Music is a well attested band-wide influence, no specific member first-person attribution *to this debut* was found, thus `influenced_by` remains blank.

## Real to Real Cacophony (1979) — `simpleminds-realtorealcacophony`
- **Title:** Official website explains that *Real to Real Cacophony* is correct. *Reel to Real* is a mistake introduced on certain later CD reissues and in some critical sources. Preserve the original title and ID.
- **Genres:** The SHFL page actually tags post-punk, new wave, art rock and synthpop; AllMusic adds dance-rock; Pitchfork describes electronic textures and exploratory, sometimes dissonant structures. The SHFL critic hears white funk on *Changeling* (last in genres, one track). `experimental` is warranted by Pitchfork's descriptions of soundscape experimentation.
- **Instruments:** Official 1979 credits name Burchill guitar, violin and saxophone; MacNeil keys; Forbes bass; McGee drums and percussion. Pitchfork names synthesizer passages. No identifiable make or model is verified for the record.
- **Production:** John Leckie's quoted account directly describes miking a corridor to capture a short natural reverb for drums, guitars and handclaps. `roomambience` is used rather than `heavyreverb`, `springreverb` or a brand token. MusicBrainz explicitly credits Leckie producer/mixer, though the official album page says only recorded and mixed. `John Leckie` is therefore safe.
- **Lyrics:** The Vassar analytical essay identifies geopolitical constraint in *Citizen (Dance of Youth)* and a travel narrative in *Scar*. `political`, `social` and `storytelling` describe those tracks; darker emotional score responds to foreboding, not a claim of uniformly slow music.
- **Influences:** Kerr directly acknowledges the Bowie/Roxy/Kraftwerk/Eno musical axis in a 2004 interview; the discussion expressly refers to *Real to Real*-era club play and what was in their sound. Unlike reviewers' analogies (Wire, OMD, Can), these are artist-stated. Somewhat broad temporal attribution: Claude may keep them only if applying strict album-specific provenance with the interview's contextual sentence.

## Empires and Dance (1980) — `simpleminds-empiresanddance`
- **Genre distinctions:** AllMusic calls it post-punk dance-rock and more precisely minimalist Euro-disco in the band biography. Other sources document electronic, art-rock and new-wave character. The Krautrock tag is **textural**, due to clearly documented Neu!/Kraftwerk motorik influence, not a claim that this Scottish group is literally a German Krautrock act. Funk occurs in a subset of bass grooves.
- **Original instrumentation:** Official 1980 LP credits Burchill guitar/sax, MacNeil keyboards, Forbes bass, McGee drums/percussion. Pitchfork identifies clearly electronic synth parts; Vassar discusses keyboards, organ and arpeggiated figures. The *Twist/Run/Repulsion* French spoken voice is explicitly reported by the Guardian; it is an isolated passage, and album language remains `en` (principal sung language).
- **Producer:** Leckie is identified as album producer in Guardian 2023 and common discographies, whereas original sleeve reproduces narrower 'Recorded by Leckie' and 'Engineered by Jones'. Hugh Jones must not be added as producer on that basis.
- **Influences:** Band's official history expressly attributes the industrial rhythmic model to **Neu!**, **Kraftwerk** and **Cabaret Voltaire** on *this* record. The Guardian interviews band members discussing club listening to **Chic**, **Donna Summer**, **Kraftwerk**, **Michael Rother** and **Grace Jones** while working toward this record; include those named as formative listening, not as featured collaborators. Michael Rother is not listed separately because Neu! covers his direct project in the official attribution and the nuance is preserved in source note. No producer collaboration with Giorgio Moroder exists: AllMusic merely makes a stylistic analogy.
- **Lyrical context:** Contemporary European travel and political tension are directly documented by Kerr/Leckie and in the Vassar account. The low mood represents the austere atmosphere, while energy and groove reflect rhythmic drive.
- **Production technique:** No technical claim about a specific machine, hardware sequencer, drum machine or sampler is made; therefore the `production` field is intentionally empty.

## Sons and Fascination (1981) — `simpleminds-sonsandfascination`
- **Split vs double:** Sons and Fascination was an independent eight-song original 1981 LP. Sister Feelings Call is a separate seven-track LP from the same sessions, sometimes bundled with it. *Theme for Great Cities*, *The American* and *20th Century Promised Land* belong to the original **Sister Feelings Call** LP, despite different national track rearrangements and the error in the official webpage's second 'original Sons' listing. Original French and German V2207 pressings and the 2002 two-album CD order document eight Sons titles: In Trance as Mission; Sweat in Bullet; 70 Cities as Love Brings the Fall; Boys from Brazil; Love Song; This Earth That You Walk Upon; Sons and Fascination; Seeing Out the Angel.
- **Genres:** AllMusic lists new wave, post-punk, dance-rock, alternative/indie rock, punk/new wave; 1981 original French LP is classified new wave and synthpop. Trouser Press identifies rhythmic funk, electronic ambience and a more comfortable dance groove. `artrock` is a smaller textural marker because of long-form atmospheric construction.
- **Instruments:** Official band page credits Burchill guitar, Forbes bass, McGee drums, MacNeil keyboards. Musicological essay identifies synthesizer and organ figures on the 1981 material. No credited separate drum-machine hardware is established. `synth` and `organ` are types not brands. Steve Hillage alone is credited producer; Hugh Jones and Alan Jakoby are engineers.
- **Lyrics and voice:** *Love Song* and other original Sons tracks introduce more direct emotional/social writing alongside dense, abstract images. Bass-led dynamics and Kerr's rising projection merit high dance/groove and moderate theatricality, not full later arena-rock theatricality.
- **Artist influences:** No specific first-person account establishes an album-level influence for the eight Sons recordings, so leave `influenced_by` empty. Roxy Music, Japan, Ultravox and OMD are **editorial listening comparisons only**, to be treated conservatively by Claude.

## Shared cautions
- `genres` includes music-style taxonomies from AllMusic and carefully scoped texture/track evidence; album-wide evidence should carry more weight than a one-track tag.
- `sounds_like` is editorial similarity, *not* documentary artist influence. Ignore incidental press comparisons to future bands as formative influence.
- No live or compilation recordings and no pre-1979 music from the earlier group Johnny & The Self-Abusers are described.
- The catalog audit flags *Our Secrets Are the Same* by first public commercial availability (2004), even though recorded around 1999. Its position among later albums should be verified when reached. *Neon Lights* and *Acoustic* are studio cover/re-recordings to review separately.
