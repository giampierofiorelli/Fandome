# INXS / Phase 02 — evidence notes and disagreements

Research cutoff 28 October 2026. Rows are analytically scored draft estimates, not quotes from critics; numerical 0–5 values use Fandome scale anchors. Instrument and production tokens require **evidence for the specific album**. The sources CSV contains the traceable URLs.

## 1. `inxs-listenlikethieves` — *Listen Like Thieves* (1985)

**Genre ordering.** The primary live-based groove couples funk to rock, with Pop Rock / Funk Rock and New Wave explicitly tagged by Album of the Year and Dance-Rock by AllMusic; Pitchfork argues this record shifted the band toward stadium-scale pop-rock. AllMusic also tags Rock & Roll, Album Rock, Punk/New Wave, Alternative/Indie Rock, College Rock, and Contemporary Pop/Rock, moved to the rear as taxonomy rather than dominating sound. Classic Rock Review directly notes rhythm-and-blues flavour on 'Kiss the Dirt', making `rnb` a limited track flavour. More guitars and fuller ensemble dynamics separate this record from *The Swing*. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/inxs-listen-like-thieves/) · [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/18339-inxs-listen-like-thieves.php) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r9885) · [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2015/08/1985-inxs-listen-like-thieves/).

**Sounds and instruments.** Original six-piece album credits and song review support multiple guitars, bass guitar, acoustic guitar (This Time), drums, percussion, keyboards and sax. The 'What You Need' review specifically describes a funky **synth bass** as well as sax and interlocking guitar, and 'Shine Like It Does' has orchestral-sounding **synthesizers**. We do not assert real orchestra, string section or any specific keyboard model from these descriptions. [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2015/08/1985-inxs-listen-like-thieves/).

**Production.** Chris Thomas is the credited producer, not engineer David Nicholas. Andrew Farriss and Kirk Pengilly recall that Thomas arranged *full-group* live-style performance with arena monitoring and deliberate leakage in Rhinoceros Studios. This supports `liverecording` and `roomambience` as in-studio techniques. Synth bass supports presence of synthesis; documentation of patch programming is insufficient to assert a production token. No make/model asserted. [Classic Pop interview](https://www.classicpopmag.com/interviews/inxs-listen-like-thieves-interview/) · [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/andrew-farriss-discusses-35th-anniversary-of-inxs-breakthrough-listen-like-thieves/).

**Scales (4,2,4,3,2,3,4,4).** Driving guitars and forceful drums, some but subordinate synths, generally upbeat hooks, expressive yet band-centred drama, straightforward songwriting, solid 1985 studio finish and dance-friendly rhythms. The difference from polished *Kick* is material. `voice` raspy/clear/powerful are successive rough, clean and projected Hutchence deliveries. Lyrical love/storytelling and introspective treatment are evidenced by the critic's song readings, not generalized sexual assumptions. [Classic Rock Review](https://www.classicrockreview.com/2015/08/1985-inxs-listen-like-thieves/).

**Comparisons vs influences.** Critics liken tracks to **U2** and **Roxy Music**; these are in `sounds_like` only. `influenced_by` blank without source for specifically motivating this recording. `landmark=1`: pivotal chart and production shift independently discussed by Pitchfork. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/inxs-listen-like-thieves/).

## 2. `inxs-kick` — *Kick* (1987)

**Genre ordering.** Core Pop Rock + Funk Rock + Dance-Rock is supported in contemporary assessments, Farriss’s direct account and current album metadata; New Wave persists but is not the lead genre. AllMusic's Album Rock, Alternative Pop/Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock, College Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock remain lower-priority. Album of the Year `bigmusic` is a descriptive class of anthem scale, not a claim of a specific influence. *The Loved One* explicitly receives a blues-rock reading from the band itself; per-track genre only. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/18340-inxs-kick.php) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/r9886) · [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/andrew-farriss-talks-inxs-kick-album-track-by-track-566768) · [Classic Pop](https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/classic-album/classic-album-inxs-kick/).

**Instruments.** Original studio band has guitars, Beers's bass, Jon Farriss's drums/percussion, Andrew's keyboards, Pengilly sax. On 'Need You Tonight' Farriss confirms a Roland TR-707 drum machine, Emulator II-generated bass timbre and guitar, supplemented by live rhythm section and rototoms. 'Never Tear Us Apart' prominently uses **synthesized / sampled string timbres on keyboard**, according to Andrew and Tim. The acoustic instrument token `strings` is excluded because the recorded timbre was apparently generated on keyboards. Saxophone and keyboards are clear on multiple tracks; no assertion of external brass section. [Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-track-inxs-need-you-tonight-367581) · [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/andrew-farriss-talks-inxs-kick-album-track-by-track-566768) · [Apple Music Kick Deluxe](https://music.apple.com/us/album/kick-30th-deluxe-edition/1313408906).

**Production.** Both the recording engineer and original producer interviews describe a **digital multitrack / analog Studer backup and analog drum elements** (Farriss recalls digital plus analog in the same sessions). Therefore both `digitalrecording` and `analogtape` are supported. The two precise devices `tr707` and `emulatorii` are specifically tied to this album, as are beat programming, sampled keyboard-bass, overdubs and stereo vocal phase effects. Do not claim the whole album was fully electronic or a rigid drum-machine recording; instrumental tracking was largely live. Chris Thomas **produced**; Bob Clearmountain **mixed** and is not a producer. [Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-track-inxs-need-you-tonight-367581) · [Chris Thomas liner interview](https://albumlinernotes.com/Kick.html).

**Scales (4,3,4,4,2,5,4,5).** Punchy funk and rock plus lean synthesized rhythm sound, generally euphoric choruses contrasted with sensual/melancholic ballad, theatrical vocal phrasing, polished yet not sonically avant-garde, dancefloor strength. Spoken voice type reflects 'Mediate' rhythmically recited words; no claim that the entire LP is spoken. The lyrics include sensuality (Need You Tonight), love (Never Tear Us Apart) and social/political comments on select songs (Guns in the Sky and Mediate). [LA Times 1987](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-10-25-ca-16190-story.html) · [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/andrew-farriss-talks-inxs-kick-album-track-by-track-566768).

**Influences and comparison:** Journalists compare the band's wider history to Stones-like bar-band rock, but the comparison is not sufficiently specific to Kick, so both `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` are blank. The Loved Ones *cover* is excluded as an influence. `landmark=1` for international breakthrough and distinctive sound. Date 12 vs 19 October 1987 does not affect year. The official band site inadvertently truncates one song; original LP includes Tiny Daggers.

## 3. `inxs-x` — *X* (1990)

**Genres.** AOTY primary Pop Rock and tags Sophisti-Pop and Big Music; AllMusic explicitly lists Dance-Rock, College Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock; the album's published genre summary also identifies Alternative Rock. Despite increased stadium ambition, the group mostly continues the *Kick* funk-inflected dance groove. No evidence sufficient for a separate named electronic/acidhouse genre across the whole record, even if a single's rhythm has later been compared to acid house. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/x-mw0000316019) · [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/x/828782374) · [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/18341-inxs-x.php).

**Instruments.** AllMusic credit list gives band electric guitar, keys, bass, drums, sax and guest Charlie Musselwhite's harmonica; some original performer lists additionally describe Jon Farriss and Pengilly as contributing programming. 'Suicide Blonde' opens with *sampled* Musselwhite harmonica, distinct from actual session harp on 'Who Pays the Price' and 'On My Way'. Therefore instrument harmonica + production `sampling` are both justified; do not assume it was simply performed live for the single. MusicBrainz original release track credits directly name Jon Farriss and Kirk Pengilly for `programming`; no device model is verified. The album does not have a verified specific drum machine or sampler brand. [AllMusic credits](https://www.allmusic.com/album/x-mw0000316019) · [MusicBrainz original credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/f4079efa-81ba-4c1f-8222-a20e8764112e) · [musician account](https://www.harpsurgery.com/charlie-musselwhite-part-i-komedia-brighton-18th-november-2008/) · [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/x/828782374).

**Production and voice.** Chris Thomas is third consecutive album's credited producer. Dance and sample manipulations support `sampling|programming`, with the latter directly documented in original track credits by MusicBrainz. `voice` rough/sensual chest voice, clear melodic songs and projected choruses; leave any pitch processors blank. Musical scale (4,3,3,3,2,4,4,4) denotes a polished but less experimental continuation of the previous sound, with ballads and darker moments. Lyrical heartbreak and social concerns are lower-confidence than sensual love, and should be checked against full lyric context. [Official INXS](https://www.inxs.com/music/x/) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/x-mw0000316019).

**Sources disagreement:** AOTY's `sophistipop|bigmusic` may over-classify an essentially guitar-forward dance-rock record; retained at lower weight under instruction to retain sourced album genre tags. No album-specific cited inspiration from any artist: `influenced_by` blank. `landmark=0` because it is a successful extension of *Kick* rather than a major recorded-sound pivot.

## 4. `inxs-welcometowhereveryouare` — *Welcome to Wherever You Are* (1992)

**Genres.** Sources describe a considerably rawer alternative rock presentation and reduced overt dance-rock; general Pop Rock, Alternative/Indie Rock, Album Rock, Contemporary Pop/Rock and College Rock style metadata remain valid. **Orchestral rock** is directly named by contemporary Los Angeles Times criticism (new token `orchestralrock`, several songs). 'Not Enough Time' is explicitly termed pop-soul by *The Independent* (`popsoul` some tracks). LA Times describes rock blended with funk (`funkrock`, some tracks), plus relatively pure pop on two songs. This is **not** primarily grunge: Apple describes how the album responded to an industry context, not that INXS became a grunge band. [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-08-09-ca-6018-story.html) · [Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/rock-records-going-public-with-the-personal-andy-gill-listens-to-inxs-discovering-the-beatles-and-sophie-b-hawkins-herself-1538425.html) · [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/welcome-to-wherever-you-are-mw0000083089).

**Instruments.** Standard guitars, bass, drums, Pengilly sax, Farriss keyboards; *official group* specifies a **sitar** and **60-piece Australian Concert Orchestra** on album. Original pressings: orchestra on 'Baby Don’t Cry' and 'Men and Women' only, plus Sunil de Silva guest percussion on 'Not Enough Time', and Deni Hines backing vocals on 'Not Enough Time' and 'Strange Desire'. `orchestra` is the verified token; no invented individual strings / woodwinds / brass credits or specific sitar brand. [INXS shop](https://aushop.inxs.com/products/welcome-to-wherever-you-are-limited-edition-cherry-red-lp) · [original edition credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/inxs/welcome-to-wherever-you-are-album_frcung.html).

**Producers / techniques.** Both **Mark Opitz and INXS** are album producers according to original pressings. Bob Clearmountain is mixer only. The record is described as using **distorted guitar** and special treated vocals; the critic notes reversed guitars on 'Not Enough Time' but does not prove a specific piece of reversal hardware; we conservatively include `sonicmanipulation`, not `backmasking` or an invented device. `stringarrangements` comes from credit for orchestra arrangers Mick Kenny and Andrew Farriss. These are **arrangers**, not producer credits. An orchestra tracked live **in a studio** does not make this a concert/live album. [Official INXS](https://www.inxs.com/music/welcome-to-wherever-you-are/) · [original pressing credits](https://www.chartsurfer.de/artist/inxs/welcome-to-wherever-you-are-album_frcung.html) · [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-08-09-ca-6018-story.html).

**Scales (3,2,2,4,4,3,3,3).** Compared with *Kick* and *X*, variable intensity, more raw guitars, occasional synthetic / psychedelic textures, several dramatic orchestral sections and a mood often more serious and inward; experimental score higher because unusual Eastern-like sounds, reverse guitars, vocal alterations and orchestra. Some tracks remain highly danceable, so aggregate groove moderate. 1992 reviews disagree over quality: *The Independent* strongly values the style variety; *LA Times* finds it somewhat unfocused. Scales reflect contrasting textures, not consensus on artistic quality. [Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/rock-records-going-public-with-the-personal-andy-gill-listens-to-inxs-discovering-the-beatles-and-sophie-b-hawkins-herself-1538425.html) · [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-08-09-ca-6018-story.html).

**Lyrics / comparisons / influence.** Farriss himself says Beautiful Girl began with concern for his new daughter Grace, supporting `love|introspection`; sensuality is discussed in 1992 reviews, and 'Men and Women' considers gender tensions. Beatles psychedelic, Roxy Music *(Avalon)*, the Saints *(All Fools Day)* and Pink Floyd *(The Wall)* are **contemporary critics' comparisons**, not artist-acknowledged album influences. The Saints track/song context was the album *All Fools Day*, recorded by the Saints. In `sounds_like`, named albums in brackets follow Fandome rules; no such artist belongs in `influenced_by`. [Andrew Farriss interview](https://www.songwritingmagazine.co.uk/interviews/beautiful-girl-inxs) · [Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/rock-records-going-public-with-the-personal-andy-gill-listens-to-inxs-discovering-the-beatles-and-sophie-b-hawkins-herself-1538425.html) · [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-08-09-ca-6018-story.html).

**Caveat:** `landmark=0` because although historically adventurous and critically admired in part, no strong claim of cultural-landmark status is made by sources. `confidence=high` for primary sound/credits, but genre taxonomy and scoring remain provisional. *Welcome* 3 Aug 1992 per band, 4 Aug 1992 per Apple; no disagreement over year.
