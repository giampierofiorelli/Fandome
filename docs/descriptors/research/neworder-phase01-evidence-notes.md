# New Order — Phase 01 evidence notes (original studio LPs only)

Research cutoff: 26 October 2026. Values 0–5 are **Fandome editorial assessments**, not numbers quoted from reviewers; source passages support the assessment, not an exact score. Genre evidence is per album, and genres of a single song are placed late. Broad pop/rock genre-family tags sit last, after specific styles. "College Rock" is an editorial style used by AllMusic (mostly a broadcasting context), not a claim about a specific synthesizer or recording practice.

## Movement (1981) — `neworder-movement`
- **Sonic basis and scores:** AllMusic and Pitchfork describe a stark post-punk record in the immediate wake of Joy Division. The original album emphasizes live drums, Hook's bass and interlocking guitars, with some electronic programming. Energy 3; electronic 2; mood 1; theatrical 1; experimental 3; polish 2; tempo 3; groove 1. The darker mood is much more reliable than a specific BPM estimate.
- **Voice:** Sumner's reserved, often understated singing (`soft`); Peter Hook sings on "Dreams Never End" and "Doubts Even Here"; Gilbert contributes spoken vocals to "Doubts Even Here" (`spoken`). No unsupported falsetto or pitch processing.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz original eight-song credits document guitars, bass guitar, drums, synthesizers, Sumner's melodica and programming. `electricguitar` represents recorded electric guitar arrangements; `keyboards` is attested as Gilbert’s general instrument role in contemporary accounts; `synth` comes from exact track credits. No named synthesizer model is attributed to this album.
- **Production:** `programming` is directly credited; **Martin Hannett** is the sole producer. Chris Nagle is engineer, Flood and John Leckie assistants, NOT producers. Hannett's gloomy studio treatment does not by itself establish `heavyreverb` or `analogtape`.
- **Genres:** AOTY post-punk, Gothic Rock and Coldwave tags; AllMusic synth-pop classification refers to transitional elements, not a dance-pop album. Ned Raggett at SHFL explicitly labels the album New Wave and Rock, so these tokens have named support.
- **Lyrics and references:** `introspection|identity` is a conservative interpretation of the transition-period themes, not an inference that Ian Curtis wrote these songs. Pitchfork draws a direct sonic line to Joy Division; `influenced_by=Joy Division` is critical attribution of predecessor-band sound, **not** an outsider collaboration.
- **Disagreement:** At release it appeared hesitant and too close to Joy Division; Tom Ewing later championed its coherence. Landmark=1 refers to historical significance as New Order's debut, not its sales or contemporary acclaim.

## Power, Corruption & Lies (1983) — `neworder-powercorruptionlies`
- **Sonic basis and scores:** Rhino, Pitchfork and Qobuz discuss both sequenced electronic tracks and guitar/bass-led songs, including "Age of Consent" and "Leave Me Alone." Energy 3; electronic 3; mood 3; theatrical 2; experimental 3; polish 3; tempo 3; groove 3.
- **Instrumentation:** Qobuz original LP credits show guitar, synthesizer, melodica, bass guitar, drums, percussion and programming. Synthesizer sequences and drum machines are corroborated by contemporary member interviews in Classic Pop and The Quietus. Liner notes at Cerysmatic Factory place the Emulator, Oberheim DMX and Prophet-5 in the original album sessions, beyond their better-known role on the standalone single "Blue Monday"; Apple Music also discusses the LinnDrum in this album context. `alphasynthauri` is specifically named in the album interview with Morris (new token).
- **Production:** Producer credited simply **New Order**, rather than listing its four members as four independent producers. Michael Johnson engineered; he was not credited as producer. `emulator` means E-mu Emulator (not `emulatorii`). `oberheimdmx`, `prophet5` and `linndrum` are exact documented machines, not generic substitutes. `programming|drumprogramming` follow the band and track credits. No Fairlight: Gilbert explicitly says it was unaffordable.
- **Genres:** Album-wide synthpop, postpunk, newwave, alternativedance; genre names `electropop` ("5 8 6") and `coldwave` ("Your Silent Face") are documented by Qobuz **at track scope** and therefore last. AllMusic's `collegerock` is a new token. The exact AllMusic `Alternative/Indie Rock` goes to `alternativeindierock`.
- **Influences:** Marc Zisman (Qobuz professional album commentary) explicitly identifies Kraftwerk and Giorgio Moroder as musical influences on **this album**. Both are therefore in `influenced_by`, not merely `sounds_like`. Do **not** add Donna Summer just because a separate single drew on "Our Love".
- **Edition caveat:** "Blue Monday", "Temptation" and "Everything's Gone Green" are **not** on the original eight-track LP. Their rhythms, samples and influences cannot independently support this row. Later bonus tracks must not leak into album descriptors.

## Low-Life (1985) — `neworder-lowlife`
- **Scores:** AllMusic explicitly describes seamless fusion of rock and heavily sequenced electronic music. Energy 3; electronic 3; mood 2 (intimate/melancholy but with bright tracks); theatrical 2; experimental 3; polish 3; tempo 3; groove 3. The album mixes storytelling ("Love Vigilantes"), introspection, club songs and the elegiac instrumental "Elegia".
- **Instruments:** Qobuz original track credits confirm guitars, bass, synths, drums and programming. Hi-Fi News names a **Voyetra-8** synthesizer, used by Gillian Gilbert (`voyetra8`, new production model token). It also describes an E-mu Emulator and the sampled frog sound in "The Perfect Kiss". Sputnikmusic notes harmonica on "Love Vigilantes" (scope one track). Sequencer and drum machine arrangements are backed by AllMusic and album reviews; `sampler` is a functional instrument, `sampling` is the process.
- **Production:** New Order credited. **Michael Johnson is an engineer**, not a named producer. `emulator` is the early Emulator referred to by members, not automatically the later Emulator II. `voyetra8` and `emulator` are model-specific, not generic synth inference. Include `programming|drumprogramming|sampling`; no documentary basis to assign a particular DAW.
- **Genres:** AOTY synthpop, alternativedance, newwave, postpunk. AllMusic includes dancerock, alternativepoprock and **College Rock**; its Pop/Rock umbrella supports last-position rock. `dancepop` is explicitly listed by critic Ned Raggett at SHFL; it is a secondary style behind synth-pop/alternative dance.
- **Influences:** The sources establish an existing stylistic lineage, not a verified named **album-specific** musical influence. Keep `influenced_by` empty, even though Kraftwerk shaped the band's work generally.
- **Edition caveat:** The 1985 original is eight tracks. Remixes, outtakes, live performances and bonus alternate takes on later definitive editions are excluded.

## Brotherhood (1986) — `neworder-brotherhood`
- **Scores:** Peter Hook directly confirms that side A was developed as an electric guitar band and side B chiefly by Bernard Sumner using sequencers. Energy 3; electronic 3; mood 3 (both bitter and exuberant moments); theatrical 2; experimental 2; polish 3; tempo 3; groove 3. *Bizarre Love Triangle* does not alone determine groove/electronic ratings for the entire album.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz nine-track original LP credits show guitars, bass guitar, synthesizer, drums and programming for the band; Hook's 2026 interview explicitly confirms sequencers. `drummachine` is supported by the sound/1986 album notes but its exact make/model on Brotherhood is not conclusively established. Do not copy Low-Life's Voyetra-8 merely because the instrument was in the band inventory over this period.
- **Production:** Written, recorded and produced by **New Order** according to the band's official site/Rhino; engineer Michael Johnson is not a co-producer. `programming|drumprogramming` supported by Qobuz programming credits and described electronic side. No model-specific drum machine is assigned.
- **Genres:** AOTY gives Synthpop, Alternative Dance, New Wave, Post-Punk, Jangle Pop; jangle/post-punk predominantly on the rock side. AllMusic adds Dance-Rock, Alternative Pop/Rock and College Rock. `rock` is a broad umbrella last, not a replacement for postpunk.
- **Lyrics:** Romantic ambiguity, introspection and irony supported by song readings; the ending "Every Little Counts" motivates the low-precedence irony tag. This is an editorial, not a songwriter quote.
- **Disagreement and edition caveats:** Hook and Apple Music stress the strict guitar/electronic LP split. Retrospective views diverge on whether the division is a strength. The first LP has **nine tracks**; "State of the Nation" appears on the original CD and other later editions, not on the original vinyl sequence. Shep Pettibone's 12-inch single remix of "Bizarre Love Triangle" is excluded (not an original LP track). Do not credit Pettibone as an album producer.

## Method notes
- `sounds_like` is an explicitly editorial sonic comparison, not a claim of influence or borrowing. Only documented per-album influence appears in `influenced_by`.
- A producer is a person/group whose **recording** production is credited, never merely someone who mixed, mastered, engineered, contributed a 12-inch remix or supervised reissues.
- The source file links to original track metadata and journalistic evidence; neither the genre columns nor instrument columns are deduced simply from band identity.
- The exact years and original song lists were cross-checked with label archives. All comparisons should be reviewed as proposals (`status=draft`).
