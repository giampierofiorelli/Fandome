# Chic — Phase 01: evidence notes

All notes reflect the original studio albums, not later deluxe remixes, re-recordings or compilations. Scalar ratings are Fandome editorial judgments anchored to the guide, not measurements. Explanations below separate instrumental credits, the artists' production statements, critical genres and interpretation.

## CHIC (1977) — `chic-chic`
- **Genres:** `disco|funk|soul|rnb` from Alex Henderson's AllMusic description and album styles. `eurodisco` is the specific Euro-disco term used by the critic, placed after the dominant forms; `quietstorm` is AllMusic's retrospective tag and applies to the softer material rather than the two big singles. See genre-evidence CSV.
- **Scales:** energy 4, tempo 4, groove 5: “Dance, Dance, Dance” and “Everybody Dance” anchor the record with long, dance-led rhythms; the slower material prevents an indiscriminate 5 for tempo. electronic 1: Rodgers/Edwards/Thompson live guitar, bass and drums dominate, with electric keys but no verified synth or programmed rhythms. mood 4: euphoric dance material mixed with gentler love songs. theatrical 2: polished ensemble presentation and refrain rather than dramatized leads. experimental 2: unexpected rhythmic treatment but familiar song-based structures. polish 4: purposeful New York studio tracking and fine session arrangements.
- **Voice/lyrics:** smooth, clear mixed voices, some ensemble choruses; Norma Jean Wright and supporting singers appear in the original personnel; themes drawn from the dance songs and romantic selections. `choral` represents ensemble refrains, not a classical choir.
- **Instruments:** bass, guitars, live drums, keyboards, percussion, orchestral strings, saxophones, flute, trumpet, other winds, harp and vibraphone/bells are transcribed from original LP credits reproduced by vocalist David Lasley and Qobuz/Rhino. `mallets` represents David Friedman's vibraphone. `brass` is present alongside trumpet because trombone is specifically credited; not a separately verified horn section on each track.
- **Production:** Nile Rodgers tells Sound on Sound that the original Chic rhythm tracks were played live without click, then overdubbed; 24-track 3M tape was used on the classic recordings. On “Dance, Dance, Dance”, Bob Clearmountain filtered frequencies to obtain a megaphone-like voice, hence `vocalfilter`. On “Everybody Dance” Rodgers' rhythm playing controlled the timing of a Clavinet, supporting `sonicmanipulation`. Both are track-level details: they do not imply these effects run throughout the album. `sampling`, synth models, automated pitch correction and programmed drums are deliberately absent.
- **Producers:** LP credits name Bernard Edwards, Nile Rodgers and Kenny Lehman. Executive producers Marc Kreiner/Tom Cossie are **not** producers here. The wording of some sources does not assign a track-by-track share to Lehman; flag for Claude review, but do not suppress his original record credit.
- **Influences:** Rodgers cited Roxy Music as an inspiration for Chic's identity, artwork and stage concept. No source establishes that the *audio* of this LP directly imitates Roxy Music. `influenced_by` is consequently blank. `sounds_like` names are editorial comparison only, not claimed influences.
- **Landmark/confidence:** debut and two signature dance tracks, 1 / high. **Critical disagreement:** Henderson notes dismissive contemporary rock reviews and later recognition of the sophisticated music.

## C'EST CHIC (1978) — `chic-cestchic`
- **Genres:** `disco|funk|soul` supported by AllMusic, Herb Music and label/credit context; `rnb` the AllMusic parent category; `quietstorm` applied only to smoother songs such as “At Last I Am Free”, and is ranked last. No automatic `house` or `postdisco` just because the album was later influential.
- **Scales:** energy 4, tempo 4 and groove 5: the principal singles “Le Freak” and “I Want Your Love” are extremely danceable but non-dance ballads are present. electronic 1: Rodgers, Edwards, Thompson, acoustic/electric keyboards, live strings and horns are documented; a machine-driven record is not. mood 4: euphoric singles alongside melancholy. theatrical 2: stylish group vocals rather than operatic dramatization. experimental 2: patient instrumental construction; polish 5: highly controlled studio production, overdubbed strings and horn passages.
- **Voice/lyrics:** smooth ensemble voices and romantic songs; “Le Freak” is celebratory dance music with an origin in a frustrated refusal at Studio 54, verified by Rodgers' Fresh Air interview; “At Last I Am Free” is introspective yearning, so mood is not 5.
- **Instruments:** Philharmonie de Paris discographic credits corroborate bass, guitar, drums, acoustic and Fender Rhodes electric piano, keys, orchestral strings, trumpet, saxophone, trombone and percussion. `chimes` denotes Jose Rossy's tubular bells, as supported by credits; this is an already-existing token in `src/data/instruments.ts` even though absent from the user's shorter example list.
- **Production:** Sound on Sound explicitly says foundations tracked in Power Station Studio B and strings overdubbed in Studio A, later horns and lead vocals; Rodgers describes an all-live, no-click rhythm take and 24-track 3M tape. `liverecording|overdubbing|analogtape` do **not** mean a concert album. Do not misread the album as electronically sequenced disco.
- **Producers:** Edwards and Rodgers; some sources list associate producers but they are excluded. **Critics' disagreement:** Birchmeier's AllMusic review sees the debut formula repeated, whereas Andy Kellman (AllMusic Chic/C'est Chic reissue) and David Burke (Classic Pop) value its refinement.
- **Influence policy:** No album-specific sonic antecedent adequately documented, so `influenced_by` empty. `sounds_like` are independent listening comparisons.
- **Date caveat:** AllMusic and CHICtribute display different 1978 day/month release dates, so only the undisputed **year** is committed. The album ID normalizes the apostrophe and diacritic.
- **Landmark/confidence:** includes “Le Freak” / “I Want Your Love”; 1 / high.

## RISQUÉ (1979) — `chic-risque`
- **Genres:** disco, funk, soul and R&B as documented by AllMusic, label/Rhino and Classic Pop. AllMusic also categorizes the ballad material as quiet storm. Lower-ranked `quietstorm` is **some tracks**, not the full dance-oriented album.
- **Scales:** energy 4 and groove 5: “Good Times”, “My Feet Keep Dancing”, “My Forbidden Lover” central. tempo 4: club music balanced against slow songs. electronic 1: credited live band, keys and strings, no record-specific drum machines documented. mood 3: energetic surfaces but poignant lyrics and ballads. theatrical 2: smooth yet dramatic ballad moments. experimental 2: complex interplay within pop/dance structures; polish 5: meticulous orchestral disco studio sound.
- **Lyrics:** “Good Times” reworks Depression-era optimism as commentary on economic conditions (Rodgers as quoted by Classic Pop). Add `social|irony` after more prevalent celebratory and romantic themes; heartbreak supported by “A Warm Summer Night” and “Will You Cry”.
- **Instruments:** Qobuz/Atlantic credits and SoulAndFunkMusic list Bernard Edwards bass, Nile Rodgers guitar, Tony Thompson drums, keyboardists, percussionists and session strings/horns (trumpet, saxophone, trombone). `brass` includes trombone, separate from `trumpet` token. No named drum machine or plug-in evidence.
- **Production:** left blank. Rodgers' classic-era live rhythm/overdub/tape descriptions in Sound on Sound concern the group generally, not a verified session report for this particular album. `sampling` is **not** assigned merely because later hip-hop records re-used the “Good Times” bass groove.
- **Producers:** Edwards and Rodgers in licensed liner-style credits. "Good Times" is an original Chic composition, not a Sugarhill Gang production.
- **Critics' disagreement:** Critics celebrate its disco legacy, yet David Burke stresses its darker social meaning; both coexist. 1 / high confidence.

## REAL PEOPLE (1980) — `chic-realpeople`
- **Genres:** `funk|soul` are explicitly prominent in Rhino's account; `disco|rnb` remain confirmed in AllMusic metadata but decline in importance; Rhino identifies new-wave and guitar-rock influences **as texture** rather than wholesale genre conversion. `quietstorm` is a small-scale ballad description. `contemporaryrnb` is AllMusic's exact catalog style, but retrospectively applied to a 1980 LP; its genre evidence includes this caveat and ranks it last.
- **Scales:** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3: lower-key tempos and fewer peak disco anthems, as Theakston states. electronic 1: guitar/bass/drums/keys/strings are credited; no verified synth or drum machine. mood 2: Rhino identifies an unusually melancholy turn; theatrics 2, experiment 2 and polish 4 reflect more varied arrangements yet continued studio discipline.
- **Lyrics:** retrospective label and critical readings emphasize people and substance over flashing dance slogans. Themes `social|introspection|love|heartbreak`; last two reflect romantic tracks and AllMusic song list, but are interpretive, not an exhaustive lyric annotation.
- **Instruments:** original 1980 LP credits and Atlantic/Qobuz tracks verify guitar, bass, live drums, keys, Chic Strings and percussion. Do not carry 1977-79 saxophone/trumpet credits into 1980 without an album source.
- **Production:** left blank because no 1980-session source confirms tape machine, overdub procedure or hardware for this particular album. Nile Rodgers' general description of the Chic classic-era process is recorded in sources but deliberately not encoded as a record-specific production fact.
- **Producers:** Bernard Edwards and Nile Rodgers in LP, AllMusic, and Qobuz credits.
- **Critics' disagreement:** Rhino values the adventurous funk/soul/rock blend; Alex Henderson (AllMusic) considers it pleasant but less significant than the 1970s peak. Both assessments are preserved. Landmark 0, confidence medium.

## Cross-album caveats
- All `sounds_like` entries are editorial resemblance judgments, not claimed influences or chronological causal relationships. All four `influenced_by` values are intentionally blank because documented Roxy Music and KISS references chiefly relate to image / branding, not verifiable sonic influence of a specific record.
- Credits are largely album-wide. Nile Rodgers has explicitly said that the organization intentionally avoided naming which musician played on each song; do not pretend album personnel necessarily performed on every song.
- Original full-length LPs only. No CD bonus mixes, digital edits, or later remasters are treated as independent genres/works.
- **Production scope caution:** source supports track-level `vocalfilter` and `sonicmanipulation` on the debut. Technical claims are not transferred automatically to *Risqué* and *Real People*; their `production` cells are intentionally blank without specific session evidence.
