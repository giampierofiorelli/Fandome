# Lene Marlin — phase 01, evidence and editorial scoring

Scope: Four original studio albums (1999, 2003, 2005, 2009). Release and producer data are drawn from labelled original masters, original CD credits, and contemporary criticism; review comparisons are not artist-declared influences. Five or six sourced genres per album, ranked by predominance and source scope. No compilation reissues. Scalar anchors are 0–5 (see Fandome guide). See the companion `sources.csv` and `genre_evidence.csv` for full URLs and more granular evidence.

## 1. Playing My Game (1999) — `lenemarlin-playingmygame`

* **Edition:** original ten tracks; contemporary March announcements but Norwegian Virgin 27 April 1999 CD event confirmed by MusicBrainz. Extended regional releases contain acoustic/live/remix bonuses not reflected in descriptor. The core album includes *Sitting Down Here*, *Unforgivable Sinner*, *Where I'm Headed*, *A Place Nearby*.
* **Sound / ranked genres:** `acousticpop|poprock|softrock|pop|ballad`. Pop Rescue hears delicate acoustic guitar and bass in *Sitting Down Here*, soft synthesizer colours on the title ballad, guitar urgency on *Unforgivable Sinner*, and piano at *A Place Nearby*. A European original CD is explicitly styled Soft Rock and Pop Rock; Qobuz lists Pop. Pressing-specific style labels should not be confused with remixed versions.
* **Instrumentation and production:** Qobuz and original CD identify Jørn Dahl (piano, keyboards, programming), Bernt Rune Stray and other guitars, Wallen Mjäland (bass, tambourine), Karl Oluf Wennerberg (hi-hat/cymbals/tambourine) and Lene on one guitar track. These justify `acousticguitar|guitar|piano|keyboards|bass|tambourine|percussion`, `programming`. No credited specific sampler/synth/drum-machine model was located; do not invent one. Label production credits are `Jørn Dahl|Hans Olav Grøttheim` (aka Hans G). Neither credited engineer nor mixer is a third producer.
* **Scales:** energy 3 (driving hits balanced with gentle songs); electronic 2 (programmed keys coexist with substantial guitars); mood 2 (reflection and longing more than celebration); theatrical 2; experimental 0; polish 4; tempo 3; groove 2. `voice=soft|clear`; singer's warm restraint instead of belting. Lyrics love, heartbreak, introspection, identity, nostalgia. Sonic comparisons with Natalie Imbruglia, the Corrs and Alanis Morissette in Pop Rescue; those names go in `sounds_like`, not `influenced_by`.
* **Disagreement:** the debut received an enthusiastic contemporary reception; later Pop Rescue found many slower cuts less memorable. Neither is evidence for a wildly different genre or stronger vocal belting. Album landmark: 1 for breakout and defining hits.

## 2. Another Day (2003) — `lenemarlin-anotherday`

* **Edition:** original ten tracks confirmed by 2003 Apple Music/European Virgin CD. Later variants include non-album extras. Lene's 2003 VG Q&A places studio recording from October 2002 to summer 2003 in London and Oslo.
* **Sound / ranked genres:** `acousticpop|softrock|poprock|pop|ballad`. Lollipop calls the album acoustic-based pop; the original European Virgin CD styles include Soft Rock and Pop Rock. musicOMH describes spiky guitar on *Sorry*, gentle string colours, a darker and more intimate character than the debut, and low-level vocals over throbbing bass on *Fight Against The Hours*. Lollipop hears some Pro Tools-textured atmospherics on *From This Day*, but it is a small studio detail rather than a full trip-hop record.
* **Instruments / production:** the original pressing lists guitarist Richard Parfitt, bass Jesper Mattsson, drums Makoto Sakamoto, percussion Martin Ditcham, keyboards/programmer Pete Davis, additional programmer James Sanger, and named cello, violin and viola session players. Individual guitar credits differentiate electric guitar on *Whatever It Takes* and Lene's playing on *My Love*. Pro Tools engineering is explicitly credited to Matthieu Clouard. For that reason `programming|protools`, without assuming digital editing, autotune or an electronic bass. Original CD lists **Mike Hedges** as producer. Qobuz appears to conflate album audio labels with Steve Lee and video directors; original CD is the cleanest source. Avoid unsupported Steve Lee credit in producer field.
* **Scales:** energy 2; electronic 2 (programming evident but guitars dominate); mood 1 (dark, bereavement, separation); theatrical 1 (intimate); experimental 0 (conventional melodic structures); polish 4; tempo 2; groove 1. Soft and clear voice, never operatic. Central lyrics separation, regret and heartbreak. A musicOMH critic draws comparison to the Cranberries on *You Weren't There*, while Lollipop hears Dido and Norah Jones; all are editorial sound comparisons.
* **Disagreement:** musicOMH finds some strong songs amid similar-feeling arrangements; Lollipop praises the album's emotional honesty. Both agree on acoustic music and introspection, which support a consistent descriptor. Landmark: 1 as major 2003 comeback and second defining catalogue entry.

## 3. Lost in a Moment (2005) — `lenemarlin-lostinamoment`

* **Edition and authorship:** original eleven-track Virgin/EMI album released 13 June 2005. No unrelated remix or later bonus in row. Marlin wrote the songs; Stargate produced. Source-backed instrumentalists in CD metadata and Qobuz include guitarists Lene, Bernt Rune Stray and Jarl Ivar Andresen; keyboard/piano credits for Tor Erik Hermansen, Hallgeir Rustan and Mikkel Storleer Eriksen; bassist Kim Nordbæk, drummer Fredrik Wallumrød, cellist Anne Britt Sævik Årdal.
* **Genres:** `acousticpop|poprock|pop|softrock|ballad`. IOL review identifies largely acoustic, slow melodies and just two faster tunes; musicOMH notes full studio polish and a rockier *How Would It Be*, with cello, strings and piano used sparingly. The album is not an electronically led R&B recording despite Stargate's association with R&B/pop acts. Use no electronic dance genre from those biographical associations.
* **Production uncertainty:** Producer `Stargate` is explicitly listed, but original visible credits provide **no specific electronic programming technique, DAW, Auto-Tune or drum machine**. Keep `production` empty (unknown rather than none). The fact that the record is produced professionally does not justify `protools` or `digitalediting` without a source. Instrument list is concrete and fairly full.
* **Scales:** energy 2, electronic 2 (keyboards under a fully credited band; could be 1–2), mood 2, theatrical 2, experimental 0, polish 5, tempo 2, groove 1. The moderate electronic score is an editorial estimate from piano/keyboard studio blend, not a claimed programmed-drum credit. Songs focus on heartbreak and romantic vulnerability. Soft, clear lead voice; not forceful rock singing. *Lost in a Moment* has less rhythmic propulsion than *Playing My Game*.
* **Disagreement:** contemporary musicOMH strongly criticises uniformity and polished sound, whereas Independent Online praises natural vocal delivery and acoustic/rock guitar balance. South China Morning Post explicitly compares the singer's sound with Wilson Phillips, Suzanne Vega, Dido, Natalie Imbruglia and Alanis Morissette; only the three most readily relevant similarities are represented in `sounds_like`. They are **not** established influences. Landmark 0: significant follow-up, but not clearly a defining album relative to debut and comeback.

## 4. Twist the Truth (2009) — `lenemarlin-twistthetruth`

* **Edition and production:** original ten tracks released 30 March 2009, produced by Even Johansen (aka Magnet) and Børge Fjordheim. Original label-level Qobuz credits also list Renate Engevold with `Producer` on *Do You Remember* only; treat her as a **minor track-level producer** and verify this individual attribution against physical sleeve if available. Contemporary Dagsavisen and 730.no verify the Johansen/Fjordheim collaboration.
* **Genres:** `acousticpop|folk|pop|softrock|country|blues`. Dagbladet specifically hears gentle folk, country and blues colours; the latter two are minor accents and correctly ranked late, not leading genres. The National identifies stark acoustic arrangements, faintly macabre melancholy and playful brass on *Have I Ever Told You*. Full studio album still has recognisable Marlin pop structures; it is not an electronic or folk-only genre switch. Qobuz's Pop market classification is supplementary.
* **Instruments:** Qobuz credits include guitar (Lene Marlin / Stray / Kristiansen), piano and Rhodes electric piano, organ in Dagbladet critical description, drums and percussion (Fjordheim / Johansen), bass and double bass, banjo, dobro, violin, viola, cello, trumpets and trombones, glockenspiel, vibraphone (map to existing `mallets`), and occasional synth. These are credited **across different tracks**, not all on each song. The instrumental long tail is kept because the record is characterised by a variety of gentle ornamental textures.
* **Production:** `programming` as Qobuz credits Even Johansen's programmer role on *Story of a Life*, *Learned from Mistakes* and *Have I Ever Told You*. `stringarrangements` on *Learned from Mistakes* is directly credited to Sally Herbert; do not infer orchestra-wide production or named DAWs. No documented tape machine/DAW model or vocal plugin.
* **Scales:** energy 2; electronic 1; mood 2; theatrical 1; experimental 2 (acoustic/folk/country palette compared with earlier polished pop, still conventional writing); polish 4; tempo 2; groove 1. Lyrics introspection, heartbreak, love, nostalgia, storytelling. `voice=soft|clear` agrees with BT observation that production leaves room for her understated vocal. A review likens earlier music to the Corrs and another to a restrained Suzanne Vega-like atmosphere; editorial only, not causal influence.
* **Disagreement:** Dagbladet is particularly positive about production beauty but frustrated by songwriting similarities to previous work; Bergens Tidende finds it understated but forgettable, while The National is harsher about lyrical bleakness and praises occasional brighter brass. Landmark 1 primarily represents the deliberate pastoral production reinvention and is therefore an editorial flag, not a commercial superlative.

## Artist influence policy

The 2003 VG artist Q&A records inspiration from lived surroundings, not a named musician (https://www.lenemarlinfanclub.it/en/rubriche/chatvg2003.asp). She named Magnet as music she was then listening to, and his producing the fourth album is separately documented. This does not establish influence on her songwriting. Her later account of meeting Joni Mitchell in New York is not enough either. **No verified ARTIST INFLUENCES names**; `influenced_by` empty for all four rows. Avoid introducing comparisons as causal claims. This is not a negative statement about her actual influences, only a source-evidence constraint.
