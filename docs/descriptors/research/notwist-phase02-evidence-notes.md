# The Notwist — Phase 02 evidence notes

**Research cut-off:** 27 October 2026. These notes justify judgements about these three albums only. The numeric scales are original, evidence-led album-level judgements, not numerical claims by reviewers. Read the associated CSVs for source-specific attribution. Empty `influenced_by` is deliberate when only comparisons are documented.

## Shrink (1998) — `notwist-shrink`

- **Recording and credits:** The band's own Bandcamp edition confirms 15 May 1998 and the ten original songs [S01]. The 1998 sleeve reports a 1997 recording at Uphon with Mario Thaler and O.L.A.F. Opal on recording/mixing [S03]; album personnel transcribed from liner notes separately give **Galore** as producer [S04]. **Do not promote Thaler or Opal to the producer column** merely for recording and mixing. 'Galore' is a credited producer name, not a genre or a band influence.
- **Stylistic breadth:** The strong axes are indie electronic, rock and increasingly exploratory digital manipulation. AllMusic explicitly lists indie electronic, indie rock, IDM, techno, experimental techno and ambient techno [S02]; its biography identifies trip-hop and post-rock influences [S06]. Band-uploaded contemporary appraisal finds free-jazz textures and pop song structures; Pitchfork identifies old-timey folk elements [S01, S05]. Narrow styles sit after the dominant hybrid; jazz and folk are track-level, not claims that the whole album is jazz or folk.
- **Scales (energy 3; electronic 3; mood 2; theatrical 1; experimental 4; polish 3; tempo 3; groove 2):** Lively guitar and break-beat sections coexist with restrained vocal melodies; the digital techniques are pronounced but acoustic jazz and rock players remain. Frequent structural surprises, bleeps and stylistic crossings justify experimental 4, not a claim of extreme avant-garde. The sober vocal texture offsets higher rhythmic energy. These are comparative judgements based on [S01, S02, S05, S07].
- **Voice/lyrics:** Acher's low-key, lightly delivered singing supports `soft|clear` [S01, S05]. `introspection` is cautious, inferred from contemporaneous lyrics discussed in [S01] and the record's reflective character, and is weaker evidence than the other descriptors; do not infer more themes from later albums.
- **Instruments:** Band personnel credits specify guitar, bass, drums, keyboards, samples, trumpet, trombone, vibraphone, percussion, saxophone, flute, bass clarinet, cello, double bass, theremin and a Yamaha RY30 drum machine [S04]. Album-specific PopMatters criticism independently hears vibraphone and bass clarinet on Your Signs [S01]. The instrument token `mallets` represents credited **vibraphone**, which is not a distinct token in the supplied list. `bassclarinet` was already proposed in Phase 01; retain until Claude adds it. Do not infer synthesizer models from the IDM genre.
- **Production:** `sampling` directly corresponds to credited samples; `hornarrangements` to Micha Acher brass arrangements in the release credit transcription [S04]. `yamahary30` is an **additional production token** for the specifically named Yamaha RY30 [S04]; model evidence is a liner-note transcription, not an independently accessed physical sleeve. Opal and Thaler are not producers. Micha Acher describes how the group began accumulating, editing and recombining recorded fragments on this album [S07].
- **Influence vs resemblance:** PopMatters characterises the horns on Your Signs as Bacharach-inspired [S01], supporting **Burt Bacharach** as a limited critic-documented musical reference. AllMusic likens riffs to Dinosaur Jr. and electronic production to Pan Sonic [S02]; these are in **sounds_like**, not musical influence. Radiohead's *Kid A* came later, so the comparison is retrospective sonic proximity only [S01].
- **Disagreement and confidence:** The more narrowly techno-oriented AllMusic tags contrast with critical descriptions of jazz and pop; their scope is separated. Producer/RY30 credits are second-hand transcription, so confidence is **medium**. Original release May 1998 differs from later US release dates [S01, S02].

## Neon Golden (2002) — `notwist-neongolden`

- **Edition and production:** Original European 2002 date and ten-track running order take priority over the 2003 US issue and later Bandcamp/US bonus tracks [S08, S13, S14]. The original CD booklet lists **Galore** as producer, Mario Thaler and O.L.A.F. Opal as **recording/mixing**, with brass and string arrangements by Micha Acher [S13]. This distinction determines the `producers` field. The 2002 interview with Micha Acher specifies 15 months of assembling and editing recordings, and that the title track was finished late [S07].
- **Genres and disagreement:** Songs link indie pop, electronic samples and guitar music, in the canonical indietronica intersection [S08, S09]. Pitchfork, RTÉ and The Independent agree on that balance [S09–S11]. `electronica` is a specific term in the artist's album page; OndaRock labels it indietronica and kraut-pop [S29], while Album of the Year also labels folktronica, ambient pop and glitch pop [S28]; `breakbeat` is occasional [S08], while `blues` pertains especially to the acoustic guitar of the title track [S11]. The Independent suggests an alt-country/Americana angle on the whole record [S10], disputed by the stronger electronic-pop descriptions; it is deliberately ordered last and tagged **texture**. Neither that interpretation nor cinematic strings warrants pretending the record is a country album.
- **Scales (energy 2; electronic 3; mood 2; theatrical 1; experimental 3; polish 4; tempo 2; groove 2):** Mostly hushed melody, patient pulses and introspective lyrics; electronic clicks, loops and cut-ups are substantial but coexist with acoustic guitar, piano and live drums. Long editing and detailed arrangements support polish 4. The energetic Pilot is not the album-level norm [S07, S09, S11].
- **Voice/lyrics:** Markus Acher's hushed, restrained vocals appear in contemporary Pitchfork and Hot Press accounts [S09, S12]. Love, emotional uncertainty, heartbreak and introspective self-appraisal recur, particularly Consequence and One with the Freaks; the labels are interpretive and supported by [S09, S10, S11]. Avoid making the repeated emotional imagery into a source-free biography.
- **Instruments:** Acoustic guitar and banjo [S08–S10], saxophone and bass clarinet [S09, S13], cello and double bass [S13], keyboards and piano [S13], organ and percussion [S15], Mellotron [S10, S12], acoustic/drum loops and synth [S09, S10], strings and brass [S10–S13], flute [S08, S12]. Album credits also specify conga and regional hand percussion [S13]; generic `percussion` avoids introducing unverified mappings of every drum subtype. Synth is named explicitly by the Independent, not assumed from electronica [S10].
- **Production:** `sampling` is described by RTÉ [S11]. Pitchfork describes slicing and looping of Acher's vocal on a track, supporting track-level `vocalchops` [S09]. Brass/string arrangements are directly credited [S13]. Hot Press specifically hears **E-bow** [S12]. Micha Acher describes detailed electronic recombination [S07], supporting `sonicmanipulation`. No exact synth model, plug-in or sequencer is documented.
- **Influence discipline:** New Order and Depeche Mode are *critic comparisons* in RTÉ [S11]; Belle & Sebastian, Lambchop, Wheat and Aphex Twin likewise appear in comparisons [S10, S12, S15]. Therefore they are **sounds_like** only. There is no reliably documented musician influence specific enough to fill `influenced_by` for this album. A sampled/composed element on Solitaire is **not** a musical influence: one booklet transcription credits *Drowning By Numbers* to 'Gary Numan' [S13], while secondary listings attribute the material to **Michael Nyman**; this unresolved discrepancy is excluded from `influenced_by` and should be resolved using an image of the original booklet before adding a sampled-work relationship. The 2003 bonus songs are not new album rows.
- **Landmark:** A defining indie-electronic album, recognised contemporaneously and in retrospective writing [S09, S15]; landmark 1, high confidence.

## The Devil, You + Me (2008) — `notwist-thedevilyoume`

- **Date and producer roles:** Official band release page dates the album 2 May 2008 [S16], distinct from later international releases and reviews. Original issue credits explicitly list **The Notwist** as producer and **Olaf Opal** as producer/co-producer and mixer [S18]. Mario Thaler is not the producer here, nor are the orchestral arranger Daniel Glatzel and recording engineer Tobias Lehmann [S17, S18].
- **Sound and genre:** Guitar-based indie melodic forms coexist with electronic rhythms, glitches, synthesizers and orchestral strings [S19, S21, S22]. Original sleeve categorises some sound as *art rock* [S18]. The low-frequency dub breaks noted by Pitchfork are **track** evidence, not a dub album [S19]. Contemporary/experimental classical touches derive from actual orchestration, but the entire album is not relabelled classical or instrumental music [S20]. Sources differ over whether this feels a restrained pop album or a forward electronic experiment [S19, S21, S23].
- **Scales (energy 2; electronic 3; mood 1; theatrical 2; experimental 3; polish 4; tempo 2; groove 1):** The record remains richly electronic, but a soft voice, anxious slow passages and string-led songs offset the occasional strong beat [S19, S21, S22]. More orchestral breadth than Neon Golden, still largely intimate and melancholic. Exaggerated 'club' or overtly theatrical values would be unsupported.
- **Instruments:** A full Andromeda Mega Express Orchestra participation is documented with credited violin, viola, cello, double bass, trumpet, trombone, tenor/baritone sax, clarinet, bassoon, flute, oboe, harp, vibraphone, organ, melodica, drums and percussion [S17, S18]. `woodwinds` covers oboe since the supplied whitelist allows the family; `mallets` encompasses vibraphone, `gobletdrum` the credited tombak. Guitar and synth are also independently heard by Pitchfork and the Washington Post [S19, S22]. Use `guitar` for some tracks rather than assigning electric guitar to all.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` reflects documented orchestra arranged by Daniel Glatzel [S18, S20]; electronic changing sounds support `sonicmanipulation` [S19, S21]; `distortion` pertains to explicitly distorted/power-drill guitars [S19, S22]. No specific drum machine or DSP hardware is documented: all model tokens remain absent.
- **Voice, lyrics and influence:** Soft, poised and clear Acher singing [S19, S22]; fragmented meditations on personal belief, mortality, uncertain perspective and gravity are directly explained by Markus Acher [S20]. Acher also explicitly mentions commissioning **Morton Feldman-like** layered instrumental writing from Glatzel [S20], making Morton Feldman an evidence-backed musical reference in `influenced_by`. Do **not** list Dax Pierson there: his accident shaped Acher's thoughts and dedication, **not** a documented musical influence [S20]. Nico and Snow Patrol are audible associations drawn by reviewers [S19, S23], and aren't producers/influences.
- **Landmark:** A valuable later exploratory album, but Shrink and Neon Golden are stronger career-defining records. We assign landmark 0 without devaluing this record. High confidence for instrumentation and producer roles; all scales remain editorial judgements.
