# Franco Battiato — Phase 03: album-specific evidence and decisions

Research date: **26 October 2026**. All four are **1979–1982 original studio albums**. This file documents editorial scale choices and non-obvious descriptors, plus source disagreements. URLs are cross-indexed in `battiato_phase03_sources.csv`; every genre is independently indexed in `battiato_phase03_genre_evidence.csv`.

## Method and boundary

- The numeric 0–5 scales describe the **albums themselves**, not Battiato's work across decades. Scores are **reasoned editorial estimates**, not sourced measurements. They are grounded in descriptions from Rolling Stone Italia, OndaRock, Rockol and personnel/instrument credits; all remain draft.
- The instruments field is **credited instrumentation for these original recordings**, generally album-wide; it does not claim each sound appears in every track. Where labels use guitar without qualifying acoustic/electric, an electric-guitar token is used only with guitar-rock context and other sources identifying electric parts.
- Genre ordering is an album-level prioritization. Any marginal genre described by genre-database metadata or only a song remains at the end. Genre evidence flags `whole album`, `some tracks`, `texture` and low-strength associations. `canzonedautore` / `classicalpop` and `arp` / `ems` need validator and taxonomy additions.
- `influenced_by` is **blank on all four**. No album-specific documented influence by a particular musician was sufficiently demonstrated. Gurdjieff, Guénon and quotations of poetry and classical repertoire are intellectual and textual references, not verified music-artist influences; Peter Gabriel/Bowie comparisons are editorial `sounds_like` *only*. No automatic transfer from Phase 01's artist-level influence report.
- The two 1981–82 titles already exist in `catalog.ts`; the 1979 and 1980 titles do not. No previous phase albums are repeated.

## L’era del cinghiale bianco (1979) — `battiato-leradelcinghialebianco`

**Sources:** official Battiato discography, original-issue MusicBrainz, Universal Music Italia 40th-anniversary credits; Fabio Zuffanti in Rolling Stone Italia; Giulia Cavaliere in Corriere della Sera; Alessandro Carrera's scholarly chapter; language archive for *Stranizza d'amuri*.

- **Sonic/genre:** The artist's official site explicitly records the new turn to accessible pop with Giusto Pio. Zuffanti describes squared new-wave rhythms with classical melodic gestures and exotically suggestive arrangements. Cavaliere explicitly adds rock, synth-pop and prog ingredients. `progressivepop` (AOTY) precedes broad `pop` to preserve the album's specific bridging position, while the disputed metadata labels `neoclassical` and `europop` appear last. `canzonedautore` is a database's explicit named style, not a claim that all songs sound like conventional singer-songwriter folk.
- **Instrumentation:** Roberto Colombo and Antonio Ballista keyboards; Alberto Radius guitar; Julius Farmer bass; Tullio De Piscopo drums and percussion; Giusto Pio violin; piano duo Danilo Lorenzini and Michele Fedrigotti on *Luna indiana*. Universal track credits verify most. `strings` and `orchestra` are not added because string arrangements and orchestral direction do not prove an audible independent orchestra on most tracks.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` from Universal's explicit Giusto Pio *String Arranger* credits. No presumed EMS synth model, sequencer, special compressor, drum machine or vocal manipulation.
- **Voice, language, themes:** Battiato's tuneful, usually clear and occasionally gentle solo delivery; Italian on most tracks, documented Catanese Sicilian on *Stranizza d'amuri*, ISO 639-3 `scn`. `spiritual` (title / *Il re del mondo*) and `social` (e.g. *Magic shop*) lead; `love` for one track and war recollection in the Sicilian song. No inference of Arabic lyrics from references to Damascus.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 2, mood 3, theatrical 2, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 2. Full band and prominent dance-influenced beat, but more violin and organic playing than the 1980–82 records. Melancholy / tenderness balances uplift; a major formal shift away from Phase 02's avant-garde works.
- **Producer / landmark:** Angelo Carrara sole original credited producer. Pio is violinist, composer and arranger, not credited original producer. Landmark=1: first major EMI pop phase and a defining record.
- **Conflicts:** MusicBrainz dates first LP **10 September 1979**, Sentireascoltare indexes **10 October**. Use only the year in descriptor. AOTY's `progressivepop` and AllMusic `neoclassical` are not equally persuasive; secondary styles are flagged. Universal's 2019 reissue bonus versions, demos and live cuts are excluded.

## Patriots (1980) — `battiato-patriots`

**Sources:** official Battiato album page; original-lineup Universal Music Italia credits; 2008 physical reissue sleeve transcription; William Ruhlmann at AllMusic; Rolling Stone Italia; Rockol essay; AOTY genre index.

- **Sonic/genre:** New-wave/art-pop/synthpop album listings align with Ruhlmann's synth-forward pop-rock analysis. Specific keyboard signatures and classical piano/violin accents explain secondary AllMusic `neoclassical` and `classicalpop`; these are **not** assertions that the record is a contemporary-classical or fully orchestral album. `europop` is a secondary AllMusic tag.
- **Instrumentation:** Franco Battiato EMS synthesizer; Filippo/Phil Destrieri's Oberheim and ARP synths, organ, harp (harp credited on Vinileria reissue), and woodwinds (Universal's category may be a broad database mapping); Antonio Ballista piano; Giusto Pio violin; Alberto Radius guitar; Gigi Cappellotto bass; Flaviano Cuffari drums; Gianfranco D'Adda percussion; Eugenio Spanò declamation. Source original sleeve calls the synthesizer simply `EMS` and `ARP`, not a verified EMS VCS3 or ARP2600 model.
- **Production:** `oberheim|arp|ems` are **device family** attestations from performer credits. `oberheim` exists in Fandome; `arp` and `ems` are proposed new tokens. No inferential `emsvcs3` or `arp2600` assigned. Not enough documentary evidence for studio overdubbing, tape effects or particular vocal processing.
- **Voice, lyrics, languages:** primarily Battiato's clear tenor-ish voice; Eugenio Spanò's **reciting** supports `spoken`. Official website calls *Arabian Song* Battiato's first track sung in Arabic, justifying `it|ar`. `social`, `irony`, `political` for *Up Patriots to Arms*, and memory/reflection for *Prospettiva Nevski*. Literary quotations of Leopardi/Pascoli/Carducci are text references, not `influenced_by` musicians.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 3, mood 2, theatrical 3, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 2. Energetic title song balances slower storytelling and cool synthesizers; the album has more rigid/electronic surfaces than 1979, though still a hybrid band record.
- **Producer / landmark:** Original-sleeve and 2017 Universal both credit Angelo Carrara; Battiato and Giusto Pio arranged/co-composed. Some **2020 remaster** digital metadata calls Battiato `Producer` and Carrara `ExecutiveProducer`. Do **not** back-project those modern metadata roles on the 1980 original. Landmark=1: essential transitional album with two enduring signatures.
- **Conflicts:** AllMusic incorrectly implies *Patriots* was his **first EMI album**; original 1979 *L'era del cinghiale bianco* demonstrably precedes it. Avoid this in data. New LP has seven songs: later multilingual bonus tracks are excluded. Credited `Harp` (Vinileria) and `Woodwinds` (Universal) should remain easy to review because original OCR metadata is not instrument-per-track precise.

## La voce del padrone (1981) — `battiato-lavocedelpadrone`

**Sources:** original EMI credit transcription (Musicbase); 1981 label-served Qobuz metadata; Universal album page; Davide Bassi's OndaRock landmark study; Fabio Zuffanti's Rockol and Rolling Stone historical critical accounts; AOTY and MusicBrainz genres.

- **Sonic/genres:** Seven compact, catchy pop songs with synthpop/new-wave timbres and tightly disciplined arrangements. AOTY explicitly labels Art Pop, New Wave, Synthpop, Canzone d'autore **and Italo-Disco**. The last is a contested retrospective label for an **1981** album (genre boundary/chronology, not a dominant Italo disco record); it appears near the end with `some tracks` / low evidence. Musicbase also lists Prog Rock; only lingering ornate writing justifies its position last. The `pop` identity is independently established in specialist criticism.
- **Instruments:** Filippo Destrieri keyboards; Alberto Radius guitar; Paolo Donnarumma bass; Alfredo Golino drums; Claudio Pascoli saxophone; Donato Scolese vibraphone (mapped to general **`mallets`**, deliberately **not** a synthesizer); Madrigalisti di Milano choral voices; Giusto Pio string arrangements. `strings` is included on corroborated Pio classical/string treatment; if importer needs precise ensemble evidence, drop this token pending physical-sleeve check. No brand/model of synth or drum machine is substantiated.
- **Production:** `stringarrangements` explicitly from Qobuz credits. The Madrigalisti chorus supports `choral` in **voice**, but choral singing does not entail studio `layeredvocals` or a specific vocal effect, so no spurious production tag.
- **Lyrics and language:** social criticism and satire in *Bandiera bianca* and *Centro di gravità permanente*; spiritual seeking in the center-of-gravity metaphor; reminiscence in *Cuccurucucù* and sensuality in *Sentimiento nuevo*. Mostly Italian, with a clearly sung English hook in *Summer on a Solitary Beach*; `it|en` denotes recurring minor English text, not a bilingual English-version edition. Spanish-sounding phrase in *Sentimiento nuevo* is not treated as a whole Spanish recording.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 3, mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 5, tempo 3, groove 4. Compared with the two earlier records, this has more uniform hook-centered melodies, clearer high-finish band/choral mix and dance-leaning movement. `mood=4` captures summery surface **without** denying cynicism in lyrics.
- **Producer / landmark:** Original EMI vinyl/transcription lists **Angelo Carrara** alone. Reissue/Qobuz 2021 credits list Battiato as additional producer. Use original to avoid conflating arranger/composer and rerecording roles. Landmark=1: defining million-selling album.
- **Other conflicts:** OndaRock's punk/electronics references describe **musical updating**, not a blanket `punk` genre tag. AOTY's Italo-disco metadata is disputed, kept low-confidence genre evidence; `influenced_by` stays empty because named composers/performers quoted in the lyrics are **references**, not documented album-specific musical shaping.

## L’arca di Noè (1982) — `battiato-larcadinoe`

**Sources:** 3 December 1982 EMI/MusicBrainz first-release record; Universal and original-sleeve credit transcription; Fabio Zuffanti's Rolling Stone account; AOTY genre; Giovanni Botti retrospective; Qobuz/EMI 2008 reissue metadata.

- **Sonic/genres:** Art Pop, New Wave and Synthpop are explicitly tagged by Album of the Year; MusicBrainz adds pop rock, electronic, pop and rock. Zuffanti stresses darker apocalyptic material on the first three tracks, contrasting with the dance-oriented closer. `prog` is **at most a weak late-listed** genre claim (a tertiary Wikipedia genre infobox calls it progressive rock, while qualified critics primarily describe pop/electronic). Review before import if no qualified explicit `progressive rock` source can be accepted; **drop `prog` if strict verified genre terms required**.
- **Instrumentation:** Original 1982 album's credits: Filippo Destrieri keyboards; Alberto Radius guitars (rock electric parts); Paolo Donnarumma bass; Alfredo Golino drums; Giusto Pio violin; Madrigalisti di Milano choir. Synthesizer-led texture supported by critical accounts and synthpop genre analysis. The original is an ensemble record, not purely synthesized. Neither percussion nor piano is explicitly credited as an instrument independently, so omitted.
- **Production:** intentionally **empty**. Recent Qobuz metadata lists Shane Dempsey / Pietro Pellegrini / Luigi Tonet as `Programmer` for the **2008 remastered digital edition**; no evidence these names or processes refer to the **original 1982 session**. Do not guess sequencing, drum machines, studio synth brands, reverb or sidechain. The choir belongs to `voice=choral` rather than a false `layeredvocals` production claim.
- **Lyrics:** political/social and spiritual/allegorical material coexist (*Radio Varsavia*, *L'esodo*, *La torre*, *Scalo a Grado*), alongside movement/dance and irony in closer *Voglio vederti danzare*. All originals sung chiefly in Italian; **English song titles do not establish English-language vocal delivery**.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 3, mood 1, theatrical 4, experimental 3, polish 4, tempo 3, groove 3. A darker, somewhat more declamatory and apocalyptic album than *La voce*, but its closing dance song lifts the groove above the slower tracks. The seemingly conflicting bright closer is represented in source notes.
- **Producer / landmark:** Angelo Carrara sole original producer, Pio co-composer/arranger. Landmark=1 within his career (not the same broad mass breakthrough as 1981): deliberately sharp stylistic and lyrical pivot after pop success. Source disagreements over popularity/criticism are not converted into numerical measurement.
- **Conflicts:** Some digital platforms label 1 January 1982 as release date for digitized versions. MusicBrainz first Italian LP dates **3 December 1982**. No live or reissue bonus cuts included. The 2008 digital metadata must not be mistaken for 1982 session evidence.

## Cross-record disagreements & import cautions

1. `progressivepop` and `pop` coexist for 1979 (specific + broader); different publications differ over how much progressive rock remains.
2. AOTY crowd-submitted genres are useful leads, **weaker** than specialist reviews and original label documents; genre_evidence strength discloses that distinction.
3. The titles *La voce del padrone* and *L’arca di Noè* are already present in current `catalog.ts`. Their lowercase Fandome normalized IDs use the existing ID derivation and need **no catalogue insertion**, but their descriptor rows are new.
4. Musicbase and physical-record sources preserve primary `Producer` vs `Arranger` distinctions. Digital remaster metadata may reassign duties retrospectively. The `producers` column is consistently **Angelo Carrara**.
5. The original-sleeve session-music details are attached to the original 7-track programs only; 2019 or 2020 anniversary editions with new demos/live cuts are **not separately described**.
6. New token proposals are **not** installed in GitHub. Claude must extend taxonomies / validator or remove unsupported tags on review.
