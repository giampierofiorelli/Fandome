# Mina — selected landmark albums, Phase 01: evidence and judgments

**Research date:** 2026-10-25. **Selection policy:** the request is the *most important albums*, not a complete discography. Select historical breakthroughs, artistic turning points, critical significance and lasting cultural reference, including notable records with previously issued songs. This is **not a ranking by singles alone**; only four pivotal first-period LPs are included here. Future phases should select rather than mechanically enumerate every studio album.

**Repository audit:** `src/data/catalog.ts` has artist id `mina` and just four seeded albums: *Studio Uno* (1965), *Frutta e verdura* (1973), *Mina Live ’78* (1978), *Mina Celentano* (1998); `docs/descriptors/mina.csv` absent when checked. Preserve generated id `mina-studiouno` for the existing album. Note generated catalog IDs are based on normalized ASCII letters/digits; 1964 self-titled gets `mina-mina`, **not** a duplicate of 1971 self-titled (which later needs `mina-mina1971` or another reviewed disambiguator).

**Historical release vs compilation**: In 1960 LPs were commonly constructed from previously issued 45 rpm sides. *Tintarella di luna* and *Il cielo in una stanza* are documented and usually catalogued as studio albums in Mina's official album chronology although technically assembled recordings. Mark `type=studio` with explanation rather than erasing their landmark role or pretending they were coherent new sessions. *Studio Uno* also anthologizes pre-existing singles; it is not one live TV performance or a soundtrack. Distinguish an album from a same-named 1959 single or 1960 four-track EP, and do not score any later re-recordings/remixes.

**Genre policy:** All documented style labels, including weaker database taxonomies, are retained and given precise album-vs-track-vs-retrospective scope. `italianpop`, `boogiewoogie` and `musicaleggera` are proposed new tokens, subject to adding them in `scenes.ts`, `genreFamilies.ts`, validator and UI mappings. Keep `itpop` distinct: it often denotes a later indie/modern Italian pop scene, not a synonym for mid-century Italian popular music. `jazz` on the 1960 second LP has **single-track scope**; it is not an all-jazz LP. `adultcontemporary` and `europop` are modern database tags, not genre terminology used in 1960. Existing `standards`, `vocaljazz`, `bossanova`, `rockandroll`, `traditionalpop` are used as allowed Fandome tokens.

**Producers:** Strictly documented personnel only. Arrangers and orchestra directors are **not automatically producers**. The 1964 original LP explicitly credits **Wladimiro Albera** as producer in a first-pressing sleeve transcription [M-ORIG], crosschecked as Vladimiro Albera in another discography [M-EN]; **Augusto Martelli** is arranger/leader, **Giordano Bruno Martelli** is a supervisor, not a second credited producer. Original album-level producer credit remains **unverified** for both 1960 LPs and *Studio Uno*, so `producers` is blank; David Matalon's role in early singles [T-FIN] cannot simply be copied onto the whole LP. Do not mistake songwriter/copyright owner for producer.

## Tintarella di luna (March 1960) — `mina-tintarelladiluna`
- **Why landmark:** Original 12-song debut, containing her defining 1959 rock-and-roll hit; retrospective critical account links early success to arrival of young Italian popular culture [T-MB,T-IT,T-FIN,T-REVIEW]. `landmark=1`, `confidence=medium`: significance is secure, but recording/production details are fragmentary.
- **Genres:** pop and rock-and-roll first, with boogie-woogie explicitly named for this LP in discography; retrospective Euro-Pop/Italian Pop/Adult Contemporary and broad Pop/Rock taxonomy are lesser evidence [T-IT,T-AM,T-FIN]. The LP also has ballads such as *È vero*, so do not treat it as 100% rock.
- **Scales (editorial):** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` for mixed uptempo songs and slower ballads; `mood=4` for bright playful dominant material while some tracks are reflective; `electronic=0` for period acoustic-band arrangements; `theatrical=3` for prominent projected vocals; `polish=3` normal mono commercial studio. `experimental` deliberately **blank** because original-production assessment is too limited to score fairly.
- **Voice/language/themes:** `powerful|clear` is a general but period-appropriate expert account of her delivery, consistent with this repertoire; **English appears in *Whisky* and *My Crazy Baby***; `irony` describes the playful lunar-tanning narrative of the title song and `love` additional romantic material. These are notable topics, not all tracks.
- **Influences and similarity:** no claim that Connie Francis, Brenda Lee or Wanda Jackson demonstrably influenced this *album*; they are editorial sonic comparisons in `sounds_like` only. Producers left blank as above.

## Il cielo in una stanza (June 1960) — `mina-ilcieloinunastanza`
- **Why landmark:** 12-song album with Gino Paoli's canon-defining romantic song. This is the original Mina version, not 1969/1988 rerecordings nor the same-named 1960 4-track EP [C-MB,C-IT,C-EN]. `landmark=1`, `confidence=medium` owing to limited whole-LP criticism.
- **Genres:** original `pop`/Italian `musica leggera`; AllMusic tags Italian Pop, Euro-Pop and Adult Contemporary as **retrospective** labels, not period movements. `traditionalpop` reflects orchestral backing. Jazz specialist lists *Invoco te*: **one-track `jazz` marker** only, final genre and weak [C-JAZZ].
- **Music and scales:** Tony De Vita directs the original orchestra, except Giulio Libano for final B-side track; neither is verified as producer [C-IT]. `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2` reflect significant slower songs but also rhythmic novelty tracks; `mood=3` deliberately mixed rather than rating the whole record on one intimate ballad; `electronic=0`, `theatrical=3`, `polish=3`. `experimental` left blank, rather than deduced from historically innovative title-song lyrics.
- **Lyrics/voice:** `love|sensuality|heartbreak` covers title song's metaphoric intimacy and romantic tracks, balanced against comic material. `clear|powerful` describes bright and projected Italian singing, not a formal operatic timbre. `language=it` applies to original Italian LP, not later translations. Gino Paoli is **author of title-song, not a documented artist influence on Mina's album**, so `influenced_by` remains empty [C-GINO].

## Mina (1964) — `mina-mina`
- **Why landmark:** first Ri-Fi LP of 12 **new recordings**, as opposed to 1960 single-based LPs; jazz and Latin standards program; first-pressing critical distinction, and high chart performance [M-IT,M-ORIG,M-JAZZ,M-CHART]. This **must not be confused with Mina's other self-titled LP of 1971**.
- **Genres:** `jazz|vocaljazz|standards` dominate; extensive repertoire of *The Nearness of You, Angel Eyes, Stella By Starlight, You Go to My Head, Stars Fell on Alabama, Everything Happens to Me* appears in jazz specialist study [M-JAZZ]. `bossanova` covers *Insensatez*, `latin` the Brazilian/Mexican/Spanish-language portion [M-OFFICIAL,M-EN]. AllMusic Italian Pop/Euro-Pop/Adult Contemporary remain weaker catalogue tags [M-AM]. No genre cap.
- **Production:** Producer **Wladimiro Albera**, arrangement/orchestra **Augusto Martelli**; do not transcribe Giordano Bruno Martelli's supervision as production [M-ORIG]. The 2025 rarities set reproduces performance dates but its *alternate takes* **must not replace** the original album versions [M-OFFICIAL].
- **Scales:** `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2` for predominantly measured vocal standards with occasional bossa pulse; `electronic=0` orchestral acoustic performance; `mood=2` sentimental/melancholic standards; `theatrical=2` relatively intimate crooning compared with variety show; `experimental=2` uncommon multinational jazz/Latin scope within Italian pop; `polish=4` controlled orchestral studio. Editorial calibrated against guide, not a quantified acoustic analysis.
- **Voices/words/languages:** `clear|powerful` restrained jazz phrasing with occasional power; Italian, English, Portuguese, Spanish are documented on original record [M-EN,M-ORIG]. `love|heartbreak|introspection` jazz standard texts. Four compared singers remain `sounds_like` only; no artist/producer testimony establishing direct inspiration by them for this LP was found.

## Studio Uno (March 1965) — `mina-studiouno`
- **Why landmark:** original 12-track Ri-Fi LP tied to Rai variety-era dominance; Rai Cultura identifies multiple hits and its 1965 commercial primacy; qualifies for landmark selection [S-RAI,S-OFF]. Existing catalog ID `mina-studiouno` must be preserved.
- **Genres:** mainstream original `pop` and Italian orchestral popular song; AllMusic specifically lists `italianpop`, `europop`, `adultcontemporary`, with broad Pop/Rock umbrella `rock` **last and very low weight**. `traditionalpop` source-backed by Martelli's orchestra as a descriptive, not exact database, style; no proof of whole-album soul/rock band.
- **Production/arrangement:** Almost all recordings are arranged/conducted by **Augusto Martelli**, except *Città vuota* by **Piero Gosio** [S-RAI]. These are **not** producer credits. Without corroborated production roles, `producers` is blank.
- **Scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` mixed rhythmic and sustained songs; `electronic=0` original orchestral acoustic arrangement; `mood=3` romantic yearning vs lively songs; `theatrical=4` strongly projected dramatic popular vocals (Nina Zilli specifically describes difficulty of long sustained high register in *L'ultima occasione*) [S-RAI]; `experimental=1` conservative song structures despite cross-national adaptations; `polish=4` tightly arranged Ri-Fi commercial recordings.
- **Lyrics/languages:** `love|heartbreak|identity` recurring romance and *Io sono quel che sono*; Rai confirms **all 12 sung in Italian** although they derive from Italian adaptations of French, US, and UK recordings. Do not label album English because *So che mi vuoi* is an Italian-language adaptation of a Lennon–McCartney composition [S-OFF,S-RAI]. Songwriters are not automatically documented personal influences; `influenced_by` empty.
- **Disagreement:** Some general summaries say the album's songs were all performed during Studio Uno; Rai Cultura notes only **a few** were presented during the third edition. Conservatively state it is a TV-associated album, **not** an audio soundtrack recorded live during a broadcast [S-RAI,S-IT].
