# Ella Fitzgerald · Phase 04 · evidence notes

**Research date:** 29 October 2026. **Scope:** three original studio LPs; no live, greatest-hits, retrospective compilation or posthumous reissues. The values in the descriptor rows are Fandome editorial judgments, with evidence below. Do not treat `status=draft` as external peer review.

## 1. Ella Fitzgerald Sings Sweet Songs for Swingers (1959)

**Original issue and scope.** 1959 Verve LP **MG V-4032**, twelve titles. This is an original studio LP recorded in two sessions, 24 November 1958 and 11 July 1959, not a retrospective of other released albums. AllMusic's *album* record and some digital metadata incorrectly suggest 1958 as the release year, even though material was recorded in July 1959. The original pressing discography and the official Ella discography both support 1959. [off; sweet_allmusic; sweet_issue; sweet_releases; sweet_jazzdisco]

**Arranger versus producer.** The unidentified **Frank DeVol Orchestra** backs Ella; DeVol is arranger and conductor, **Norman Granz is the producer**. Archival lacquer discs at the Smithsonian include 1958 Ella/DeVol sessions for eight pieces, and session discographies document Harry **Sweets** Edison on trumpet in those recordings. Since the full orchestra membership remains unidentified, the instruments field is deliberately restricted to `orchestra|trumpet`; do **not** confidently invent saxophone, strings, bass, piano and drums from other DeVol projects. [sweet_smithsonian; sweet_jazzdisco; sweet_allmusic; sweet_qobuz]

**Musical content.** Scott Yanow notes an alternation of midtempo swing numbers and ballads, including *Let's Fall in Love*, *Moonlight Serenade*, and *Gone with the Wind*. Styles `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing|americanpopularsong` reflect the specific album classifications; `americanpopularsong` is repertoire/category rather than a distinct instrumental texture. [sweet_allmusic; sweet_qobuz]

**Scores and qualitative fields.** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 because alternating swings and ballads prevent a consistently fast or dance-oriented score; mood 3 spans romantic and wistful pieces; theatrical 2 because elegant, restrained standard singing; experimental 1 classic arrangements; polish 3 conventional professional studio orchestration. Electronic 0 is acoustic ensemble, *not* a guess about recording-tape technology. `clear|soft|powerful` captures the contrast between delicately phrased ballads and Ella's stronger dynamics. Lyrics: love, heartbreak, irony, introspection; these follow the original songs rather than genre conventions. [sweet_allmusic; sweet_qobuz; sweet_releases]

**Production:** empty. There is evidence of arranging and orchestra but no original-album source specifying a distinct sound-recording technique, processor or machine used for this album. `influenced_by` blank: neither covered composer nor arranger qualifies as named album-specific influence. `sounds_like` is editorial comparison only.

## 2. Ella Fitzgerald Sings the George and Ira Gershwin Song Book (1959)

**Original release and catalog.** **Already present** as an Ella album in Fandome `catalog.ts` but absent from the current Ella descriptors CSV on 29 October 2026. The original 1959 five-LP Verve set (**MG V-4029-5**) had **53 released vocal titles** in five volumes; this is **one album**, not five independent Fandome studio rows. Retrospectives often say **59 recorded songs**, and digital services list 59 or more pieces, including additional studio material and Riddle instrumentals; do not conflate their expanded track counts with the original five-LP programme. [gersh_jazzdiscography; gersh_reissue; gersh_qobuz; gersh_apple; gersh_thespian]

**Production and instruments.** **Norman Granz** produced the recording. **Nelson Riddle** arranged and conducted his studio orchestra. **Val Valentin** engineered recordings, not a producer. Verve rights-holder track credits and recording personnel document strings, brass/reeds, piano, guitar, bass, drums, percussion; Apple Music specifically names celeste on *But Not for Me* and rumba percussion on *He Loves and She Loves*. Our selection `orchestra|strings|brass|saxophone|trumpet|trombone|piano|guitar|doublebass|drums|percussion|celesta` reflects the source's session players and standout timbres; no synthesizer or digital tape. Production labels `hornarrangements|stringarrangements` denote these documented orchestral charts, not unverified recording equipment. [gersh_musicbrainz; gersh_qobuz; gersh_apple]

**Genres and arrangement.** The album moves between intimate ballads, Broadway theatre tunes, warm vocal jazz and full-band swinging arrangements. Source categories include `vocaljazz|jazz|standards|traditionalpop|swing|bigband|americanpopularsong|earlyjazzvocals`; latter is a library taxonomy for vocal tradition, not proof the album predates 1959. `swing` and `bigband` apply to many tracks rather than each string ballad. [gersh_allmusic; gersh_review; gersh_apple]

**Scores and themes.** energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 balance swinging and slow standards over a very long album; mood 3 includes hopeful theatrical numbers and aching torch songs; theatrical 3 includes theatrical repertoire interpreted with restraint; experimental 2 for diverse, imaginative arrangement without avant-garde methods; polish 4 for intricate Hollywood orchestra. Vocal timbre clear/powerful/soft. Thematic values love, heartbreak, irony, storytelling and party reflect lyrics in Gershwin selections such as *Someone to Watch Over Me*, *Let's Call the Whole Thing Off* and *Clap Yo' Hands*. Editorial `sounds_like` Sinatra (same Nelson Riddle orchestral jazz approach) and Sarah Vaughan (jazz vocal styling), **not** an influenced-by claim. [gersh_apple; gersh_allmusic; gersh_qobuz]

**Landmark** 1: centerpiece of Verve Song Book work, long-standing specialist critical appreciation and signature Riddle collaboration. `influenced_by` remains empty, because the Gershwins as covered writers, Riddle as credited arranger, and Granz as producer are not evidence of direct album influences. [gersh_udiscover; gersh_allmusic]

## 3. Get Happy! (1959 **provisional**)

**Major issue-year conflict that needs manual review.** Official Fitzgerald discography and AllMusic list **1959**; MusicBrainz release group lists US/UK original copies dated **1959**; **Jazzdiscography**, **Jazzdisco** and vinyl-pressing index **45cat** list the first Verve US mono MGV-4036 as **1960**. Keep `year=1959` provisionally to maintain the official artist working chronology, but require a first-issue contemporary source (original LP advert/catalogue/physical deadwax/date) to resolve before final review. This isn't a recording-versus-release issue to hand-wave: some sources contradict even on earliest issue. No exact release date invented. [off; get_allmusic; get_releasegroup; get_jazzdiscography; get_verve; get_45cat]

**Original LP versus expanded CD.** 1959/1960 original **12-song** LP runs *Somebody Loves Me* through *St. Louis Blues*; expanded 1998/1999 reissues add *A-Tisket A-Tasket* and alternate take *The Swingin' Shepherd Blues*. This is an original program of studio material compiled from previously **unissued or session-leftover studio recordings**, not a greatest-hits compilation or a new re-recording in the 1990s. However, because Fandome excludes compilations, Claude should **explicitly decide whether an original multi-session LP of leftovers counts as studio**. Treat classification as `studio` provisionally; do not change silently. [get_jazzcodex; get_musicbrainz; get_allmusic]

**Session credits.** The original LP combines Hollywood studio recordings made from **July 1957 through September 1959**. **Nelson Riddle**, **Frank DeVol**, **Paul Weston**, **Russell Garcia** and (on *Like Young/Cool Breeze*) **Marty Paich** are arrangers/conductors; not substitute names in `producers`. **Norman Granz** is credited producer. Session sources document orchestra and rhythm section with piano, guitar, bass, drums and brass (trumpet/trombone); some ensembles differ, so no false claim of a single unified band. There are no independently verified period studio machine/effect techniques, so `production` is empty. [get_allmusic; get_jazzcodex; get_jazzdiscography; get_musicbrainz; get_verve]

**Sound, scales and genres.** Album differs from a tightly arranged, single-coherent Song Book; brassier swing and happy rhythmic romps sit alongside slower standards. AllMusic explicitly categories Vocal Jazz, Traditional Pop, Standards, American Popular Song, Early Jazz Vocals; blues appears as the specific *St. Louis Blues* (genre last, some-track only). Jazz and swing stylistic character is documented by Verve material and Yanow. energy/tempo/groove 3 and mood 4 reflect generally upbeat material with some soft ballads; electronic 0, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3. Soft and powerful, agile scatting Ella voice; lyrics love, heartbreak, irony, party. `landmark=0`: historically valuable but less central than the Song Books. [get_allmusic; get_jazzcodex; get_musicbrainz]

`influenced_by` blank for lack of album-specific testimony. Neither source songwriters nor colleagues are used as influences. `sounds_like` is limited editorial analogy (Anita O'Day; Sarah Vaughan).

## Sources, taxonomy and cautions

- All genre items have individual evidence rows; source taxonomy categories `americanpopularsong` and `earlyjazzvocals` are **already in** the currently checked Fandome Genre type and validator. They need no new tokens. Their utility for recommendation weights is still an editorial issue; do not conflate them with unique sound.
- An older phase import note said these taxonomy values were not yet recognised; **the repository has been updated** by 29 Oct 2026. No new genres, instruments or production tokens required by these three rows.
- Some websites attach the year **1958** to a digital *Sweet Songs* even though the original LP includes July **1959** recordings. Prefer issue evidence 1959, distinct from original session dates.
- *Gershwin Song Book* 1959 is the **same** Fandome album regardless of five separate LP volume catalog numbers and reissued five- or six-disk sets.
- All `status=draft`; this batch is structural validation only. `Get Happy!` year and classification are import review blockers.
