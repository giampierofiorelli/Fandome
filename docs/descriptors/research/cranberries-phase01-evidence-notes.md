# The Cranberries — Phase 01: evidence notes

**Cutoff:** 25 October 2026. **Method:** Original 1993/1994/1996/1999 studio albums, not expanded deluxe editions. All eight 0–5 fields are **editorial evaluations** using the Fandome guide anchors, never measured BPM, sound-source percentages or numerical critical consensus. Empty columns deliberately indicate unsupported album-specific production information. Each genre has one separately cited row, including lower-confidence secondary labels.

## `cranberries-everybodyelseisdoingitsowhycantwe` — *Everybody Else Is Doing It, So Why Can’t We?* (1993)

- **Genre hierarchy.** Dream pop first because Robert Christgau literally described the record in those terms, and [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/the-cranberries-everybody-else-is-doing-it-so-why-cant-we/) discusses its dreamlike/ethereal contrast with grunge. AllMusic's adult alternative/alternative pop-rock/alternative indie-rock/Celtic rock/contemporary pop-rock labels are included exactly as named, normalising punctuation only, but ordered behind the sonically more distinctive term. Jangle pop and indie pop are weaker secondary genre tags; the case for jangle is independently supported by [Noel Hogan's Johnny Marr explanation](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/guitarists/noel-hogan-cranberries). Christgau's 'techno-folk-rock' is the sole basis for lower-ranked folk-rock texture: **this is not evidence of techno programming**.
- **Personnel/instruments.** The [original credits](https://cranberriesworld.com/music/discography/everybody-else-is-doing-it-so-why-cant-we/) and [MusicBrainz release](https://musicbrainz.org/release/e9bcc944-9e81-4a4a-80c2-1b271a23608f) identify Dolores O'Riordan (acoustic guitar), Noel Hogan (guitars), Mike Hogan (bass), Fergal Lawler (drums/percussion). MusicBrainz credits electric and acoustic guitar on specific core tracks. No synthesizer, drum-machine, processor, software or orchestra is inferred. **Production empty:** Stephen Street's credits do not independently document a tokenised production technique.
- **Producers.** Stephen Street only. Some sessions with Pearse Gilmore were **scrapped**; the song 'Them' credits Gilmore in the *2002 Complete Sessions* reissue, **not on the 12-track 1993 original**, hence he must not appear in this row.
- **Influences.** [ABC's Noel Hogan interview](https://www.abc.net.au/listen/doublej/music-reads/features/the-cranberries-unlikely-success-noel-hogan/10790326) specifically names the Smiths, the Cure, Echo & the Bunnymen in the band's formative period. [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/guitarists/noel-hogan-cranberries) quotes Hogan's aim to play in Johnny Marr's jangly, rhythmic style. These support **influenced_by**, while The Sundays and Cocteau Twins remain sonic comparisons and are not recorded as claimed influences.
- **Scales.** Energy **2** (many restrained songs, occasional eruptions), electronic **1** (credited guitar-led setup), mood **3** (uplifting Dreams versus yearning ballads), theatrical **2** (normally intimate with expressive spikes), experimental **1** (conventional structures despite unusual vocal inflections), polish **4** (Street's pristine, spacious arrangements), tempo **2**, groove **2** (mostly medium/slow and not club-oriented). Vocals *soft|powerful|clear* are supported by Pitchfork's contrast between breathy and forceful performance. Lyrical heartache, yearning and reflection follow AllMusic's themes.

## `cranberries-noneedtoargue` — *No Need to Argue* (1994)

- **Genre hierarchy.** AllMusic supplies five album-wide named pop/rock and alternative-pop/rock substyles. Dream pop appears last as a qualified texture based on [SPIN's 1994 dream-pop context](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2019/10/cranberries-no-need-to-argue-review/). **Do not label entire album grunge:** the heavy 'Zombie' arrangement is conspicuously exceptional; [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/no-need-to-argue-r204376/review) calls it the album's distorted departure.
- **Instrumentation and production.** [Sleeve](https://cranberriesworld.com/music/discography/no-need-to-argue/) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/9ef84fe9-1f59-31ac-a67a-ebb57a50ff25): electric/acoustic guitars, bass, drums/percussion, Dolores's keyboards; string parts credited as written and directed by her. `distortion` is directly supported on **Zombie only**; `stringarrangements` from the liner, neither is asserted to apply to every song. No assumed synthesizer preset, sampler, DAW or effects chain.
- **Themes/scales.** Love and breakups dominate many tracks; childhood/family reflection informs 'Ode to My Family' and anti-violence/protest informs 'Zombie' and 'The Icicle Melts'. Energy **3** accommodates one striking rock outburst amid calmer tracks; electronic **1** band-led; mood **2** predominantly sorrowful; theatrical **3** unusually forceful vocal accent; experimental **1** largely accessible; polish **4** Street studio; tempo **2**, groove **2** (not dance-oriented). Powerful and soft delivery coexist.
- **Critical disagreement.** [AllMusic's Erlewine](https://www.allmusic.com/album/no-need-to-argue-r204376/review) hears a successful consolidation. [Robert Hilburn, LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-10-02-ca-45376-story.html) hears diminished songwriting revelation despite appealing drama. [Washington Post's Mark Jenkins](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1994/10/19/cranberries-branching-out/074b0bdf-a1ea-4d86-970c-b16f4a868d18/) hears echoes of the Sundays; this informs `sounds_like` only. No source was found **documenting a specific new direct album influence**; `influenced_by` is intentionally empty.

## `cranberries-tothefaithfuldeparted` — *To the Faithful Departed* (1996)

- **Sound, scores, genre.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/to-the-faithful-departed-mw0001989609) describes harder, enlarged rock with fiery, almost punk-like passages; the six tagged genres are the source's exact album styles/genre plus explicit rock, **not a whole-album punk genre**. Critic mentions of earlier Cranberries dream pop explicitly contrast with **this album**, so dream pop is not assigned to it automatically.
- **Personnel/instrument evidence.** [Original liner](https://cranberriesworld.com/music/discography/to-the-faithful-departed/) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/82246e50-4b03-46ac-be4d-57576eef174e) identify band guitars, bass, drums/percussion, keyboards, and mandolin; strings directed by **Michael Kamen** on 'War Child', 'The Rebels', 'Cordell'; Richie/Michael Buckley saxophones and Bruce Fairbairn trumpet on 'Salvation'; credited *whistle* by Dolores; Henry Daag's **musical saw** on 'Bosnia'. `whistle` does not claim a particular type of whistle. `musicalsaw` is a new token for the credited saw. These are **selected tracks**, not pervasive sax/trumpet/saw across the LP.
- **Production.** `stringarrangements` is independently documented by the Kamen orchestration/direction; **Kamen was arranger, not producer**. Producers are **Bruce Fairbairn and The Cranberries**; Mike Plotnikoff was engineer/mixer, not producer. Subsequent compilation-only 'Ave Maria' duet with Pavarotti and the 'Go Your Own Way' cover are NOT on the base 1996 album, nor is 'God Be with You'. Even 'Cordell' varies by original territory edition: only 1996-era recordings count, not added later tracks.
- **Lyrics/ratings.** Loss, Bosnian war, addiction, Lennon murder, childhood and fame justify politics|social|introspection|nostalgia|heartbreak|storytelling. Energy **4**, electronic **1**, mood **1**, theatrical **4**, experimental **2**, polish **4**, tempo **3**, groove **2**: based on larger drums/riffs, rare quieter tributes, orchestral flourishes and serious subjects. The emotionally strained, sometimes growling delivery supports *powerful|raspy|soft* rather than treating raspy as the only register.
- **Critic disagreement must remain visible.** [AllMusic's Neil Z. Yeung](https://www.allmusic.com/album/to-the-faithful-departed-mw0001989609) retrospectively praises the weightier rock move, while [AllMusic's Ned Raggett reviewing the reissue](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/to-the-faithful-departed-the-complete-sessions-1996-1997--mr0000369732), [The Irish Times in May 1996](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/rock-1.45642) and [Christgau](https://www.robertchristgau.com/get_artist.php?id=1405) criticised its bombast and blunt political rhetoric. Landmark=1 is for importance in artistic trajectory, **not positive unanimous reception**; overall confidence medium.

## `cranberries-burythehatchet` — *Bury the Hatchet* (1999)

- **Genre hierarchy.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/bury-the-hatchet-mw0000000810) names adult alternative pop/rock, alternative pop/rock, alternative/indie rock, Celtic rock, contemporary pop/rock and pop/rock. [Secondary album genre listing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_the_Hatchet_%28album%29) adds folk rock, indie pop and jangle pop. These last three are **low-strength** and fall last; do not confuse one song's folk guitar with the entire record being traditional folk. A calmer adult alternative descriptor carries the most weight.
- **Instrumentation/techniques.** [Original sleeve](https://cranberriesworld.com/music/discography/bury-the-hatchet/) and [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/dc911ee7-8549-45a4-816c-9937631881a4) establish guitars, bass, drums, percussion and Dolores's keyboards. [The Irish Times review](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/rock-pop-1.177655) independently identifies **piano in 'Dying in the Sun'**, while AllMusic explicitly reports string and horn chart flourishes; `stringarrangements` and `hornarrangements` are therefore grounded, but neither implies specific keyboard software or a named horn performer. Mixed by Mike Plotnikoff: he is **not a credited producer**. Album producers: **The Cranberries and Benedict Fenner**.
- **Scales.** Energy **2**, electronic **1**, mood **3**, theatrical **2**, experimental **1**, polish **5**, tempo **2**, groove **2**, based on mostly measured, polished mid-tempo pop-rock. The heightened polish reflects AllMusic's explicit immaculately constructed character; it does not imply electronic polish. Themes largely private love and relationships, with isolated socially concerned songs.
- **Critical disagreement.** [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/bury-the-hatchet-mw0000000810) applauds compositional consistency and softer direction, while [Kevin Courtney in The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/rock-pop-1.177655) criticises the songwriting and arrangements severely. It isn't marked landmark because the band's first two albums remain the defining records. `sounds_like` is an editorial resemblance, not a claimed influence or producer credit.

## Editorial scope and omissions

- **Four phase rows only**, and all four appear already in `src/data/catalog.ts`; existing `docs/descriptors/cranberries.csv` returned 404 on the inspected `descriptors` branch.
- **No external live records.** Compilations, alternative masters, the 2002 *Complete Sessions* bonus tracks and expanded 2018/2020/2024 reissues do not get new album rows. Recordings by Dolores O'Riordan as a solo act also belong under a separate artist.
- Do not mistake genre-taxonomy labels `adultalternativepoprock`, `alternativepoprock` and `alternativeindierock` for mutually exclusive sounds; AllMusic assigns multiple labels to the same albums. `celticrock` and `janglepop` are new Fandome tokens with literal sourced labels; no substitution with a similar existing token.
- `sounds_like` is editorial; only the 1993 row has documented influence assertions. Empty `production` or `influenced_by` means **unknown/not researched to a usable source-specific standard** rather than proof of absence.
