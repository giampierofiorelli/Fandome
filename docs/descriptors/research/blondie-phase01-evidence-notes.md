# Blondie — Fandome descriptor evidence notes, Phase 01

Scope: original album configurations only. The 2001 remaster bonus tracks, live bonus recordings and subsequent single mixes are **not** treated as original-album evidence. Scales are independent editorial judgements, not source-reported numeric measurements.

## blondie-blondie — Blondie (1976)
- **Genre order:** AllMusic assigns punk/New York punk, new wave, dance-rock and contemporary pop-rock. William Ruhlmann and Stephen Thomas Erlewine describe the band's 1960s girl-group sound: `girlgrouppop` is a documented **texture**, intentionally last, not evidence of original-era recordings. See D1, D3.
- **Energy 4, tempo 4, groove 3:** Ruhlmann describes an energetic, hard-edged band combining punk and melodic pop; not an unbroken dance record (D1).
- **Electronic 1:** MusicBrainz original-session entries credit Farfisa, piano, Roland synthesizer and electric piano, but foreground a guitar/bass/drum band (D2).
- **Mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 2:** Tender girl-group balladry and sneering punk irony coexist. Harry's performances are deliberately stylized; the early approach favoured direct takes over Chapman's later detailed construction (D1, D4).
- **Voice `clear|powerful|soft`:** Ruhlmann contrasts the rock attack and the deliberately tender 1960s-style material (D1). Vocal categories are qualified listening-based judgements.
- **Instruments:** D2 lists guitar, bass, drum kit, Farfisa organ, piano, electric piano, Roland synth, and percussion. These are **album-era credits**; each instrument need not appear on every track.
- **Production `onetake`:** Stein says Gottehrer's production used a one-take approach on the first two records (D4). This does **not** imply every vocal or overdub was literally first-take.
- **Producers:** Richard Gottehrer is principal producer; MusicBrainz explicitly credits Craig Leon as co-producer of selected early 1976 recordings including “X Offender” and “In the Sun.” Do not reclassify Leon's engineering as production on every track (D2).
- **Lyrics:** Romantic themes, innuendo and arch satire are identified in Ruhlmann's review (D1); `sensuality` is supported by the specific reviewed innuendo, not assumed from Harry's image.
- **Influenced by:** D1 and D3 explicitly attribute the 1960s Shangri-Las/Crystals girl-group inheritance to the record's idiom; artists in `sounds_like` beyond those are editorial sonic comparisons.
- **Landmark 1:** Foundational group debut, not a claim of greatest commercial success.

## blondie-plasticletters — Plastic Letters (1978)
- **Date:** Keep **1978**, matching catalog.ts and early 1978 Chrysalis promotion and UK chart. MusicBrainz has a US entry with `1977` and some sources document Japanese distribution in late 1977. This is an edition/territory discrepancy rather than a second album; check original release evidence if catalog policy changes (P3–P5).
- **Genres:** AllMusic tags New Wave, Punk, New York Punk, Contemporary Pop/Rock, Dance-Rock (P1). Chrysalis's contemporary press kit calls it hard-edged rock and roll, hence `rockandroll` (P3). Do not infer disco from the “Once I Had a Love” demo on later bonus editions.
- **Energy 4, tempo 4, groove 3, electronic 1:** Fast guitar-based performance remains dominant; organ and synth are a smaller part of the arrangement, as confirmed in session credits (P1–P3).
- **Mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 2:** The set spans humorous observations, spy-story situations and hook-driven songs. Reviews dispute the songwriting success rather than the existence of these approaches (P3, P6).
- **Voice `clear|powerful`:** Lead-vocal credit for Harry (P2), assessed in context of punkier arrangements; no claimed specialised vocal processing.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz credits guitars, bass, drums, Farfisa, grand piano, Polymoog, Roland synth and vibraphone (P2). `mallets` reflects specifically credited vibraphone. The MusicBrainz broad album instrumentation can be repeated over track entries; it is **not** a claim that vibraphone and Polymoog sound on every song. “Strings” assigned to keyboardist Destri is ambiguous, so no acoustic-strings token.
- **Production `onetake|polymoog`:** The one-take approach is named in Stein interview (D4). Polymoog is directly credited on this album (P2). `polymoog` is a machine token; the separate `synth` token belongs in instruments.
- **Producers:** Gottehrer credited; Rob Freeman's engineering does not make him a producer (P2).
- **Lyrics:** `love`, `irony`, `storytelling` from “Denis”, the humorous “I'm on E” description and spy narratives in the contemporary press (P3). A track-specific subject ranks as secondary.
- **Influenced by:** Empty: no sufficiently direct, album-specific influence attribution established beyond genre kinship. `sounds_like` is editorial.
- **Critic disagreement:** The 1978 Chrysalis press kit champions the record; retrospective Ruhlmann is significantly less positive. Confidence high on factual sound/credits, not on critical consensus (P3, P6).
- **Landmark 0:** Important transitional studio album, not as defining as the debut or Parallel Lines.

## blondie-parallellines — Parallel Lines (1978)
- **Genres:** Overall new-wave/pop-rock/pop record with an especially notable power-pop quality (L1–L2). `disco` applies chiefly to “Heart of Glass”; it belongs last. Punk/New York punk remains a lesser stylistic trace (L1, L7).
- **Energy 4, tempo 4, groove 4:** Up-tempo rock singles and the prominent dance hit give it a strong rhythmic profile; groove 4 does not mean all tracks are four-on-the-floor (L1, L7).
- **Electronic 2:** The core is electric guitars, bass, live drums and keyboards; “Heart of Glass” introduces a more electronic rhythm apparatus (L3–L5, L8).
- **Mood 4, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4:** Bright, punchy hooks and highly deliberate Chapman arrangements; unusual rhythmic programming on one major track but largely accessible songwriting (L1, L5–L6).
- **Voice `clear|powerful|soft`:** Harry's forceful delivery contrasts with softer melodic songs; this is a qualified listening-scale assessment, not a technical microphone claim (L1).
- **Instruments:** Band credit names guitars, bass, drums, electronic keyboards and Robert Fripp's guest guitar (L8). Harry and Burke discuss synth/rhythm-box additions, and MOJO identifies a sequencer in “Heart of Glass” (L3–L5). `drummachine|sequencer` are **track-limited** but historically significant; do not treat these as the instrumentation of all 12 original cuts.
- **Production `rolandcr78|analogtape|overdubbing`:** Burke identifies the CR-78 (L4); Chapman describes tape-era sync difficulties and repeated separate tracking (L5–L6). The token `analogtape` refers to the recorded multitrack workflow, **not** later digital remasters.
- **Influenced by:** In Harry's own account, Chic, Donna Summer and Giorgio Moroder were inspirations for the disco direction, especially “Heart of Glass” (L3). They influenced **one key sound**; not all of the LP.
- **Lyrics:** From source-discussed songs: desire/romantic fixation (“One Way or Another”), remembered relationships and cheeky irony (L1, L6). The themes and scales reflect editorial synthesis rather than musicological measurements.
- **Producer:** Mike Chapman alone (L8); do not list engineers or Nicky Chinn.
- **Landmark 1:** Ruhlmann, the Guardian and multiple retrospective interviews place it as the breakthrough and defining LP (L1, L7).

## blondie-eattothebeat — Eat to the Beat (1979)
- **Genres:** Album-level new wave and pop/rock/dance-rock (E1). Apple Music additionally names power pop and points to punk, disco, reggae, funk, Motown and girl groups (E3). `disco|reggae|funk|girlgrouppop|motown` are lower-ranking because they describe individual tracks or stylistic colour; reggae specifically concerns “Die Young Stay Pretty” (E5).
- **Energy 4, tempo 4, groove 4:** High-intensity drumming and disco-rock tracks coexist with quieter pieces, so the album is energetic without a uniformly rapid tempo (E1, E3, E5).
- **Electronic 2:** Original recording credits show guitar, drums, bass and keyboard band. Do not assume that the drum machine used on Parallel Lines was reused here (E2).
- **Mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4:** Styles vary, including wistful “Union City Blue” and frenetic “Dreaming”; Chapman continued a studio-polished approach (E3–E4). The theatrical and experimental scores are editorial judgements.
- **Voice `clear|powerful|soft`:** Harry's varied delivery is reflected in fast rock, dreamy new-wave songs and lullaby material (E3, E5).
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz confirms guitars, Nigel Harrison bass, Clem Burke drums, Destri keyboards and Randy Hennes harmonica on the title song (E2). Harmonica is a **one-track detail**, not a dominant instrumental base.
- **Production:** Blank deliberately. Producer and studio locations are proven, but no specific machine, vocal-treatment process or eligible production-technique token was established for **this** album; no copy-forward from Parallel Lines.
- **Producer:** Mike Chapman only; Peter Coleman and David Tickle are engineers (E2, E4).
- **Influenced by:** Apple Music specifically calls out ABBA's “Dancing Queen” as an inspiration for “Dreaming” and Ennio Morricone as a stylistic reference in the album (E3). These are **limited influences**, not general collaborators.
- **Lyrics:** Love/longing, reflection and irony supported by reviewed “Dreaming”, “Union City Blue” and album tonal contrasts (E3, E5); more diffuse than debut.
- **Critic disagreement:** AllMusic's Ruhlmann considers this a weaker reiteration of Parallel Lines and diffuse stylistically; Apple Music and Ian Wade value its variety, power-pop vitality and production (E1, E3–E4).
- **Landmark 1:** Recognisable Dreaming/Atomic/Union City Blue-era album and major part of 1979–80 chart identity, although less critically consensual than Parallel Lines.

## Cross-album methodological caveats
- `newyorkpunk`, `girlgrouppop`, `motown` and equipment/process tokens `onetake`, `polymoog`, `rolandcr78` are new; add to the authoritative token lists, genre family graph, validator and tests before validation. All are documented in the accompanying genre/source files.
- Separate named performers from **credited producers**; track-specific co-production is noted explicitly for the debut.
- Some MusicBrainz recording relationships repeat album-level session instrument lists across tracks. The presence of an instrument is supported at album scope, not frequency or every track.
- `sounds_like` is an openly editorial non-causal comparison; do not import it as `influenced_by`.
- The scores and genre order are proposal-level editorial assessments; status is deliberately `draft` for all rows.
