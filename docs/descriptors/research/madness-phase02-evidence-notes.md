# Madness — Fandome phase 02 evidence notes
Research checked **28 October 2026**. Original LP/CD editions, not deluxe bonus tracks or live LPs. All 8 scales are editorial judgments informed by the cited descriptions, not measurements.

## Keep Moving (1984) — `madness-keepmoving`

**Original album and styles:** The original UK 1984 studio album, distinct from the alternate US track-list configuration and later expanded reissues. Ska, sophisti-pop, reggae and blue-eyed soul are specifically named as genres in the album overview; AllMusic also lists new wave, punk/new wave, ska revival and contemporary pop/rock. The `leftfieldpop` tail is ONLY for the song *Michael Caine*, per *Classic Pop*. The latter's Bowie-like vocals are a critic comparison, hence `sounds_like=David Bowie`, not `influenced_by`.

**Instruments and production:** Original LP documentation lists Mike Barson keyboards, Chris Foreman guitar, Mark Bedford bass, Lee Thompson saxophone, Dan Woodgate drums/triggers, Luis Jardim percussion and TKO Horns. The original vinyl credit names David Bedford for **string arrangements**; this supports `strings` and `stringarrangements` even if a short secondary credits extract omits individual string players. AllMusic additionally lists harmonica among the original LP instrumental credits. `triggers` does *not* establish a LinnDrum or another particular model: no `drumprogramming`, `linndrum` or `synthprogramming` is assigned to this album. The single features spoken contributions from actor Michael Caine, which justify subordinate `spoken`. Langer and Winstanley are credited producers.

**Scores:** `energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3` reflect moderate pacing and retained bounce despite an increased soul/pop focus; `electronic=2` denotes amplified band and keyboards, not an undocumented synth lead. `mood=3` blends light melodies and darker topics, `theatrical=3` for character portraits without the earlier vaudeville excess, `experimental=3` for an adventurous pop/soul turn, `polish=4` for studio arrangement and string parts. The BBC/spy-film-style *Michael Caine* should not alone dictate the album genre or mood.

**Themes:** *One Better Day* homelessness, *Time for Tea* glue sniffing and tragedy, *Give Me a Reason* domestic abuse, *Michael Caine* stylised spy narrative. Social storytelling, irony, love and nostalgia are song-level themes. `influenced_by` is blank: a Bowie *comparison* is not a band-confirmed album influence.

## Mad Not Mad (1985) — `madness-madnotmad`

**Styles and sound:** Pop, new wave and two-tone appear in the album overview; AllMusic adds ska revival, contemporary pop/rock and punk/new wave style labels. The album's subtle reggae feels are noted in AllMusic criticism. *Classic Pop* identifies **bossa nova** on *Yesterday’s Men*, **calypso** on *Uncle Sam* and faux-**Motown** on *Burning the Boats*; these belong in the genre tail, not as album-wide identities. The record trades much of Barson's keyboard warmth for programmed beats and mid-1980s synth textures. Do not add `synthpop` as a genre purely from the presence of synthesizers without a genre-specific source; the notes may describe synth-pop *textures*, not a taxonomic album label.

**Original liner credits:** Guitar Chris Foreman, bass Mark Bedford, sax Lee Thompson; Steve Nieve keyboards, Roy Davies piano, Judd Lander harmonica, Luís Jardim percussion, Gary Barnacle horns, David Bedford musical direction with named string players. Dan Woodgate is **explicitly credited with programming the LinnDrum and keyboard sequences**, as well as drums, by the reproduced sleeve notes (spelled Lynn Drum there); this supports `drummachine|synth|drumprogramming|linndrum|programming`. `stringarrangements` reflects named Bedford string leadership/arrangements. The mere existence of a horn section is not enough evidence for the particular production token `hornarrangements`, so it is excluded. The primary actual producers are Clive Langer and Alan Winstanley; **Matt Howe, Mark Saunders, Richard Sullivan** receive assistance/engineering credits in other sources, not primary album production. Do not copy Musicbase's ambiguous Matt Howe 'producer' without reconciling the original sleeve.

**Scores:** `electronic=4` reflects the shift to programmed drums and sequences; `polish=4` the refined, somewhat clinical 1985 production; `energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3` mixed bossa nova/soul/new wave arrangements. `mood=2` includes regret, ageing, and pointed satire, `theatrical=3` for continuing character songs, `experimental=2` because the production is fashionable rather than exploratory.

**Themes/comparisons:** *Yesterday's Men* ageing and lost opportunity; *Uncle Sam* US political satire; *Burning the Boats* privatisation; *The Sweetest Girl* is a **cover of Scritti Politti**, so Scritti Politti (Songs to Remember) is a sonic comparison, **not** an influence. `influenced_by` left blank, no direct album-specific artist declaration found.

## Wonderful (1999) — `madness-wonderful`

**Styles:** A real 1999 studio album, not the contemporaneous live *Universal Madness* nor a greatest-hits set. Ska/pop album tags, new wave / ska revival / punk-new-wave / contemporary pop-rock metadata, original release's pop-rock and reggae descriptors. *Classic Pop* expressly refers to *Elysium* as **chamber pop**; put it after the dominant styles. The 1999 set's first pressing includes 11 tracks; no deluxe bonus singles were imported.

**Credits:** Original Virgin UK CD names Barson keyboards, Foreman guitar, Bedford bass, Woodgate drums, Thompson saxophone, brass players Jason McDermid (trumpet) and Mike Kearsey (trombone), Pablo Cook percussion, string orchestra (London Session Orchestra) and Simon Hale arranging/conducting strings, plus Steve Donnelley on **dobro** in *The Wizard*. Terry Edwards credited for brass arrangements on specific tracks; both `hornarrangements` and `stringarrangements` are directly supported. Langer and Winstanley are credited album producers. Ian Dury guests on *Drip Fed Fred*, Sarah Cracknell on *Elysium*; neither a featured guest nor a cover origin is automatically an `influenced_by` entry. The Dobro is confined to one track and therefore last.

**Scores:** `electronic=2` studio band and keys; `polish=4` arranged brass/strings; `theatrical=4` distinctive character material and animated vocal narration. `energy=3, tempo=3, groove=3` combine buoyant singles with a contemplative second half; `mood=3` bright ska with loneliness/alcoholism stories; `experimental=2` because the album mostly pursues familiar songcraft.

**Themes and comparisons:** *Lovestruck* alcoholism, *4am* loneliness, *Drip Fed Fred* gangster satire, *Elysium* chamber-pop reflection. *4am* was rewritten/re-recorded after Suggs's solo debut, not a new co-billed collaboration. Ian Dury is in `sounds_like` only (an explicitly noted Madness sonic model); he actually sings on this record, but his participation **alone** is not evidence of influence. The Kinks comparison reflects English domestic and street storytelling, not direct testimony about this particular LP.

## The Dangermen Sessions Vol. 1 (2005) — `madness-thedangermensessionsvol1`

**Type, identity and genres:** Issued officially as **Madness**, studio album of 13 tracks (2005), despite the performance pseudonym The Dangermen and the cover concept. Not a live album and NOT a compilation of previously released recordings. Contemporary Guardian review confirms songs were *recorded in a studio* after Camden pub shows. Official Madness product description calls it ska/reggae covers; rocksteady is named by retailer synopsis, and the *Exclaim!* review hears dub accents on *Israelites* plus a 2 Tone-related British club ska feel. Pop/rock and punk/new-wave are low-weight AllMusic general style tags. Studio album designation follows the band's official site, not a genre guess.

**Credits and disputed production:** Original album credits attribute production **by track** to Dennis Bovell (1–7,9,11,13), Steve Dub and John 'Segs' Jennings (1,10,12), and Madness (1,8). Their names all belong in the `producers` field. The *Exclaim!* writer inaccurately claimed Langer and Winstanley produced the 2005 LP; contemporaneous original record personnel supersede that claim, so they are **excluded** from the 2005 producer column. Instrumentation includes standard seven-piece guitar/bass/drums/keyboards/sax, horn guests, programmed synth, David Bedford string arrangements with violinists, Dennis Bovell timbales and steel pans; `programming|stringarrangements` only. A spoken intro on *I Chase the Devil* supports secondary `spoken`; this does not mean all songs are narrated.

**Scores:** `energy=3, tempo=3, groove=4` a more relaxed but rhythm-centred ska/reggae set; `electronic=2` live-band sound supplemented by specific programming, `mood=4` the music is often playful despite some heavy political/source lyrics, `theatrical=3` playful alter ego presentation, `experimental=2` largely straightforward Jamaican covers, `polish=3` cleaner studio recordings than the raucous pub renditions but not glossy.

**Influence rules:** Prince Buster is a documented formative influence on Madness in phase 01's artist-wide sources, but merely covering him **here** does not justify album-specific `influenced_by`. Likewise Desmond Dekker, Max Romeo, Bob Marley, The Kinks and The Supremes are **covered artists**, not automatically production influences. *AllMusic* explicitly compares the approach to UB40's *Labour of Love*, so that belongs in `sounds_like`. Some track origins are reggae reinterpretations of other songs rather than the famous original pop version, per Suggs; preserve the skanked arrangements.

**Edition warning:** 2023 expanded edition has additional dub/bonus tracks; its songs *Shame & Scandal (Dub)* and *Girl Why Don't You? (Dub)* cannot be used to exaggerate the original LP's dub character. The source's review of *Israelites*, on the actual 2005 edition, is the foundation for the `dub` tail genre.
