# INXS — Phase 01: evidence notes (28 October 2026)

## Method and editorial distinction

Descriptors are original, provisional assessments calibrated to Fandome's 0–5 anchors. They are not quotes or measured audio analyses. Genre styles have **per-tag sources** in `genre_evidence.csv`. Instruments require credited personnel or track-specific editorial observations; no machine model is supplied from genre alone. The scene is Sydney's touring pub-rock / new-wave crossover, not a fixed genre. All records are in English and all phase rows are marked `draft`.

**Where sources conflict:** historical local release dates take priority over digital metadata. A first-person musician or producer record takes priority on specific production/influence claims; a reviewer can establish *sounds_like* but not *influenced_by*. AllMusic style tags sometimes function as radio/market labels; these appear late in the genre list and are marked `album style tag` rather than falsely treating them as dominant sound.

## 1. `inxs-inxs` — *INXS* (1980)

**Release and people:** the official band site identifies 13 October 1980, co-producers **Duncan McGuire and INXS**, and instruments for the original six musicians (including bassist Garry Beers' **double bass**, Kirk Pengilly's **saxophone**, Andrew Farriss' keys/guitar). https://www.inxs.com/music/inxs/ . Original LP pressing also lists McGuire and INXS as producers: https://www.shugarecords.com/products/inxs-inxs-lp-new-sealed-90184-1-y-atco-1984-usa-vinyl-record .

**Genres:** critics identify new wave and Jamaican ska throughout; Stereogum cites 'Doctor', 'Jumping', 'Body Language' as ska-oriented, 'On A Bus' as pop-rock, and 'Newsreel Babies' as new wave. Synth-pop is not inferred simply because there is a keyboard: AllMusic explicitly calls the early record a new-wave synth-pop variation, and Stereogum notes a specific synth line in 'Learn To Smile'. https://stereogum.com/1814274/inxs-albums-from-worst-to-best/franchises/counting-down ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/r9873/review . Remaining radio/marketing genre tags come from AllMusic's album styles.

**Instruments / production:** guitar, keyboards, electric bass, acoustic double bass, saxophone and drums follow official personnel; synthesizer has the specific 'Learn To Smile' review support. Production deliberately left **empty**; nocturnal sessions and limited budgets do not establish any precise machines, tape processing or technique. Tim Farriss' later reference to old tape machines describes the pre-1982 sessions in the aggregate, not a definitive equipment list for this exact record. https://kingfm.com/ixp/295/p/inxs-tim-farriss-interview-2025/

**Scales:** energy 3 from brisk but less heavy early arrangements; electronic 2 from synthesis/keys within live band; mood 3 for mixed lively and tense songs; theatrical 2 (Hutchence not yet in his later huge presentation); experimental 2 for shifting genres; polish 2 for uneven early recording; tempo 3, groove 3 for ska bounce and some dance-pop traces. These are review-informed editorial calibrations, not external measurements.

**Voice/lyrics/similarity:** reviewers describe Hutchence learning to control his voice; `clear|raspy` is a cautious early-vocal interpretation. No lyric tags from insufficient album-specific discussion. XTC sonic parallel is documented in the debut's retrospective discussion; Joe Jackson and Graham Parker are *Trouser Press* comparisons; Adam Ant is a **vocal** comparison by Stereogum. None is a self-reported influence, so `influenced_by` stays blank. https://trouserpress.com/reviews/inxs/

**Critical dissent:** Stereogum sees interesting foreshadowing, whereas *Trouser Press* and AllMusic see an unfocused or unremarkable debut; this is why `landmark=0`, `confidence=medium`, not a declaration that the record lacks influence.

## 2. `inxs-underneaththecolours` — *Underneath the Colours* (1981)

**Producer, release and personnel:** INXS official site dates 19 October 1981. AllMusic credits **Richard Clapton** alone as producer, Tim Farriss additionally with synthesizer, and the basic guitar/keys/sax/bass/drums/percussion lineup. The album's original pressing also credits Pengilly sax and Jon Farriss percussion. https://www.inxs.com/music/underneath-the-colours/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/underneath-the-colours-mw0000201402 ; https://recordsale.de/en/artists/inxs/albums/underneath-the-colours

**Genre-specific findings:** Album of the Year tags pop-rock, new wave and post-punk. AllMusic lists dance-rock and other long-tail styles. Stereogum describes a **funky bass part on 'Stay Young'**, lingering ska bounce there, and more stripped post-punk in the sound while the band is beginning to emphasise rock guitar. `funk` and `ska` are explicitly lower-priority **some-tracks** tags, not album-wide assertions. https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/18336-inxs-underneath-the-colours.php ; https://stereogum.com/1814274/inxs-albums-from-worst-to-best/franchises/counting-down

**Scales:** energy 3, electronic 2, mood 2, theatrical 2, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 3, groove 3. A more coherent yet still uneven performance: slightly more studio polish than debut, balanced guitars and synths, funk bassist style but not yet dense club music. The mix of sparse and punchy songs is why danceability isn't rated 4.

**Voice / lyrics / analogies:** clearer and occasionally rough-edged male lead (`clear|raspy`); `sensuality` is supported by Stereogum's specific assessment of title-track vocal/textual delivery. Bowie comparison comes directly from Stereogum's hearing of 1970s Bowie on this record, so belongs solely to `sounds_like`. XTC is a more tentative editorial early post-punk parallel. No documented album-specific band influence was located; `influenced_by` blank.

**Production left blank:** Clapton's improved recording fidelity does not demonstrate a specific studio process like compression or noise-gating. *Trouser Press* says drums are isolated in the mix; other reviewers prefer this album's audio quality. Do not turn this dispute into a technique token. `confidence=medium`.

## 3. `inxs-shaboohshoobah` — *Shabooh Shoobah* (1982)

**Producer / edition:** 16 October 1982 on the band site; **Mark Opitz** is sole credited producer. Original vinyl credits independently list Opitz and the six players; 'The One Thing' used Paradise Studios, remaining original tracks Rhinoceros Recordings. https://www.inxs.com/music/shabooh-shoobah/ ; https://albumlinernotes.com/Shabooh_Shoobah.html

**Genres, scope, and instrumental detail:** Main new-wave/post-punk/pop-rock energy is corroborated by AllMusic and Stereogum. Apple Music identifies the bass/guitar dance push, synth on 'The One Thing', **rototoms** and sax on 'Golden Playpen', and a final ska trace on **'Black and White'**; thus `ska` is present but last. `funk` likewise refers to specific syncopated arrangements, not a fully funk-based album. The editorial *postpunk* designation comes from Stereogum's album-wide description; the AllMusic 'college rock' and other tags are taxonomic footnotes. https://music.apple.com/us/album/shabooh-shoobah/828785109 ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000650283 ; https://stereogum.com/1814274/inxs-albums-from-worst-to-best/franchises/counting-down

**Voice / lyrics / scales:** stronger but rasp-tinged Hutchence with melodic clear phrasing: `powerful|raspy|clear`. Stereogum interprets 'Jan's Song' as indigenous/social concerns, 'Golden Playpen' as celebration of drunkenness, and the album highlights love/sensuality. `love|sensuality|party|social` lists album themes without pretending every track is political. Energy 4, electronic 2, mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 4, groove 4: better recorded/mixed, more energetic and syncopated, despite several restrained tracks. `landmark=1` as their first widely released international breakthrough; AllMusic and official site confirm.

**Production:** despite improved studio technology as Tim Farriss recounts, neither machine model nor exact recording treatment is established in the reviewed original credits. Leave **production blank** rather than infer `analogtape`, `drumprogramming`, `tr808`, or `heavyreverb` from era/style. No specific outside musical influence is directly attributed to this record. `sounds_like` U2/The Police reflects an editorial post-punk/new-wave sonic comparison only, not influence documentation.

## 4. `inxs-theswing` — *The Swing* (1984)

**Split production:** *Original Sin* was produced by **Nile Rodgers**, sung with backing vocals from guest **Daryl Hall**, and recorded in New York. The remainder was produced by **Nick Launay** in the UK, with other studio work credited. The producer order reflects nine Launay tracks against one Rodgers track, **not** perceived fame. https://www.inxs.com/music/the-swing/ ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/the-swing/828784636 ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000190409

**Genres / texture:** AllMusic categorises dance-rock, new wave, club/dance and rock variants; Apple and uDiscover describe the larger band transition toward funk-rock, polished pop and dance rhythms. uDiscover explicitly labels 'Original Sin' a **funk-pop** hybrid (`popfunk`, limited track scope); post-punk is historical texture, not the dominant weight. The original album contains both relatively aggressive tracks and slower 'Burn For You'/'Johnson’s Aeroplane' passages, so mood and tempo remain mixed. Sources: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/inxs-the-swing-album/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/mw0000190409

**Instruments / arrangements:** physical LP credit transcription names Andrew Farriss / William Motzing for **string arranging**; period reviews identify actual orchestral/string color on 'Johnson’s Aeroplane'. Kirk Pengilly's sax, guitars, bass, drums/percussion and keys are sourced from LP credits; no instrument model is inferred. https://recordsale.de/en/artists/inxs/albums/the-swing ; https://www.warewolfreviews.com/inxs-reviews/the-swing-inxs-review

**Production token detail:** `stringarrangements` is robustly credited. `digitalreverb` is **new token**; the producer Nick Launay discusses the recognizably digital reverbs in this precise INXS album's sound, and explains widespread 1980s digital-effects adoption. But his equipment examples (Eventide Harmonizer / Lexicon / AMS / Yamaha DX7) are explicitly a wider period comparison. **Do not add these exact machines as definitely recorded on The Swing.** If Claude's policy requires an explicit machine-by-machine session log even for the generic digital reverb effect, keep only `stringarrangements` and remove `digitalreverb`. https://tapeop.com/interviews/105/nick-launay

**Influences vs. analogy:** Launay specifically says **Talking Heads and XTC** were musical influences on *The Swing*: album-specific `influenced_by` is therefore warranted. Andrew Farriss additionally invokes **Blondie** as trailblazers he wanted to follow, in 2017 reissue recollections; lower-confidence than Launay's explicit statement. These names also can appear in `sounds_like` only for independent aesthetic judgment; the same artist may legitimately occur in both categories. **Chic** is listed only as a funk-guitar *sonic comparison*, not an influence automatically inferred from Rodgers producing the opening song. **Midnight Oil** is a specific Stereogum analogy for 'Dancing On The Jetty', not a documented influence. **Duran Duran** is editorial resemblance of the dance-rock/new-wave crossover, not a claimed direct influence. https://tapeop.com/interviews/105/nick-launay ; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/inxs-the-swing-album/ ; https://stereogum.com/1814274/inxs-albums-from-worst-to-best/franchises/counting-down

**Lyrical evidence:** Original Sin concerns interracial love/racial prejudice, independently covered by Australia's National Film and Sound Archive and label Rhino. 'Dancing on the Jetty' is described as protest and social conflict by Stereogum. Thus `social|political|love|sensuality`; avoid reducing the entire LP to a protest work. https://www.nfsa.gov.au/stories/articles/inxs-swing-40th-anniversary ; https://www.rhino.com/article/single-stories-inxs-original-sin

**Scales and dissent:** energy 4, electronic 3, mood 3, theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 4, tempo 4, groove 5. Very danceable rhythm section with bright/dark contrasts; recording is polished, but still much more live-band guitar/bass/drum than fully electronic pop. Some critics judge it slicker and less cohesive than *Shabooh Shoobah* while others mark it a major artistic advance. NFSA's Andrew Farriss recollection suggests doubts about overproduction; Hutchence admired Launay's sound. `landmark=1`, `confidence=high` for core facts and medium for named digital effect and Blondie link.
