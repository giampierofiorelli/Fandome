# Jennifer Lopez — Phase 01: evidence and scoring notes

## Scope and method
Album-level scores are original editorial judgments on the guide's 0–5 anchors, not measurements. Each rating below is tied to reviews and credits for this album; no rating is inherited from Jennifer Lopez's general reputation. A genre appearing on only one track is placed late with scope `some tracks`/`texture` in the separate genre evidence file. A credited instrument may be heard on an individual track; the descriptor is not a track-by-track instrumentation grid.

### On the 6 (1999) — `jlo-onthe6`
**Key sources:** Heather Phares, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/on-the-6-mw0000238554); Elysa Gardner, Los Angeles Times, 30 May 1999 (https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1999-may-30-ca-42384-story.html); original CD MusicBrainz detailed personnel (https://musicbrainz.org/release/ce526821-a483-3554-a7aa-693520257063); Epic/Sony track metadata on Qobuz (https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/on-the-6-jennifer-lopez/0888880316592).

- **Genre order:** Latin pop first for the crossover identity, then dance-pop, R&B and pop. Adult contemporary comes from the catalogue styling and numerous smooth ballads. Latin soul/soul is explicitly associated with Lopez's characterization; hip-hop is a more limited presence tied to rap-inflected tracks and collaborations. Latin is the umbrella genre. In a 30 May 1999 LA Times review, salsa, flamenco and disco are each identified on particular cuts, and therefore appear last as track-level genres/textures.
- **Eight scales:** energy **3** (ballads temper the energetic second half); electronic **3** (programmed dance/R&B tracks alongside acoustic Latin sections); mood **4** (many sunny, assertive passages; some romantic melancholy); theatrical **2** (polished but generally conversational delivery); experimental **1** (standard late-1990s song structures); polish **5** (multistudio pop album with extensive studio treatment); tempo **3** (ballad and club mixture); groove **4** (dance singles and Latin rhythms account for a substantial share, though not every song).
- **Voice/lyrics/language:** soft and clear lead vocals; the duet *No Me Ames* and Spanish rendition *Una Noche Más* support `es` alongside `en`. Love and sensuality dominate; celebration in *Let's Get Loud* and self-assertion/identity elsewhere. Guest rappers are not coded as Lopez's principal vocal technique.
- **Instruments:** credited keyboards (Lawrence P. Dermer, Eric Kupper), piano (Paquito Echavarria, Alvin West), Latin percussion (Edwin Bonilla), saxophone (Kenny Anderson), trumpet (Randall Barlow/Douglas Michels), trombone (Hernan Mulet), guitar (René Toledo), acoustic guitar (Manny López), violin (Jack Stamates), plus recorded bass, strings and drum tracks. These are **not** deduced from the Latin-pop label.
- **Production:** sampling on *Feelin' So Good* is documented against Strafe's *Set It Off*; drum programming is supported by the recordings' programmed beats/rhythm credits. `overdubbing` is evidenced by documented separately recorded strings/vocals on individual tracks. No unverified named hardware.
- **Producers:** Rodney Jerkins; Poke & Tone (Trackmasters); Cory Rooney as a *co-producer* where credited; Alvin West; Sean Combs; Emilio Estefan Jr.; Kike Santander; Lawrence P. Dermer; Ric Wake; Darrell Branch/Lance Rivera; Dan Shea and Juan Vicente Zambrano (specific tracks/editions). Do **not** count Jennifer Lopez solely because she is credited executive producer. Names are not general album-only credits.
- **Critic split:** Phares respects the club/Latin blend while pointing out similarities among the opening ballads. Overall high confidence on production/sonic direction. `influenced_by` empty: comparative genre origins or samples are not proof of specific artistic influence.

### J.Lo (2001) — `jlo-jlo`
**Key sources:** Stephen Thomas Erlewine, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/jlo-mw0000103727); Sal Cinquemani, Slant, 11 Feb 2001 (https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/jennifer-lopez-j-lo/); Natalie Nichols, LA Times, 21 Jan 2001 (https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-jan-21-ca-14899-story.html); J.Lo MusicBrainz release and track personnel (https://musicbrainz.org/release/a31f319a-75a2-3c1e-9c1a-82d3b630cc4f).

- **Genre order:** dance-pop and R&B dominate in two contemporary reviews; Latin pop, funk, hip-hop and electronic-dance textures were identified by LA Times, with adult contemporary applying mostly to the ballads. Genres are not limited to pop because catalogues often group everything under Pop/Rock.
- **Eight scales:** energy **4** (club singles are abundant, though interspersed with slow material); electronic **4** (AllMusic singles out programmed, thin/skittering drum-machine texture); mood **4** (bright/independent/romantic blend); theatrical **2** (close rather than show-tune vocal delivery); experimental **1** (mainstream structures and producer-led format); polish **5** (high-budget, multistudio sound); tempo **4** (dance tunes outweigh slow ballads); groove **4** (regular four-square dance pulse and R&B swing).
- **Vocals and production:** `soft|clear`; dense backing harmonies and vocal arrangements discussed by Slant justify `layeredvocals`. Original J.Lo personnel identifies drum-machine and keyboard programming on *I'm Gonna Be Alright*; the same track credits a Sugarhill Gang sample, hence `drumprogramming|sampling`. No Auto-Tune tag inferred from era.
- **Instrumentation:** original track credits name keyboard and drum-machine programming; guitars, piano, trumpet, trombone, bass, and percussion appear on specified Latin/R&B tracks. AllMusic's critique of thin drum-machines also corroborates the electronic score.
- **Language:** English and Spanish: LA Times identifies both; tracks including *Cariño*, *Dame* and *Si Ya Se Acabó* substantiate it.
- **Producers:** Ric Wake, Cory Rooney, Troy Oliver, Sean Combs, Mario Winans, Kip Collins, Dan Shea, Jose Sanchez, Frank Rodriguez and others with explicit original track production roles. **Peter Wade Keusch is not automatically a producer merely because he is an engineer, mixer or additional producer on some tracks.** Jennifer Lopez is described by one reviewer as co-producing part of the album, but the specific MusicBrainz credits consulted often say executive producer; the row deliberately avoids treating that assertion as an unambiguous album-wide formal producer credit.
- **Lyrics:** relationships and sexual desire; self-worth/identity in *Love Don't Cost a Thing* and *That's Not Me*. `sounds_like` is editorial: Janet Jackson and Madonna comparisons are made directly by reviewers; those references stay out of `influenced_by`.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Slant calls the retro styling lively yet argues the record is incohesive; AllMusic finds too few hooks for its length and more competent dance tracks than ballads. These reservations help set `experimental=1` but do not make `polish` low.

### J to tha L-O!: The Remixes (2002) — `jlo-jtothalotheremixes`
**Key sources:** Sal Cinquemani, Slant, 1 Feb 2002 (https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/jennifer-lopez-j-to-tha-l-o/); William Ruhlmann, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/j-to-tha-l-o-the-remixes-r572119); detailed 2002 CD release relations MusicBrainz (https://musicbrainz.org/release/6ea874cb-efa9-4e77-94e9-71b2f2100084); Guinness World Records (https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/78361-most-successful-remix-album); NME/Dele Fadele (https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-nme-6225-331785).

- **Why include:** not a regular compilation. These rebuilt mixes were central to the 2001–02 hip-hop/dance crossover and the remix album reached US No. 1, an unprecedented result. Canonical scope is the **11-track US version**.
- **Genre order:** hip-hop/rap-R&B revision dominates the first four tracks, dance-pop/house and Latino house occur in the club-mix sequence, and Hi-NRG is an **individual-track** label for Hex Hector's version of *Waiting for Tonight*. `latinhouse` is a new exact term from the NME review. The adult-contemporary ballad *Alive* is an exception. Other territories append Spanish-language remixes and must not be merged silently.
- **Eight scales:** energy **4** (vigorous opening rap and club segment, offset by *Alive*); electronic **4** (remix programming and club electronics, but some live/sampled parts); mood **3** (assertive and celebratory singles mixed with darker remixes and final ballad); theatrical **2** (guest MC features rather than theatrical lead singing); experimental **2** (song reconstructions with new vocal/rap material yet familiar pop structures); polish **4** (radio-ready remixes with disparate sources); tempo **4** (high in the club sequence; slower hip-hop tracks); groove **4** (not every track is literally club-ready, contrary to a generic dance-remix assumption).
- **Voice:** soft pop lead and **rap** by prominent guests, with clear lead phrases; order represents role of vocal styles across compilation, not a claim that J.Lo herself raps.
- **Instrument/production evidence:** original MusicBrainz credits bass on the *I'm Real* Murder mix (Marty Osterer) and string arrangement for *Alive* by Bill Ross; Slant directly describes acoustic guitars on Metro's *Walking on Sunshine*. Sampling is documented on *I'm Real* (Rick James) and *If You Had My Love* Darkchild remix (Michael Jackson). Production = `sampling|stringarrangements` only; no software or machine model asserted.
- **Producer caveat:** the `remixer` field is **not automatically equivalent to** an original-recording `producer` credit. Irv Gotti, 7 Aurelius, Troy Oliver, Cory Rooney, Poke & Tone, Sean Combs, Mario Winans, Alec DeRuggiero, Peter Wade Keusch, Dan Shea and Richie Jones have identifiable credited production roles across songs. Hex Hector and Pablo Flores may have crafted defining remixes but are only entered in evidence notes unless a *producer/co-producer* role is verified for this specific recording. Metro is also credited primarily as remixer; related additional-production names are separately documented.
- **Disagreement:** Slant values the transformed remixes and accepts a certain adventurousness; AllMusic criticizes rap-led pacing and lyrical mismatches, praising mainly the later club sequence. Hence `confidence=medium` for album-level aggregation.
- **Influences:** Rick James/Michael Jackson appear as *sampled sources*, not documented creative influence for the entire album. `influenced_by` deliberately blank.

### This Is Me… Then (2002) — `jlo-thisismethen`
**Key sources:** Stephen Thomas Erlewine, AllMusic (https://www.allmusic.com/album/this-is-me-then-mw0000229367); Betty Clarke, Guardian, 29 Nov 2002 (https://www.theguardian.com/music/2002/nov/29/popandrock.artsfeatures2); Sal Cinquemani, Slant, 20 Nov 2002 (https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/jennifer-lopez-this-is-me-then/); MusicBrainz CD relations (https://musicbrainz.org/release/9b18c398-8d77-49c9-aa3f-43efd5cdfc9f); Qobuz original Epic/Sony track credits (https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/this-is-methen-jennifer-lopez/k1yve288yyrkc).

- **Genre order:** dominant R&B/urban soul and pop, with explicitly named classic soul, early-1980s pop-funk and soft-rock allusions. Hip-hop belongs mainly to sampled/guest-rap tracks. A contemporary review names a seventies Motown feel and funk basslines. AllMusic retains generic metadata tags for dance-pop and Latin pop, but **its own text explicitly says glitzy dance-pop is reduced**: these tags appear last with `texture` evidence to avoid misrepresenting the record.
- **Eight scales:** energy **3** (subdued romantic midtempo focus and some rap/beat-driven numbers); electronic **3** (live band recordings coexist with samples and programmed drums); mood **3** (sensual confidence with emotional longing); theatrical **2** (vocal intimacy rather than dramatic belting); experimental **1** (conventional pop/R&B structures despite recognizable sampling); polish **5** (refined, layered studio arrangements and mixing); tempo **3** (moderate and ballad-heavy); groove **3** (strong R&B swing but markedly less dance-floor-focused than J.Lo).
- **Instruments:** Omar Hakim credited on acoustic drums, Verdine White on bass, Dan Shea on keys/acoustic guitar, René Toledo and Kamil Rustam on guitars; Larry Gold credited arranging and conducting strings; B-Money specifically credited with turntable scratches on *Jenny from the Block*. These make electronic=3 rather than 5.
- **Production:** `sampling` includes Teddy Pendergrass (*Still*), Schoolly D (*I'm Glad*), Debra Laws (*All I Have*) and the three *Jenny from the Block* referenced recordings documented by MusicBrainz. Troy Oliver's keyboard/drum programming on multiple songs substantiates `drumprogramming`; Larry Gold credited string arrangements on *Still*, *The One* and *Dear Ben* support `stringarrangements`.
- **Voice/lyrics:** soft/clear romantic lead with guest rap (LL Cool J, Jadakiss and Styles P), with lyrical focus on love, sensuality and autobiographical identity. *Dear Ben* anchors the specificity of intimate themes. Language English for original core tracklist.
- **Producers:** Cory Rooney, Troy Oliver, Dan Shea, Davy Deluge, Focus…, Kevin Veney, Loren Hill, Rich Shelton, Reggie Hamlet, David McPherson, Ron G and Poke & Tone have credited production roles. Bruce Swedien and Peter Wade Keusch appear in recording/mixing and are **not** promoted to producers without a production credit.
- **Documented influence:** Betty Clarke in The Guardian explicitly says Lopez’s new style is *based on Diana Ross*, which supports `influenced_by=Diana Ross` for this album (not for the previous discs). Other Motown/Marvin Gaye references are comparative, not separately treated as direct influences.
- **Critical context:** Erlewine highly praises the coherent, lush, soul-oriented record despite dismissing some individual lyrics; The Guardian praises its soul aesthetic but criticizes celebrity-centric writing, whereas Slant is more reserved. Its retreat from dance-pop takes precedence over broad genre tags for ordinal position.

## Cross-album caution
Only Diana Ross is included as a documented album-level influence, based on The Guardian's explicit critique. The other three `influenced_by` cells are blank. Reviewer comparisons and sampled recordings belong to `sounds_like` or `production=sampling`, never automatically to influence. Do not confuse remix performers/MCs with album co-billing: the album artist remains Jennifer Lopez.
