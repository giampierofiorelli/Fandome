# Fandome — Madonna incremental Phase 05: album-specific research notes

Research snapshot: 2026-10-24. **New albums only:** *Rebel Heart* (2015), *Madame X* (2019), and *CONFESSIONS II* (2026). No descriptions or CSV rows from previous batches are copied here.

## Method and cautions

- Independent professional criticism provides observations on sonic characteristics and songwriting; original label/artist credits establish names, dates, samples, instruments and production responsibilities. Producer interviews establish first-person intentional influence. Their roles are not interchangeable.
- The eight 0–5 integers and `landmark` are **Fandome editorial mappings**, not numerical measurements made by the quoted authors. They synthesize multiple **album-specific** observations, not simply the artist's genre. `confidence` reports evidence support for the entire row, not inter-rater calibration; `status=draft` until Claude or a second reviewer checks it.
- **Album versions matter**: *Rebel Heart* has 14-track standard and expanded 19-/25-track configurations; *Madame X* has 13-track standard and 15-track deluxe; *CONFESSIONS II* has 12-track physical standard and 16-track digital/deluxe album. Published reviews often discuss expanded editions. Ratings below are qualitative album-level profiles informed by reviews; this is **not** a numerical track-by-track average. Claude should revisit any values if a variant-specific recommendation engine requires it.
- `sounds_like` means sonic comparison, not creative influence; `influenced_by` requires evidence about creative inputs. Empty is deliberate. A credited collaborator is **not** evidence that the album was influenced by that collaborator. Track-level sample/interpolation claims must not be falsely generalized to all tracks.
- The single-valued `voice` field and single `mood` integer cannot represent all guest voices, vocal effects or contrasts. Keep diversity in notes/evidence; do not conflate nuanced performance with one uniform voice. If the schema evolves, use per-track descriptors.
- The type and release year are factual; numerics, genres, mood, scene and `landmark` are critically evaluated editorial choices. These rows have not undergone blind second-person review.

## `madonna-rebelheart` — Rebel Heart (2015)

Sources: [official credits](https://www.madonna.com/products/rebel-heart); [Jessica Hopper, Pitchfork, 13 March 2015](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/20293-rebel-heart/); [Alexis Petridis, Guardian, 5 March 2015](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/mar/05/madonna-rebel-heart-review); [Sal Cinquemani, Slant, 26 February 2015](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/madonna-rebel-heart/); [Kitty Empire, Observer, 8 March 2015](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/mar/08/madonna-rebel-heart-review-observer-basqued-polemicist).

- **Identity / type:** official artist discography and reviews identify Madonna's 2015 studio album. The album is absent from the six-entry seed snapshot checked in the earlier batch; proposed new ID follows the guide's `artist-id + title slug` rule.
- **Genres = `pop|dance|electronic`:** gospel house on “Living for Love,” EDM/hyperpop-leaning production on “Bitch I'm Madonna,” and a large ballad contingent. The guide cannot encode finer “house” or “EDM” tags; names in musical evidence are descriptive rather than arbitrary new genre tokens.
- **Energy = 3**: some forceful programmed dance cuts, but substantial ballads and restrained songs offset them. Source examples: “Living for Love” vs “Joan of Arc,” “Ghosttown,” “Rebel Heart.”
- **Electronic = 4**: documented synth and drum-programming contributions, EDM and house textures predominate over the full soundscape, though acoustic guitar, piano and orchestra also appear in the album credits; not 5.
- **Mood = 2**: emotional aftermath, insecurity and breakups are prominent in the critics' accounts, despite defiant or celebratory tracks; darker than a uniformly euphoric pop record.
- **Theatrical = 3**: larger-than-life confrontational songs contrast with confessional ballads; standard high-budget dramatic pop rather than uniformly maximalist theatricality.
- **Experimental = 2**: “Bitch I'm Madonna,” “Illuminati” and “Body Shop” offer odd textures, but critics describe a generally contemporary mainstream pop idiom with uneven stylistic convergence, not a sustained avant-garde approach.
- **Polish = 4**: complex multiteam, layered professional studio production; “polish” is production finish, **not** reviewer approval or songwriting quality.
- **Tempo = 3**: coexistence of quick dance material and much slower/midtempo ballads; no source-provided album median BPM, so this is a coarse qualitative estimate rather than a measured statistic.
- **Groove = 3**: many rhythm-forward tracks, but not a continuous dance set; reviewer comparison of two internal strands prevents a score of 5.
- **Voice = `clear`**: mainstream sung lead is more frequent than rap, despite rap features and occasional altered vocals. This categorical shortcut masks track-level variation.
- **Lyrics = `heartbreak|identity`**: documented romantic pain and public persona / self-reflection are central across different songs; Catholic references occur in some tracks but should not erase broader themes.
- **Language = `en`**: English predominant in the main recorded album. **Scene = `2010s EDM pop`** is a temporal production context, not a claim of geographic belonging.
- **Producers:** Madonna, Diplo, Avicii, Kanye West, Ariel Rechtshaid and Toby Gad each have confirmed track production roles in official credits. List is a selection of significant credits, not exhaustive and not a claim that each produced every song.
- **`sounds_like` and `influenced_by` are empty**: specific tracks are compared by reviewers to other work, but a meaningful whole-album sonic peer or documented album-wide creative influence is not established with sufficient specificity.
- **Landmark = 0**: substantial era album but the cited critics do not reach a consistent consensus that it defines the whole discography in the way required for 1. `confidence=medium` due chiefly to heterogeneous record and edition differences. Second reviewer may revisit.
- **Disagreement:** Petridis finds introspection the strongest part; Empire often prefers provocation and other specific songs. This disagreement affects evaluation of the record's *quality*, less the shared evidence about its contrasted styles.

## `madonna-madamex` — Madame X (2019)

Sources: [official credits and editions](https://www.madonna.com/products/madame-x); [Rich Juzwiak, Pitchfork, 18 June 2019](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-madame-x/); [Ben Beaumont-Thomas, Guardian, 4 June 2019](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/jun/04/madonna-madame-x-review-her-most-bizarre-album-ever); [Kitty Empire, Observer, 8 June 2019](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2019/jun/08/madonna-madame-x-review-splendidly-bizarre-return-to-form).

- **Genres = `pop|latin|electronic`:** broad constrained taxonomy proxy for pop built from reggaeton, fado textures, batuque, baile funk and electronica; this is **not** an assertion that all Lusophone or Cabo Verdean traditions are 'Latin'. The existing taxonomy is too coarse and should be expanded by Claude before cultural-category ranking. Avoid treating `latin` as a comprehensive technical genre label. If that is not acceptable, replace it with `dance` only after review.
- **Energy = 3 / Tempo = 3 / Groove = 3:** dance tracks (“Medellín,” “I Don't Search I Find,” “Faz Gostoso”) juxtapose slower fado-inspired music, midtempo R&B, sparse or ceremonial rhythmic material; a single fast, club-oriented average would mislead.
- **Electronic = 4:** critics identify Auto-Tune, digital vocal edits, programmed trap/reggaeton and synth-led arrangements; some live guitar, piano and the Orquestra de Batukadeiras provide vital acoustic/communal contributions, ruling out 'fully electronic'.
- **Mood = 3:** alternating festive rhythmic tracks and darker politically concerned or introspective sections; source descriptions make 'mixed' safer than unqualified euphoria.
- **Theatrical = 4:** concept persona, sharply contrasting character voices, choral/grand disco passages (“God Control”), stylized “Dark Ballet”; drama is part of the **music and presentation**, not merely marketing.
- **Experimental = 4:** atypical structural collage and global rhythmic hybridization widely described by critics, though judgments of success differ. The assessment tracks unconventional production, not assumed cultural authenticity.
- **Polish = 4:** elaborately mixed, high-budget processing, multiple texture layers. Polished and artistically cohesive are different concepts.
- **Voice = `clear`:** primary conventional lead singing appears alongside distorted/processed and rapped delivery; current one-token schema does not express that range. Consider voice-feature expansion if it matters for recommendation precision.
- **Lyrics = `political|identity`:** reviewed themes include gun control, oppressed identities, social justice, alienation and personas. Some love and sexual songs also present.
- **Language = `en|es|pt`:** English and documented Spanish/Portuguese vocal sections. **Scene = `Lisbon global pop`** refers to writing/recording inspiration via Portugal; does not claim origin of all incorporated traditions.
- **Producers:** Madonna, Mirwais, Mike Dean, Diplo, Jeff Bhasker each appear in album credits. Mike Dean is both executive producer and producer on certain individual songs; do not mislabel his all-track executive role as all-track co-production.
- **`sounds_like`/`influenced_by` left blank**: journalists compare isolated moments to other artists, and Orquestra de Batukadeiras is a *credited performing collaborator*, not a generic documented album influence. Traditions such as fado are not individual artists and must not be inserted in the `influenced_by` artist-only column.
- **Landmark = 0**: notable stylistic detour, but expert consensus on lasting artist-defining status is divided. **Confidence = high** for documented sonic diversity (not an endorsement of artistic success).
- **Disagreement:** Juzwiak calls the cross-cultural concept artistically scattered; Empire praises the resultant fusion; Beaumont-Thomas sees inspired tracks next to striking failures. Preserve this difference in conclusions, not in factual credit fields.

## `madonna-confessionsii` — CONFESSIONS II (2026)

Sources: [official release, editions, detailed credits](https://www.madonna.com/products/confessions-ii); [Shaad D'Souza, Pitchfork, 3 July 2026](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-confessions-ii/); [Alexis Petridis, Guardian, 2 July 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/jul/02/madonna-confessions-ii-album-review); [Eric Henderson, Slant, 3 July 2026](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/madonna-confessions-ii-album-review/); [Nick Levine, NME, July 2026](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/madonna-confessions-ii-review-3954470); [Brian Hiatt interviewing Stuart Price, Rolling Stone Canada, 19 October 2026](https://ca.rollingstone.com/music/madonna-confessions-ii-making-interview-stuart-price/); [Lucy O'Brien, Guardian, 7 July 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/jul/07/madonna-nostalgia-confessions-ii).

- **Identity = official capitalization `CONFESSIONS II`**, released 3 July 2026 by Warner Records. This batch follows **16-track digital configuration**. 12-track physical standard has a different balance of extended reflective tracks; Claude must document its source if it selects another edition. ID = `madonna-confessionsii` using the repo's documented normalization.
- **Genres = `dance|electronic|disco`:** 16-track continuous mix draws on deep/acid/Chicago/Detroit house, French touch, 2-step and vintage disco; guide currently lacks a `house` token. `disco` is a secondary umbrella, not a claim that the work simply repeats the 2005 album.
- **Energy = 4 / Tempo = 4 / Groove = 4:** front two-thirds are rhythmically forceful and continuous, but later section includes slower, acoustic, garage/trip-hop tinges (“Fragile,” “Betrayal,” “The Test,” “L.E.S. Girl”). Continuous DJ mix does **not** justify blanket all-5 values, particularly for standard/digital variant comparison.
- **Electronic = 4:** documented programmed drums, keyboards, synthesizers and samples, counterweighted by guitars, piano and occasional strings. Official track credits support nonzero acoustic presence.
- **Mood = 3:** house euphoria/cohesion offset by loss, grief, regret and reconciliation in the final movement; a 5 would erase the album's own contrast.
- **Theatrical = 3:** an intentionally staged continuous sonic arc and spoken passages, with emotionally intimate closing songs; less consistently dramatic than her theatrical character concepts.
- **Experimental = 3:** shivery acid house, 2-step, vintage breaks and adventurous segues in professional mainstream club production; innovative combinations but not obscure avant-garde sound art.
- **Polish = 5:** precisely mixed seamless DJ-set design, interlocking programming/arrangements and extensively credited engineering. High polish does not by itself imply every track is a good song.
- **Voice = `clear`:** main sung lead predominates, with softer/spoken material and featured voices not captured by this single-value representation.
- **Lyrics = `nostalgia|introspection`:** Madonna and Price's reported autobiographical writing, early NYC memories, family grief and healing recur. Dance liberation, love and communal experience are also major themes; the two-token cap forces prioritization.
- **Language = `en|es|fr`:** predominantly English Madonna vocals with Feid collaboration and featured spoken French from Stromae. Genre-guide field should ideally distinguish sung vs featured spoken languages in future.
- **Scene = `Chicago house revival`** shorthand for sonic reference, **not** asserting the record was produced in Chicago. NME interview describes stronger Detroit/Chicago house than predecessor, and Price cites house mixtapes.
- **Producers:** Madonna and Stuart Price credited across core album; Andrew Watt and Cirkut co-produced Danceteria and L.E.S. Girl. Additional producer/collaborator credits remain in official source and should not be stripped of their role: Arca, PARISI, Tainy, Martin Garrix, Osrin, Mirwais, etc.
- **`sounds_like = Róisín Murphy`** is a cautious editor-side album-wide comparison specifically grounded in Pitchfork's analogy to the album *Róisín Machine*. Not evidence of a collaboration or influence and not a similarity score; Claude should assess whether the reference is useful for bridging recommendations.
- **`influenced_by = Lil’ Louis|Inner City`** rests on *named, track-specific sample/interpolation credits* for “I Feel So Free” (Lil' Louis “French Kiss”) and “Bring Your Love” (Inner City's “Good Life”) plus Price's interview about classic-house mixtape references. These are **track-level documented inspirations**, not a claim that every song resembles them. A third possible reference `Mr. Fingers` is **not** included because the interviewed source describes listening references and the reviewer a stylistic comparison rather than an explicit named interpolation on the track.
- **Landmark = 0:** despite several strongly positive 2026 reviews, long-term artist-defining importance cannot be established confidently within three months of release; this is a **provisional** editorial classification that may change after historic perspective. **Confidence = high** for musical features and credits, not a claim that 'landmark 0' has high objective certainty.
- **Disagreement:** the Guardian hears a welcome but overlong nostalgic return with no equal to “Hung Up”; Pitchfork describes a musically vital autobiographical return; Slant emphasizes cohesion/comfort. Different aesthetic judgments align on house palette, mix continuity and autobiographical back half.
