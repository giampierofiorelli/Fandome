# Giorgio Moroder – Phase 06: evidence notes

Audited 25 October 2026. **Original release program, not later expanded CDs.** Scores (0–5) are editorial comparisons to the Fandome scale anchors, not claims contained verbatim in a review. Genre evidence is separately mapped per style and scope. `influenced_by` is blank for all four: familiarity, covers, performers and sonic analogies are **not** documented compositional influences. The listed `sounds_like` artists are editorial sonic analogies rather than assertions of borrowing.

## moroder-electricdreams – Electric Dreams (1984)

**Source editions and identity:** The original 1984 Virgin European LP and Epic American LP share ten songs but vary in order. Compare [E-DK] and [E-LP]. This is a **various-artists soundtrack**, not a wholly Moroder album. The official Moroder site [E-OFF] lists **only his four contributions** (including the Philip Oakey duet), not the LP's complete ten-song program. Do not treat that shortened artist discography list as an entire album. [E-AFI] provides institutional, track-level composer/producer credits.

**Genres:** The complete original LP is catalogued [E-AM] as new wave, synth pop, contemporary pop/rock and soundtrack, confirmed against its non-Moroder artists. Genre order reflects the record's multi-artist presentation, not the title hit alone. `pop` is a broad parent. Individual instrumentation on [E-MB] includes Jeff Lynne's guitars and Richard Tandy's synth; electronic=5 would be inaccurate.

**Scales:** `energy=3` and `tempo=3`: moderate fast pop is interrupted by melodic instrumental scenes and more restrained songs. `electronic=3`: electronic programming/keyboards, but Jeff Lynne's guitar-driven contributions and other band recordings make a fully synthesized rating impossible [E-MB,E-LP]. `mood=4`: bright romantic hit and upbeat radio songs outweigh occasional dramatic cues, an editorial whole-LP judgement, not identical track moods. `theatrical=3` cinematic motifs coexist with ordinary radio pop. `experimental=1` songs mainly retain radio structures. `polish=4` different teams deliver major-label production (credits [E-AFI]). `groove=3` some tracks invite dancing, not a non-stop dance LP.

**Voice/themes:** `clear|powerful|instrumental`: multiple lead singers (Oakey, Boy George, Helen Terry, Jeff Lynne, P.P. Arnold) contrast with Moroder instrumentals. This is a range, not a claim all five singers have exactly the same voice. `love|storytelling` applies chiefly to romantic songs and the computer-romance film tie-in; limited to major topics, not an inventory of all lyrics. English on sung tracks. No documented external artists influenced the **album itself**; leave blank.

**Producer credits:** `Giorgio Moroder|Jeff Lynne|Steve Levine|B.E.F.|Greg Walsh|Don Was`, all documented for particular LP tracks [E-LP,E-AFI]. This is not a claim Moroder produced the complete 10-track score. Greg Walsh shares *Chase Runner* production with B.E.F.; Don Was produced P.P. Arnold's *Electric Dreams*. [E-OFF]'s singular generic Moroder producer label reflects *his subset*, not every recording.

**Landmark / confidence:** `landmark=1` for Moroder's highly characteristic *Together in Electric Dreams* film-era work, not because the entire LP is his solo project. `high` for strong metadata and track-level production; scoring remains reasoned editorial interpretation.

## moroder-metropolis – Metropolis (1984)

**Original year dispute:** [M-ORIG], [M-1984], and multiple 1984 first pressings [M-COL] demonstrate original 1984 availability. Moroder's current webpage [M-OFF] and Apple digital edition instead date their issue **January 22, 1985**; that is a later edition/date, not grounds to erase the 1984 LP. The film itself is Fritz Lang's 1927 work restored/tinted and newly scored by Moroder in 1984; note Moroder's website contains historical film-year errors [M-ES,M-DIST]. Do not label this the original 1927 score, and do not substitute the entire instrumental film score for the **ten-song commercial LP**.

**Genres:** `soundtrack` contextual; `electrorock`, `rock`, `poprock` characterize vocal-led side of LP, while `synthpop|electronic` capture synthesizer-based themes and some songs [M-ORIG,M-ES,M-DIST]. *Electronic Sound* reproduces Moroder's own explanation that an audience test favoured a **more rock-oriented sound**: this is not evidence for a named rock-artist influence. Genre family and track scopes appear in separate CSV.

**Scales:** `energy=4`, `tempo=4`: forceful uptempo rock/pop appears on most of ten tracks, though the instrumental cue tracks offset it. `electronic=3`: synth scoring and pop electronics coexist with guitars, live drum/rock arrangements, strings and external bands [M-ORIG]. `mood=2`: industrial-machine film subject and urgent/darker lyrical framing, with exceptions. `theatrical=4` intentionally grand film-music staging and dramatic rock singers. `experimental=2` unusual silent-film reconstruction but conventional mainstream rock songs: **film novelty is not equivalent to musical experimentation**. `polish=4`: major-label recordings, orchestral/string and studio personnel. `groove=3`: driving rhythms across much of the set but not a dance album.

**Voice/themes:** `powerful|raspy|clear|instrumental` is a range of credited leads (Freddie Mercury, Pat Benatar, Bonnie Tyler, Billy Squier, Adam Ant, Jon Anderson) plus Moroder's *Machines* and *The Legend of Babel*. Do not describe the whole record as instrumental. `storytelling|social|love` is mainly the human-versus-machine story and romantic plot / lyrics, with varying song-specific focus. English leads.

**Producer precision:** Overall original LP credits production/mix by Giorgio, but the physical sleeve [M-ORIG] additionally credits **Freddie Mercury and Reinhold Mack** on *Love Kills*, **Neil Giraldo and Peter Coleman** on *Here's My Heart*, **Paul Dean** on *Destruction*, and **Billy Squier and Mack** on *On Your Own*. These names are retained because the column is all credited **track producers**, not only overarching Moroder. Pete Bellotte is a songwriter on several pieces, **not** included as producer. Critic Ebert [M-CRIT] disliked parts of the newly added music despite acknowledging the restoration. The album has stronger authorship and source coverage than unanimous critical approval.

**Landmark / confidence:** `landmark=1` for unique Moroder-edited and scored classic film project. `high` despite differing *quality* judgments. Sounds-like Queen/Loverboy/Cars is editorial sonic positioning, not a documented influence.

## moroder-innovisions – Innovisions (1985)

**Type decision: `compilation`, contested, not a new uniform studio session.** Moroder's official album discography [I-OFF] lists a 1985 album; the 2020s reissue label [I-REP] markets it as **eleventh studio album** but explicitly says it collects pieces from his earlier catalogue, including 1985 mixes, and the physical 1985 LP [I-LP,I-CHART] has ten songs. *Baby Blue*, *From Here to Eternity* and *The Chase* are listed as 1985 remixes; these do **not** make the seven other prior collaborations into freshly recorded tracks. Accordingly the best Fandome taxonomy for **the album as issued** is `compilation` (rather than `studio` or `remix`). Claudes may decide to change type after reviewing original first-press promotional material; disagreement must be preserved.

**Genres:** AllMusic and reissue label name synth-pop [I-AM,I-REP]; label calls it electropop (not a substitute for the **distinct** synthpop tag); original LP is categorized electronic, disco and synth-pop [I-LP]; Qobuz dance [I-QOBUZ]. Disco is most clear on older pieces and remixes; not dominant over every song. **No new genre tokens.**

**Scales:** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=4`: upbeat singles and disco reworks balanced with melodic midtempo pieces. `electronic=4`, not 5, because original credits include Arthur Barrow's bass and Richie Zito's guitar alongside synthesizers [I-LP]. `polish=4`: well-finished 1980s studio/mastering approach with remix processing. `experimental=1` conventional song material, regardless of innovatively titled LP. `theatrical=2` mostly compact pop song staging. `mood` **blank**: no credible whole-LP criticism resolves its mixed romantic/club tone. `lyrics` **blank**: track names do not prove text themes. `voice=clear|powerful` indicates English-language conventional foreground pop singing in various collaborations; no claim of a single vocal personality. `sounds_like` is contextual; `influenced_by` blank.

**Producers:** only Giorgio Moroder appears as original album producer [I-OFF,I-LP]. **Brian Reeves is credited for remixes/mix/engineering**, not elevated to album production. `landmark=0` due retrospective rather than uniquely defining new work; includes it this phase because it is a documented 1985 official Moroder catalogue collaboration/rework and was preselected. `confidence=medium`, mainly because generic studio/compilation classification is contested and track-level mixing roles vary.

## moroder-philipoakeygiorgiomoroder – Philip Oakey & Giorgio Moroder (1985)

**Release/collaboration:** Original nine-track Virgin/A&M release 19 July 1985 [P-OFF,P-ORIG]; later reissues append seven tracks or alternate mixes [P-AM,P-LP] and are **excluded**. The title repeats Giorgio Moroder's name as co-artist; use artist room `Giorgio Moroder` and Fandome ID `moroder-philipoakeygiorgiomoroder`, **not** Human League. The duet *Together in Electric Dreams* first appeared in 1984 on the soundtrack described above; the recording may appear in both albums' contexts, but album **rows** are distinct.

**Genres:** Synthpop and new wave are AllMusic whole-album styles [P-AM], Europop is Evan Cater's band comparison [P-AM], while dancepop/Hi-NRG are scoped genre labels from [P-AOTY]. `electronic` follows actual drum-machine and multiple synthesizer credits [P-ORIG,P-LP], **not** a claim every note is electronic. `pop` is the broader songwriting family. No `technopop` inferred simply because a review calls *Human League* a technopop group.

**Scales:** `energy=3`: driving dance tracks, some slower melodic songs. `electronic=4` because Barrow programmed drums and played synth, but also bass and Zito electric guitar [P-ORIG]. `mood=3`: positive romantic hooks alongside the downcast *Good-bye Bad Times*, so mixed rather than ecstatic 5. `theatrical=2`: presented as studio synthpop songs with expressive Oakey lead, not opera or film staging. `experimental=1`: traditional verse/chorus designs, as discussed by Evan Cater [P-AM]. `polish=4` mainstream multilayered studios. `tempo=3` songs mostly mid to brisk, with exceptions; `groove=4` consistent synthesizer pop beats, not one continuous club mix.

**Voice/lyrics:** Oakey's characteristic big and cool baritone supported by signed critics [P-AM,P-ART] leads most tracks (`clear|powerful` is a coarse permissible approximation; strong qualifier in notes); Elizabeth Daily/Joe Esposito backing harmonies [P-ORIG]. Short instrumental *In Transit* is **not** treated as a prevailing lead voice type. `love` from explicitly romantic named songs, but other themes are underdocumented. English lyrics. Only Moroder is credited producer; Oakey is songwriter/vocalist, Reeves engineer, Barrow arranger/programmer, none automatically co-producers [P-ORIG].

**Comparisons/disagreement:** Cater in [P-AM] says the record sounds close to the Human League and discounts most non-hit songs; later album aggregation [P-AOTY] supplies positive dance-pop/Hi-NRG stylistic tags, **not an independent glowing review**. `sounds_like=The Human League|Heaven 17|Visage` is a sonic analogy, not proof of influence. Landmark `0` for *album* (the hit itself is a landmark Moroder song, but one hit doesn't force a landmark album). `confidence=high` for personnel, nine-track set and explicit genre labels.
