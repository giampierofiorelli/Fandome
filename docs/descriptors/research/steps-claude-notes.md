## Reception reasoning

- Róisín Machine 4/4/2. Importance 4: among her best (Guardian puts it level with Overpowered and Hairless Toys), album of the year for Albumism, but no source calls it career-defining. Acclaim 4: MusicCritic 82 (Pitchfork 8.0, AllMusic 90); Metacritic 86 is reported by search snippets but the page returned 403. If Metacritic 86 is confirmed, 5 is defensible given the year-end lists. Mainstream 2: underground club record on Skint/BMG, one producer.
- Tears on the Dancefloor 3/3/4. Importance 3: comeback after 17 years, UK #2, Gold. Acclaim 3: positive but thin coverage (HuffPost 4/5, Clash 7/10). Mainstream 4: songs were picked from songwriting-camp submissions supplied by management (Tozer: "It was mainly our new management Fascination who have brought the music to the table"), and the band works to a formula ("it's got to sound like a Steps record"). It is not trend-chasing, so not 5.

## Artist-level first-person influences

  - Matthew Herbert: "he taught me that with discipline comes freedom." (He produced Ruby Blue, so he counts as a collaborator, not an influence on that album.)
