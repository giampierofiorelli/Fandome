# U2 — Phase 04: evidence notes

Scope: exactly three previously undescribed releases (1995–2000). Values are album-level **editorial judgments based on album-specific sources**, not technical measurements; stylistic tags restricted to individual tracks sit last. See corresponding `sources.csv` and `genre_evidence.csv` for URLs and scopes.

## u2-originalsoundtracks1 — Original Soundtracks 1 (Passengers, 1995)

**Identity / classification.** The record is credited **Passengers**, a five-member project consisting of U2 plus Brian Eno, not a standard U2-only studio release. Most notional soundtracks were created for imaginary films. A few pieces were used in actual screen works. Therefore the album is `studio` rather than a straightforward commissioned-film `soundtrack`. U2.com credits **Brian Eno alone** as producer, while Howie B is a participating musician/processor/mixer, not a credited album producer. Listed as a landmark *collaboration* worth including, but `landmark=0` for the core U2 defining-albums flag. [Official album](https://www.u2.com/products/passengers-original-soundtracks-1) · [1995 press release](https://www.u2songs.com/news/press_release_passsengers_original_soundtracks_1).

**Genre evidence.** Ambient and electronica are album-level descriptions in AllMusic, consistent with contemporary LA Times criticism of atmospheric electronics, slow-changing pulses and mostly instrumental compositions. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/original-soundtracks-1-mw0000176297) · [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-11-05-ca-64974-story.html). The `ambientpop`, `downtempo` and `experimental` labels come from the SPIN genre/rating aggregation and do not justify reclassifying every song. [SPIN/AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/genre/6-electronic/1995/spin/). Alternative pop/rock is limited to the more song-based passages. `opera` occurs in Pavarotti’s Italian tenor middle section of *Miss Sarajevo*, not throughout the album. Film-score concept does not justify assigning `soundtrack` as a sonic genre without independent sonic evidence.

**Scales (0–5).** `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2`: soft synth beds and measured pulses dominate, with a few rhythm-led exceptions (*Elvis Ate America*). `electronic=4`: Eno’s credited synths, sequencers and sound treatments are foregrounded, but band guitar, bass, drums and piano remain. `mood=2`: elegiac *Miss Sarajevo*, reflective *Your Blue Room* and atmospheric instrumentals outweigh brighter moments. `theatrical=2`: mostly low-key performance; operatic guest passage is an exception. `experimental=4`: improvised ambient/film-imagining framework and sonic treatments depart markedly from U2 verse-chorus rock. `polish=3`: professional recorded/mixed textures, occasionally rough by design. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/passengers-original-soundtracks-1), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/original-soundtracks-1-mw0000176297), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-11-05-ca-64974-story.html).

**Voice / language.** Many pieces are instrumental, so `instrumental` leads. Bono is largely restrained and sometimes rough-spoken; there is Eno and Holi singing; Pavarotti brings operatic delivery on one track. English is the majority vocal language, Italian appears in *Miss Sarajevo* and Japanese in Holi’s *Ito Okashi*. Do not expand these language values to soundtrack titles or Japanese-sounding samples without actual lyrics. [1995 liner](https://www.u2station.com/u2ography/albums/passengers-original-soundtracks-1.php), [U2 lyrics](https://www.u2.com/pages/song/miss-sarajevo), [Ito Okashi research](https://www.u2songs.com/news/passengers_at_30_song_by_song).

**Instruments.** Eno: **synth**, **sequencer**; The Edge/Bono: **guitar**; Bono: **piano** on *Beach Sequence*; Edge: **organ** on *Your Blue Room*; Adam: **bass**; Larry: **drums**, **percussion**, sequenced rhythm; David Herbert: **saxophone** on *United Colours*; Craig Armstrong: **string arrangements** for *Miss Sarajevo* (strings present); Howie B: record scratching represented by **turntables**. The latter three are limited to individual pieces, not whole-album dominance. [Sleeve transcription](https://www.u2station.com/u2ography/albums/passengers-original-soundtracks-1.php), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/158592ed-4ec7-47c0-8fe8-dbcefffc745c).

**Production.** Eno’s sleeve treatments/sequencing support `programming` and `sonicmanipulation`; Howie B’s first-person explanation of treating the band’s recordings as material to break down supports `sampling` but does **not** establish any named sampler model. Craig Armstrong is explicitly credited for `stringarrangements`; AllMusic lists digital editing in album credits (`digitalediting`). LA Times describes Bono’s falsetto on *Your Blue Room* (`falsetto`), limited to that song. No named synthesizer model inferred. [Howie B](https://www.xsnoize.com/interview-howie-b-discusses-producing-u2-on-1997s-pop-album/), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/original-soundtracks-1-mw0000176297), [sleeve](https://www.u2station.com/u2ography/albums/passengers-original-soundtracks-1.php), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-11-05-ca-64974-story.html).

**Lyrics and resemblance.** On mainly instrumental material, thematic tags apply only to songs: *Miss Sarajevo* supports political/social concerns, *Elvis Ate America* cultural irony, and *Your Blue Room* reflective intimacy. Sonic-neighbour entries Brian Eno, U2’s *Zooropa* and The Orb are **resemblance judgments**, not documented influences. No `influenced_by` artist is supplied: Bono’s remark about wanting to co-write with Eno as Bowie/Talking Heads did documents a collaborative model, not album-specific musical influence. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/passengers-original-soundtracks-1), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/original-soundtracks-1-mw0000176297).

**Disagreements / edition notes.** U2.com gives 7 November 1995, while discography databases list 6 November for some markets; year is fixed at 1995. Original fourteen tracks only: Japanese bonus remixes do not inform these tags. Critical responses vary; drummer Larry Mullen subsequently disliked its experimental detour, whereas Edge praised it. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/passengers-original-soundtracks-1), [u2songs](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/passengers_original_soundtracks_1_album_original_release).

## u2-pop — Pop (1997)

**Identity / production credit conflict.** U2.com’s summary reads **Flood, Howie B** under produced-by. The original sleeve transcription instead has **Produced by Flood; additional production by Howie B and Steve Osborne**. Flood himself referred to co-production on some particular tracks. To avoid omitting a name explicitly credited as producer by the band's current official source, this draft uses `Flood|Howie B`, with this conflict flagged for Claude’s track-by-track verification. **Steve Osborne is excluded**: only additional production is documented in the original sleeve, and Nellee Hooper’s preparatory work did not earn a released-album production credit. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/pop), [original sleeve](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_pop_album_original_release), [Flood interview](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop).

**Genre evidence.** AllMusic explicitly classifies the record as dance-rock, alternative/indie rock, college rock and related pop/rock categories. Pitchfork calls it a rock record incorporating trip-hop, rap and breakbeat. The contemporary LA Times names dance, techno, disco, sampling and trip-hop in connection with the textures; these latter styles are **texture or specific-song markers**, not equal-weight classifications of the complete record. The contemporary *Sunday Times* expressly disputed classifying *Pop* as a full dance/techno album. Hence dance-rock comes first and rap/techno/disco are last. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/pop-mw0000090394), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/u2-pop/), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-03-02-ca-33843-story.html), [Sunday Times](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/sunday-times-uk).

**Scales (0–5).** `energy=4`: thumping, fierce *Discothèque*/*Mofo* mix with quieter final third. `electronic=3`: prominent samplers, sequenced grooves and programming alongside predominantly real kit and electric guitar. `groove=3`: more dance-influenced than normal U2, but the slow ballads and spiritual songs prevent a dance-floor rating. `tempo=3`: rhythmic first half offset by measured tracks. `mood=2`: playful exterior overlays dark, spiritual and unsettled lyrics. `theatrical=4`: exaggerated sonic contrasts and dynamic Bono delivery; do not inflate rating based on the PopMart stage show. `experimental=3`: pronounced dance/electronic borrowings but recognizable rock structures. `polish=3`: extensive elaborate studio treatment, yet Flood and Howie B describe reworking and rush, not uniformly glossy perfection. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/u2-pop/), [LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-03-02-ca-33843-story.html).

**Instruments.** The 1997 sleeve explicitly gives Bono guitar/voice; Edge guitar/keyboards/voice; Adam bass; Larry drums/percussion/programming. `electricguitar` reflects amplified guitar sonics described in reviews. Howie B’s turntable decks, studio sampling units, and programmed percussion substantiate `turntables`, `sampler`, `sequencer`, `drummachine`. Pitchfork describes a cowbell on *Discothèque*. **Do not treat all drum tracks as drum-machine tracks**: Flood stated that about 95% of finished drums were real. [Sleeve](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_pop_album_original_release), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/u2-pop/).

**Production / named hardware.** The original Flood/Howie B 1997 engineering interview documents drum-loop building and `sampling`, dry/reworked drums, `overdubbing`, recorded 24-track tape, and the specific **Atari ST/C-Lab Creator, Akai S1000/S1100/S3200, Kurzweil K2000, Cubase and Logic**. `analogtape` refers to their specifically described Otari analogue multitracks; `wallofsound` is a percussion treatment noted for *Mofo*, not the whole record. `distortion` is audible and named by Pitchfork for that and other tracks. Generic `programming` supports credited keyboard/drum programming. `drumprogramming` is limited in finished mixes, as real drums replaced many demo loops. *Do not add Pro Tools, TR-808 or any other famous machine merely because of the era.* [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/u2-pop/).

**Lyrics and influences.** Spiritual and inward questions recur (*Wake Up Dead Man*, *If God Will Send His Angels*, *Please*); irony (*Discothèque*) and political preoccupations (*Please*) are secondary. Howie B describes playing **Les Baxter and James Brown** records to inspire band jams. Flood explicitly says the *Playboy Mansion* riff was inspired by the **Turtles**' recording of *You Showed Me* and *Do You Feel Loved* by a **Naked Funk** song. Those are documented creative **influences**, not sampled songs. *Massive Attack* and *The Prodigy* (particularly *Mofo*) and *Oasis* (on *Staring at the Sun*) are only critical **sonic comparisons**; no evidence they were deliberately emulated. [Howie B](https://www.xsnoize.com/interview-howie-b-discusses-producing-u2-on-1997s-pop-album/), [Flood](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/u2-pop/).

**Disagreements / edition notes.** Earlier *Pop* sessions began with Nellee Hooper, but album is not credited to him as primary producer. Flood called the loops tools to start writing and said most finished drums were real. Howie B recalled remaking *Please* **after** the album for a single; this subsequent single version must not be used as evidence for album strings/orchestration. Original 1997 album only, not 2002/other greatest-hits re-recordings or tour versions. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/flood-howie-b-producing-u2s-pop), [Howie B](https://www.xsnoize.com/interview-howie-b-discusses-producing-u2-on-1997s-pop-album/), [u2songs](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_pop_album_original_release).

## u2-allthatyoucantleavebehind — All That You Can’t Leave Behind (2000)

**Identity / producers.** Existing catalog ID must remain `u2-allthatyoucantleavebehind`, with curly apostrophe in catalog title. Original sleeve and U2.com credit **Daniel Lanois and Brian Eno** as producers; **Steve Lillywhite, Mike Hedges, Richard Stannard and Julian Gallagher** only have *additional production*, mixing or related roles: exclude from `producers`. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/all-that-you-can-t-leave-behind), [original liner](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_all_that_you_cant_leave_behind_album_original_release).

**Genre evidence.** AllMusic classifies this as adult-alternative pop/rock, alternative pop/rock, contemporary pop/rock, album rock and alternative/indie rock; Pitchfork tags it rock. *Billboard* describes a gospel/retro-soul inflection in *Stuck in a Moment*; Pitchfork calls *In a Little While* doo-wop and notices breakbeat drumming on *New York*. The latter four are **some-tracks** tags, not the principal style of the album. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/all-that-you-cant-leave-behind-mw0000101186), [Billboard](https://www.u2station.com/news/2000/09/track-by-track---u2s-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind.php), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/8329-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind/).

**Scales (0–5).** `energy=3`: *Beautiful Day*, *Elevation*, *Walk On* are forceful while much of side two is restrained. `electronic=2`: Eno’s synths are credited across many songs, programming occurs on two, but guitars/drums/bass dominate. `mood=3`: hopeful declarations balance grief, disappointment, social doubt. `theatrical=3`: arena choruses alternate with conversational ballads. `experimental=1`: a return to familiar songs after *Pop*. `polish=4`: refined melodic, layered, tightly arranged Lanois/Eno studio album. `tempo=3`: medium rock tempos with ballads. `groove=2`: rhythm matters, but this is not a dance-oriented record. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/all-that-you-can-t-leave-behind), [Rolling Stone](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/rolling_stone_101800_1117), [Billboard](https://www.u2station.com/news/2000/09/track-by-track---u2s-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind.php), [Apple editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/all-that-you-cant-leave-behind/1443100430?uo=4).

**Instruments.** Track credits support **electricguitar**, **bass**, **drums**, **percussion**, **synth**, **piano**, **keyboards**, **strings** (*Kite*, credited to Edge), and **brass** (*Stuck in a Moment*, Paul Barrett). Eno supplied synth parts and programming; Lanois additional guitar on several tracks. No assumption that all tracks use strings/brass. Organ appears on the region-dependent bonus *The Ground Beneath Her Feet*, therefore excluded from this **original 11-track main-album** descriptor. [Original liner](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_all_that_you_cant_leave_behind_album_original_release), [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/5c60fe39-5eb4-4997-a5e0-c0f0bd7907e7).

**Production.** `programming` credited to Eno on *Beautiful Day* and *Grace*; `stringarrangements` specifically by Eno on *In a Little While*; `layeredvocals` from Billboard’s description of *Beautiful Day*; `falsetto` expressly for gospel-inflected *Stuck in a Moment*. Avoid assuming specific synth brand, machine, tape format, or delay gear. [Liner notes](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/u2_all_that_you_cant_leave_behind_album_original_release), [Billboard](https://www.u2station.com/news/2000/09/track-by-track---u2s-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind.php).

**Themes / resemblance.** Lyrics alternate hope, grief, interpersonal rescue and spiritual concern: *Beautiful Day*, *Walk On*, *Stuck in a Moment* and *Peace on Earth* provide cross-track themes. `sounds_like` compares the record to the earlier *The Joshua Tree* (U2 archive cites NME comparison) and Van Morrison (Pitchfork’s appraisal of its soul-tinged tracks). These are editorial resemblance links, **not** actual artist influences. `influenced_by` stays empty for lack of specific musical-influence documentation; *Walk On*’s dedication is not a musical influence. [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/all-that-you-can-t-leave-behind), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/8329-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind/), [Billboard](https://www.u2station.com/news/2000/09/track-by-track---u2s-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind.php).

**Criticism / editions.** Rolling Stone admired the renewed focus on craft; Pitchfork’s 2000 review was considerably less enthusiastic. Do not flatten this disagreement into unanimous acclaim. U2.com names 12th track *The Ground Beneath Her Feet* UK-only; main standard record is 11 songs and variations do not generate separate rows. This is `landmark=1` as a widely identified career reset, not on sales alone. [Rolling Stone](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/rolling_stone_101800_1117), [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/8329-all-that-you-cant-leave-behind/), [U2.com](https://www.u2.com/products/all-that-you-can-t-leave-behind).
