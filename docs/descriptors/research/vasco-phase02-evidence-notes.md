# Vasco Rossi — Phase 02: research and evidence notes

Scope: original 1982/1983/1985/1987 studio LPs, not bonus tracks, remasters, later live renditions or compilations. All scalar values are Fandome editorial judgments based on the cited original albums (not sourced numeric ratings). `status=draft` throughout. No unverified influence claims entered.

## `vasco-vadoalmassimo` — *Vado al massimo* (1982)

**Styles.** MusicBrainz release-group tags rock/pop rock/hard rock/pop; Treccani identifies original cantautorato rock. Rockol's reviewed tracks are more precise: the title track is reggae with rock refrain; *Splendida giornata* is almost Italo disco, while *Canzone*, *Ogni volta* and *La noia* are ballads. That warrants placing reggae/italodisco lower than full-record rock and songcraft. *Hard rock* is a community tag despite OndaRock noting the step back from the 1981 abrasiveness: keep as a low-weight subgenre, with this disagreement preserved. Sources: https://musicbrainz.org/release-group/d54ae310-ff52-38d7-a66a-0f8cdc95733d ; https://www.rockol.it/news-743777/vasco-rossi-anniversario-album-vado-al-massimo-recensione-canzoni ; https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/vascorossi/ ; https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/vasco-rossi_%28Lessico-del-XXI-Secolo%29/

**Instrumentation.** Italian article and original-sleeve metadata document guitars by Solieri/Riva/Ferro, two bassists, drums, keyboards, strings (Mauro Pagani) and wind/horn credits (Rudy Trevisi). Source is a band credit, not a conclusion from genre. The available credits do not substantiate specific sax/trumpet on an individual song here: retain generic `brass` for the source's *fiati*? No: the Italian term *fiati* means wind instruments of different kinds; it is **not necessarily brass**. For conservative import, change `brass` to `woodwinds` only if booklet confirms woodwind. The source says *fiati* (winds), without identifying the type. We therefore deliberately omit a wind-family instrument from this provisional row until a precise LP booklet can be checked.

**Recording and personnel.** Recorded and mixed at Fonoprint, Bologna. Guido Elmi credited producer. Biancani credits himself assistant to production and effects in 1982, contradicting some transcription database's blanket producer entry. The database also says programmed by Biancani, so `programming` is tentative pending jacket scan. No specific synthesizer hardware established. Sources: https://www.biancanimaurizio.it/produzioni/ ; https://musicbase.net/vado-al-massimo ; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vado_al_massimo

**Scales.** `energy=3`, `tempo=3`: medium-rock peaks offset by slow ballads. `electronic=1`: predominantly band despite possible effects/programming. `mood=2`: the album has upbeat tracks but grief, nostalgia and disillusionment recur. `theatrical=3`, `experimental=1`, `polish=3`, `groove=2`: rock-rock/reggae/pop blend, relatively direct production. The specific critic comparison of the reggae single to Loredana Bertè’s *E la luna bussò* goes in `sounds_like=Loredana Bertè (Bandabertè)`, NOT influenced_by. This comparison is track-scoped. Source: https://www.rockol.it/news-743777/vasco-rossi-anniversario-album-vado-al-massimo-recensione-canzoni

**Landmark:** 1 in his early career per Treccani and Rockol, albeit less significant than the next LP. **Confidence medium** because Vado 1982 credit transcriptions differ.

## `vasco-bollicine` — *Bollicine* (1983)

**Styles.** OndaRock categorises the album **new wave, rock**, discusses *Ultimo domicilio conosciuto* and *Mi piaci perché* as new-wave tracks, and identifies *Giocala* as yacht rock specifically. AllMusic styles Italian Pop / Contemporary Singer-Songwriter; original sleeve is shelved Rock/Pop Rock. This provides a multi-layer Fandome genre set without treating the yacht-rock one-off as dominant. Sources: https://www.ondarock.it/recensioni/pietremiliariitaliane/1983-vasco-rossi-bollicine/ ; https://www.45cat.com/vinyl/album/cln25101 ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/bollicine-mw0000743387

**Original 1983 instrumentation.** Solieri/Riva/Elmi/Dodi Battaglia guitars; Golinelli/Romani bass; Gherardi/Melotti drums; Vitolo/Fraser piano and electric piano; multiple synthesists; Rudy Trevisi sax and percussion; string ensemble and backing singers credited. Original sleeve transcription explicitly names Prophet synthesizer (subsequent song-level credit identifies Prophet 5), Minimoog and synthesizer programming by Luigi Tonet and Maurizio Biancani. Source: https://www.45cat.com/vinyl/album/cln25101 ; https://musicbase.net/bollicine ; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollicine_(album)

**Production.** Guido Elmi & Maurizio Biancani both producer/mix, independently confirmed by Biancani's official page. No `sampling`, `overdubbing`, or Synclavier token without clearer originals. `prophet5` and `minimoog` are traceable signature hardware, not guesses from a new-wave label.

**Scales.** `energy=4` rock vitality, `electronic=2` keyboards used with electric band; `mood=2` ironic and socially sharp with vulnerable love songs; `theatrical=4` anthemic refrains, `experimental=2` stylistic jumps/spoken samples, `polish=4` polished studio sound, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`. We do NOT apply `sampling`: the spoken break in *Bollicine* and historic speech references are not enough to identify a sampled recording technique from reliable technical sources. `sounds_like=David Bowie (Low)` is a reviewer analogy *only for Ultimo domicilio conosciuto*. Sources: https://www.ondarock.it/recensioni/pietremiliariitaliane/1983-vasco-rossi-bollicine/ ; https://www.rockol.it/news-709416/vasco-rossi-bollicine-anniversario-37-anni-storia-album

**Landmark:** 1, strong source agreement including retrospective Italian national rankings; confidence high for original roles and broad genre scheme, subjective scale judgments remain drafts.

## `vasco-cosasuccedeincitta` — *Cosa succede in città* (1985)

**Catalogue status.** This original studio LP is absent from current `vasco` row, so add its exact id `vasco-cosasuccedeincitta` before running descriptor validator. Sony's Italian label store identifies the original issue on 9 June 1985 and lists nine tracks; do NOT use the 2002 AllMusic reissue date. Source: https://store.sonymusic.it/products/cosa-succede-in-citta ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/cosa-succede-in-citta-mw0000476015

**Music.** RSI explicitly distinguishes its softened, commercially oriented pop-rock sound, stronger synthesizer role and reflective songwriting from earlier hard rock; OndaRock names *funk* rhythms and ballads. *Cosa c'è* is a rock opening, while *Toffee*, *Dormi, dormi*, *Cosa succede in città* and *T'immagini* provide different lyrical sides. The long-tail hardrock token is a **borderline genre**: RSI says the opening track looks back to hard rock while the bulk of the album moves away from it. Place last and drop it if a stricter track-level basis is preferred. Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/cultura/musica/speciali/Quando-Vasco-ha-chiuso-con-la-vita-spericolata--3367863.html ; https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/vascorossi/ ; https://www.rollingstone.it/musica/storie-musica/cosa-succede-in-citta-quando-la-vita-ha-presentato-il-conto-a-vasco-rossi/1012832/

**Personnel.** Original sleeve transcriptions list electric/acoustic guitars (Solieri, Battaglia, Gianolio, Riva), bass (Romani/Golinelli), Lele Melotti drums, Trevisi sax and percussion, Vitolo Hammond/piano, Zanotti synth and Joe Amoruso piano. The original LP credit uses `Joe Amoruso electric piano` on title song while the general wiki table uses `piano` without specifying type: keep `electricpiano` with original transcription, note the discrepancy. Sources: https://vinylstory.wixsite.com/brunomalasomma/post/vasco-rossi-1985-cosa-succede-in-citt%C3%A0 ; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosa_succede_in_citt%C3%A0

**Production.** Elmi & Biancani; Maison Blanche Modena. PCM-1610 digital mastering is attested on a 1985 sleeve transcription. Do not confuse **digital mastering** with digital multitrack recording. Accordingly production is intentionally blank, rather than misleading `digitalrecording`. Sources: https://vinylstory.wixsite.com/brunomalasomma/post/vasco-rossi-1985-cosa-succede-in-citt%C3%A0 ; https://www.biancanimaurizio.it/produzioni/

**Scales.** `energy=3`, `electronic=2` (more synth layers but a live electric-rock core), `mood=2` (disillusion), `theatrical=3`, `experimental=1`, `polish=4`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` (funk rhythms). `sounds_like=Enzo Jannacci`: critic's comparison of his earlier sarcastic observational songwriting, not an artist-declared album influence; evidence is weaker for overall sound, consider removing on editorial audit. Sources: https://www.rsi.ch/cultura/musica/speciali/Quando-Vasco-ha-chiuso-con-la-vita-spericolata--3367863.html ; https://www.rollingstone.it/musica/storie-musica/cosa-succede-in-citta-quando-la-vita-ha-presentato-il-conto-a-vasco-rossi/1012832/

**Landmark:** 1 for a documented career transition despite mixed retrospective criticism. Confidence high on factual credits and date.

## `vasco-cechidiceno` — *C’è chi dice no* (1987)

**Style and theme.** OndaRock's critical retrospective notes growing pessimism, introspection, atmospheric keyboards and reflective ballads; Ar.Disc. identifies the political and cultural refusal of conformity expressed in the title song and 'Blasco Rossi'. Treccani confirms this thematic turn from youthful satire. It remains a rock album, with a smaller proportion of aggressive songs than early-1980s releases; avoid hardrock and newwave tokens when an album-specific reviewer does not name them. Sources: https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/vascorossi/ ; https://ardisc.it/ce-chi-dice-no/ ; https://www.treccani.it/magazine/lingua_italiana/articoli/scritto_e_parlato/Vasco.html

**Original credits.** Elmi and Biancani original producers; Melotti drums, Golinelli/Romani bass, Solieri/Riva/Elmi guitars, Rudy Trevisi sax/keys/congas/drums, Fio Zanotti keys. Rudy's sax appears in *Ridere di te*, *Ciao*, and *Brava Giulia*; congas play on several tracks. The vinyl credits say Serse May programmed the sound using Emulator II, an Akai system and Macintosh. Map only verified model `emulatorii`, generic `programming`; omit unverified Akai-specific machine and unspecified sampling. Source: https://vinylstory.wixsite.com/brunomalasomma/post/vasco-rossi-1987-c-%C3%A8-chi-dice-no ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/ce-chi-dice-no-mw0000468625

**Scales.** `energy=3`, `electronic=2` for acoustic band/synth/keyboards interplay, `mood=1` (critics emphasize dark distrust), `theatrical=3`, `experimental=1`, `polish=4` for mature production, `tempo=3`, `groove=2` rather than dance-pop. `lyrics` prioritizes `social|identity|introspection` for title track, *Blasco Rossi*, then heartbreak, romance and irony. Source: https://ardisc.it/ce-chi-dice-no/ ; https://www.ondarock.it/monografie/vascorossi/

**Reception:** contemporary popularity and present-day critical disagreements are kept in the source file rather than treating chart success as production commercialism. The 19 March 1987 date has artist-backed contemporary ANSA confirmation. Sources: https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/cultura/musica/2026/03/19/vasco-rossi-ce-chi-dice-no-esce-nel-1987-dopo-39-anni-rimane_8bb07213-deb7-4075-8a62-9d14b66245a7.html ; https://www.debaser.it/vasco-rossi/ce-chi-dice-no.

**Landmark:** 1, key 1987 break and defining work. Confidence high for original personnel and conceptual direction; descriptor scales editorial.
