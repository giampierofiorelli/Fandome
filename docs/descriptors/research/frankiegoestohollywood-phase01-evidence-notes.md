## frankiegoestohollywood-welcometothepleasuredome — *Welcome to the Pleasuredome* (1984)

- **Genres.** MusicBrainz release-group tags document `synthpop`, `dancepop`, `alternativedance`, `electronic`, and `pop`; Album of the Year independently names `newwave` and `poprock`; AllMusic names `dancerock` and `clubdance`. The US first-pressing metadata lists `ballad` (mostly *The Power of Love*); Pitchfork explicitly describes the *War* cover as `hinrg`. Those minor styles go last because they are song-specific. Pitchfork's broad **Rock** website shelf is not treated as a definitive whole-album genre.
- **Energy 4 / tempo 4 / groove 4.** The extended title track and *Relax*/*Two Tribes* emphasize a hard, driving, dance-floor pulse; the cover material and *The Power of Love* ballad prevent a 5 across the album. Pitchfork, AllMusic and JazzRockSoul discuss those contrasts.
- **Electronic 4.** Gearnews documents the Roland Jupiter-8, Fairlight CMI IIx, Synclavier II and PPG Wave 2.3 alongside LinnDrum. MusicBrainz and session notes also document real drums, bass, guitars, percussion and Steve Howe's acoustic guitar, so 5 (all electronic) would be misleading.
- **Mood 3.** Hedonistic and witty songs coexist with cold-war anxiety and a dramatic love ballad. Unlike the electronic, production and personnel values, this is an editorial whole-album judgement.
- **Theatrical 5 / experimental 4 / polish 5.** Pitchfork describes elaborate spoken fragments, huge contrasts, extended tracks and costly digital manipulation. AllMusic calls the album overblown and focuses on Horn's production; neither makes it fundamentally inaccessible avant-garde music.
- **Voice `powerful|spoken`.** Holly Johnson's striking forceful performance is discussed in Pitchfork and Johnson's own Electronic Beats interview. Spoken political and erotic introductions/interludes appear in the original sequence; `spoken` is secondary, not a dominant lead vocal mode.
- **Instruments.** `synth`, `drummachine`, `sampler`, `synthbass`, `keyboards` come from Trevor Horn's described synth-bass programming and Gearnews's instrument-by-instrument history; `guitar`, `bass`, `drums` from original MusicBrainz and JazzRockSoul band credits; `percussion` from Luís Jardim; `strings` from Anne Dudley's *The Power of Love* arrangement; `acousticguitar` from Steve Howe's title-track contribution. No real brass is asserted merely because a synth imitates brass.
- **Production.** Direct source for `jupiter8`, `synclavier`, `fairlight`, `linndrum`, `ppgwave23` is Gearnews (author cites conversations with JJ Jeczalik and Trevor Horn). `synthprogramming`/`drumprogramming` confirmed by Horn in Classic Pop. `sampling` follows the Fairlight and named samples; `stringarrangements` from album credit. Fairlight prominence is disputed: Gearnews argues Jupiter-8 and Synclavier were more important on final album than many retrospectives suggest. Distinct **Fairlight CMI IIx** is recorded with Fandome's general `fairlight` token, whereas `ppgwave23` preserves the exact model.
- **Lyrics.** Sex/pleasure on *Relax* and *Krisco Kisses*, anti-war politics on *Two Tribes*/*War*, love on *The Power of Love*, public identity and camp/irony across the record. Tag order reflects thematic prominence, not lyrics in all tracks.
- **Producer only Trevor Horn.** MusicBrainz lists Stephen Lipson as **additional producer** on several tracks. Another secondary site lists him simply as producer; the specific track-credit distinction takes precedence. Lipson is therefore deliberately excluded from `producers`.
- **Sounds like.** Art of Noise is an editorial electronic-sampling comparison (shared ZTT/Horn methods, not an album influence); Soft Cell and Eurythmics are contemporary synth/dance-pop sonic neighbours as discussed in Pitchfork. They are not treated as documented influences.
- **Influenced by empty.** Johnson names Bowie/Ziggy Stardust as a model for the band's *stage persona/concept*, but the interviews do not establish a specific musical influence *on the album's sound*. Bowie, Marc Bolan/T. Rex, and Lou Reed are recorded separately as ARTIST INFLUENCES in the import memo. Covers, guest artists and sampled sources are **not** influences.
- **Landmark 1.** Landmark cultural and production status is substantiated by Pitchfork's retrospective, the album's chart record and the 1001 Albums listing.
- **Critic disagreement.** Pitchfork assesses the album's innovative queer/theatrical force while quoting hostile contemporary reviews about overproduction; AllMusic praises the singles but notes the extravagant length. These disagreements affect judgement, not personnel credits.

## frankiegoestohollywood-liverpool — *Liverpool* (1986)

- **Genres.** Original 1986 pressing explicitly lists `poprock`/`synthpop` and broader `rock`/`electronic`/`pop`; AllMusic lists `dancerock`, `alternativedance`, `clubdance`. MusicRadar, quoting band memoir/producer, establishes the album's overall more rock-oriented direction. The Club/Dance tag has low rank, because the record is not a straight dance album.
- **Energy 4 / tempo 3 / groove 2.** Guitar riffs, striking drums and large choruses dominate *Rage Hard* and *Warriors of the Wasteland*, but *Is Anybody Out There?* and parts of *Maximum Joy* are spacious/slower. The restrained groove value distinguishes it sharply from the debut despite AllMusic's dance-rock label.
- **Electronic 3.** MusicRadar documents Synclavier and sampled/digital percussion layered onto real drums, guitar and bass. This is neither a purely live rock document nor primarily synth dance music.
- **Mood 2 / theatrical 4 / experimental 2 / polish 5.** More anxious and severe tone; stadium-size refrains and occasional sample manipulation remain; structures are shorter and more conventional than the debut. Lipson/Horn's elaborate studio work and high-budget digital mixing warrant 5 polish.
- **Voice `powerful`.** Johnson's dramatic/wailing force noted by The Quietus and JazzRockSoul. A guest operatic layer on *Maximum Joy* is **not** incorrectly tagged as Holly's lead voice being opera-trained.
- **Instruments.** Original MusicBrainz credits and JazzRockSoul establish guitar, bass, drums, keyboards, strings, brass and percussion. `synth`/`sampler`/`drummachine` come from MusicRadar's account of Lipson, Gill and electronic percussion; the *Maximum Joy* Synclavier is specifically named by The Quietus and JazzRockSoul.
- **Production.** `synclavier`, `tr808`, and `emulator` follow MusicRadar's quotation of Gill about TR-808 triggers and Emulator samples and its Lipson account; `drumprogramming`/`sampling`/`synthprogramming` are album-session work; `digitaltape` refers to the two Sony 24-track digital recorders used at Horn's mixing stage. **Sony PCM-3324** is *not* claimed because this article does not identify the precise machine model. Richard Niles earns `stringarrangements` and `hornarrangements` (but **not** a producer credit). `heavyreverb` is supported for the closing song, so it represents a more limited feature.
- **Lyrics.** Political and social class-frustration themes on *Warriors*, *Kill the Pain*, *For Heaven's Sake* discussed by The Quietus; personal uncertainty and longing on *Is Anybody Out There?* from JazzRockSoul/MusicRadar. The language is English.
- **Producer only Stephen Lipson.** MusicBrainz marks Trevor Horn as **executive producer**; MusicRadar says Horn remixed/oversaw final stages. Those activities do not qualify him for `producers` under the supplied rule. AllMusic loosely describes a Horn-produced album; prefer primary credits.
- **Sounds like.** The Quietus explicitly likens elements of *Lunar Bay* to Propaganda's industrial-pop palette; MusicRadar likens the band's 1986 rock shift to Def Leppard. Those comparisons are not recorded as influences. The specific *A Secret Wish* album is identified for Propaganda, but Def Leppard has no named album in the source.
- **Influenced by `Ratt (Invasion of Your Privacy)`.** *Liverpool* guitarist Brian Nash recounts drummer Peter Gill taking the rhythmic feel of Ratt's *Lay It Down* (originally on *Invasion of Your Privacy*) as a model for *Rage Hard*. MusicRadar quotes Nash's first-person account. This is a **documented track-specific musical influence**, unlike the other resemblances.
- **Landmark 0.** Important as their second/final studio album, but the more conventional and divisive LP is not considered the defining landmark compared with the 1984 debut.
- **Critic disagreement.** AllMusic's Alex S. Garcia suggests a stronger, more focused album than the debut; The Quietus finds only intermittent high points, and MusicRadar details the hostile 1986 response. These reviews also differ on whether the rock turn was artistically successful.
- **Special credit/edition warning.** Do not import B-side covers (*Suffragette City*, *Roadhouse Blues*) or later reissue bonus tracks as if they were on the eight-track original. Do not transfer machinery details from the 1984 *Relax* sessions to this album.
