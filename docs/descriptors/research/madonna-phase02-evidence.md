# Fandome — Madonna Phase 02: three new albums ONLY

**Date:** 24 October 2026  
**Status:** EDITORIAL DRAFT / secondary verification and independent scoring still needed.  
**Scope:** `m-prayer` (1989), `madonna-erotica` (1992), `madonna-bedtimestories` (1994).  
**Non-scope:** No 1983, 1984 or 1986 albums; no rewrites of Phase 01 data; no previous catalog audit.

## Evidence policy

All 0–5 values are **our proposed qualitative codings** of critical observations and first-hand creator accounts. The 0–5 scores are not values asserted or measured by the cited writers. The CSV is provisional and every `notes` string ends `(unsure)`. Do not let this draft silently enter production rankings or recommendations. We used signed critical reviews, contemporary criticism and creator interviews; official artist listings only for record identity. The reviewers' opinions of quality are not numeric acoustic descriptors.

The current taxonomy offers no literal `gospel`, `house`, `hip-hop soul` or `trip-hop` genre, so allowed parent genres are used and some precise vocabulary appears in the `scene` column and supporting evidence. Importers should not pretend `pop|dance|rnb` means that house is exactly represented.

`voice` is a lossy one-label field; theatrical/soft voices and sung/spoken delivery vary by track. `tempo` is a subjective typical pacing category, **not a BPM estimate**. `electronic` reflects audible production/instrument balance, **not an inventory of all devices used**. `landmark` is a proposed role in the *artist's catalog* rather than a review score.

## 1. Like a Prayer (1989) — `m-prayer` — existing catalog ID

**Source-grounded observations**
- Maura Johnston (Pitchfork, 2017) contrasts the gospel choir and funky title song with the assertive "Express Yourself", the delicate "Cherish", the exploratory Prince duet "Love Song", and the unusual taped/recut ending "Act of Contrition". She notes the breakup and family-oriented lyrics and more exposed singing, hence lyrical and sonic variety.
- Stephen Thomas Erlewine (AllMusic) identifies a broad palette stretching from funk ("Express Yourself", "Keep It Together") to the haunting "Oh Father". In evaluating energy/groove, do **not** mistake Shep Pettibone's famous *single remix* of "Express Yourself" for the original album mix. Johnston explicitly makes that distinction.
- Co-producer Patrick Leonard describes substantial live playing and substitution of drum machines by live performers, **but also** says much of the bass is synthesizer-generated. Hence neither "all-acoustic" nor "all-electronic" is accurate.
- Contemporary Madonna interview (SongTalk, 1989, later reproduced by fan community) describes the gospel/organ conception of the title song; it corroborates creative intent but the republication is a secondary host.

**Reasoning for proposed scales**
- `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3`: an album-level midpoint of vigorous funk/dance songs and reflective ballads. No numeric BPM claim.
- `electronic=2`: live rhythm sections and choir are prominent, with synth-bass and synth textures persisting; sensitive distinction from pure electronica.
- `mood=3`: celebratory or uplifting passages counterbalance difficult relationship and family narratives.
- `theatrical=4`: expressive gospel choruses, grand ballad arrangements and emotionally emphatic performances.
- `experimental=3`: more ambitious stylistic juxtaposition, tape effects and arrangement diversity than her earlier streamlined pop.
- `polish=4`: sophisticated studio arrangements but some deliberate vocal roughness/exposure.
- `voice=powerful`: the prominent gospel/anthemic material warrants the dominant descriptor, with substantial softer exceptions.
- `lyrics=spiritual|identity`: central but incomplete summary; heartbreak and social messages are also present.
- `genres=pop|rnb|rock`: domain-limited approximation; gospel and funk features explicitly noted here.
- `landmark=1`: critics identify a defining artistic milestone.

**Sources:** https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-like-a-prayer/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/like-a-prayer-mw0000198738 ; https://www.madonnarama.com/posts-en/2014/03/21/patrick-leonard-madonna-and-i-wrote-the-like-a-prayer-album-in-two-weeks/ ; https://fancommunity.madonna.com/blogs/entry/19536-songtalk-summer-1989/

## 2. Erotica (1992) — `madonna-erotica` — NEW ID / not in `seed.ts`

**Source-grounded observations**
- Harry Tafoya's Pitchfork retrospective (22 Feb 2026) distinguishes nightclub release from a somber, ambivalent emotional palette. Discusses Shep Pettibone and André Betts, layering/sampling, relentless house beats, melancholy and long-mix structure, with slower or lighter interludes and stylistic outliers.
- Sal Cinquemani (Slant, 2007) stresses the icy and alienated impression despite dance rhythms. The songs are not simply about sex: difficult intimacy, rejection and troubled relationships dominate stretches of the record. Important contrast between the **singles** and album mood.
- Eric Henderson (Slant, 2022) traces chill house kicks, dense synths, dark atmosphere and an AIDS-era context. This aligns with `mood=1` despite `groove=4`.
- Nick Levine (i-D, 2017) interviewed Pettibone, Betts and co-writer Shimkin. Their testimonies support songwriter/producer attribution and the sample/track construction. **Producer credit is not an `influenced_by` relationship.**

**Reasoning for proposed scales**
- `energy=3`, `tempo=3`: substantial club momentum but long moody/slower interludes; cannot equate `groove` with `tempo`.
- `electronic=4`: house drum programming, synth layers, samples and club treatment are central, while guitar and contrasting instruments appear.
- `mood=1`: cold, ominous, uneasy, melancholic/alienated even when rhythmically danceable.
- `theatrical=3`: "Dita" and shifting performed attitudes, but delivery is often deliberately restrained.
- `experimental=3`: club mix sequencing, unusual tonal juxtapositions and longer atmospheric forms.
- `polish=4`: intricate studio construction; perceived rawness is an aesthetic decision rather than poor production.
- `groove=4`: house/disco movement remains central despite low emotional brightness.
- `voice=soft`: frequently breathy and detached; spoken lead passages and dramatic cuts are exceptions.
- `lyrics=sensuality|heartbreak`: high-level reduction; loss, AIDS, defiance, identity and queer liberation are important additional themes.
- `genres=pop|dance|rnb`, `scene=early-90s New York house`: house is absent from allowed genre taxonomy.
- `landmark=1`: central and unusually contested work in critical retrospectives; not a claim that everyone likes it.

**Sources:** https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-erotica/ ; https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/madonna-erotica/ ; https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/madonna-erotica-retrospective-review/ ; https://i-d.co/article/madonna-erotica-by-the-people-who-helped-create-it/

## 3. Bedtime Stories (1994) — `madonna-bedtimestories` — NEW ID / not in `seed.ts`

**Source-grounded observations**
- Owen Pallett's Pitchfork review (2017) emphasizes warm mellow R&B, soft/lush ballads, hip-hop-soul/new-jack-swing elements and parts of the album he considers inessential. The latter is a **critical preference**, not a technical score.
- Sal Cinquemani (Slant, 2003) describes guitar plus Dallas Austin's R&B beat on "Secret", Babyface's bittersweet "Take a Bow", and the Nellee Hooper/Marius de Vries electronic approach to the Björk-written "Bedtime Story". The electronic track is an exception rather than the median album sound.
- Chris Willman (Los Angeles Times, 23 Oct 1994) hears mostly relaxed midtempo slow jams, hip-hop touches, and little above midtempo. He singles out "Don't Stop" as a danceable exception and "Bedtime Story" for its near-ambient feel.
- Source disagreement: Pallett is markedly skeptical of the album's effectiveness while Cinquemani interprets its lyrical vulnerabilities as a strength. Their sonic observations overlap. These disagreements must not be translated into `polish` or `experimental` scores mechanically.

**Reasoning for proposed scales**
- `energy=2`, `tempo=2`, `groove=2`: overall calm, slow/mid-paced R&B with exceptions ("Don't Stop", "Human Nature").
- `electronic=3`: sampled/programmed R&B rhythms and ambient-electronic title song coexist with acoustic guitar and conventional ballad arrangements.
- `mood=2`: wistful/bitter romantic tone and defensive lyrics, not uniformly gloomy.
- `theatrical=2`: deliberately closer, softer singing despite occasional confrontation.
- `experimental=2`: mainstream contemporary R&B and pop foundations, one distinctive experimental electronic song.
- `polish=4`: crafted, layered major-studio R&B arrangements across multiple producers.
- `voice=soft`: album-level prevailing restrained vocal delivery; "Human Nature" defiance remains a counterexample.
- `lyrics=heartbreak|identity`: yearning, romantic losses and self-defense; narrower than all track themes.
- `genres=pop|rnb|soul`, `scene=1990s contemporary R&B`.
- `landmark=0`: provisional as an album-level 'defining' binary; significant songs and shift to R&B acknowledged. This is not a quality verdict. Claude should flag if the project defines landmarks more broadly.

**Sources:** https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/madonna-bedtime-stories/ ; https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/madonna-bedtime-stories-album-review/ ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-10-23-ca-53563-story.html ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-08-14-ca-26966-story.html
