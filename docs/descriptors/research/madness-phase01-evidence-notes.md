# Madness — Fandome phase 01 evidence notes
Research checked 28 October 2026. The 1979–82 original UK studio albums only (not later deluxe editions). Original analytical judgments are tentative: `status=draft`. Each individual genre is linked to its named album-focused source in `madness_phase01_genre_evidence.csv`.

## One Step Beyond... (1979) — `madness-onestepbeyond`
**Scope:** Stiff's original 1979 LP. This is the first studio album, not a later bonus-disc compilation. Eleven styles are sourced, but ska and 2 Tone carry far more weight than the one-song jangle-pop and rock-and-roll references (Classic Pop; AllMusic; Shfl; discography).

**Values:** `energy=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=5`: Classic Pop describes an urgent, hectic record and Shfl describes a fast 2 Tone ska album. `mood=4`, `theatrical=4`: bright humour, character sketches and vaudevillian delivery; `experimental=2`: stylistic deviations are playful but preserve recognisable songs. `electronic=2`: electric amplified band with keyboards, not synthetic sequencing; `polish=3`: original studio LP of short sessions, no evidence for lo-fi or heavily processed treatment. These are editorial scale judgments, not measurements.

**Instrumentation:** Original LP personnel: Suggs voices, Mike Barson keyboards, Chris Foreman guitar, Mark Bedford bass, Lee Thompson saxophones, Dan Woodgate drums/percussion. The record's prominent brass-like lead is *saxophone*, not sufficient evidence for a full brass section. The spoken/shouted title introduction by Chas Smash supports `spoken` as secondary lead-voice type. Do not import Prince Buster cover personnel or later reissue artists.

**Production:** left EMPTY. Quick recording is documented, but that alone cannot prove `liverecording`, `analogtape`, `overdubbing`, a drum machine or particular effects; avoid attributing one from the era. Producers are Langer and Winstanley, not the songwriter of a cover.

**Lyrics:** exuberance, ironic everyday characters (e.g. *In the Middle of the Night*) and relationships (*My Girl*); the title song and *Swan Lake* are largely instrumental, but the vocal tracks establish the lyrical mix. `influenced_by`: Prince Buster's effect is specifically acknowledged by Suggs (Guardian). The original band's admiration for Kilburn and the High Roads (including *Handsome*), The Kinks and Deaf School is explicitly attested by Suggs, Barson, Foreman or Thompson (Seven Ragged Men). These are formative influences, not just cover-song credits. Reviewer sonic comparisons to Chas & Dave and Ian Dury go into `sounds_like`; the distinction is maintained even if Ian Dury was independently influential.

**Critical variation:** New Wave and R&B appear as source genres alongside ska; early records are rarely pure Jamaican ska despite how they were marketed. Shfl's vaudevillian reading captures presentation and sound, not a claim every track is vaudeville.

## Absolutely (1980) — `madness-absolutely`
**Scope:** original 14-track LP, not the 2010 edition with live recordings. The second album holds to ska and 2 Tone but Shfl documents honky-tonk piano, Farfisa organ, music hall, R&B, vaudeville and blues-based *Solid Gone*. The blues and Motown labels belong to individual songs; Motown specifically to *Embarrassment* in Suggs's interview. Shfl's `modrevival` tag is low-weight and flagged as a proposed new token.

**Values:** `energy=4`, `groove=4` and `tempo=4` reflect the offbeat bounce, dance numbers and brisk songwriting; the instrumental *Return of the Los Palmas 7* is more relaxed. `mood=3`: playful music repeatedly carries darker lyrics, including racism, gentrification and fear. `theatrical=4` (character song, strong stage idiom); `experimental=2` (mostly compact pop songs); `electronic=2` (organ/keys in conventional band); `polish=3` (clean studio recording without evidenced modern studio sheen).

**Instrumentation:** Original album personnel explicitly credit piano, organ, saxophones, guitar including sitar/slide guitar, bass, drums, trumpet, percussion, mallet instruments (vibraphone and marimba), harmonica. `mallets` is the existing Fandome instrument token for vibraphone and marimba, not an invented new token. `production=farfisa` is grounded in the *Shfl* review's explicit Farfisa organ reference. Do not infer heavy overdubbing or a drum machine.

**Lyrics:** *Baggy Trousers* childhood school nostalgia; *Embarrassment* race/family prejudice; *Disappear* gentrification; *Shadow of Fear* anxiety; relationship tension in *In the Rain* and *You Said*. Suggs directly describes *Embarrassment* as mixing Motown and Elvis Costello in his NME interview, supporting an album-specific Elvis Costello influence and comparison; Motown is not an individual musician and cannot enter `influenced_by` as a person. Greil Marcus's negative Blues Brothers comparison is retained only as a low-weight critic comparison.

**Critic disagreement:** Rolling Stone's contemporary dismissal of the second album (quoted in Classic Pop) clashes with later reassessments and UK success. Genre tags in Shfl and AllMusic are broader than the album's strongest ska/pop identity.

## 7 (1981) — `madness-7`
**Scope:** original 1981 13-track LP only. Infobox identifies 2 Tone, ska, pop and reggae; AllMusic adds New Wave, ska revival, Punk/New Wave, contemporary pop/rock. Its pop character is darker than the bright genre tags indicate. `influenced_by` left EMPTY because no reliable direct album-specific named artist attribution was found; the Kinks resemblance is a critic's reading, so `sounds_like` only.

**Values:** `mood=1`: accounts focus on routine, death, workplace drudgery, laboratory animal cruelty and riots (*Cardiac Arrest*, *Grey Day*, *Tomorrow's Dream*, *Day on the Town*). `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` reflect a mix of buoyant numbers and comparatively restrained storytelling. `theatrical=3`, `experimental=2`, `polish=3`, `electronic=2`: characterful but conventional seven-piece band. Scores reflect whole album, not the summer feel of recording in the Bahamas.

**Instrumentation:** Album personnel explicitly identify piano, organ, vibes/marimba (`mallets`), tubular bells, sitar, saxophones, trumpet, guitar, bass, drums and congas. No uncredited synthesizer or device has been filled. `production` is EMPTY: producer Alan Winstanley recalled large amounts being re-recorded in London following the Compass Point sessions. Rerecording a song is not proof of `overdubbing` or `digitalrecording`.

**Lyrics:** `social|introspection|storytelling|political|irony`. Classic Pop documents tragic work and healthcare narratives and an animal-laboratory song. The Kinks comparison applies most clearly to *Day on the Town*; ABBA comparison concerns *Shut Up* and is not an artist-confirmed influence.

**Critic disagreement:** Upbeat dance-band reputation underestimates sombre stories; editorials should not transfer track- or song-specific sound into a dominant album genre. The Wikipedia page warns of incomplete sourcing: supplement with Classic Pop and album-focused AllMusic metadata, so confidence `medium`.

## The Rise & Fall (1982) — `madness-therisefall`
**Scope:** original British LP named *The Rise & Fall* (often AllMusic displays *Madness Presents The Rise & Fall*). Do not treat the different US *Madness* compilation as a new LP or mix the bonus tracks. Musically the album broadens from 2 Tone into pop, New Wave, music hall, orchestral pop, rocksteady, reggae and jazz.

**Values:** `experimental=4` for broader narrative structures and genre variation (Classic Pop's experimental assessment), `polish=4` because brass and string arrangements are documented. `theatrical=4` for concept-like vignettes and music-hall writing. `mood=2`: wistful childhood scenes and family warmth are offset by war, ageing and social commentary. `energy=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` reflect mixed tempos rather than early-album all-out ska. `electronic=2`: keys but no sourced dominant synth/drum machines.

**Instrumentation and production:** LP personnel: Barson keyboards/piano/harmonica; Thompson saxophones; Foreman guitars; Bedford bass AND double bass; Woodgate drums; Smyth trumpet; extra percussion and specifically David Bedford brass/string arrangements. These credits directly justify `brass|strings`, `hornarrangements|stringarrangements`; **David Bedford is an arranger, not a producer**. Langer and Winstanley remain the only two documented producers. Trevor Horn was considered and met the band but DID NOT produce the LP (Classic Pop).

**Lyrics:** Nostalgic *Our House* and title track; *Blue Skinned Beast* criticises the Falklands War; *Primrose Hill* and *That Face* consider ageing; *Mr Speaker* is a comic asylum story. `influenced_by=The Kinks|Ian Dury` draws on band members' explicit long-standing admiration of Ray Davies/Kinks and Dury, independently supported by AllMusic’s account of this LP’s Kinks/Dury-derived concept and locality. This is stronger than a critic's analogy alone. `sounds_like` includes the Kinks' *Village Green Preservation Society* with album name and a separate critical *Sgt Pepper* album-level analogy; these are critic comparisons, not attributed artist influences.

**Critical disagreement:** some classify the album primarily as pop/new wave, while retrospective critics emphasise music hall and non-ska material. These are compatible with a crossover album; rank the pop directions first. The Indian-style vocal delivery on *New Delhi* is described critically, but there is no documented specific Indian-music genre to add from that single track.
