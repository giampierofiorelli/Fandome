# Blur — Phase 02 — evidence notes

Research date: 26 October 2026. Source repository: `giampierofiorelli/Fandome`, branch `descriptors` read-only. Exact artist ID `blur`. All four albums appear in `src/data/catalog.ts`; `docs/descriptors/blur.csv` did not exist at time of lookup. This file describes ONLY 1997–2015 albums; Phase 01's 1991–1995 albums are not reproduced.

**Method.** Fandome's eight numerical scales are interpretive integer album-level judgments anchored to the project's reference points; they are not measurements. Each album-wide genre is separated from a narrower track/texture genre in the genre-evidence CSV. `sounds_like` is an editorial sonic comparison, not an influence assertion. A documented group reference to Pavement supports `influenced_by` only for *Blur* (1997); the remaining influence fields are blank. Instrument lists aggregate **credited instruments heard on at least some tracks**, not a claim that every instrument appears on every recording. MusicBrainz specific-edition credits may contain version-specific details. AllMusic and Guitar.com search-indexed text could be inspected through public search results, though direct automated site access sometimes returned 403; do not imply an opened full page when that was unavailable.

## Blur (1997) — `blur-blur`
- **Genres:** Alternative/indie rock predominates (AllMusic, Guitar.com). Britpop is the abandoned historical frame and is intentionally **not** a genre token here; alternative rock, art rock, slacker rock, noise pop and neo-psychedelia are explicit secondary album-database tags; punk fuzz, psychedelia, and dub appear in selected passages (Pitchfork). The album is **not** uniformly punk/dub/psychedelic. Scope documented per token.
- **Energy 3:** aggressive bursts (“Song 2,” “Chinese Bombs”) offset reflective slower work (“Beetlebum,” “Death of a Party”); **electronic 1:** a band album with organ/keyboards and theremin on “Song 2” but no electronic dominant source; **mood 2:** introspective and disaffected with lively outliers; **theatrical 2:** reduced character performance and rawer singing than Parklife; **experimental 3:** noise episodes and “Essex Dogs” deconstruction, but most songs recognisable; **polish 3:** professional production but deliberately raw textures; **tempo 3** and **groove 2:** mixed tempos, occasional dub grooves, but fundamentally guitar listening music.
- **Voice:** Albarn predominantly clear and soft; stressed/raspy moments; spoken delivery in “Essex Dogs”.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz recording credits list Damon Albarn’s Hammond organ, keyboards and piano, Graham Coxon’s guitars and **theremin on “Song 2”**, plus acoustic guitars, bass, drums and percussion. Theremin should not be assumed to dominate the album.
- **Production:** Guitar distortion and intentionally lo-fi performance/sonic approach are reported in professional reviews; avoid unverified machines, digital software, recording tape type. *Most* tracks produced by **Stephen Street**, but “I’m Just a Killer for Your Love” is **produced by Blur**, specifically verified by MusicBrainz and a contemporary Albarn interview. `Stephen Street|Blur` is therefore deliberate.
- **Influences:** Pitchfork names the band's contemporary fondness for **Pavement** as a factor in the stylistic turn. Similarities to Sonic Youth and the Kinks are editorial, not a documented list of inspirations for the recording.

## 13 (1999) — `blur-13`
- **Genres:** Experimental/art rock and a strong electronic studio layer dominate; contemporary reviewers also identify **trip-hop**, **dream-pop**, and **prog-rock** components, while gospel explicitly describes “Tender” and glam rock mainly describes “Bugman.” **Ambient-pop** comes from an AllMusic retrospective; Guardian names art-pop, and the album-style database independently names art rock. Avoid describing entire record as trip-hop or gospel.
- **Energy 3:** alternation of abrasive “Bugman” and slow sprawling “Caramel”; **electronic 3:** synths, programming, heavily edited improvised recordings, but acoustic kit and electric guitars remain central; **mood 1:** unusually bleak heartbreak and exhaustion; **theatrical 3:** stretched performances and large gospel choir counterposed to fragile singing; **experimental 4:** long pieces, studio collages and disruptive effects; **polish 3:** skilled studio techniques but intentionally ragged balance; **tempo 2** and **groove 2:** more spacious and fractured than dance-focused.
- **Voice/lyrics:** “Tender” features **London Community Gospel Choir** (MusicBrainz), while “Coffee & TV” has Coxon's prominent lead vocal. Heartbreak and introspection are recurrent critical themes.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz credits acoustic/electric guitar, bass and double bass, drums/percussion, keyboards, synth, melodica, banjo, saxophone. A single guest contribution is not assumed to be on all tracks.
- **Production:** Dave Rowntree explained that **William Orbit supervised** recording and **Damian LeGassick extensively cut/pasted and restructured** improvised studio material. These support `sonicmanipulation|programming|overdubbing` rather than unproved named software. Orbit produced the primary album, while the standard edition separately credits **Blur** for “Trailerpark”; engineers like LeGassick are not producer credits.
- **Critical disagreement:** Some 1999 reviews praised experimental ambition; others called it unfocused. Neither judgment is concealed; confidence refers to source reliability, not critical approval.

## Think Tank (2003) — `blur-thinktank`
- **Genres:** Alternative/indie rock as broad base; electronic, dub and hip-hop-inflected beat textures and art-rock looseness are documented by critical sources. Jazz is a **track-level** reference tied to saxophone and extended jams; Britpop is mainly historic catalogue tagging, not the leading sound. The Marrakesh orchestra is a specific recorded ensemble, **not** proof that album is afrobeat or 'world music' throughout.
- **Energy 3:** occasional drive (“Crazy Beat”) among subdued pieces; **electronic 3:** loops, edits, studio effects and drum programming mixed with real bass/drums/orchestra; **mood 2:** uneasy, reflective, sometimes jaunty; **theatrical 2:** intimate and restrained singing; **experimental 4:** relocation, improvisations and orchestral collages; **polish 3:** ambitious edit-heavy recording but not uniformly glossy; **tempo 2**, **groove 3:** generally mid/slower but several beat-oriented tracks.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz: bass, drum kit, Damon Albarn guitar/keyboard, orchestra, cello and goblet drum on “Out of Time”; saxophone in several cuts; regional instrument **`gobletdrum`** is proposed because the credits use “goblet drum”. It is a *track-level* instrument only. Graham Coxon is **only credited for guitar on “Battery in Your Leg”** in the standard release, so he must not be assumed present on all tracks. Instruments remain aggregates.
- **Production:** Ben Hillier and **Blur** are the album's major producers; **Norman Cook** is named on “Crazy Beat” and “Gene by Gene”; **William Orbit** is named on “Sweet Song” in MusicBrainz. **James Dring** is credited with programming, not full album producer; **Jason Cox** appears as assistant/additional producer and engineer, not included under primary producing roles. Dave Rowntree describes **cutting and pasting recorded orchestra performances**. Those details support `programming|sampling|sonicmanipulation`, not claims about a particular sampler.
- **Inspiration:** Marrakesh recording setting is a documented contextual influence, but is not a named *artist* and does not belong in `influenced_by`.

## The Magic Whip (2015) — `blur-themagicwhip`
- **Genres:** Alternative/indie rock core and adult alternative pop-rock (AllMusic). Pitchfork documents **dub** on “New World Towers” and **ambient washes / electro-psych** on “Thought I Was a Spaceman”; ambient and electro-psych are textures, not renamed as ambient-pop genres. A secondary album-style listing explicitly names art rock, neo-psychedelia and new wave. Britpop is intermittent retrospective echo, not a majority classification.
- **Energy 3:** higher-energy opening guitar songs vs large slower middle and closing stretch; **electronic 2:** substantial synth accents and programmed percussion, but majority drums/bass/guitar performance; **mood 2:** travel alienation and homesickness with some uplift; **theatrical 2:** restrained adult vocal; **experimental 3:** ad hoc jams restructured through editing, album nevertheless accessible; **polish 4:** later clean reconstruction/mixing; **tempo 2** and **groove 2:** majority mid/slow, few dance-driven pieces.
- **Instruments:** Guitar, bass, drums, keys, acoustic guitar, synth and strings are attributed in Qobuz/MusicBrainz/AllMusic; **MicroKorg** specifically appears in Qobuz track credits, a named instrument model stored under `production` as **new token `microkorg`**. Recording notes describe Coxon's particular Stratocaster during Hong Kong sessions but no exact model variant, so do not add a model token.
- **Production:** First five days in **Hong Kong's Avon Studios** yielded spontaneous full-band ideas; Coxon and producer Stephen Street reorganized and overdubbed the material in 2014–15; credited production includes **Damon Albarn, Graham Coxon and Stephen Street** (MusicBrainz/AllMusic). Additional percussion/drum programming is credited on particular songs (Qobuz/AllMusic). Criticism over polished vs episodic passages is acknowledged in the numerical scales.

## Checks and cautions
- `microkorg` (production token) and `gobletdrum` (instrument token) must be added to Fandome's respective label dictionaries before running the repository validator.
- Producer names indicate **production/co-production, not songwriting/mixing alone**. Band Blur production is credited on individual tracks of 1997 and 1999 albums; do not conflate with session instrumental credit.
- No special soundtrack, bonus discs, 1998 remix release `Bustin’ + Dronin’`, live albums or 2023 album have been silently included. Phase 03 can study *The Ballad of Darren* (2023) and evaluate any landmark remixes separately.
