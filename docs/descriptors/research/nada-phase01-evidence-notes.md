# Nada — phase 01 | source-based evidence notes and scores

Original releases only. Evidence references are repeated per album in `nada_phase01_sources.csv`. Genre evidence explicitly distinguishes whole-album genre, arrangement-based editorial inference, and limited individual tracks. A source naming an *underlying song's* genre does not prove the *new recording* belongs to it. Scales use Fandome 0–5 anchors; blanks mean insufficient evidence, never zero. `voice`, lyrics, scores and descriptions are original editorial assessments; technical terms and producers need source confirmation.

## Nada (1969) — `nada-nada`

- **Original edition:** RCA Talent TSL 10444, 12 tracks. Several originals and Italian adaptations (including French/English-language material). Distinguish translated songs from original foreign-language recordings. Opening with *Senza te* (after Eric Charden) and including the Italian *Yellow submarine* does not turn the LP into folk-rock, English-language pop or Beatles-influenced songwriting.
- **Credits:** Hit Parade Italia credits *Franco Migliacci* for production. Individual tracks conducted by A. Coggio, R. Cini and P. Pintucci. Choral group Cantori Moderni di Alessandroni appears on *Una rondine bianca* and *Les Bicyclettes de Belsize*; the Pyrañas participate on tracks 3, 10, 12. Those artists are not credited as album producers.
- **Genres:** contemporary period of *musica leggera* / Italian melodic pop; the vocal-pop `pop` umbrella, with possible beat-era group backing on limited tracks. `beat` is a **weak/track-bound** tail genre because SentireAscoltare actually characterises the commercial Nada material as MORE melodic and LESS beat than peers. The `ballad` tail is an editorial song-form inference; both are flagged for critical review. Never assign rock, psych, soul or electronic pop without track-specific evidence.
- **Instrumentation:** `orchestra` is demonstrably credited; details of particular woodwinds/brass/electric guitar or rhythm-section players on the 1969 record were not established in sufficiently specific original-issue documentation. Do not infer these instruments just because a beat band was present. No machine/recording technique survives the evidence threshold, so `production` and `electronic` are blank.
- **Editorial scales:** energy 3 (forceful pop climaxes and contrasting gentler covers); mood 2 (longing and melancholy tempered by more upbeat tracks); theatrical 3 (big vocal delivery but not conceptual rock theatre); experimental 0 (conventional festival/cover song structures); polish 3 (professionally conducted pop); tempo 3 (mixture of medium and brisk songs); groove 2 (not principally dance music). `raspy|powerful` is Nada's known forceful/graing texture during early era; original album evidence for this precise timbre remains limited, hence medium overall confidence.

## Io l'ho fatto per amore (1970) — `nada-iolhofattoperamore`

- **Original edition:** RCA Italiana PSL 10484, 12 numbered tracks, including a two-part *C'è una luce / Credo* medley. Includes previously released 1969 Nada songs and new 1970 studio recordings, original Italian pop, and translated covers (*I've Lost You*, *The Girl From Salina*, *Little Green Bag*, *Silver Bird*). This is a regular contemporaneous studio LP with mixture of recordings, **not** a later retrospective greatest-hits compilation.
- **Production:** Franco Migliacci only. The album credits Antonio Coggio as arranger with Ruggero Cini, Tony Mimms, Paolo Ormi and Piero Pintucci conducting selected tracks; never substitute conductors for credited producers.
- **Genres:** `musicaleggera|italianpop|pop` explicit broad setting. `orchestralpop` justified by the distributed orchestral conductors on multiple tracks (arrangement inference). `ballad` is a limited melodic-song-form interpretation, `beat` a tentative long-tail attribution to *Un passatempo* and select late-sixties repertoire; should not dominate ranking. These last two are editorial rather than directly attributed review taxonomy.
- **Instruments/production:** documented orchestra; other individual instruments are not named reliably enough at LP level, so field includes only `orchestra` and leaves production/electronic blank. The 1969 songs reused here do not provide new evidence of specific studio machines.
- **Scales:** energy 2, mood 2 and tempo 2 for mostly measured relationship songs; theatrical 3, experimental 0, polish 3, groove 2 are conservative. Songs' very different source repertoire (Elvis versus George Baker) is not evidence that this recording shares every style of the originals. Medium confidence.

## Ho scoperto che esisto anch'io (1973) — `nada-hoscopertocheesistoanchio`

- **Original edition:** RCA DPSL 10619, November 1973, ten tracks. A modern Qobuz/Apple version has twelve tracks because it adds 1977 *L'amore è tutto qui* and *Lemme lemme*. The two bonus tracks are **not** part of the original LP and must not affect genre/ratings. Original side B closes with seven-minute *Esisto anch'io*.
- **Credits:** producers Piero Ciampi and Gianni (Giovanni) Marchetti, according to Hit Parade Italia and per-track Sony metadata. Marchetti also arranged/conducted. Pino Pavone is mainly lyricist/co-writer. *Lui è un folle* has separate writing credits and is not a Ciampi composition; no need to pretend every track has same authors.
- **Instrument sources:** SentireAscoltare describes *archi*, *piano* and *orchestra*, plus guitars (including a distorted/compressed electric guitar and another with tremolo) and harmonica on *Confiteor*. `strings|piano|orchestra|electricguitar|guitar|harmonica` and `distortion|tremolo` are **documented but some occur in one track**. The production term `stringarrangements` is an editorial structural label supported by Marchetti's credited orchestration and the reviewed string parts. It is not a specific studio device. Do not assert echo chambers, analog tape, named synths, Pro Tools or multitracking.
- **Genres:** canzone d’autore is primary; orchestral pop is an explicitly *arrangement-derived* secondary tag. Italian melodic/pop context continues but lyrics and structures disrupt it. Ballads/musica leggera are minor structures/sonic palette rather than unchanged commercial songwriting; no ungrounded album-wide prog tag. Critic hears Pink Floyd-like opening in one track but **not** evidence of a declared album influence; `influenced_by` stays empty.
- **Scales:** energy 2; electronic 1 from documented electric guitar amid predominantly acoustic/orchestral sound sources; mood 1 for desolation, unsettling vignettes; theatrical 4 for dramatic and sardonic delivery; experimental 2 for lyric/arrangement juxtaposition and long finale; polish 4 for orchestrated recording; tempo 2 and groove 1. Lyrical range includes irony, identity, love, societal observations, storytelling and hints of sensuality. This record is a landmark in Nada's move toward independent interpretive choices.
- **Influence/collaboration rule:** Nada explicitly calls Ciampi crucial to her artistic growth in 2025, but he is the actual composer/producer/collaborator on this LP. Keep him in phase-01 artist influences, not inside a direct album `influenced_by` entry merely for his authorship. `sounds_like` as editorial sonic relationship is intentional but could be left blank if Claude's catalog treats own collaborator as disallowed comparison.

## 1930: Il domatore delle scimmie (1975) — `nada-1930ildomatoredellescimmie`

- **Date dispute:** Treccani and some biographies call this a 1974 record. The original RCA label TPL1 1157, May 1975 pressing record, specialist ItalianProg, Hit Parade Italia and historical catalog give **1975**, used here. Secondary retrospective interviews occasionally misdate it 1973; these are incorrect relative to label.
- **Original album:** twelve tracks. Henryk Topel producer and principal musical author/arranger; Nada contributed the words for *Sexy rosa*. The Reale Accademia di Musica musicians include Aldo Colangelo (keyboards), Gianfranco Coletta, Henryk Topel and Enzo De Luca (guitars), Carlo Bruno (bass), Roberto Senzasono (drums), with backing voices. L'Isola album review confirms acoustic guitar, Latin percussion and a conspicuous rock piano. **No synth synthesizer is identified.**
- **Studio techniques:** original record engineers Ubaldo Consoli and Enzo Martella named by historical sleeve details, but their names are not `production` tokens; no effect/technique or hardware is explicitly documented. Production column blank.
- **Genres and scope:** detailed L'Isola reviewer hears rhythmic Latin percussion on title-track, rock piano in *Alte sfere*, prog passages in *Idea comune* and *Cinema*, a folk ballad on *Lady Onion*. Specialist ItalianProg explicitly says that despite the prog band the album's actual overall songwriting is *canzonettistico*. So `italianpop` and `poprock` first, then `folk|prog|ballad` as **selected-track genres**. There is no justification for a full `prog`/`progressiverock` identity, or Latin as whole-album genre solely from one opening texture. Several songs explore social satire, fables, and intimate singing; not a generic 1975 prog suite.
- **Scales:** energy 3, electronic 1 (electric group against acoustic and piano arrangement), mood 3 (satire and playful sections alongside serious stories), theatrical 3, experimental 2, polish 3, tempo 3, groove 2. Novel cross-genre passages and some unexpected arrangements are real, but it remains pop-song-centred.
- **Crediting:** On original physical LP references the record artist is *Nada* and her backing group is named in press and documentation. Do not register the backing musicians as all co-billed on physical cover without primary proof. **No direct artist/producer-stated outside-artist influence for the 1975 album** was found, so `influenced_by` remains empty.

## Method limits

Album-specific review coverage for 1969 and 1970 is scarce; Hit Parade Italia and original release documentation provide firm chronology, players as orchestral ensembles, and production roles, while `beat`, `ballad`, and `orchestralpop` remain lower-confidence editorial inferences. The genres are deliberately ordered to suppress these low-confidence tags. Do not fill them with unsupported named instruments, synths or producer credits. Later reviewer views on 1973 and 1975 differ on degree of prog, experimentalism, and pop orientation, and those differences are recorded above.
