# Laura Branigan — Phase 02 evidence notes (Fandome)

**Prepared:** 29 October 2026. Scope: original studio recordings only, **three incremental rows**. Source IDs are keys in `laurabranigan_phase02_sources.csv`. Descriptors express album-level editorial judgements tied to the quoted credits and the original arrangements, not numerical measurements. `status=draft` throughout.

## Touch (1987) — `laurabranigan-touch`

- **Release and scope:** official artist website gives 7 July 1987; Apple Music gives 30 June. The original vinyl has **11 tracks**; "Statue in the Rain" was a CD-only extra, not a new studio album. The source artist page and reissues list 12. Use the original album as one Fandome entry, no extra edition. [t_official; t_apple; t_chartsurfer; t_session]
- **Genres:** AllMusic explicitly identifies adult contemporary, contemporary pop-rock and dance-pop. Original vinyl is tagged synth-pop and pop-rock; MusicBrainz tags pop/electronic; an original US CD is tagged disco. `ballad` expresses the substantial number of slower songs singled out by Bryan Buss. Distinct tags are retained in weighted order; this is **not** an all-disco or all-dance album. [t_allmusic; t_musicbrainz; t_chartsurfer; t_rhino]
- **Eight scales:** energy **3** and tempo **3**, because vigorous programmed dance tracks alternate with numerous reflective ballads; electronic **4** because AllMusic and Qobuz explicitly say synth-heavy, and Sessiondays credits Linn electronic drums and programming across the original album; mood **2** for frequent moody, wounded love songs; theatrical **4** for dramatic phrasing and power ballads; experimental **1** for conventional structures; polish **4** for professional studio layering and articulated strings; groove **3** because dance tracks are prominent but not dominant. [t_allmusic; t_qobuz; t_session; t_rhino]
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear|soft`: AllMusic emphasizes large dynamic vocal power and quieter passages, including "The Power of Love". [t_allmusic; t_qobuz]
- **Instruments:** `synth|keyboards|drummachine|guitar|drums|strings` are specifically supported by player credits: Bob Marlette and Rick Palombi keyboards and guitars; UK Stock–Aitken–Waterman Linn drum parts; Carlos Vega acoustic drums on "Cry Wolf"; Kim Scharnberg strings on "Power of Love" and "Cry Wolf". A generic `drummachine` is used because "Linn" does **not** unambiguously distinguish LM-1 from LinnDrum. No undocumented bass instrument added. [t_session; t_allmusic; t_qobuz]
- **Production:** `drumprogramming|programming|stringarrangements` derives from explicit drum-programming/PPG engineering and string arrangements. Do not imply `analogtape`, a specific Linn model, a particular digital workstation or `sampling` without proof. [t_session]
- **Producers:** main original LP credits are David Kershenbaum for tracks 1–5 and 8–11, plus Stock–Aitken–Waterman for tracks 6–7. **The Latin Rascals** are marked *additional production* for "Name Game" and should not be promoted to main producers. The official artist page broadly calls Albert Cabrera and Tony Moran producers, while sleeve transcription calls the Latin Rascals *additional*; absent proof of a separate main production credit, they have been omitted from the strict `producers` field. [t_session; t_official; t_rhino]
- **Themes / language:** relationship longing, heartbreak and adult introspection dominate; English lyrics. [t_allmusic; t_official]
- **Similarity versus influence:** Rhino explicitly likens "Spirit of Love" to the **Pet Shop Boys**; Buss compares the breadth of Branigan's vocal technique to **Celine Dion**. Both are editorial `sounds_like` judgements, **not influence claims**; covers by Jennifer Rush and Stevie Nicks' later cover of "Cry Wolf" do not make those artists influences. `influenced_by` is blank. [t_rhino; t_allmusic]
- **Evidence quality:** high for arrangements, players and contrasts; genre tags partly from community-discography editions, so the relative order remains editorial.

## Laura Branigan (1990) — `laurabranigan-laurabranigan`

- **Release and identity:** the 1990 *Laura Branigan* is distinct from the 1982 debut *Branigan*. Original artist site, Apple and Qobuz agree **30 March 1990** and **ten tracks**; AllMusic says April 1990 and some secondary discographies March 21. Year-only CSV stays 1990. [b90_official; b90_apple; b90_qobuz; b90_allmusic]
- **Genres:** Qobuz files original album as pop; AllMusic says contemporary pop-rock; original Atlantic German LP is explicitly tagged synth-pop; contemporary CD pressing is listed Disco. Sessiondays describes a Hi-NRG dancefloor return for "Moonlight on Water" and an AC hit in "Never in a Million Years". `disco|hinrg|adultcontemporary` are **track/subset** genres, weighted lower. Do not equate `hinrg` with `dancepop` or `synthpop`. [b90_qobuz; b90_allmusic; b90_musiksammler; b90_musiksammlercd; b90_session]
- **Eight scales:** energy **3**, tempo **3**, groove **3** reflect mid/upbeat tracks plus ballads; electronic **4** follows the explicitly extensive drum programming, sequencing, synths and keyboards; mood **3** spans club-oriented tracks and vulnerable love songs; theatrical **4** follows AllMusic's vigorous vocal evaluation; experimental **1** conventional commercial studio-pop arrangements; polish **4** for dense, professional multi-producer sessions. [b90_session; b90_allmusic; b90_musiksammler]
- **Voice:** `powerful|clear` from William Ruhlmann's description of persistent vigorous singing. [b90_allmusic]
- **Instruments:** credited keyboards and synthesizers (Steve Lindsey, Peter Wolf, etc.), guitar (James Harrah, Dann Huff), bass (Larry Klein, Michael Dunlap), drums (Jeff Porcaro and session drummer parts), percussion (Paulinho da Costa and Lenny Castro), piano (Randy Kerber), sax (Brandon Fields). Studio programming is captured under `production`, **not** guessed as a particular drum-machine make. [b90_session; b90_musiksammler; b90_qobuz]
- **Production:** `drumprogramming|programming`; extensive track-level sequences and programmed drums confirmed. No claim of named sound-model software or hardware without exact evidence; a SynthAxe credit exists for one track but has been conservatively omitted from the principal instruments. [b90_session; b90_musiksammler]
- **Producers:** official lists Richard Perry, Branigan, Matt Aitken, Steve Kipner, Peter Wolf, Peter Bunetta, Steve Lindsey, Clif Magness, Dennis Matkosky. Original LP also assigns **Rick Chudacoff** production on "Unison". **Doug Morris** is identified as *executive producer* by official credits and original sleeve, therefore excluded despite Qobuz automated track metadata calling him a producer. [b90_official; b90_musiksammler; b90_qobuz]
- **Themes / language:** romance, heartbreak, celebratory dance-song themes, English. Source-specific lyrics research is less deep than production credits; no speculative `identity` or `political`. [b90_official; b90_allmusic]
- **Similarities / influences:** no album-specific first-person influence statement or defensible critic comparison was found. Covering Vicki Sue Robinson, Bryan Adams or other songwriters does not establish `influenced_by`; both relation fields are intentionally empty.
- **Evidence quality:** medium because of multiple producer metadata variants and genre classes describing subsets rather than the entire album.

## Over My Heart (1993) — `laurabranigan-overmyheart`

- **Release and scope:** the artist website and Apple Music date the original 12-song Atlantic album **17 August 1993**. Some European releases are catalogued 20 August 1993; neither is a separate Fandome album. [omh_official; omh_apple; omh_spanishcharts]
- **Genres:** Atlantic/Apple classifies pop; AllMusic and the original 1993 CD classify pop-rock; AllMusic's critical review explicitly places the ballad-heavy content near adult contemporary. An original pressing is styled `ballad`, `electronic`, `downtempo` and `house`; the last is a **minority dance component** (not the album as a whole). Avoid treating the one Spanish cover or one Sesotho wedding song as evidence for whole-album latin or worldmusic. [omh_apple; omh_allmusic; omh_musicbase]
- **Eight scales:** energy **2**, tempo **2** and groove **2** reflect a mostly slow romantic programme (original opener listed at 77 BPM), with occasional rhythmic breaks. Electronic **2** follows credited keyboards and programmed/sampled passages alongside live bass/drums and acoustic percussion. Mood **2** from loss and longing; theatrical **3** because her clear, dynamic voice still carries dramatic moments but intimate ballads prevail; experimental **1** for familiar arrangements with two unusual album tracks; polish **4** for the sophisticated studio players and Phil Ramone sessions. [omh_allmusic; omh_beatport; omh_official; omh_musiksammler]
- **Voice:** `clear|powerful|soft`: Mike DeGagne explicitly singles out the freshness and clarity of the singing, while dramatic ballads require controlled dynamic delivery. [omh_allmusic]
- **Instruments:** original sleeve credits and AllMusic list keys, guitars, bass, acoustic drums, percussion, alto/tenor sax and **marimba** in the South African sessions; Mmabana Children's Choir sings on the final song. `marimba` is now part of the checked Fandome instrument vocabulary. Do not create an artificial new token; omit instruments with no named personnel. [omh_allmusic; omh_musiksammler]
- **Production:** official source credits Clay Ostwald and Jorge Casas with `programming`, and Roy McDonald with sampling/computers; `sampling` therefore documented even if the exact songs using it aren't all specified. No named sampler hardware or DAW established. [omh_official; omh_allmusic]
- **Producers:** Phil Ramone; Laura Branigan; Billy Branigan; Clay Ostwald; Jorge Casas, with exact track assignments in original CD credits: Ramone mainly 1–6 and 8–10; Billy 7,11; Branigan 7,11,12; Ostwald and Casas 6. **Stevie Godson** is explicitly an *executive producer* (not a main producer), so excluded. [omh_musicbase; omh_official]
- **Language:** predominantly English; "Mujer Contra Mujer" is sung in **Spanish** (`es`) and "Mangwane (The Wedding Song)" includes **Sesotho** (`st`), reinforced by available language metadata. These languages are **track-level minority** cases, but Fandome supports a list. [omh_spanishcharts; omh_lyricsweb; omh_official]
- **Lyrics:** mostly love and heartbreak; introspection and storytelling also in "How Can I Help You Say Goodbye"; `identity` is a minor theme on "Mujer Contra Mujer". None of the writers covered is automatically an artistic influence. [omh_allmusic; omh_official]
- **Critics' disagreement:** Mike DeGagne at AllMusic is critical of the ballad-heavy, conventional songwriting, while Album of the Year indexes a noticeably more positive **Entertainment Weekly** contemporaneous review. Confidence is medium despite strong player and track evidence. [omh_allmusic; omh_aoty]
- **Similarities / influences:** no verifiable album-specific influence declaration found. The Spanish Mecano cover and Gloria Estefan's writing credits are not influences; both relational columns empty.
