# Taylor Swift — Fandome Phase 01 evidence notes (2026-10-26)

## Global decisions

**Album scope:** four last *new original* studio projects from 2020–2025, chronological. The Taylor's Version recordings are re-recordings of historical material and are deliberately excluded. Other original Swift records from 2020 (folklore) and before are outside this *user-requested* range, not yet completed. **Discography is therefore NOT complete**.

**Album id:** `taylorswift`, from `catalog.ts`. All four album titles already appear. `docs/descriptors/taylorswift.csv` is absent on this inspected branch. No albums to add. Every row has exactly 27 columns, is `draft`, and every `notes` contains no more than 15 words. No co-billed albums: featured HAIM, Bon Iver, Post Malone, Florence Welch and Sabrina Carpenter are track features rather than full co-headlined albums.

**Scale anchors:** The eight 0–5 values are original editorial judgments from *each album's* sources, not an arithmetic average of track metadata. These mark whole-album typical properties; higher genre relevance appears first. Empty `influenced_by` is a deliberate evidence abstention, not a statement that Swift has no influences. Career-level influences are documented separately in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md` and MUST NOT be copied into each album.

## evermore (2020) — `taylorswift-evermore`

**Edition and genres:** Original 15-track 11 December 2020 release, excluding later bonus tracks *right where you left me* and *it's time to go*. AllMusic explicitly categorizes the record as **Indie Folk**, **Adult Alternative Pop/Rock**, **Alternative/Indie Rock**, **Pop**, **Contemporary Singer/Songwriter** (the last is a new token). AllMusic also gives Pop/Rock as broad category. NME and Pitchfork discuss country storytelling, roots/blues on *cowboy like me*, and limited synth-pop sheen on *gold rush* and *long story short*. Preserve these as track-scoped, subordinate genres, not full-album genre replacements. Sources: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/evermore-mw0003459663), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/taylor-swift-evermore-review-2835933), [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-evermore/).

**Ratings:** `energy=2` for softly sustained ballads interrupted by a few full-band numbers; `electronic=2` for acoustic piano/guitar basis with drum-machine and synth layering, not a fully acoustic album; `mood=1` for grief, rejection, resignation, mortality despite some hopeful songs; `theatrical=2` for restrained singing despite character-driven murder ballad; `experimental=3` for *closure*'s skewed rhythmic structure and distinctive digital interludes; `polish=3` for natural and carefully composed studio recordings without maximalist glossy hits; `tempo=2` most tracks slow to moderate; `groove=1` mostly contemplative, even when live and programmed drums recur. NME and Pitchfork reviews above.

**Instrumentation:** [Pitchfork's track-by-track **original liner-note credits**](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-evermore-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/) explicitly identify Aaron Dessner on acoustic/electric guitars, piano, bass, synthesizers, keys and drum-machine programming throughout. Yuki Numata Resnick violin, Clarice Jensen cello, Logan Coale upright bass, James McAlister synths/drum machines, and diverse horns, harmonium, glockenspiel and banjo are documented by track. Lap steel on *'tis the damn season* and *cowboy like me* belongs in `steelguitar`; *Gold Rush* DX7 and OP-1 are actual credited instruments/models, not predicted 1980s gear. On *marjorie*, Justin Vernon is credited on **Prophet X**; this is the new exact `prophetx` token, NOT Prophet-5. **`op1`** is a new model token. `fieldrecordings` sourced to *no body, no crime* and the title track, not just atmosphere. `stringarrangements` derived from credited Bryce Dessner orchestration for recurring violin/cello parts; `hornarrangements` explicit Benjamin Lanz on *'tis the damn season*. Do not credit Jack Antonoff as a producer of *ivy*: he is a songwriter there; production belongs to Aaron Dessner. BJ Burton and James McAlister have **additional production only** on *closure*, excluded from producers. All producer names (Aaron Dessner, Taylor Swift, Jack Antonoff, Bryce Dessner) appear under explicit *Produced by* on at least one original track. William Bowery is co-writer but not a producer. [Original credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-evermore-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/).

**Voice/lyrics:** predominantly soft/clear, with emphatic peaks on *evermore* and *tolerate it*. Main lyrical tendency: semi-fictional stories of love, rejection, grief and retrospect; *no body, no crime* dark irony. [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/taylor-swift-evermore-review-2835933). `sounds_like` compares musical *character* editorially (Dessner/The National guitar/piano arrangements, Bon Iver sonics, etc.), not causal influence. HAIM and Bon Iver are collaborators but are **not** in influenced_by.

## Midnights (2022) — `taylorswift-midnights`

**Edition:** The 21 October original **13 tracks** are the record. At approximately 3AM she issued **seven additional tracks**, an expansion of the same album, not a fifth studio album and not included in this descriptor. In particular **Aaron Dessner is NOT a producer on the original 13 tracks**; he appears on 3am extras. [Pitchfork release credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-midnights-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/). On the thirteen, `Lavender Haze` and `Karma` carry Sounwave/Jahaan Sweet / Keanu Beats **producer or co-producer** roles; Braxton Cook's credit is **additional production only**, excluded. Keanu Beats' appearance is track 11 `Karma`, not a generic album co-writer.

**Genres:** NME and Guardian identify primary **synth-pop** / pop, while Guardian additionally describes electronic pop and beats with **trap**, **house**, and **dubstep** influences. These are tagged **texture**, never represented as full song genres. The Observer describes occasional R&B movement, and NME hears industrial texture in *Vigilante Shit*. Unlike 2020, not an indie-folk record: isolated *Snow on the Beach* recalls that phase but does not justify whole-album indiefolk. [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/taylor-swift-midnights-review-3332805), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2022/oct/21/taylor-swift-midnights-review-small-hours-pop-rich-with-self-loathing-and-stereotype-smashing), [Observer](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2022/oct/23/taylor-swift-midnights-review-poised-between-self-flagellation-and-pure-bliss).

**Scores:** `energy=3` most songs restrained but rhythmic; `electronic=4` drum programming and synths lead arrangements with live players still credited; `mood=2` darker doubt/confession offset by wry or affirmative singles; `theatrical=2` comparatively understated vocal register with theatrical flashes; `experimental=2` unusual warped voice but standard pop composition; `polish=5` meticulous layered high-fidelity studio; `tempo=3` mostly midtempo; `groove=3` several danceable pulses but not a non-stop dance LP. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-midnights/).

**Instruments and production:** Official [Pitchfork full liner credit listing](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-midnights-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/) explicitly identifies Jack Antonoff on Juno 6, Prophet 5, Moog, DX7, Minimoog; these are separate exact machines (`rolandjuno6`,`prophet5`,`moog`,`dx7`,`minimoog`). The **Juno 6**, not Juno 60 or Juno 106, is repeatedly specified. Also piano, acoustic/electric guitars, drums, percussion, synths, bass, mellotron, Wurlitzer, organ, violin, brass/woodwind instruments, kalimba and Omnichord. `omnichord` requires new `instruments.ts` token; `pitchedvocals` on *Midnight Rain* and `vocalfilter` in reviews refer to heard processing, **do not assert Auto-Tune as the exact tool**. No Pro Tools / Logic / named drum-machine model is documented by cited record-level credits.

**Narrative:** insecurity, social expectation and gender roles, revenge, nostalgia and romantic privacy. `sounds_like` compares muted vocals and production to Lorde, Eilish, and Del Rey as the reviews do, without treating those comparisons as named **influences**.

## The Tortured Poets Department (2024) — `taylorswift-thetorturedpoetsdepartment`

**Edition policy:** 19 April **original 16-track** edition. Two hours later the artist released *The Anthology*, 15 more songs for a 31-track extended edition. **Same original studio album, not a distinct additional Fandome row.** The broader Anthology is vital context for critics, but the genre/scale/instruments/producers in this row are anchored to the **original first 16 tracks**; do not import physical 2025 editions as new albums. [Pitchfork release report](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-the-tortured-poets-department-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/), [Metacritic discography](https://metacritic.com/music/the-tortured-poets-department/taylor-swift).

**Genres:** AP names synth-pop, folk and country among the album's phases; Guardian discusses a restrained 1980s pop-rock approach mixed with electronic textures. These are not equally prominent: pop/synthpop first, acoustic folk and country-related material secondary. **No generic `opera` or `musicaltheatre` merely because the title sounds theatrical.** [AP](https://apnews.com/article/b24d2275df5cf78ffa9069c61b9825ec), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2024/apr/19/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-review-fame-fans-and-former-flames-in-the-firing-line), [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-the-anthology/).

**Scales:** `energy=2` predominantly quiet midtempo tracks with larger climaxes (*Florida!!!*, *Who's Afraid of Little Old Me?*); `electronic=3` synth and acoustic split between Antonoff and Dessner; `mood=1` extended grief and resentment; `theatrical=3` vocally/storytelling theatrical on central tracks; `experimental=1` conservative structures across 16; `polish=4` controlled major-label studio layers; `tempo=2`, `groove=2` some metronomic electronic pop, mostly not dance music. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-the-anthology/), [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-album-review/).

**Credited performers:** Original-release [MusicBrainz liner transcription](https://musicbrainz.org/release/58a7abd7-de6a-4c3d-80b7-cd7fd45a5d0a) gives Antonoff on synths, programming, drums, guitars, piano, Mellotron, cello; Aaron Dessner on keyboard, piano, bass synthesizers and other instruments and studio drums/cello/violin. Vocal guest Post Malone is not a musician on every track; Florence Welch is only on *Florida!!!*. `programming` and `drumprogramming` supported by credited performance / production; `layeredvocals` is NOT asserted from an ordinary backing-vocal credit alone; uncertain **exact** Auto-Tune, DAW and hardware models are left out. Taylor herself credited piano in full personnel. Some databases show **Louis Bell** as a *producer of Post Malone lead vocals only* on Fortnight: do **not** add him as an album record producer. Some databases also have producer names scoped to portions of a song; primary album producer/co-producer list remains Swift, Antonoff, Dessner. The *Anthology*-only vocoder and gear cannot be carried into the 16-track album simply because same release family.

**Critic disagreement:** Pitchfork and Slant argue the length blunts emotional force; NPR emphasizes the introspective poetic narrative and blend of music eras. Those are differing assessments, not contradictory credit facts. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-the-anthology/), [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/taylor-swift-the-tortured-poets-department-album-review/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/04/19/1245630721/taylor-swift-tortured-poets-department-review).

## The Life of a Showgirl (2025) — `taylorswift-thelifeofashowgirl`

**Edition:** the October 3, 2025 **12 original songs**. On 25 September 2026 *The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore* added **four previously unreleased tracks**. This is an expanded 2025 album, **not a separately numbered 13th original studio album**; those songs are excluded from this 2025 base row, as are separate remix/acoustic bundles. [Reuters original](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/taylor-swift-says-showgirl-album-reflects-joy-recent-tour-2025-08-14/), [Reuters Encore](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/taylor-swift-releases-encore-her-hit-showgirl-album-2026-09-25/).

**Genres and disagreements:** Guardian hears primarily **soft rock** and a **relaxed disco** style, **not** the maximalist electropop fans might expect from Max Martin. AllMusic gives Pop and Contemporary Singer/Songwriter with Pop/Rock umbrella. Pitchfork files broad Pop/R&B, yet this only supports R&B as secondary/texture, not dominant. NME's hip-hop-influenced percussion is a rhythmic comparison, **not** a claim the record is hip-hop or trap. A Cambridge musicologist documents deliberate genre nostalgia in *Opalite* and other tracks. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2025/oct/03/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-review), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-life-of-a-showgirl-mw0004596064), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-review-3897387), [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-humanities/article/taylor-swifts-the-life-of-a-showgirl-is-musically-nostalgic-for-a-time-long-past/6E02BABCC183169316C96702F148DAB0).

**Scales:** `energy=3` some bright and punchy choruses counterbalanced by quieter passages; `electronic=2` instrument-heavy drums/bass/guitars with keyboards/programming, rather than the fully synth-driven 1989 style; `mood=4` overall buoyant romance tempered by digs at rivals and disillusionment about fame; `theatrical=3` concept, characters and narratives, with comparatively restrained vocal performances; `experimental=1` familiar pop song forms; `polish=5` professional studio detail and multi-player orchestration; `tempo=3` midtempo; `groove=3` disco-inflected rhythms but not a front-to-back club record. **Do not rate energy=5 or groove=5 from promotional claims.** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2025/oct/03/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-review), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-review-3897387).

**Production, exact credits:** [Pitchfork's original full 12-track credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-the-life-of-a-showgirl-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/) independently aligns with [Taylor Swift's official original Opalite master](https://www.taylorswift.com/tloasopalitecredits/) on Swift, Max Martin, Shellback **all three as actual producers**. *Opalite* Shellback specifically credits **Omnichord**, guitars, bass, drums, percussion and programming; Taylor Swift, Max Martin and Shellback clap together. Other originals feature sax/trumpet/trombone sections, cello/violins, organ, woodwinds, harp, mandolin, bassoon, and other musicians; producer trio are not the sole instrumentalists. `hornarrangements` and `stringarrangements` have explicit album arrangement credits. Digital editing engineer is explicitly named for *Father Figure*, so `digitalediting` is limited production value. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-the-life-of-a-showgirl-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/), [official Opalite](https://www.taylorswift.com/tloasopalitecredits/).

**George Michael caution:** *Father Figure* **interpolates** George Michael's song: Billboard explicitly says the song **does not sample the original recording**. George Michael appears as **songwriter**, but `sampling` is deliberately absent from production. An interpolation is neither a recorded sample nor documented musical influence on Swift; therefore `influenced_by` stays empty. Sabrina Carpenter is a **featured vocalist** on title track, not a co-billed album co-artist or producer. Taylor says the Eras Tour's joy informed concept, not that an identifiable musical artist influenced this album. [Pitchfork original credits](https://pitchfork.com/news/taylor-swift-releases-new-album-the-life-of-a-showgirl-listen-and-read-the-full-credits/), [Billboard on interpolation](https://ca.billboard.com/music/music-news/taylor-swift-interpolates-george-michael-father-figure-1236079723/), [Reuters interview](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/taylor-swift-says-showgirl-album-reflects-joy-recent-tour-2025-08-14/).

**Critical disagreement:** Guardian and Pitchfork are notably cooler than NME about the artistic payoff; showgirl visual/theatrical framing did not translate into uniformly maximal dance production. The numbers above judge actual recordings and review accounts, not implied showbiz scale. Compare [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl/), [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/album/taylor-swift-the-life-of-a-showgirl-review-3897387).
