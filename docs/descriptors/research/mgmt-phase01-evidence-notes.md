# MGMT – Phase 01: evidence notes

All numeric scores are editorial album-level judgments against the Fandome 0–5 anchors. **These are drafts, not an independent second-person review.** Evidence below is keyed to the sources CSV. Subjective sonic analogies are stored in `sounds_like`, not used as proof of actual influence. The exact genre tokens represent the source's named genre, not substitutions for similarly named genres.

## mgmt-timetopretend — Time to Pretend (EP, 2005)
- **Chronology:** Cantora EP, 2005, six songs. Distinct early recordings of *Time to Pretend* and *Kids*, not the later LP versions. The band's own [Song Exploder interview](https://songexploder.net/mgmt), `ep-songexploder`, confirms this. Later remasters and vinyl anniversary editions must not change the original year.
- **Genre and scores:** `ep-allmusic` describes homemade laptop electro jams, contrasting with the later expansive Dave Fridmann production; `ep-dis` quotes VanWyngarden describing the older EP songs as more electronic and sarcastic. Electronic 4, polish 2 and experimental 2 reflect that raw, largely synth-origin sound; energy/groove are moderate-to-high on the two defining songs, balanced by the six-track EP.
- **Instruments:** AllMusic's original-credit database lists VanWyngarden on guitar (`ep-allmusic`), and band account describes early keyboard/synth composition (`ep-songexploder`); **no particular drum-machine model or sampled source has been verified**. Only generic `guitar` is used because the original credits do not specify acoustic/electric subtype.
- **Production:** Empty intentionally; laptop demo sound alone is insufficient to establish a named piece of equipment or studio technique. Original producer David Molinari is credited by AllMusic; keep separate from later producer Dave Fridmann.
- **Themes and voices:** Sarcastic rock-star fantasy in title track; other tracks cover youth, attachment and abandon (`ep-dis`, `ep-podscript`). Soft/clear indicate co-sung voices; technical layers not asserted.
- **Influences:** None asserted for the EP specifically. The Flaming Lips inspiration refers to the better documented development of *Time to Pretend* and is included with the first LP only; `ep-songexploder`, `gen-influences5`.

## mgmt-oracularspectacular — Oracular Spectacular (2007)
- **Album version:** Some retailers date physical reissues or broad release 2008; `os-pfork` reviewed it October **2007** and `os-interview` confirms US release that October. Fandome's 2007 record and slug are correct.
- **Styles:** Album-level cross of indietronica and psychedelic pop/neo-psychedelia (`os-dork`, `os-allmusic`). Specific tracks bring new-wave synth-pop, disco and 1970s prog traces (`os-pfork`); `Electric Feel` has expressly named electro-funk; folktronica is a secondary database classification and belongs late in the list. No genre limit used.
- **Scales:** Energy 4/groove 4 from hit-led dance rhythms, tempered by an acoustic segment and longer progressive arrangements. Electronic 4 from prominent synth hooks but live/strummed parts. Mood 4 mixes exuberance with anxiety and nostalgia. Experimental 3 from structural detours. Polish 5 derives from Dave Fridmann's compressed, deliberately grand studio sound (`os-allmusic`).
- **Instruments and production:** Keyboard/synth and acoustic guitar specifically discussed by Pitchfork (`os-pfork`), drums and bright keys by AllMusic (`os-allmusic`); synth, drums, bass and guitars represent the band/recorded sound but precise credited subtype should be checked by Claude where only a review exists. AllMusic notes distorted drums and meticulously layered keyboards: use `distortion`; Pitchfork explicitly describes falsetto on *Electric Feel*. Vocal-layering techniques are omitted because the critical language does not establish a separate production credit.
- **Producers:** Both MGMT and Dave Fridmann credited in recording credits (`os-credits`).
- **Influences:** The Flaming Lips reported as an inspiration for *Time to Pretend* development (`gen-influences5`), which forms a central portion of the LP. Other named musicians belong to editorial `sounds_like`, not historical influence. No guest is treated as an influence.

## mgmt-congratulations — Congratulations (2010)
- **Styles:** Shift from electro-pop hits to grand psych/art-rock compositions; `con-pfork`, `con-mradar`, and `con-guardian` describe prog excursions, psychedelic pop, organ and instrumental contrasts. Textural guitar-rock and softer psychedelic folk sit behind the main neo-psychedelic axis. Genre `psychedelicfolk` is a weaker, track-scoped category.
- **Scales:** Energy 3/electronic 2 due to organ/synth as components of a predominantly recorded rock band; mood 3 oscillates from playful to vulnerable; experimental 4 reflects *Siberian Breaks* and abrupt style changes; polish 4 denotes carefully arranged, densely textured mixes. Tempo 3/groove 2 reflect relatively little sustained dancing.
- **Instruments:** MusicBrainz per-track recording credits and AllMusic (`con-mbrainz`, `con-allmusic`): electric guitars, bass, drums, synth, piano, organ, sampler, omnichord, percussion, strings, electric sitar, glockenspiel, oboe, saxophone, panflute and harmonica all appear on actual recordings. `strings` is a credited performance, not grounds to infer a detailed orchestration process.
- **Production:** `sampling`/`fieldrecordings` from Matt Asti sampling instruments and field recordings in album credits; Sonic Boom's modular synth is documented on Song for Dan Treacy (`con-mbrainz` / original recording credits). Layered vocal harmonies are noted in album reviews, but no unverified model (or software) added. Dave Fridmann **mixed** this album; the album credits only MGMT and Sonic Boom as producers (`con-columbia`).
- **Lyrics and influenced_by:** Fame, sincerity and homage in interviews. *Song for Dan Treacy* explicitly honors Television Personalities and the Eno composition acknowledges Eno, with the musician himself interviewed on his musical heroes (`con-pfork-int`,`con-nme`). The artist's 2013 retrospective explicitly singles out **Skip Spence and Mayo Thompson** as the template for *Congratulations* (`gen-influences4`). Sonic Boom is a collaborator/producer and Jennifer Herrema guest, not album `influenced_by` entries.
- **Disagreement:** Pitchfork admires boldness but calls the record cluttered; The Guardian is mixed. This is a landmark **for the group's own artistic trajectory**, not a claim of consensus critical acclaim.

## mgmt-mgmt — MGMT (2013)
- **Styles:** Critics identify neo-psychedelia and psychedelic pop shifting toward dense noise-pop, art-pop and electronics (`self-criticdb`, `self-pfork`, `self-nme`, `self-guardian`). `psychedelicfolk` occurs mainly on the *Introspection* cover and is thus last. Beware treating a **cover song or its source artist Faine Jade** as documented influence in `influenced_by`.
- **Scales:** Electronic 4 due to distorted synths, computer-like beats, and fragmentary arrangements; experimental 5 for abstract structure, sonic manipulation and disrupted melodies. Mood 1 from the anxious, introspective tone; polish 4 reflects complex, well-engineered studio work but intentionally murky sound. Energy 3, tempo 3 and groove 1 reflect mostly non-danceable compositions (`self-pfork`, `self-guardian`, `self-relix`). Theatrical 2 for subdued singing amid grand processing.
- **Instruments:** Synth and guitar/drums are identified across album reviews, small percussion/bells by NME; exact synthesizer makes are not confirmed. **Do not convert digitally imitated woodwinds to actual wind instrument credits.** `bell`/finger-cymbal textures appear but credits for a particular model are absent.
- **Production:** Sonic manipulation and distortion are supported by reviews and artist studio interview (`self-guardian`, `self-nme`, `self-relix`). `distortedvocals` is limited to *Alien Days* as explicitly described by AP but its prominence is low across full LP. No `sampling` inferred from the cover of Faine Jade or critics' analogies.
- **Producers:** MGMT and Dave Fridmann both have producer credit (`self-columbia`, `self-allmusic`). 2013 recording was substantially the duo at Tarbox with Dave, *not* the touring band's playing sessions (`self-relix`, `self-columbia`).
- **Influences:** Empty. Suicide comparisons from critics are `sounds_like` only; Faine Jade is **covered**, not entered as an influence; Phish is a historical preference, not a documented musical source for this album.
- **Disagreement:** NME and Guardian praise the creative risk, while The Independent calls the arrangements unengaging. The row retains high factual research confidence, but `landmark=0` as earlier major identity-shaping records are stronger candidates; this is an editorial decision.
