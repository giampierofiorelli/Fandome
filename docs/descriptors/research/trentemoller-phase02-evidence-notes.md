# Trentemøller — Phase 02 | album-specific evidence and calibration

**Cut-off:** 24 October 2026. **Scope:** `Lost` (2013), `Lost Reworks` (2014), `Fixion` (2016), `Obverse` (2019) only. This is an increment to Phase 01, not a replacement for it. Every value is an editorial *draft*. The 0–5 scores are calibrated judgments over original standard-release album content, not individual standout tracks, average BPM calculations, listening-session measurements or claims by record labels. Keys in square brackets identify exact sources in `trentemoller_phase02_sources.csv`. Each genre, including narrow track-scoped styles, has its own evidence record.

## 1. Lost (2013) — `trentemoller-lost`

**Edition and date.** Twelve-track original release, 23 September 2013 on In My Room [L_BC, L_OFF]. Avoid the separately released **Lost (Instrumental Version)**; it is not a second set of songs or a different original album. Some streaming releases have different durations for the closing track *Hazed*. Do not mix instrumental bonus presentations into this descriptor. The album brings Low (Alan Sparhawk and Mimi Parker), Jana Hunter, Marie Fisker, Jonny Pierce, Ghost Society, Kazu Makino and Sune Rose Wagner into Trentemøller's production [L_BC, L_RA]. These are performing/co-writing collaborators, **not automatically co-producers**. The AllMusic personal credits specifically list **Anders Trentemøller as producer for Lost** [L_AM].

**Genres.** Electronic/electronica are the connective tissue, but the first half of the album leans unusually far into rock song forms and guest-fronted indie [L_RA, L_LBF, L_GA]. Krautrock appears in several long, motorically rhythmic instrumentals and is a *substantial* rather than blanket classification [L_RA, L_PF]. The official store's dream-pop, shoegaze and indie-rock tags are kept with their scope explicitly indicated [L_BC]. GAFFA notes slowcore; most persuasive on the Low-led introduction and Jana Hunter's hushed singing, rather than on the propulsive tracks [L_GA]. The rarer labels are retained **because they are evidenced**, not because the record should be recommended as a uniform example of each: `altcountry`, `ambient`, and `postgoth` occur around *The Dream* [L_RA, L_BEA], `postpunk` on *Never Stop Running* [L_BEA], `dubstep` texture there [L_PF], `industrial` on *Still on Fire* [L_PF], `artpop` for *Gravity* [L_APP], `electrorock` around *River of Life* [L_RA]. RA specifically names gothic Americana and primitive electro as an influence palette [L_RA]; these are subordinate categories rather than the album's principal genres. `newwave` is a low-strength, track-level tag for new-romantic rhythmic/vocal inflections [L_BEA, L_ART], not a wholesale 1980s revival.

**Scales:** `energy=3` mixes simmering, beatless openings with emphatic mechanical or rock climaxes [L_RA, L_PF]. `electronic=3` because synthesizers, loops and sound design remain fundamental yet guitars, singers, strings, brass and live/band textures are unusually prominent [L_LBF, L_PF]. `mood=1` reflects largely bleak, paranoid and melancholic noir associations, despite tenderness on *The Dream* and *Candy Tongue* [L_LBF, L_GA]. `theatrical=4` is supported by RA's account of grand showmanship and contrasting dramatic passages [L_RA]. `experimental=4` reflects unorthodox combinations and extended compositions but stops below the scale maximum given clear verse/chorus songs [L_PF, L_RA]. `polish=4`: intricately produced and detailed, with deliberately damaged analogue textures [L_OFF, L_LBF]. `tempo=3`: rhythmic tracks coexist with slow and drifting sequences [L_RA]. `groove=2`: some danceable cuts but not a predominantly club-oriented album [L_PF]. These numbers **do not transfer from the earlier house/techno albums**.

**Voice and themes.** `soft|clear|instrumental` acknowledges breathy/understated Low, Hunter, Fisker and Makino vocals, a relatively articulate Jonny Pierce/Sune Wagner presence, and five primarily instrumental tracks [L_BC, L_PF]. Language `en` applies to sung material, not instrumentals. `love|heartbreak|introspection` comes from *Candy Tongue*, *Deceive*, emotionally uncertain song framing and the artist's own plural interpretation of the title. These are prominent thematic threads, **not a claim that all seven vocal tracks share them** [L_LBF, L_PF, L_ORAL]. No reliably verified political/social theme is added.

**Reference artists and influences.** `sounds_like` contains *sonic analogies*: The Soft Moon and Suicide (label text), Depeche Mode (The Line of Best Fit), Lower Dens (Pitchfork), Neu! / Mercury Rev (RA), Portishead (reviewer band-production analogy) [L_BC, L_RA, L_LBF, L_PF]. Some are **the sound of a guest's band** and should not be presented as the guest's documented causal influence. `influenced_by` is **empty**, since artist-specific historical favorites do not establish that each shaped *Lost*. `landmark=1` for a defining independent, full-band/vocal pivot; `confidence=high` despite sharp evaluative disagreements.

**Disagreement.** Chris Todd (The Line of Best Fit) sees the record as newly coherent and especially accomplished, while RA's Tony Naylor sees spectacular craft but excess and emotional hollowness; Pitchfork's Puja Patel prefers its intricate instrumental construction and finds several vocal arrangements less successful [L_LBF, L_RA, L_PF]. Agreement about sound does not imply agreement about artistic quality.

## 2. Lost Reworks (2014) — `trentemoller-lostreworks`

**Qualification.** A distinct, commercially issued remix album with stand-alone Danish, Australian, and U.S. reviews; this clears the Phase 01 plan's documentary threshold [R_OFF, R_GA, R_TM, R_SLUG]. Original seven-track **2014-09-01 vinyl** is the canonical scoring edition [R_OFF, R_NBH]. AllMusic reports **2014-09-02**, plausibly a storefront date; original year remains 2014 [R_AM]. A ten-track digital version adds Pinkunoizu's *Gravity*, Trentemøller's *Deceive* Club Mix, and the instrumental Toydrum mix. These are **edition variants**, not additional albums. A 2014 *Deceive* remixes EP is already partly represented here and is not separately counted.

**Remix personnel vs producers.** The record explicitly attributes mixes/versions to **Trentemøller**, **Toydrum** (James Griffith and Pablo Clements), **Jenny Wilson**, **T.O.M. And His Computer**, and **Unkwon** [R_OFF, R_NBH]. Pinkunoizu appears **only on the extended digital edition** [R_OFF]. These are **remixer/version credits**, not a verified, album-level list of people formally credited `Producer` / `Co-Producer`. AllMusic's release page exposes only a `Primary Artist` in the album credit block [R_AM]. Consequently `producers` is intentionally **blank**: do not convert remixer, performer, original-master producer, writer or project-leader credit into a production credit. Claude may revisit only with explicit track producer credits in a credit-bearing edition.

**Genre scope.** AllMusic expressly tags `experimentaltechno`, `downbeat`, `alternativerock` / `indierock`, broadly `poprock`, and `house` [R_AM]. The label describes Trentemøller's *River of Life* in retro `electroclash`, T.O.M.'s version in **electro-gaze** (`electrogaze`, new genre) and Unkwon's *Deceive* in broken beat (`breakbeat`) [R_OFF]. Toydrum retains spiky electro-rock; Jenny Wilson leans into melancholic pop [R_OFF]. These are distinct mixes of *the same source songs* and should not be averaged into a generic Lost score. Genres correspond to their mixes; the top-ranked electronic tag is the only truly consistent album-level label.

**Scales:** `energy=3`, `electronic=4`, `mood=1`, `theatrical=3`, `experimental=3`, `tempo=3`, `groove=3` [R_OFF, R_TM, R_SLUG]. Compared with *Lost*, reworks tend toward more electronic and sometimes more dance-oriented textures, but several are subdued. `polish` **blank** because reviewers provide insufficient reliable, edition-consistent comparison of engineered rawness versus sheen. `voice=soft|clear|instrumental` for reused verse performances and alternate instrumentals; `language=en` for sung tracks. **Lyrics intentionally blank**: lyrical fragments from *Lost* do not make a newly composed unified theme for this remix album. `sounds_like=Goldfrapp|Depeche Mode` are reviewer sonic comparisons, not influence claims [R_TM, R_SLUG]. `influenced_by` blank. `landmark=0`: worth its own descriptor as a documented remix project, but not a defining studio LP. `confidence=medium` owing to edition and credit ambiguities.

**Disagreement.** The Music argues these remixes form a more cohesive experience than *Lost*, even as song repetition undercuts it; GAFFA is more reserved about what the collaborators actually achieve, while SLUG is warmly approving [R_TM, R_GA, R_SLUG]. Genre agreement is not a quality rating.

## 3. Fixion (2016) — `trentemoller-fixion`

**Original album and credits.** 16 September 2016, twelve original tracks. The current official Bandcamp purchase page appends a **thirteenth optional Redefine rework**. Do **not** score it as part of the original 12-track LP [F_BC, F_AM]. The label explicitly says **written, arranged, produced and mixed by Anders Trentemøller**. Marie Fisker wrote/sang four tracks, Jehnny Beth two, Lisbet Fritze one; additional instruments by Lisbet Fritze, Jeppe Brix and Jakob Høyer are performance credits, not co-production [F_BC]. Some primary copy misspells *My Conviction* and calls the last track *When the Shadows Fall*. Use the standard twelve-track *Where the Shadows Fall* title; do not assume bonus track is part of 2016 edition.

**Sound and genre.** The artist tells MusicRadar *Fixion* is considerably **more synth-based than Lost**, with prominent string synths, a five-string bass, mostly machine drums, distorted effects, vintage hardware and processed plugin samples [F_MR, F_RV]. AllMusic specifically assigns `experimentaltechno`, `idm`, `indieelectronic` and alternative/indie rock [F_AM]. Exclaim names **new wave and synth-pop** [F_EX]. OndaRock argues that post-punk and **darkwave** permeate much of the album, identifying specifically **electropop** in *Circuits* [F_OD]. Danish newspaper reviews support *goth-pop* and isolated krautrock; the official store tags dream pop, indie rock, shoegaze and electro [F_DK, F_BC]. Artist/label name electropunk for its tougher moments, especially *Circuits* [F_BD, F_BC], and describe *November* as an **ambient** breather; *My Conviction* is funk-like [F_BD, F_DK]. Track-scoped styles remain low in ordering but are kept as named.

**Scales:** `energy=3` (pulse-heavy pieces balanced by drifting songs); `electronic=4` (record is predominantly synths/samplers/drum machines but has genuine guitars, bass and drums); `mood=1` (nighttime, loneliness, threat and dark-romantic color, with a few hopeful tracks); `theatrical=3` (narrative instrumental drama but more disciplined than the sprawling *Lost*); `experimental=3` (some unusual transitions, processed voice and synth design within fairly recognizable song structures); `polish=4` (intentionally shaped tape/analogue grit, meticulous sound engineering, not indiscriminately glossy); `tempo=3` (ballads/ambient sections alongside *River in Me* and *Circuits*); `groove=2` (rhythm significant but main audience not continuously the dance floor) [F_MR, F_BD, F_AM, F_EX, F_OD].

**Voice and language:** `soft|clear|powerful|instrumental`: Marie's dreamy lead, Beth's more assertive delivery, Lisbet's softer voice and the instrumental numbers; language `en` [F_BC, F_EX]. Although Trentemøller experiments with his own treated vocal, this doesn't justify labeling the entire record spoken/rap/choral [F_BD]. `lyrics=introspection|love|heartbreak` is supported by the original lyrical texts available on the official album page: *One Eye Open* uses a self-revelation theme, *Spinning* and *Complicated* explore a troubled relationship, while *Where the Shadows Fall* revolves around memory/fear [F_BC]. This is a **song-based thematic plurality**, not a claim about every instrumental. The evidence also supports `identity` as a subordinate theme through *One Eye Open*; this has been retained in the corrected row.

**Documented influence versus resemblance.** `influenced_by=The Cure|Joy Division`: Trentemøller himself calls tracks on *Fixion* tributes to The Cure in a 2018 interview, and describes deliberate use of Joy Division-style string-synth sounds to Reverb [F_NBH, F_RV]. The presence of Jehnny Beth **does not make Savages an influence**; she is a collaborator [F_BC, F_BD]. `sounds_like=The Cure|Joy Division|New Order|OMD|Editors|Cocteau Twins` uses critics' explicit sonic comparisons (some specific to a song) [F_GU, F_OD, F_EX, F_DK]. Do not conflate overlapping lists: a documented influence may also be a reviewer-observed sonic resemblance, but each column carries its own evidence meaning. `landmark=1` as the defining sustained new-wave/dark-electronic chapter. `confidence=high` for personnel/sonics, with the normal reservations around stylistic gradation.

**Disagreement.** AllMusic reads *Fixion* as focused and commanding; Exclaim hears relative sameness; the Guardian is explicitly critical of its debt to Joy Division and the Cure [F_AM, F_EX, F_GU]. A Danish Soundvenue summary praises the kinetic *Circuits* and the detailed mix [F_DK]. The Cure influences are directly confirmed by the artist irrespective of whether some critics consider the resemblance excessive.

## 4. Obverse (2019) — `trentemoller-obverse`

**Original studio release.** 27 September 2019, ten-track standard album, 57 minutes [O_BC, O_AM]. The primary credits clearly say **written, produced and mixed by Anders Trentemøller**; he plays most instruments, with additional parts by Jeppe Brix, Manoj Ramdas, Silas Tinglef, Jakob Falgren and Jakob Høyer. Guest singers are **Rachel Goswell**, **Lina Tullgren**, **Lisbet Fritze** and **jennylee** (Jenny Lee Lindberg of Warpaint). Their song-specific lyric/composition contributions are not *co-producer credits* [O_BC]. Lisbet sings two songs and Anders wrote the text of one; no invented co-production credits.

**Creation and genre taxonomy.** In his first-person album statement, Anders explicitly says that he began without needing to make the music reproducible with a stage band. Reverse synths, deliberate distortion, non-linear writing and extended instrumentals are therefore album-specific rather than inferred from his earlier work [O_OFF, O_NBH]. The label and AllMusic both support **dream pop** and **shoegaze**; AllMusic additionally lists indie electronic, new-wave/post-punk revival, alternative/indie rock and experimental techno, while its biography attributes synthwave and krautrock elements specifically to this LP [O_BC, O_AM, O_BIO]. The official text gives *Foggy Figures* the particularly narrow descriptor **neo-ambient meets drum and bass** and a separate review notes the track's darkwave beat; these are carefully marked **one-track textures** [O_OFF, O_UTR, O_ECL]. `electropop` is a DJ Mag France description reproduced in the artist's original press text, and is therefore a valid though secondary album style, distinct from `synthpop` [O_OFF]. `electronica` is the review's primary category [O_STD], while AllMusic assigns the broad `poprock` umbrella [O_AM]. `neoambient` is a new token from the official formulation and is not silently collapsed into `ambient`.

**Scales:** `energy=2` (some distorted/crushing peaks and *Try A Little*, but the typical section is long and low-intensity); `electronic=4` (synthesizers, tape processing and electronic rhythm with significant real guitars, drums and vocal timbres); `mood=1` (dark, lonely and wistful, with warmth at points); `theatrical=3` (film-like builds and contrast but relatively low-key delivery); `experimental=4` (explicit studio-first sonic experiments, divergent track architecture); `polish=4` (carefully recorded and sound-designed with intentionally imperfect analogue saturation); `tempo=2` (predominantly slow-to-medium pacing); `groove=1` (the album is more for attentive listening than dancing, despite breakbeat *Foggy Figures*) [O_OFF, O_AM, O_STD, O_MAG]. None is inferred from an earlier Trentemøller record.

**Voice and themes.** `soft|clear|instrumental` recognizes multiple featured voices and a substantial instrumental share; `language=en` applies to songs with lyrics. `love|heartbreak|introspection|nostalgia` maps to individual official texts: *One Last Kiss to Remember* evokes a departing romance and remembrance, *Try a Little* emotional disillusionment and self-reflection, *In the Garden* inner uncertainty, *Blue September* remembrance of happier times [O_BC, O_STD]. It does **not** imply that the instrumental tracks carry literal song lyrics.

**Direct influence and editorial resemblance.** `influenced_by=Slowdive|Beak>`: the album statement connects the Slowdive-like *Cold Comfort* to inviting Rachel Goswell, and Trentemøller specifically says he purchased and deployed a Korg Micro Preset on *Obverse* after being impressed with how **Beak>** used it [O_OFF, O_MAG]. The latter is a narrow documented *gear/sound* influence, not a claim the band directed the full LP. `sounds_like=Slowdive|Warpaint|Interpol|Joy Division|Beak>|Daniel Avery|The Cure` is an editorial sonic playlist built from reviews and guest-relevant style clues [O_STD, O_CV, O_ECL, O_OFF]; none of these is automatically a causal influence. `landmark=1`: a clear defining shift into elaborate studio-created, shoegaze-inflected instrumentals; `confidence=high`.

**Disagreement.** AllMusic praises the expressive instrumental arrangements and contrast, the Evening Standard regards the overall flow as technically impressive but sometimes passive, and The Electricity Club questions the length/atmospheric pacing of several tracks [O_AM, O_STD, O_ECL]. These different appraisals do not undermine agreement on the sound.

## Global abstentions, caveats and excluded items

- `Lost Reworks` lists **no producer names** because names on remix title lines do not independently verify the `Producer` role. No blanket assignment of production to named guest singers on studio albums.
- Genre evidence records specify `whole album`, `substantial`, `some tracks`, `one track`, `style influence` and similar scopes. Fandome's `genres` field cannot store that nuance by itself; **Claude should keep the supplementary evidence** so a single experimental break does not dominate recommendations.
- New Phase 02 genre strings (not in branch list, and not among Phase 01 proposed additions): `altcountry`, `electrogaze`, `electropunk`, `gothicamericana`, `indieelectronic`, `krautrock`, `neoambient`, `postgoth`, `slowcore`. These are **not typos for existing types**; the phase import requires explicit registration and appropriately mapped parents.
- Existing `postpunk`, `shoegaze`, `experimentaltechno`, and `idm` were proposed as **new** in Phase 01. If Phase 01 has not yet been imported, Claude must install both phases' genre changes before validating Phase 02.
- *The Science of Fixion* is a **documentary/behind-the-scenes project** in the artist's music index, not a distinct 2016 studio audio album [P_SCI]. *Lost (Instrumental Version)*, single/remix EPs and alternate formats are explicitly excluded from the four rows. *Live in Copenhagen* (2013) is a documented concert release but not selected as a landmark for this incremental corpus.
- The **self-titled `trentemøller` album is not yet released on the 2026-10-24 cut-off**: the label currently gives **13 November 2026** [P_2026]. Do not create an album descriptor on pre-release singles or misleading store dates.
- All `sounds_like` names are sonic analogies (some track-scoped). Only directly evidenced, album-relevant influences are placed in `influenced_by`. No limit was imposed on lists, only `notes` at 15 words.
