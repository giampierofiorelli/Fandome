# Scorpions — Phase 02 — Research notes and evidence decisions

Research: 28 October 2026. Four studio albums; incremental after Phase 01. Album-year dating follows original European issue. These notes do not reproduce protected lyrics or image artwork.

## Taken by Force (1977) — `scorpions-takenbyforce`

- Official group discography: album first issued 4 December 1977, drummer Herman Rarebell's debut and the final studio album with Uli Jon Roth. [Official Scorpions](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/taken-by-force/) and [original-edition credits](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Taken_by_Force/1377830). Do not convert 1978 North American issue into album year.
- Sound: strong hard-rock riffs on `Steamrock Fever` and `He's a Woman, She's a Man`, more expansive and melodic pieces including `We'll Burn the Sky` and `The Sails of Charon`. AllMusic labels the album `Hard Rock`, `Heavy Metal`, `Album Rock`, and `Arena Rock` (four exact current taxonomy tokens), the last two a little less time-specific. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/taken-by-force-mw0000231794); [Gary Hill](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/taken-by-force-mr0001069116).
- Roth discusses his *own* composition `The Sails of Charon`: he brought a **flamenco guitar influence** to it, played both rhythm guitars and bass there, alongside drummer Herman Rarebell. This alone justifies a tail-position `flamenco` style (not album-wide), but **not** a fictional flamenco ensemble or a particular flamenco artist in `influenced_by`. [Roth interview](https://www.eonmusic.co.uk/interviews/uli-jon-roth-reveals-the-scorpions-songs-he-played-all-the-guitars-on-chat-his-solo-works). The regular album-wide credits remain Rudolf Schenker/Roth electric guitars, Buchholz bass, Rarebell drums. The exceptional individual song personnel do not erase Buchholz's presence on the record.
- Lyrics thematic grouping derived from published original track titles/lyrics on official site: `We'll Burn the Sky` grief/love, `I've Got to Be Free` personal identity and social pressure, `The Riot of Your Time` culture and change. A comparison between `We'll Burn the Sky` and Blue Öyster Cult's `Don't Fear the Reaper` is **lyrical** in AllMusic: not added as a sonic similarity without evidence. Production: Dierks, no unverified technical token. Scales high energy, mixed emotional colour, moderate experiment from Roth's extended guitar piece.

## Lovedrive (1979) — `scorpions-lovedrive`

- The band's original February 1979 credit list includes **both Matthias Jabs and Michael Schenker** on guitars; the original sessions were September–December 1978. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/lovedrive/); [MusicBrainz detailed credits](https://musicbrainz.org/release/2f730c38-ef3a-4947-8b66-fd704d9b8861); [original LP metadata](https://www.metal-archives.com/albums/Scorpions/Lovedrive/539253). The mix of lead-guitar credits is specific to tracks; neither artist is a listed producer.
- AllMusic's whole-album styles: hard rock, heavy metal, album rock, arena rock. The slower `Always Somewhere` and acoustic sections of `Holiday` counterbalance the faster `Another Piece of Meat` and `Can't Get Enough`. The latter's rhythm and melodic choruses support danceable `groove=3`, while electronic remains 0 (band instruments). [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/lovedrive-mw0000191636).
- `Is There Anybody There?` has unusually explicit **reggae rhythmic guitar/chords**, as separately noted by [Metal Music Archives](https://www.metalmusicarchives.com/review/lovedrive/250127) and [a track-by-track review](https://2loud2oldmusic.com/2022/09/14/scorpions-lovedrive-1979-album-review-the-scorpions-collection-series/). `reggae` is last (one track only), not promoted above album-wide hard rock. `Holiday` acoustic guitar confirmed in [review](https://www.metal-archives.com/reviews/Scorpions/Lovedrive/35061) and track credits. MusicBrainz lists Matthias Jabs slide guitar in its recording relationships, included as a secondary instrument.
- Dierks is sole producer. Do not insert a wah-wah pedal, a particular amp or tape machine without independent song-/album-specific source.

## Animal Magnetism (1980) — `scorpions-animalmagnetism`

- Official original issue 31 March 1980; Jabs lead/slide/rhythm guitar and Rudolf acoustic/lead/rhythm, Buchholz bass, Rarebell drums. [Official artist page](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/animal-magnetism/); [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/53085b0f-5638-4048-9d1c-9db9c22d6ec0). Credits also list Allan Macmillan for strings/horns, individual violin, viola, cello, oboe and French horn performers. These are **real but subsidiary** layers; they do not mean an album-wide orchestra, so the instrumental list puts band before the chamber/horn instruments.
- Matthias Jabs reports **he first used the talk box while recording The Zoo in late 1979**, to evoke the atmosphere of New York's 42nd Street. Assign `production=talkbox` as an effect on **guitar**, not lead vocals; do not invent device brand or model. [Jabs interview](https://theeditingroomfloor.blogspot.com/2010/08/matthias-jabs-sultan-of-scorpions-six.html).
- AllMusic lists hard rock/heavy metal, album rock/arena rock, plus `hairmetal` and *Pop-Metal*. The latter requires the **new** literal token `popmetal` under the owner's no-silent-mapping rule; both are weaker **retrospective** classifications. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/animal-magnetism-mw0000191635). Apple Music highlights a dark grinding title piece, a fast almost-punk track and the more melodic `Only a Man` recalling **Boston**. Thus `sounds_like=Boston` is editorial **single-track** and not a direct creative influence. [Apple Music editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/animal-magnetism/1440924575). Its retrospective critical standing is mixed; `landmark=1` refers to memorable band tracks and transitional importance, not a claim of universal acclaim.
- Stronger dark/low mood (2) relative to Lovedrive, with riff repetition and distinctive guitar effect. No synth or programmed beat source verified.

## Blackout (1982) — `scorpions-blackout`

- Official 29 March 1982, Klaus Meine lead vocal; Matthias Jabs and Rudolf Schenker electric and acoustic guitars; Buchholz bass; Rarebell drums; Don Dokken named on backing vocals. [Official](https://www.the-scorpions.com/album/blackout/). Dierks primary producer and detailed recording locations verified in [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/release/11937296-8fc7-3779-a59e-dd82774629b8).
- Klaus Meine, speaking **first-hand**, recounts losing his voice during sessions with Dierks at a French villa and returning after vocal surgery and recovery; Don Dokken's provisional demos are **not** the final lead recording. Meine says he is unsure exactly whether remnants of Dokken backing vocals are on specific tracks. Do not mistake provisional demo vocals for final studio lead singing. [Meine on surgery](https://www.loudersound.com/features/scorpions-50-years-of-rocknroll-interview); [on Dokken](https://noisecreep.com/scorpions-klaus-meine/). Vocal timbre classified `powerful|clear|raspy` as on Phase 01. No sound processing should be inferred from medical treatment.
- AllMusic tags hard rock, heavy metal, album rock, arena rock, contemporary pop/rock, hair metal. The last two are retrospective style labels, hence placed last. The album is unusually concise and energetic: `Blackout`, `Dynamite` fast riff tracks; `No One Like You` melodic hook-heavy single; `When the Smoke Is Going Down` restrained closing ballad. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/blackout-mw0000191756); [review](https://www.allmusic.com/album/release/blackout-mr0001179093); [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/blackout/1440921750). `powerballad` not listed: no source here identifies the *album itself* as that genre (rather than a song type). No unconfirmed specific analog/digital studio machines recorded in `production`.

## Research limits and traceability

- `influenced_by` is blank across this phase because musical parallels (including e.g. Boston or Zeppelin) do not demonstrate direct, album-specific artist/producer attribution of an influence. Roth's identified **flamenco idiom**, meanwhile, is a style not a named musician.
- Original label/artist details outrank secondary biography; source CSV tells whether a result is first-person/official, credit relationship, or contextual critical commentary. AllMusic's taxonomy fields may be backfilled for modern audiences, especially `hairmetal`, `arenarock`, `popmetal`.
- `production` is intentionally blank except `talkbox` on *Animal Magnetism*. Studio dates and personnel do not alone establish a named machine, reverb algorithm, recording format, or technique.
- Albums, not individual tracks, are the unit. Track-level style is included only as low-ranked genre with explanation. No compilation, live album, or Phase 01 data is in the descriptor rows.
