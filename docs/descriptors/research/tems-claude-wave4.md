Cells:
- genres: Pitchfork "production that spans romantic Afropop, booming dembow, hedonistic synthpop, and hints of amapiano"; Native "this is an Afropop album"; Music In Africa "starting with Afropop before experimenting with trap, reggaeton and Bollywood-inspired melodies"; Crack "trap hi-hats", "'Wine' is 'a slow dancehall track'"; Pitchfork "'FYN' gets its juice from a hip-hop groove"; Native "neo-Highlife percussion is coloured by the brazy saxophone" (mapped to highlife), "The disco-pop sound", "synth-wave production" (Addicted), emo rap (Native "emo rap and Afropop bangers"), alté (Native, the keys on "Carry" draw on "the alté soundscape"), konto (Native: "Are You There?" references "Konto, a sub-genre popularised in early 2000's Ajegunle"). Single-track genres are in the tail.
- mood 4: Pitchfork "irrepressibly youthful", "spirited playfulness". polish 4: Pitchfork "plush and detailed production". groove 4: Pitchfork "'Jo' is almost impossible not to get up and dance to"; mostly party records with a few slow songs. energy, tempo, theatrical 3: mid-tempo pop. experimental 2: Native "cedes experimentation in favour of cohesion"; Pitchfork "significant range".

- instruments: Pitchfork "soft sax lines", "a deceptively simple guitar loop", "'Carry' toys with amapiano's trademark log drum", "'80s pop drums"; Native "woozy keys" on "Carry", "synth-wave production", "a rich backend of live instrumentals"; Crack "'Soundgasm' is 'a guitar-led slow burner'".

- lyrics: Native "love and sexual activity portends the centrepiece"; Pitchfork on "Are You There?": "a broken political system".
- language en|pcm: Pitchfork quotes the Pidgin line "I no fit focus on the negativity inside my country"; most hooks are in English. No source states the balance (uncertain).
- producers (Qobuz): London (most tracks; Pitchfork "London has production credits on all but two of the album's 16 tracks"), Andre Vibez (Dirty, Calm Down, Mara), 1 Mind (Are You There?, Time N Affection), Dez Wright and Kill September (Hold Me), Leandro "Dro" Hidalgo and Shanti (co-producers, Soundgasm), KDaGreat and Kel P Vibes (FYN), HIGO (Carry), Altims (Oroma Baby), Jaegen (Time N Affection). Dropped: DJ Swivel, Dylan Graham and Eddie Priest (additional), and London's additional credits. Conflict: The Native calls the opener "Sarz-produced", but Qobuz credits London (Sarz not added).
- sounds_like, each on one song of 16: Pitchfork "'Dirty' ... reminiscent of WizKid's latest album" (as of April 2022 that was Made in Lagos); "'Jo' ... feels like an ode to the P-Square heyday"; "'Addicted,' where Rema channels both Travis Scott and the Weeknd"; Native on "Addicted": "The synth-wave production echoes of '80s Michael Jackson" and "The disco-pop sound ... counts Fireboy DML and The Weeknd among its recent adaptors". Left out: Pitchfork "a beat Bad Bunny would fight to have" (playful); guests (6LACK, Chris Brown, AJ Tracey, Yseult).
- influenced_by: Music In Africa quotes Rema only on mood and process ("I didn't have a solid written plan"). Empty.
- landmark 0: the sources praise it as a "stellar debut", but none calls it defining (the canon's 2020s pick is HEIS). confidence high.

## Artist-level influences (own words)

- Asake:
  - CNN (Lauren Lee and Larry Madowo, 2023-09-22, republished at https://abc17news.com/?p=1220503): "I grew up listening to Fuji"; "It's just like a bit of Amapiano, a bit of Afrobeat, a bit of Fuji, a bit of R&B, a bit of hip hop, just to make Asake."
  - Interview Magazine (Olamide Oyenusi, 2025-11-11, https://www.interviewmagazine.com/music/afrobeats-asake-just-wants-to-get-closer-to-god): "So then I always listened to the songs he played, like [Ayinde] Barrister." and "I picked Fela [Kútì] by myself".
  - So: Ayinde Barrister, Fela Kuti (genre: fuji). Olamide is his label boss (YBNL) and A&R, so he is not an influence.
- Rema: none found in his own words (Music In Africa quotes him only on process and mood).
- Tems: Pan African Music (2020-10-16, https://pan-african-music.com/en/tems-for-broken-ears-interview/): she names Asa, Aaliyah, Lauryn Hill, Frank Ocean and Amy Winehouse as artists she listened to, but says "I wouldn't say they influenced my sound because in finding my sound, I had to stop listening to music for a while." So by her own words they are not influences. Record nothing, or record them only as listening.
- Ayra Starr: none found in the sources opened.
- Omah Lay: none confirmed. A Channels TV report (2026-04-21) says he named Wizkid, Burna Boy, Drake, The Weeknd, Bruno Mars and Harry Styles as idols, but the page returned 403 and the claim was only seen in search results, so it is not used.
