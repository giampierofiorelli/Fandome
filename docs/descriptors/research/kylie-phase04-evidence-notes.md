# Kylie Minogue — Phase 04, evidence notes

Research **26 October 2026**; each of the three rows is a proposal. Scope: original album editions, original recordings versus archival tracks noted below. Bibliography and links are in `kylie_phase04_sources.csv`, with per-genre evidence in `kylie_phase04_genre_evidence.csv`.

## `kylie-kissmeonce` — Kiss Me Once (2014)

**Release and genre selection.** Qobuz Parlophone credits original eleven songs. Joe Sweeney in [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-kiss-me-once/) explicitly centres **synth-pop and disco**, with a limited dubstep excursion. [The Guardian's song-by-song first listen](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/feb/17/first-listen-kylies-kiss-me-once) describes electropop synths on *Les Sex* and early-house flavour on *Fine*. [NME](https://www.nme.com/reviews/reviews-kylie-minogue-2-15152-313758) says Pharrell's *I Was Gonna Cancel* is electro-disco; notes a deep-house-like *Fine*, vocal cut-ups and dubstep snarls on *Sexercize*. [The Observer](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/mar/16/kiss-me-once-kylie-minogue-review-kitty-empire) highlights the album's R&B turn, together with Pharrell's funk-pop. [Album of the Year](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/14312-kylie-minogue-kiss-me-once.php) lists electro-house as a secondary classification. `electronic` is omitted as a broad extra label because critics discuss explicitly named more useful styles instead. `bubblegumpop` comes from Slant's bubblegum formulation and Qobuz/AllMusic's album description, not a fabricated separate genre. `dubstep` goes last: *Sexercize* is an exception, not the whole album.

**Scales:** energy **4** (upbeat dance chorus majority, but *Beautiful* and several slower songs); electronic **4** (synth-laden programmed recordings with credited guitars, piano, bass and percussion); mood **4** (the uplift of *Into the Blue* and *Fine* counteracts conflict/heartbreak); theatrical **3** (big choruses and camp as opposed to opera); experimental **2** (dabbling in dubstep, vocal edits and jarring R&B vs otherwise accessible pop); polish **5** (high-gloss, high-profile productions per Guardian); tempo **4** (predominantly up-tempo single lengths); groove **4** (majority dance-oriented but not uniformly nonstop).

**Voices and lyrics:** `clear|soft` for nimble, lightly delivered pop verses and more forceful climaxes; *Beautiful* uses altered duet timbres but that is not the lead voice type for the full record. `love|sensuality|identity|party|heartbreak`: Slant explicitly distinguishes the self-affirming *Into the Blue*/*Fine* from the run of sexual tracks, with intimacy and relationship themes throughout. No assumption that autobiography governs each track.

**Instrumentation and production:** Label-credit [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/kiss-me-once-kylie-minogue/0825646328062) individually identifies guitar and percussion on *Million Miles*/*Sexy Love*, bass on *Sexy Love* and *Kiss Me Once*, drums on *Feels So Good*, keyboards on *If Only* and *Kiss Me Once*, piano on *Kiss Me Once* and *Beautiful*, and programmed parts on *Into the Blue*/*Kiss Me Once*. [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-kiss-me-once/) describes clavinet on *Sexy Love*; [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/feb/17/first-listen-kylies-kiss-me-once) clearly describes prominent synths on *Les Sex* and processed handclaps on *Fine*. `vocalchops` has explicit cut-up evidence in NME and Guardian, `autotune` is specifically described in [The Guardian album review](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/mar/13/kylie-minogue-kiss-me-once-review) and in Qobuz's AllMusic editorial for *Fine*; `sampling` is supported by the critic's mention of the operatic vocal sample on *I Was Gonna Cancel*. `vocalfilter` describes the conspicuously robot-like duet treatment of *Beautiful*, heard by the Guardian; no specific software or drum machine assigned. Although the Guardian says the *Les Sex* introduction sounds like a music box, it does **not** prove a physical `musicbox` was recorded, so that instrument is excluded.

**Producers:** Every name in the row appears as **producer/co-producer** on at least one of the original eleven tracks at Qobuz. `Mike Del Rio` *Into the Blue*; `Daniel Davidsen`, `Peter Wallevik`, `Mich Hansen` *Million Miles/Sexy Love*; `Pharrell Williams` and `Kelly Sheehan` *I Was Gonna Cancel*; four *Sexercize* producers `Stefan Johnson`, `Jordan Johnson`, `Marcus Lomax`, `Alexander Izquierdo`; `Wayne Wilkins` and `Uzoechi Emenike` *Feels So Good*; `Ariel Rechtshaid` *If Only*; `Henri Lanz`, `William Rappaport`, `Joshua Walker` *Les Sex*; `Jesse Shatkin` *Kiss Me Once*; `Mark Taylor` and `Alex Smith` *Beautiful*; `Chris Loco` *Fine*. Sia is executive producer and songwriter, no production role solely from that title. Kylie also has executive credits, also excluded. Qobuz alternates spelling Jess/Jesse Shatkin, consolidated to the same credited person. `Kelly Sheehan` is retained because Qobuz explicitly labels producer on track three, not because she also sings/writes. No producer is inferred from guest vocals or writing.

**Influence versus sounds-like:** Guardian and NME compare Pharrell's cut with Daft Punk's *Get Lucky*, and Observer compares *Les Sex* with Lady Gaga. Similar artists in `sounds_like` are editorial. Neither a comparison nor producer's existing CV proves a musical influence: `influenced_by` remains blank. **Landmark 0**: an established-era album with singles but not as era-defining as *Fever*; this is curator judgment. **Confidence high** on main genres and detailed credit basis, weaker electro-house and production-effects minority tracks.

**Critical disagreements:** Slant finds thematic meaning and dance-pop energy while Guardian and Observer consider the material patchy; no critical-rating claim enters the numeric `mood` field.

## `kylie-kyliechristmas` — Kylie Christmas (2015)

**Important categorisation:** Parlophone's [2015 announcement](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/oct/02/iggy-pop-james-cordon-appear-kylie-christmas-album) and [AllMusic's 13-song tracklist](https://www.allmusic.com/album/kylie-christmas-mw0002889898) show an original commercially released **studio Christmas** album with newly recorded originals and newly performed covers. It is not a greatest-hits compilation. There are archival/reused parts, notably Sinatra's vocal and some pre-released seasonal performances, which are not treated as newly written material. The standard thirteen-track original **does not** contain the Dannii Minogue disco song `100 Degrees` (deluxe bonus); do not let that song recharacterise the original album as disco. The Snow Queen expansion and later issues are not separate albums.

**Genres/scope:** `christmas` (whole release; AllMusic style), `traditionalpop`/`bigband`/`jazzpop` (primarily the standards in the opening and near-closing sequence, explicitly supported by Guardian's big-band/orchestral review and [AOTY classification](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/genre/166-christmas/2015/the-guardian/)). `pop` applies to the new seasonal pop songs. `adultcontemporary` is weak secondary genre metadata supported by the ballad treatments. `girlgrouppop` is based on AllMusic's description of *White December* and *Christmas Isn't Christmas 'til You Get Here*; *San Francisco Chronicle* hears one latter original as Ronettes-styled, a track-specific resemblance. `poprock` refers to pop/rock reinterpretations including *Christmas Wrapping* and *2000 Miles*. `ballad` is for *Only You* and *Every Day's Like Christmas*. Nothing in the sources establishes EDM, synth-pop or disco as album-wide styles.

**Scales:** energy **2** (several brassy fast standards, but plenty of gentle 2–4 minute ballads); electronic **1** (live orchestral/brass and piano dominate even with some programmed treatments); mood **4** (deliberately cheerful but sentimental, nostalgic and occasionally wistful); theatrical **4** (Vegas-style big band opening, orchestral staging and duets); experimental **1** (traditional arrangements with some surprising collaborators); polish **4** (large, carefully arranged studios, occasionally criticised as overproduced); tempo **2** (swing and midtempo alongside ballads); groove **2** (some swinging songs, not a club album).

**Voice/lyrics:** `clear|soft` is the lead style. Frank Sinatra/Iggy Pop/James Corden guest voices are not new vocal types for Kylie herself. `love|nostalgia|party|sensuality` covers holiday affection, reminiscence, festive celebration and *Santa Baby*; neither spiritual nor political themes dominate these mostly secular songs.

**Instruments:** [AllMusic full personnel](https://www.allmusic.com/album/kylie-christmas-mw0002889898) and [2015 Qobuz credits](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/kylie-christmas-kylie-minogue/0825646736225) list explicit trumpet, trombone, tenor/alto/baritone saxophones, brass/strings, piano, electric and upright bass, drums, guitar, keyboards, percussion, violin/cello, sleighbells and handclaps. An orchestral arrangement may include players but the generic `orchestra` token is not automatically added without a verified original-standard whole-orchestra credit. The older Sinatra orchestration credits belong to a source recording; do not confuse Nelson Riddle as 2015 album producer.

**Production:** `stringarrangements` grounded in Pete Whitfield credits; Specific `hornarrangements` has been **omitted**: big-band instrumentation is documented, but Cliff Masterson is usually credited as generic arranger rather than specifically horn arranger. `programming` credited to Steve Anderson/Ash Howes/Cliff Masterson. No Auto-Tune or hardware-specific claims. `sampling` omitted: an archival Sinatra voice is confirmed, but source material/reconstruction method for that exact track was not verified well enough to assert a specific sampling technique.

**Producers:** AllMusic producer roles document `Steve Anderson`, `Charles Pignone`, `Chong Lim`, `Matt Prime`, and `Stargate` (`Mikkel Storleer Eriksen` and `Tor Erik Hermansen`). Qobuz credits Eriksen and Hermansen as distinct producers for *Every Day's Like Christmas*. Cliff Masterson is credited arranger/conductor, not a full producer in the visible credits; Kylie, Tim Blacksmith and Danny D. are executive-only. Ash Howes is generally mixer/programmer, not album producer on original 13; exclude unless a standard-track specific full producer credit can be checked. **Confidence medium** because the record combines original and archival material, and some aggregated personnel may belong to old recordings.

**Influence vs resemblance:** A critic at [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/music/article/Kylie-Minogue-Kylie-Christmas-6673347.php) hears Ronettes-style arrangement on *Christmas Isn't Christmas 'til You Get Here*. This justifies `sounds_like`, not automatically a documented creative influence. A covered song by Yazoo or The Pretenders is neither proof of influence. Critical contrast is steep: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/kylie-christmas-mw0002889898) warmly praises variety; [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2015/nov/19/kylie-minogue-kylie-christmas-review-hellish-bizarre-crimes-against-festive-cheer-ft-iggy-pop) and [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/reviews/kylie-minogue-kylie-christmas-album-review-a6769726.html) reject forced novelty and orchestral excess. **Landmark 0**: distinct seasonal work but not a defining sonic foundation for artist recommendations.

## `kylie-golden` — Golden (2018)

**Release and genre scope:** The original [BMG/Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/gb-en/album/golden-kylie-minogue/lovbebxrnkchd) 12 song album released 6 April 2018 is a mixed country/dance-pop record. [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/100054-kylie-minogue-golden.php) explicitly describes dance-pop, country pop, and electropop. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/golden-mw0003145096) finds pop plus contemporary country and club crossover; [Exclaim!](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/kylie_minogue-golden) says acoustic guitar/banjo enters songs that build to programmed dance choruses. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/kylie-minogue-golden/) makes Avicii/EDM-Americana analogy and documents acoustic guitar and specifically a **deep-house beat** on *Raining Glitter*. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2018/apr/06/kylie-minogue-golden-review-yearning-country-reinvention) explicitly describes **tropical house** beside banjo. These last dance subgenres are track-level textures, not the fundamental whole-album category. `country` is prominent but a later [Classic Pop analysis](https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/classic-album/album-insight-kylie-minogue-golden/) stresses that country cues cover only a portion; no claim it is predominantly trad-country.

**Scales:** energy **3** (big dance choruses alongside gentle *Radio On* and *Music's Too Sad Without You*); electronic **3** (live guitar/banjo/fiddle alongside synths and dance beats); mood **3** (joyous choruses with grief, loneliness and mortality); theatrical **2** (personable storytelling rather than showy stage persona); experimental **2** (novel country/electronic hybrid for Kylie, conventional individual structures); polish **4** (mainstream Nashville and London studio sheen); tempo **3** (moderate pace with dance-pop peaks and ballads); groove **4** (rhythmic majority even with acoustic parts).

**Voice/lyrics:** `clear|soft` Kylie lead; artists' backing vocals not used as lead voice category. Kylie co-wrote all standard tracks. [The Guardian interview](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2018/apr/26/kylie-minogue-on-how-ageing-breast-cancer-and-nick-cave-all-influenced-her-greatest-hits) confirms themes of ageing, changing identity, relationships, mortality; [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-golden/) discusses mortality and celebration; `storytelling` for the Nashville narratives, not all tracks. Songs combine heartbreak with escapist dance and gratitude, hence `heartbreak|love|introspection|identity|storytelling|nostalgia|party`.

**Instruments:** [AllMusic personnel](https://www.allmusic.com/album/golden-mw0003145096) names guitars including acoustic/electric, banjo (Kiris Houston / Steve McEwan), fiddle (Kiris Houston, represented by `violin`), synths (Jesse Frasure), keyboards/piano (Jon Green), bass, drums (Tom Meadows), and string arrangements (Davide Rossi). [Apple Music's album editorial](https://music.apple.com/us/album/golden/1370328243) names tambourine and chiming piano specifically on *Stop Me from Falling*. Kiris Houston is explicitly credited **slide guitar**: the instrument is recorded as `slideguitar`, requiring the new token. Do not convert to `steelguitar` without support. No banjo inferred merely from genre. Some broad AllMusic personnel might cover extra tracks on digital reissues: included instruments also have original-album reviews and first-twelve documentation, while isolated possible deluxe extras (autoharp) are excluded.

**Production:** BMG/Qobuz has `programming`, `drumprogramming` (`Raining Glitter`), and [Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/music/kylie-minogue-golden/) specifically names `pitchedvocals`; Davide Rossi credited string arrangements (`Radio On`), so `stringarrangements`. Some reviews report a dance kick and electronic textures, but no verified TR-808/909/Linn/DAW model. No autotune assumed from pitched vocals; those are different tokens.

**Producers:** On the ORIGINAL first twelve Qobuz credits `Sky Adams` (Dancing, Stop Me from Falling, A Lifetime to Repair, Live a Little, Love), `Lindsay Rimes` (Golden), `Jesse Frasure` (Sincerely Yours), `Ash Howes`, `Richard Stannard` and `Seton Daunt` (One Last Kiss and Shelby '68; Daunt co-producer Shelby), `Jon Green` (Radio On), `Francis White`, `Mark Taylor`, `Alex Smith` (Raining Glitter) and `Samuel Dixon` (Music's Too Sad Without You). `Nathan Chapman` co-writes/sings Dancing but is NOT labelled producer in Qobuz's standard-track metadata; no unwarranted promotion. `Liz Rose` writer, not producer. No guest Savoretti or extra-track-only personnel promoted.

**Influences and sounds-like:** The [EW interview](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/kylie-minogue-her-album-art-140000987.html) says country was proposed by her A&R and grew through writing in Nashville. Kylie describes the album through a Dolly Parton/dance-floor metaphor (Classic Pop); this is a *stylistic sketch*, not an unambiguous statement that Parton's compositions influenced her songs. The Pitchfork and Exclaim! Avicii analogies, plus AllMusic Kacey Musgraves and Tegan and Sara comparisons, support `sounds_like` (editorial), **not** `influenced_by`. No recording sample identified as creative influence.

**Critical disagreement:** Pitchfork and Guardian criticise the country+EDM mix and some clichéd wording; AllMusic finds it creatively adventurous, Slant hears emotional stakes and controlled production. Distinguish quality assessments from documented musical facts and scale scores. **Landmark 1**: an important new self-written, Nashville-influenced era with distinctive sonic ingredients. **Confidence high** in broad country/pop fusion and instrument/producer data; house/tropical-house scoped to particular cuts.
