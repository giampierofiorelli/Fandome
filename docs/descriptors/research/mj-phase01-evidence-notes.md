# Michael Jackson — Phase 01 evidence and judgment notes

Research date 2026-10-25. Editorial labels and score assignments use the Fandome guide. Evidence strength attaches to *specific* claims, not the artist's reputation. Sources are keyed in `mj_phase01_sources.csv`.

## Got to Be There (1972) — `mj-gottobethere`

**Genres:** AllMusic explicitly names R&B, soul, pop-soul, pop and AM pop in its album classification. The last appears as new exact token `ampop`; it must not be silently converted into adultcontemporary. Motown and music historian accounts describe songs and covers of Bill Withers and Carole King. Higher energy tracks like 'Rockin Robin' coexist with quieter ballads, so dance/pop aspects cannot be inferred from a lone single.

**Scales:** `energy 3`, `tempo 3`, `groove 3` balance rhythmic material with markedly slow covers; AllMusic calls the record varied rather than uniform. `electronic 1` is a listening-oriented editorial assessment anchored to named acoustic rhythm players and keyboard credits, not a synth classification. `mood 3` spans joy and yearning. `theatrical 3` reflects an expressive solo lead more than theatrical staging. `experimental 1` reflects conventional Motown songs and interpretations; `polish 4` reflects professional ensemble productions and chart-focused arrangements.

**Voice:** clear, powerful and occasionally soft tenor/child-soprano performance; Fandome has no `soprano` token, so no new token is invented. **Instruments:** MJDb lists Wilton Felder/James Jamerson bass, James Gadson drums, David T. Walker guitar, Joe Sample/Don Randi piano, Freddie Perren keyboards as album-associated roles. Their track-by-track assignment is not securely identified: do not assert all musicians play on all ten songs. **Production:** blank; vintage equipment is not established. **Producers:** Motown-licensed individual recording credits identify Hal Davis (title and 'I Wanna Be Where You Are'), the Corporation, Willie Hutch ('Girl Don't Take Your Love from Me'), Mel Larson and Jerry Marcellino ('Rockin Robin'). Berry Gordy appears as executive producer on the album/selected recordings, and also within the Corporation; do not count him twice. **Lyrics:** love, heartbreak, some cover-song storytelling. **Similarity:** Jackson 5, Donny Osmond, the Temptations are editorial timbral/period parallels, not claimed influences. **Landmark 0:** key solo debut, but not a later defining album; judgment distinct from commercial achievement. **Sources:** AllMusic; Classic Motown; Qobuz; MusicBrainz; MJDb.

## Ben (1972) — `mj-ben`

**Genres:** AllMusic specifies R&B, soul and pop-soul; MJDb also places album in pop. A retrospective of 'My Girl' describes a funk rhythm, so `funk` is deliberately at the end and is **track-limited**. AllMusic indexes a soundtrack style because of title song, but specifically says this LP must not be confused with the film soundtrack. Omit soundtrack as an album-wide musical genre/type.

**Scales:** `energy 2`, `tempo 2`, `groove 2` reflect heavy ballad proportion balanced with soul covers. `mood 3` mixes tender and warm material, `theatrical 3` responds to the dramatic title performance. `electronic 1` rests on period studio band players rather than any identified synthesizer. `experimental 1` and `polish 4` reflect polished established formats. All numeric values are editorial anchored choices.

**Voice/instruments:** Fandome clear, soft and powerful categories from title track and groove songs; MJDb credits Felder bass, Gadson drums, Walker guitar, Sample/Randi piano and Perren keyboards on album; no claim of Jackson on an instrument. **Producers:** Qobuz track-level identifies the Corporation, Jerry Marcellino, Mel Larson and Hal Davis; album credits independently include Bobby Taylor as a producer. Berry Gordy is credited on some recordings as producer but here represented within the Corporation, and appears elsewhere as executive producer. **No production tokens:** no specific devices or concrete processing established.

**Sources/critic tension:** AllMusic identifies a fairly varied LP and notes the huge impact of the song 'Ben'. The title track was recorded for the 1972 film, but the rest of the LP is unrelated to a film score. `landmark 1` is restricted to the album's place as his first solo U.S. number-one hit and solo-era defining recording; this is an editorial judgment, **not** equivalent to calling the full album a masterpiece.

## Music & Me (1973) — `mj-musicme`

**Genres:** R&B, soul and pop-soul are AllMusic album categories. MJDb places the record in pop. `showtunes` and `standards` occur late as a small part of the source tracklist: 'All the Things You Are' originated in the musical *Very Warm for May* and 'Morning Glow' in *Pippin*. Do not extrapolate these tags to the whole album. Despite the title and cover photo, it is not an acoustic singer-songwriter recording with Jackson playing guitar.

**Scales:** `energy 2`, `tempo 2`, `groove 1`, `mood 2`, `theatrical 2`, `polish 3`, `experimental 1`, `electronic 1`. AllMusic critic Ron Wynn hears mainstream, fairly undistinguished productions and tentative delivery; balanced against Motown's professionally arranged repertoire, `polish 3` is justified over `5`. His subdued delivery and older standards keep groove low.

**Instruments:** only MJDb-listed album musicians: Felder bass, Gadson drums, Walker guitar and Sample piano; no Jackson guitar role. **Producers:** Qobuz/Motown track credits give Freddie Perren and Fonce Mizell ('With a Child's Heart'), Berry Gordy Jr. ('Up Again', 'Too Young', other songs), Mel Larson and Jerry Marcellino ('All the Things You Are', 'Music and Me'), and Hal Davis ('Happy', 'Doggin Around'). This conflicts with some single-name full-LP credits (Hal Davis only) and an alternate third-party track attribution for 'With a Child's Heart'. We privilege granular licensed role labels and lower whole-row confidence to medium. Bob Gaudio on 'Morning Glow' is `AdditionalProducer` rather than full credited producer and is excluded. **Influence:** Classic Motown calls Jackie Wilson Jackson's childhood hero and 'Doggin Around' a tribute to him: sufficient album-specific link. Covering Stevie Wonder is not on its own proof of influence for this recording.

**Production:** empty, because 1973 analogue recording techniques are not documented for particular album takes by reliable identified sources. **Landmark 0:** no indication this set is one of his defining solo LPs; songs remain historically useful. **Sources:** AllMusic, Qobuz/Motown, Classic Motown, original MusicBrainz LP, MJDb.

## Forever, Michael (1975) — `mj-forevermichael`

**Genres:** AllMusic names R&B, soul and pop-soul. Qobuz classifies as soul. A secondary history explicitly names Philadelphia soul and some funk; this explains `phillysoul` and `funk` at lower evidence strength. MJDb labels the album disco, but William Ruhlmann (AllMusic) argues the album **lacks the contemporary dance sound** of 'Dancing Machine'. Therefore `disco` appears last and refers only to potential movement in selected cuts, not full-LP dance-floor orientation. These are real source disagreements, not hidden.

**Scales:** `energy 3`, `tempo 2`, `groove 2`, `mood 3` distinguish lively singles from prevalent ballads; `electronic 1` based on named rhythm-section instruments, not a documented synth; `theatrical 3` on impassioned lead; `experimental 1` because familiar Motown templates; `polish 4` for professionally produced and arranged repertoire. The eight scores are Fandome judgments informed by source descriptions.

**Instruments:** MJDb album-associated Felder bass, Gadson drums, Walker guitar, Sample/Randi piano. **Production:** blank; no specific named hardware, studio technique or vocal processor substantiated. **Producers:** Qobuz tracks list Brian Holland for 'We're Almost There', 'Take Me Back', 'Just a Little Bit of You'; Sam Brown III for 'One Day in Your Life' and 'You Are There'; Hal Davis for several selections; Freddie Perren and Fonce Mizell for 'I'll Come Home to You'. Edward Holland Jr. appears as writer of songs produced by Brian; despite academic-discography listing as an album producer, no equally explicit track producer credit for him was found. Caution: Qobuz spells 'Hal David' once where multiple other tracks identify Hal Davis.

**Conflicting criticism:** AllMusic calls this a maturing vocal turn but retrograde stylistically, compared with more modern Jackson 5 dance material. A database treats disco as a whole-record tag, which should be weighted low. `landmark 0` recognises historical final Motown studio release without assuming equivalent creative importance to *Off the Wall*. **Sources:** AllMusic, label Qobuz, Motown history, MusicBrainz, MJDb, secondary genre taxonomy.

## Cross-phase boundaries

This phase excludes Jackson 5/the Jacksons group LPs, later compilations, the film-only theme album of *Ben*, and anniversary remasters. The 1978 *The Wiz* is a Various Artists soundtrack with separately co-billed cast performances, not an undisputed Michael Jackson solo album; its possible inclusion must be independently evaluated in a later phase. Posthumous albums likewise need provenance/authenticity checks before cataloguing.
