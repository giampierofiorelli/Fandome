# Tiromancino, phase 01 — album evidence and disagreements

**Sources:** full clickable links in `tiromancino_phase01_sources.csv`, one per evidence record. All numerical scale ratings are editorial applications of Fandome anchors to documented album-specific musical descriptions, **not measured signal features**. Fields are left empty if evidence cannot responsibly support them.

## tiromancino-tiromancyno — Tiromancyno (1992)

- **History / ID / type:** first studio album, originally credited to *Tiromancyno* with a y; the present-day official discography labels its 1992 entry *Tiromancino*. MusicBrainz distinguishes the original 1992 album and the later archival releases. No prior catalogue item exists.
- **Genre ordering:** original album's `pop` classification and its contemporary electronic-dance description come from secondary release summary; VICE's historical reassessment identifies early funk and synth oddities. These do not establish a specific house, techno, new-jack or synthpop genre; none is entered.
- **Scales:** `energy=3`, `electronic=4`, `groove=4` for described dance/electronic orientation; `experimental=2` for VICE's unusual synthesizer breaks despite a largely commercial idiom. `mood`, `theatrical`, `polish`, `tempo` deliberately empty as album-level sources do not describe them specifically enough.
- **Voice and instruments:** lead singing style not firmly documented, so **voice blank**. `synth` explicitly described by VICE, but specific instruments and equipment besides that remain unverified.
- **Production:** empty; no source-backed specific production technique/machine established. Producer Stefano Borzi listed in secondary discography and acknowledged as a past Tiromancino collaborator on his site, but original sleeve credit still needed.
- **Lyrics/language:** exaggerated/comic character sketches (including *Il gigante di gomma* and *Cappuccetto rosso*) substantiate irony/storytelling and primarily Italian singing. The track title *Follow Me* is not enough to establish an English-language vocal track, so leave `en` out.
- **Comparison/influences:** no credible album-specific named influences; both columns blank. This prevents an unsubstantiated 1992 similarity to artists simply popular that year.
- **Landmark/confidence:** 0 / **low**. Historically an important debut, but the surviving contemporary sonic/credit evidence is sparse and Federico later dismissed it himself (Rolling Stone interview 2021).

## tiromancino-insisto — Insisto (1994)

- **Genres:** indie rock and guitar-centered experimental/psychedelic pop-rock described by VICE (2017) and DeBaser's dedicated album review (2006); art rock sourced only to discography; funk covers selected tracks, hence listed later.
- **Instruments:** DeBaser names distorted electric guitars, acoustic guitar, drums, synth, piano, bass and handclaps in specific tracks, rather than relying on a later live lineup. Instrument ranking privileges core guitar band and then keyboards/effects.
- **Production:** critic describes Zampaglione's falsetto and distorted/feedback guitars, reverse-guitar fragments and synth processing. `falsetto`, `distortion`, `sonicmanipulation` are defensible; no vintage synth model or tape machine identified, and `backmasking` would be incorrect shorthand for a reversed guitar intro.
- **Scales:** `energy=3` variable guitar rock; `electronic=2` live rhythm section with synth interventions; `mood=2` strange, lonely and uncomfortable narrative tone; `theatrical=2` mostly intimate but occasionally stylised delivery; `experimental=3` crooked arrangements; `polish=3` studio sound VICE considers comparatively controlled; `groove=2` some funk movement but not a club album. Typical album tempo insufficiently characterized, so blank.
- **Voice/lyrics:** soft voice explicitly described by DeBaser; falsetto specified in production. Narratives of imaginary friends and psychic isolation ground `storytelling|introspection|irony` (ordered introspection first). Italian throughout the reviewed vocal pieces.
- **Producers:** left blank: track writers, arrangers, and band membership are not a substitute for producer credits.
- **Influences versus sounds_like:** VICE explicitly says some songs are **inspired** by Red Hot Chili Peppers and Beck, supporting `influenced_by`; DeBaser's Marlene Kuntz comparison is an editorial likeness only, so appears in `sounds_like` instead. Battiato lyrical resemblance in a fan review does **not** establish a music influence and is excluded.
- **Landmark/confidence:** 0 / medium; pivotal reinvention but not as defining as the following album. DeBaser is a community review; VICE provides independent qualified review context.

## tiromancino-alonealieno — Alone alieno (1995)

- **Genres:** VICE's extended close reading supports recurring alternative guitar/electronic/trip-hop crossover. `ambient|dub` describe *Di quello che ho perso*, hip-hop/funk *Funko*, industrial/big beat *Federazione porno*, punk/noise rock *Punko*, acid jazz *Resto qui*. These track-specific categories come **after** broad album-level categories in the row. `artrock` is a weaker secondary discography genre. The evidence CSV distinguishes scopes.
- **Scales:** `energy=4` severe punk/industrial/guitar passages amid slow breaks; `electronic=4` prominent synths, sampling, programmed percussion; `mood=1` anxiety, alienation, isolation in the close reading; `theatrical=3` vocal interludes and manipulated sounds; `experimental=4` loops, backwards segments, cross-genre shifts; `polish=2` lo-fi fragments and rough noise alongside deliberate production; `tempo=3` slow trip-hop tracks contrasting faster punk; `groove=3` funk and hip-hop examples, not dance throughout.
- **Instruments:** VICE documents synth, acoustic and distorted guitars, clavinet on *Funko*, electric piano on *Corri*, sampled clarinet on *Resto qui*; credited bassist/drummer and keyboard roles corroborated by lineup metadata; AllMusic lists Francesco's sampling and drum programming. Because AllMusic credits a sampling technique but **not** a specific sampler device, `sampler` is omitted. `clarinet` indicates audible sampled timbre, **not** a credited live performer.
- **Production:** AllMusic directly credits Francesco Zampaglione with `sampling` and `drumprogramming`, as well as producer role; VICE directly discusses filtered and overlapped voices, distorted guitars, lo-fi guitar and reversed/processed sounds. Crucially, **do not claim Korg MS-20**: VICE says effects are *like* an MS-20, not that the instrument is credited. No source confirms a Roland, Akai, Fairlight, Pro Tools or tape model.
- **Voice, lyrics, language:** Federico sings, Laura sometimes supplies sung/recited or filtered voice; `spoken` marks an explicitly documented recitation; themes of alienation, identity, relationships and social alienation are described track by track. Italian primary vocals. `spoken` applies to Laura's recitation in a passage, not general rap.
- **Sounds_like:** journalistic, not causal, connections to Portishead (*Corri*), Jane's Addiction / RATM (*Danneggia l'erezione*), NIN and Aphex Twin (textures on certain tracks). Do not label these as sampled credits. The review identifies a sample from **The Residents**, but sampled records are explicitly excluded from `influenced_by`.
- **Influenced_by:** VICE explicitly says *Funko* is inspired by Cypress Hill/House of Pain and evokes Nine Inch Nails as a recurrent influence. These are **qualified critic claims**, not direct artist declarations; other comparisons remain in `sounds_like`. If Claude uses artist-interview-only policy instead, clear these values rather than guessing.
- **Landmark/confidence:** 1 / high given original band recollection, personnel credits, review/critical coverage. Reviewers praise experimental ambition but do not all endorse every track/production choice.

## tiromancino-rosaspinto — Rosa spinto (1997)

- **Release/year:** official band page 1997; DeBaser and Wikipedia 1997; Rockol lists 1998. Original release year **1997**, alternative year noted rather than silently changed.
- **Genres:** pop-rock/alternative rock and trip-hop from dedicated album review; VICE identifies a pivot to **dance**, critic names *techno, disco* textures. DeBaser identifies drum-and-bass specifically on *Ami mai* and *Profondo rosa*; not all tracks are drum-and-bass. `indiepop` comes from review page genre metadata.
- **Scales:** `energy=4`, `electronic=4`, `tempo=4`, `groove=4` represent significantly increased club-oriented rhythm and uptempo material, balanced against song-based pop-rock; `theatrical=3` more upfront vocal presentation; `experimental=3` still crossing styles; `polish=3` more accessible/mixed sound but no precise gloss benchmark. Mood blank because criticism diverges and no consistent album-level affect is documented.
- **Instruments:** original album lineup (Federico/Cristiano guitars, Laura bass, Francesco keyboards, Alessandro Canini drums) gives `guitar|bass|keyboards|drums`; no unsupported model named. AllMusic specifically credits `drumprogramming` at band level. Do **not** infer drum-machine brand from DnB style.
- **Producers:** Pietro Micioni is named as producer and mixer in secondary release metadata; AllMusic lists Tiromancino as producer (possible band-level credit). Both tentatively entered. Validate liner notes before locking; mixer status alone never suffices, hence Micioni is included only because the source also says **produced**. Riccardo Sinigallia's main producing phase comes later.
- **Voices/language/lyrics:** Federico lead and prominent Laura passages per VICE; voice cell left empty because no credible album-specific timbre category is identified; *Dying Again* is sung in English, primarily Italian elsewhere. Its heartbreak text, plus Italian introspective/song-based writing, warrants themes `love|heartbreak|introspection`. Do not mark authors or vocal guests as producers.
- **Sounds_like vs influenced_by:** VICE critiques a Prodigy-like approach, hence The Prodigy **sounds_like**; DeBaser says album continues US psychedelic indie **inspiration** in Jane's Addiction vein, yielding a qualified critic-level `influenced_by` entry. This does not establish a direct quote by Zampaglione. If strict autobiography is desired, leave the latter empty.
- **Critical disagreement:** DeBaser praises a varied bridge toward pop accessibility; VICE considers much of the dance pivot contrived. Confidence **medium** acknowledges subjective genre/quality disagreement without suppressing identifiable sounds.
- **Co-billing:** Daniele Silvestri is credited in his official biography as co-author/performer of *La storia* and author of *Dying Again*; he is not the co-billed album artist or a credited album producer.
