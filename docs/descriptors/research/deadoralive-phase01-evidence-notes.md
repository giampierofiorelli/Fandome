# Dead or Alive — Fandome Phase 01: research and evidence notes

Research date: 27 October 2026. Original studio LPs only. All numerical scores are independent Fandome editorial judgments for whole albums, not factual measurements. `sounds_like` is explicitly editorial resemblance, **not** evidence of an influence. Genre evidence describes whether the name applies to the whole album, some tracks or merely a texture. Credits are from original-issue databases/album transcriptions where possible; their spelling may vary. Every row remains `draft`.

## Sophisticated Boom Boom (1984) — `deadoralive-sophisticatedboomboom`

**Identity/genre.** Original Epic 1984 ten-track LP, not 2007/2016 bonus-track versions. AllMusic explicitly gives Disco, Dance-Pop, Dance-Rock, Hi-NRG and Electronic, while Album of the Year identifies synthpop, new wave, postpunk and alternative dance. The latter especially fits the older darker recordings, so `postpunk` and `newwave` are secondary/track-limited, not declarations of a uniformly goth-rock album. (AllMusic; Album of the Year; ChartSurfer original Epic pressings.)

**Instrumentation.** Period album credits specify Mike Percy on bass, Steve Coy on drums, Tim Lever on keyboards and Wayne Hussey on guitars, plus The Kick Horns and Caroline's Quartet strings; their ensembles justify brass/strings tokens. Zeus B. Held's direct recollection confirms Hussey was present early but left during production. This is a partly **live-instrument** record; no generic `drummachine` or named synth model without original-album-specific testimony. `hornarrangements` / `stringarrangements` reflect documented arranged ensembles, though original arranger-by-name is not confirmed. (Chartsurfer; original-credit transcription via LyricsWeb; Zeus B. Held first-person interview.)

**Production.** Both **Zeus B. Held** and **Dead or Alive** have a producer credit, not only Held. Tim Palmer mixed/engineered but is not a producer. The song That's The Way (I Like It) is a KC & The Sunshine Band cover; its presence **does not** make KC an `influenced_by` artist by itself. (AllMusic original LP credits.)

**Influences.** Zeus B. Held specifically states they loved **Patrick Cowley and Sylvester** as a model of uptempo gay disco. Pete Burns independently and directly confirms **Sylvester and Divine** were key influences, singling out Divine's **Native Love (Step by Step)** from *My First Album* (aka *Jungle Jezebel*). The direct sources support genuine historical influences. Sound resemblance to Soft Cell / Human League remains editorial only. (Electricity Club / Windy City Times.)

**Scores and themes.** Energy 4, groove 5: rhythmically focused dance-pop with more guitar and full band than later PWL records. Electronic 3, polish 3, experimental 3 capture the stylistic crossing. Mood 3 recognises darker tracks alongside party material. Voice `powerful|raspy|clear` reflects Burns's forceful theatrical tone; lyrics weigh love/sensuality/party, with identity a secondary reading.

## Youthquake (1985) — `deadoralive-youthquake`

**Genre/format.** Original 1985 nine-song album (not extended/remixed CD extras). AllMusic assigns Hi-NRG, Dance-Pop, Club/Dance, Dance-Rock and Contemporary Pop/Rock; Album of the Year additionally names Synthpop and Freestyle. `electronic` is a source-grounded broad umbrella supported by Sony master, 1985 credited drum machines/synths and studio reporting. Genre list covers *documented* mixed styles, not nine equally prevalent kinds. (AllMusic; AOTY; 45cat; Qobuz/Sony.)

**Instrument credits.** Original LP/personnel summary documents Tim Lever synthesizers, sequencer, sampler, guitar and saxophone; Mike Percy synthesizers, guitar and bass; Steve Coy drum machines and percussion; Pete Burns a *melodica* on I Wanna Be A Toy. These instruments appear as track- or album-level supported tokens, with `melodica` appropriately last. (Original studio-album personnel information assembled in standard album reference; cf. contemporaneous production context in Classic Pop.)

**Production.** **Mike Stock, Matt Aitken, Pete Waterman** are the producers; **Phil Harding is engineer/mixer**, not an extra producer. Classic Pop directly interviews Harding and explains the use of samples and programmed sound replacing some live parts, explicitly naming Fairlight, PPG synths and LinnDrum hardware at SAW sessions. These are the most qualified evidence for `sampling|synthprogramming|drumprogramming|fairlight|ppgwave|linndrum`, rather than generic 1980s speculation. SAW first UK #1 is associated with You Spin Me Round, not a general claim all of SAW's hits sounded the same. (AllMusic; Classic Pop; Mike Stock interview.)

**Influences.** Pete Burns explicitly endorsed Divine and Sylvester. Mike Stock recalls Burns specifically seeking SAW after hearing **Hazell Dean's Whatever I Do (Wherever I Go)**, a song first issued on *Heart First*. Therefore `Hazell Dean (Heart First)` is justified and properly connected to the album's production. Divine's specific Native Love is on *My First Album*. The later band Bananarama is editorial sound likeness only: its 1986 *True Confessions* came **after** this 1985 record, so this cannot be a documented precursor/influence.

**Scores.** Electronic 5, groove 5, polish 5 and energy 5 capture mechanised Hi-NRG and big choruses. Experimental 2 for hook-led verse/chorus writing, theatrical 5 for Burns's delivery. Mood 4 because of primarily celebratory desire and dance energy despite romantic frustration.

## Mad, Bad and Dangerous to Know (1986) — `deadoralive-madbadanddangeroustoknow`

**Genre.** A 1986 original Epic album. AllMusic expressly lists Hi-NRG, Dance-Pop, Disco, Dance-Rock and Club/Dance; original copies file under Electronic, synth-pop. Album of the Year similarly labels Synthpop / Hi-NRG. `electronic` is genre taxonomy from the original-label description, but specific 1986 drum machine **model names** have not been corroborated. (AllMusic; ChartSurfer original 1986 US/UK/Japan LP pressings; AOTY.)

**Personnel/instruments.** Album personnel identify Pete Burns voice, Tim Lever keyboards, Mike Percy bass/guitars, Steve Coy drums. Only the directly documented instruments are listed. No named instrument-model or technical effect is asserted for this album: `drumprogramming`, `fairlight`, `linndrum` have been **deliberately omitted** because sources describing PWL gear used around *Youthquake* do not prove the same devices appeared on this specific 1986 record. The `production` cell is intentionally blank until album-specific documentation is located. (Original Epic pressings; personnel listings; AllMusic.)

**Production.** The credited producers are the three members of SAW only. Engineers **Karen Hewitt / Mark McGuire / Mike Duffy**, and mixer **Phil Harding**, are not producers. Producer Mike Stock lists the original nine cuts in his discography; he and others recount session tensions. Do not include remixed 1987 12-inch editions as separate album recordings. (Mike Stock's discography; original Epic LP credits; Classic Pop fact-check.)

**Themes/scales.** The darker single Something in My House and cuts Brand New Lover / I'll Save You All My Kisses blend gothic theatricality with sexual/romantic urgency. Mood 2 and theatrical 5 reflect that tension; groove and tempo 5 reflect driving Hi-NRG. Samples from a horror film and remixes of Something in My House should not automatically be asserted for the **original album**; the well-known dialogue/sample story largely concerns the later extended single mix.

**Influence.** No album-specific directly documented external musical influences beyond band-wide history; **leave `influenced_by` empty** rather than treating AllMusic comparison as evidence. `sounds_like` analogies remain independently authored.

## Nude (1988 original Japan / 1989 UK) — `deadoralive-nude`

**Critical release-date correction.** Oricon documents the Japanese CD with original catalogue **25•8P-5160** issued on **19 December 1988**. Other retrospective discographies say 18 December; either way **1988 is the earliest original-release year**. UK/US issues were 1989. Hence `year=1988`; don't mistake the 1989 UK issue for the original. The first Japanese LP includes nine principal songs. Some Japanese CDs have instrumentals/alternate mixes as bonus; do not treat these as new songs. (Oricon Japan; EIL original Japanese LP; AllMusic US cassette listing.)

**Genre.** AllMusic album review categorises Dance-Pop, Hi-NRG, Disco and Dance-Rock, and explicitly mentions Eurodisco and pop-rock numbers. Album of the Year identifies Synthpop, Freestyle and Italo-Disco. These last two are secondary track-limited tags, not proof that the British act became an Italian disco artist. (AllMusic; AOTY.)

**Instrumentation.** Original personnel identify **Steve Coy drums**, **Tim Lever keyboards** and **Mike Percy bass** on the single Turn Around and Count 2 Ten, additional keyboards by **Andy Stennett** on the album, and female backing singers Tracy Ackerman, Laura Pallas and Jackie Raw. Conservative row uses `keyboards|drums|bass`. Guitars, precise synth models and individual drum machines are not added in the absence of adequate original album credits. (AllMusic individual credits; 1988 album personnel chronology; band membership overview.)

**Producers/technology.** **Pete Burns and Steve Coy** produced and arranged this record. Phil Bodger and Bruce Robins appear as engineers/mixers; Simon Everett carried out **digital editing** at Mayfair. `digitalediting` is the only documented specific production tag. This is the group's first self-produced album, not a third SAW release. (AllMusic original Nude credit list, original album personnel metadata.)

**Scales and uncertainty.** Energy 5 and groove 5 are driven by extended dance cuts; electronic 4 instead of 5 reflects the absence of comprehensive synthesizer and drum machine original credits even though the sound and genre remain electronic. Mood 3 balances frenetic party tracks with romantic tension; experimental 2 is mostly conventional studio pop. `confidence=medium` overall: release date, producers and genres are well supported, but the album's instruments are incompletely documented.

**Remix exclusion.** *Nude: Remade Remodelled* (1989) is a remix album of tracks from the original *Nude*, not a fifth original studio album. It is excluded as nonessential repeat content. *Rip It Up* (1987) is a remixed greatest-hits release, not a studio album and not part of this phase.
