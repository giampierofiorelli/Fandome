# Tame Impala — Phase 02 — evidence notes

**Research cut-off:** 26 October 2026. Two 27-column draft rows only. Sources are independently listed in `tameimpala_phase02_sources.csv`, with URLs, which these notes abbreviate by publication. No evidence from other Tame Impala records has been copied into instruments or production.

## tameimpala-theslowrush — *The Slow Rush* (2020)

**Identity / release / credit:** AllMusic confirms release 14 February 2020, studio record, and **Kevin Parker** as the credited producer. The Skinny documents him recording, producing and mixing all 12 tracks. Parker collaborating with Travis Scott or Mike Dean earlier is *not* an album production credit. The catalogue already has the album under `tameimpala-theslowrush`.

**Genre ordering:** Front-load neo-psychedelic/psychedelic-pop crossed with nu-disco, disco and house, as classified by Album of the Year, The FADER, Pitchfork and AllMusic. `nudisco` (AOTY) and `neodisco` (AllMusic's **Neo-Disco**) are distinct labels, both grounded in named sources. `alternativedance` and `leftfieldpop` are AllMusic album-wide styles. `phillysoul`, `adultcontemporaryrnb`, `prog` and `boombap` are texture or selective stylistic references, **not** album-wide genre claims. `pianohouse` is specifically attached to “One More Year” by the Evening Standard. `yachtrock` is used only as a minor album texture on Pitchfork's RIYL overview; *Patience* itself was excluded from the original LP. The album is not coded as broadly `rock`: in a 2020 Uproxx interview Parker cautioned that calling it rock would be a stretch. Genres are proportionally ordered rather than forced into one label.

**Scales (editorial judgment; 0–5 guide anchors):**
- `energy=3`: broad mid-paced, kinetic but not continuously high-intensity; compare “One More Year” versus “On Track” and “One More Hour”. (Pitchfork, Standard)
- `electronic=4`: synthesizers, house patterns and programmed rhythm dominate; acoustic drums, bass, piano and some guitars prevent a fully electronic 5. (Sound on Sound, Pitchfork)
- `mood=3`: more upbeat/sunnier than *Currents*, but anxieties, mortality, grief and nostalgia occur throughout. (Pitchfork, Standard)
- `theatrical=3`: large layered climaxes, yet relatively private/relaxed vocal delivery. (Pitchfork)
- `experimental=3`: nontraditional structure and loops of transformed acoustic sound alongside accessible hooks. (Pitchfork, The FADER)
- `polish=5`: meticulous rich arrangements, tapes and complex studio layering despite deliberate imperfections. (Sound on Sound, Guardian)
- `tempo=3`: much danceable moderate tempo; reflective passages temper pace. (Pitchfork, Standard)
- `groove=4`: strong disco, funk and house propulsion across album, not one uninterrupted club mix. (The FADER, Guardian)

**Lead voice:** `soft|clear`, understated/effected sung tenor rather than a second singer; Parker plays the parts. No `operatic` or `rap`. `falsetto` is a vocal treatment/register and only goes in production when album-specific evidence documents it; we do not infer from habitual singing.

**Instruments (each independently confirmed on this LP):** `synth` Sound on Sound (Oberheim/SH-1), Pitchfork (Moog); `drums` direct engineering discussion of real drums recorded by Parker; `bass` Hofner reference / live bass rerecording in Sound on Sound; `piano` “On Track” and “Breathe Deeper” (Pitchfork, Guardian); `drummachine` TR-707 on “Glimmer” (Roland and Sound on Sound); `electricguitar` phased guitar/riffs on “One More Hour” and “Posthumous Forgiveness” (Pitchfork); `percussion` conga on “One More Year” (Pitchfork); `synthbass` musical bass synth described via SH-1 synth sources and reviews; `organ` Hammond on “On Track” in The Edge review (single track, weaker evidence); `mallets` xylophone break on “Instant Destiny” (Pitchfork). Do not add physical strings solely because a critic hears *string-like* patches, and do not add instruments just because Parker owns them.

**Production: evidence per token**
- `analogtape`: Parker says roughly half the LP's drum tracks were bounced through Revox B77 / Ampex ATR-700 to create saturation (Sound on Sound p.2); this is *not* a claim the whole album was tracked entirely to analog tape.
- `overdubbing|synthprogramming|drumprogramming`: Sound on Sound interview and Parker's solo multi-track workflow, with electronic rhythmic and synthesizer arrangements heard on the LP; arranging drum-machine and synth parts specifically for the album.
- `sampling`: **two actual, spoken-recording samples** documented by Parker: an audio-gear podcast in “Glimmer” and his wife Sophie talking in “Tomorrow’s Dust” (Long Live Vinyl). Pitchfork explicitly says many instrumental parts **sound sampled but are actually performed**; never call those sampled recordings.
- `tr707`: Roland's own “Glimmer” feature and Sound on Sound document that exact model. **Do not add `tr808` on this record:** the Sound on Sound “Borderline” 808-like low-end sound is an EQ trick rather than confirmation of an actual Roland TR-808; Guardian's “acid house 808s” is a timbral comparison.
- `rolandsh1|oberheimmatrix6|oberheimmatrix1000`: Parker in Sound on Sound identifies these synthesizers used on this specific LP. New token names must be added by Claude (no approximation to a generic Oberheim model).
- `moog`: Pitchfork explicitly reports Moog synthesizers in “One More Hour”; model unspecified; don't invent Minimoog.
- `autotune`: Guardian (Dave Simpson) specifically reports Auto-Tune as sound design on this album; no claim of blanket pitch correction.
- `heavyreverb`: sustained echo-drenched vocal passages including closer (Pitchfork), and reverb on “Glimmer” machine (Roland); relative prominence supports token.
- **Excluded:** `tr808` and `linndrum` (no album-specific proof of machines), `juno106` (generic Parker career-wide gear is not sufficient to attribute to these sessions), `sequencer` (no album-specific recording credit), `fieldrecordings` (the two spoken audio samples do not by themselves establish field-recording production).

**Lyrics and emotional profile:** `introspection|nostalgia|love|identity|heartbreak`: recurring concerns time passing, grief for estranged father (“Posthumous Forgiveness”), marriage, relationships, fear of change, lost youth. Pitchfork and Standard. Language `en` throughout original standard album. `scene` names Australian scene and documented stylistic crossover, not an external group.

**`sounds_like` vs `influenced_by`:** `sounds_like` is a defended editorial similarity grounded in Pitchfork RIYL (Daft Punk, Supertramp), reviews (Steely Dan, Daryl Hall, Michael Jackson) and the FADER's dance-music parallels, **not** a claim Parker directly credited each. `influenced_by` contains (i) Parker's explicit comments that work with **Mike Dean and Travis Scott** changed his LP-making approach (The FADER), and (ii) listening to primitive acid house in the Larry Heard mode *before making this album* (The FADER). This is *production/aesthetic* influence, not a claim those artists played on the LP. **Portishead and Cypress Hill are deliberately omitted** from `influenced_by` because his Los Angeles Times statement names them as formative influences on rhythm generally, not specifically this album.

**Landmark = 1**: fourth studio album and consolidated shift towards broader disco/house/pop crossover; high confidence in both descriptions and instrumentation. Pitchfork was enthusiastic but raised concerns about overextended sections, while other critics awarded stronger praise; score describes sound not acclaim.

## tameimpala-deadbeat — *Deadbeat* (2025)

**Identity / released status / credits:** Sony confirms this was released on **17 October 2025** on Columbia and is fifth studio album. AllMusic credits **Kevin Parker** as producer and mixer. Sarah Aarons has *co-writing* credits on “Dracula”, “Oblivion” and “Afterthought”, not producer. AllMusic categorises Loren Humphrey as **additional production / engineering**, not credited main or co-producer, hence not in the `producers` field. Tape Op identifies Wave House in Injidup and recording at Fremantle; Matt Colton did mastering. Standard album (12 tracks), no remixes, live or streaming deluxe add-ons.

**Genres:** Resident Advisor marks `house|techhouse|pop` as whole-album genres and describes dance-pop; Sony's official press release uses **club-psych** (new token `clubpsych`) for the album. NME identifies industrial techno on “Ethereal Connection”, R&B on “Obsolete”, synth-pop on “Piece of Heaven”; Pitchfork hears progressive house on “Ethereal Connection”, lo-fi house on “No Reply” (new `lofihouse`) and tropical house on “Oblivion”; these are **track-only**, so at the tail. `industrialtechno` is a new literal genre label; do not silently replace with industrial, which is a different concept. Tape Op's reggaeton and trance observations describe grooves, not primary whole-album genres; these are texture. AOTY identifies melodic house, indietronica and alt-pop; a lower-confidence cross-source perspective. Critics disagree about successful dance stylistic integration (RA and Pitchfork critical; Tape Op more positive).

**Scales (editorial judgment):**
- `energy=4`: extensive club kick and extended propulsive sections, tempered by spoken/quiet demos and slower funk songs (NME, RA).
- `electronic=4`: predominantly synth/drum-machine dance music but with live drums, sampled/recorded piano and identifiable guitar (Tape Notes, LA Times, SPIN).
- `mood=1`: the anxious/self-critical language, regret and dislocation persist despite euphoric sounds (RA, Guardian, NME).
- `theatrical=2`: emotionally private vocal performances, small-room raw demo moments, against big beats (Tape Op, Pitchfork).
- `experimental=3`: nonstandard tape-performance approach and distorted drum synthesis beside formulaic pop/club structures (Tape Notes, RA).
- `polish=4`: immaculate synth mixes coexist with deliberate audible gaffes and phone demos (Tape Op, LA Times).
- `tempo=4`: long four-on-the-floor pieces balanced by midtempo tunes such as “Loser” (RA, SPIN).
- `groove=5`: explicitly conceived around Australian bush-doof dance culture (Sony, Guardian, RA) and largely club-oriented grooves.

**Vocals:** `soft|clear` (Parker's airy/detached singing); non-lyrical asides occur but do not make album-wide lead `spoken`. Some tracks have sung falsetto but category is not a separate `voice` token in guide.

**Instruments:** `synth` (TR-808/Prophet-5/Roland D-50 in Mix with the Masters; synth pads from Tape Op); `drummachine` (TR-808 + Vermona DRM1, both demonstrably on “Dracula”); `piano` (Parker confirms phone-recorded opening “My Old Ways” via LA Times); `drums` (Parker describes real live drum performances used for initial parts via Tape Notes and documented secondary transcript); `synthbass` (dance tracks and “Dracula” bass synthesis, primary tech walkthrough); `electricguitar` (“Loser” guitar-line, SPIN); `bass` (production walkthrough part 5 describes bass; instrument's acoustic/electric source is not asserted for that *part*). `percussion` not added just on rhythmic genre grounds, nor `strings` from synthetic string patches (Pitchfork), nor `sampler` hardware from sample sound.

**Production: album and per-track separation:**
- `tr808|vermonadrm1`: **Parker's actual drum system**, 808 via a guitar amplifier combined with an analogue DRM1, documented by Tape Notes / the 2026 reported technical interview. This is not inference from techno genre.
- `prophet5|rolandd50|ableton`: explicitly shown in Kevin Parker's **“Dracula”** session breakdown (Mix with the Masters). Prophet-5 and D-50 are distinct machines; the session is not evidence that every track used both.
- `soundtoysdecapitator|soundtoysechoboy|valhallaroom`: specifically named in Tape Notes vocal chain and “Dracula” walkthrough (Decapitator); represent album-era effects, not evidence of exclusive use.
- `layeredvocals`: Parker explains four-take vocal composites with hard-panned whispered layers in Tape Notes. `pitchedvocals`: “Dracula” production walkthrough explicitly discusses pitch shifting. `overdubbing`: Parker's drum-first method is followed by separately tracked overdubs. `drumprogramming`: synced electronic rhythm system.
- `analogtape`: reported Tape Notes account of recording some drums/performance on tape; **not** a claim all tracks were cut live to tape. `roomambience`: electronic drums via guitar amplifiers and room capture; recorded room sounds also retained. `distortion`: amp/circuit saturation and Decapitator are specifically demonstrated on “Dracula”. `springreverb`: Ableton convolution spring reverb on most tracks per Parker's 2026 interview. `sampling`: Pitchfork describes a sampled vocal on “Piece of Heaven”; demo snippets are retained, but the audible source is not assumed to be a third-party recording.
- **Excluded for lack of placement evidence:** `rolandsh1` (appears on Tape Notes general gear list but cannot positively assign to a *Deadbeat* recorded part), `dbx165` and generic studio gear; `autotune` has no album-specific source; `linndrum` absent. The (2026) *Dracula* studio walkthrough is about an actual released song, not a live arrangement.

**Lyrics / language:** `introspection|identity|heartbreak|love|nostalgia` reflects anxiety/social self-doubt/fatherhood/relationship tensions, plus nostalgia for dancing and youth (RA, Guardian, Pitchfork). Sung language `en`. “Obsolete” and “Afterthought” show relationships but lyrics are not universally party-celebration; omit `party` as a main theme.

**`sounds_like` editorial parallels** come from Tape Op's detailed auditory comparisons (The Orb, Panda Bear, Daryl Hall), Pitchfork and NME (Daft Punk, Beck, Enya, Brian Wilson). **Do not mistake lyric “I'm a loser, babe” as *Beck* being a declared influence.** `influenced_by` only lists: **The Strokes** explicitly named by Parker about this record on Tape Notes; **Stevie Wonder** as Parker's explicit inspiration for drum-first workflow, corroborated by transcript account; **Jeff Mills** “The Bells” and **The Beatles** as precisely credited tributes by a qualified Pitchfork critic, not automatic genre similarity; **Enya** specifically described by NME critic as shaping the synth-pop feel of “Piece of Heaven”. All last three are track-specific influences, not a claim of personal endorsement by Parker.

**Landmark = 0 (provisional)**: significant dance pivot and fifth album, but too early/mixed reception to treat it as artist-defining yet; future reception audit may revise independently of genres. `confidence=medium` because whole-album core is well documented but some styles depend on few tracks and specific synth/vocal models are documented for “Dracula” only. Review disagreement is explicit: Resident Advisor and Pitchfork fault derivative club production; Tape Op is positive about textures. This affects editorial reception, **not** evidence that the genres/instruments exist.
