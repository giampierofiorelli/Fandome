# Cameo — Phase 02 evidence and uncertainties

Research date: 28 October 2026. Source URLs refer to contemporary originals, authoritative credits, liner-note listings and expert reviews. Descriptors are Fandome judgments. Never copy evaluative prose from review sources into the `notes` column.

## Cameosis (1980) — `cameo-cameosis`

**Main sources:** Craig Lytle, *AllMusic* (https://www.allmusic.com/album/cameosis-mw0000312967); original Chocolate City CCLP 2011 LP personnel listing (https://shop-obc.michael45.com/produkt/cameo-cameosis/); AllMusic personnel and arranging credits (https://www.allmusic.com/album/cameosis-mw0000312967); genre metadata (https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/51734-cameo-cameosis.php); original chart/cut listing (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/cameosis).

**Genres:** `funk|rnb|contemporaryrnb` directly from AllMusic album labels. The Album of the Year metadata separately calls the record funk/smooth soul. `smoothsoul` and `ballad` represent the significant slower side (Lytle discusses romantic 'I Care for You' and revised 'Why Have I Lost You'), so both trail the album's dance-funk identity.

**Scales:** `energy 4` (driving 'Shake Your Pants', syncopated funk and animation); `electronic 2` (band-driven rhythm with Moog/Minimoog alongside acoustic horns and live drums); `mood 4` (animated party songs countered by tearful ballads); `theatrical 2` (group vocal exchanges and featured tenor, no full theatrical production); `experimental 1` (conventional song structures plus a revised earlier Cameo song); `polish 3` (studio string/horn arrangement credits, no evidence of highly glossy digital production); `tempo 3` (up-tempo tracks mixed with slow romantic cuts); `groove 4` (dance floor-oriented funk dominates). The fact that the single has an extended 12-inch mix **does not** change these original LP scores.

**Instruments/production:** drums Larry Blackmon, bass Aaron Mills, guitar Anthony Lockett, Fender Rhodes and Moog/Minimoog T.C. Campbell and Gregory Johnson, saxophone Arnett Leftenant, trumpets Nathan Leftenant/Arthur Young, trombone Jeryl Bright, congas Angel Allende, Irving Spice's Strings. Horn arrangements by Blackmon/Tity Brothers, string arrangements Sammy Lowe. Named Moog/Minimoog synthesizer production tokens express documented machines; no asserted drum programming, sampling, Fairlight or digital tape.

**Lyrics:** party, romantic pursuit, heartache and sensuality directly from Lytle's coverage of 'Shake Your Pants', 'We're Goin' Out Tonight', 'I Care for You' and 'Why Have I Lost You'. **Similarity** Parliament/Funkadelic is based on the early-period sound comparison in Andy Kellman's artist biography (https://www.allmusic.com/artist/cameo-mn0000944349); a judicious editorial sonic link, not a 1980 influence declaration. `landmark=1`: #1 US R&B album per original listing and recognizable 'Shake Your Pants'; within-artist defining rather than a universal canon claim. `confidence=high` for instrumentation and dominant sound; voice left blank because leads alternate.

## Feel Me (1980) — `cameo-feelme`

**Main sources:** Craig Lytle, *AllMusic* (https://www.allmusic.com/album/feel-me-mw0000838513); 1980 sleeve data and performers (https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/feel-me; https://recordsale.de/de/kuenstler/cameo/albums/feel-me); producer in MusicBrainz (https://musicbrainz.org/release/33de7dac-181f-4d2e-b208-227fa70fde01); detailed personnel/arrangements (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feel_Me_(album)).

**Genres:** `funk` explicitly AllMusic; `rnb` AllMusic main family; `soul` from album genre and romantic vocal material; `ballad` specifically Lytle's discussion of 'Feel Me' and 'Better Days'. No invented house/electrofunk/quiet-storm label.

**Scales:** `energy 3` (mixture of forceful 'Keep It Hot' and ballads); `electronic 2` (Moog, Minimoog, CS-80 and unspecified Prophet contrasted with full horn section and live band); `mood 3` (sexy/romantic sentiments alongside social subject); `theatrical 2` (Lockett's intimate tenor, without outsized staging); `experimental 1` (traditional rhythm-and-brass funk, slow soul ballads, no unusual structures claimed); `polish 3` (rich arranger/horn credits with balanced studio sound); `tempo 3` (variable); `groove 3` (prominent dance funk despite substantial ballad runtime).

**Instruments/production:** complete horn section includes saxophones, trumpet and trombone; acoustic piano/Rhodes, guitar, bass, congas/percussion and Larry Blackmon drums. `minimoog`, `moog`, `yamahacs80` are named in original personnel; unspecified Prophet does **not** justify `prophet5`. `hornarrangements` documented by original sleeve credits. Do not treat vocalist Lockett as the sole album voice. **Lyrics:** intimacy, desire, love; AllMusic identifies 'Is This the Way' as socially conscious. No specific direct sonic comparison evidenced strongly enough for `sounds_like` or `influenced_by`.

## Knights of the Sound Table (1981) — `cameo-knightsofthesoundtable`

**Main sources:** Amy Hanson, *AllMusic* (https://www.allmusic.com/album/knights-of-the-sound-table-mw0000097779); original CCLP 2019 personnel (https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/knights-of-the-sound-table); LP producer/chart database (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/knights_of_the_sound_table); specialist chronology (https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/).

**Genres:** `funk|rnb|contemporaryrnb` from AllMusic; ballad is album subcomponent; disco is a one-track reference ('The Sound Table') **last**. New wave is **not** added just because reviewer sees the band anticipating 1980s dance aesthetics.

**Scales:** `energy 4` (dance numbers 'Freaky Dancin'', 'I Like It', and vigorous band interplay); `electronic 2` (Moog/Prophet alongside bass/drums/guitar/horns); `mood 3` (upbeat tracks mixed with sentimental ballads); `theatrical 2` (group-driven stage persona but no concept theatrics on LP); `experimental 2` (transitional track styles); `polish 3` (multi-studio LP recording and horn arrangement); `tempo 4` (predominantly upbeat despite ballads); `groove 4` (dance singles and funky rhythm-led album). No inference of a drum machine from an electronic timbre.

**Instruments/production:** band drums/percussion, bass, electric guitar, acoustic piano/Rhodes, Moog and unspecific Prophet synth, tenor sax, trumpets, trombone. `hornarrangements` from Larry Blackmon and Jeryl Bright credits; `moog` from original personnel. **Nona Hendryx** features on 'Don't Be So Cool' (guest artist; not co-billed at album level). The bass player Aaron Mills is not an album producer. No digital remastering in LP production. **Lyrics:** dance, desire, romance and heartbreak. AllMusic explicitly criticizes the weaker ballads; that disagreement is preserved, while the scored `landmark=0` reflects the transitional album's lesser standing relative to Cameosis and future Word Up!.

## Alligator Woman (1982) — `cameo-alligatorwoman`

**Main sources:** Amy Hanson, *AllMusic* (https://www.allmusic.com/album/alligator-woman-mw0001878982); original Chocolate City 2021 personnel transcription (https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/cameo/alligator-woman); producer/track listing (https://www.soulandfunkmusic.com/album/cameo/alligator_woman); specialist studio-era discography (https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/cameo/); Larry Blackmon first-person interview (https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/q-cameos-larry-blackmon-reflects-121200178.html).

**Genres:** `funk|rnb` as dominant. AllMusic refers to pop characteristics on lead single 'Flirt' (`pop` long tail) and identifies 'Secrets of Time'/'For You' as softer ballads (`ballad` tail). Avoid calling album wholesale synthpop or disco: the sources instead describe *funk featuring newer synthesizers*.

**Scales:** `energy 4` (driving Be Yourself / Soul Army / Flirt); `electronic 3` (prominent Jupiter-8/Minimoog/Yamaha CS-80 alongside band instrumentation); `mood 3` (sassy and buoyant tracks set against softer intimate songs); `theatrical 2` (AllMusic says stage 'Mothership' theatrics were scaled down); `experimental 2` (new tech plus old-school funk, unusual arrangements but no claim of avant-garde form); `polish 3` (Cheshire Sound studio, synthesis and professional arranging; not maximal high-gloss pop); `tempo 4` (several crisp and short up-tempo cuts); `groove 4` (deep funk and dance-oriented singles dominate).

**Instruments/production:** Blackmon drums/percussion/vocals, Charlie Singleton guitar/keys/vocals, Michael Burnett bass, Gregory Johnson Rhodes/Moog, Kevin Kendricks Roland Jupiter-8/Minimoog/Prophet (model unspecified), Randy Stern Yamaha CS-80, Nathan Leftenant and other brass. Generic `synth` is backed by original musician credits; `jupiter8|minimoog|moog|yamahacs80` are instrument models used, not evidence of particular recording software. **No 1982 sampling or drum-programming claim**. Layered background voices are documented, but not necessarily studio `layeredvocals` production, so omitted pending engineer testimony.

**Interpretation:** The 1982 record marks a meaningful reduction of the group and a shift towards harder synth-funk, but the AllMusic review also calls it the last album substantially rooted in their classic deep funk. Interview remarks in https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/q-cameos-larry-blackmon-reflects-121200178.html expressly reject a single calculated motive for the shift. `sounds_like=Parliament` is retained as a low-weight early-period historical sonic comparison, not an influence; critics describe reduction of theatrical resemblance. `landmark=0` is a cautious Fandome editorial decision, not a judgment that the album was unsuccessful.
