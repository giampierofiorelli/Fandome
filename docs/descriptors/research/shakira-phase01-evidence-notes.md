# Shakira — phase 01 evidence notes (2026-10-26)

## Approach
Read the Fandome descriptor guide, scene/genre tokens, instruments, production tokens, catalogue, co-billed map and validator from GitHub branch `descriptors`, in read-only mode. `shakira` exists in the catalogue; `docs/descriptors/shakira.csv` does not. Ratings are album-level editorial judgments grounded in **album-specific** descriptions. `confidence` is intentionally low for the two poorly documented early records. The track evidence for genres is separated from whole-album genres in the companion CSV; extra genre tokens are declared in `CLAUDE_IMPORT.md`.

## Magia (1991) — shakira-magia
- **Originality/date**: official Colombia Columbia 12-inch LP release (MusicBrainz M1); many online CDs are bootlegs, not evidence of an official reissue. 1991 original. 1991 specific day varies; year reliable.
- **Genres**: MusicBrainz release-group tags Latin pop and pop. Pop-rock given by the album history citing the original release (M2), secondary and less certain. The Guardian says both early albums were primarily ballads (M3). Do not treat the remix of "Lejos de tu amor" as proof of album-wide dance music.
- **Scales**: energy 2 / tempo 1 / groove 1 because the album is mainly ballads (M3), with a smaller dance-oriented exception mentioned in M2. Electronic, mood, theatrical, experimental and polish remain **unknown**: producer programming credits are insufficient to measure the album-wide mix or sonic finish.
- **Voice**: empty; a reliable, album-specific vocal-character assessment was not found.
- **Instruments/production**: original-cover-credited personnel, as transcribed by M2, name Sergio Solano's acoustic/electric guitars, Antonio Arnedo's saxophone, Shakira's guitar, and Pablo Tedeschi's synthesizer and drum programming. `synth` is supported by synthesizer programming, while exact synth/drum-machine models are **not** identified. `drumprogramming|synthprogramming` refers to documented techniques, not assumed hardware. Independent sleeve inspection still desirable.
- **Producers**: Gabriel Muñoz, Miguel Enrique Cubillos, Pablo Tedeschi, explicitly labelled producer in the liner-note transcription M2. Do not promote the engineers to producers.
- **Lyrics**: love and storytelling from album-specific childhood ballad, affection and narrative subjects (M2); Spanish from official release details. No album-specific artist influence supported, leave empty; `sounds_like` likewise empty rather than conjecture.
- **Landmark**: 0. Historically notable debut, but not generally considered a defining album. **Low confidence** reflects dependence on secondary original-credits transcription and absence of original critical analysis.

## Peligro (1993) — shakira-peligro
- **Originality/date**: Columbia 12-inch original Colombia 25 March 1993, MusicBrainz P1; CDs listed as bootlegs should not guide edition data. Original catalogue omission.
- **Genres**: MusicBrainz community tags Latin pop, pop, rock and pop/rock (P1); Guardian description of the first two records as primarily ballads (M3). Rock is lower priority than Latin pop, even if tag appears at release-group level; no claim every track is guitar-rock.
- **Scales**: energy 2 / tempo 2 / groove 1 approximate album-level ballad dominance from M3; leave electronic and mood/theatrical/experimental/polish blank. Drum programming is credited, but not enough for electronic 0–5.
- **Instruments**: M2-equivalent original-jacket personnel transcription P2 names Teto Ocampo on acoustic/electric guitars, three saxophonists across material, Willie Salcedo on percussion. Do **not** add named drum-machine model. Drum programming credited to Adolfo Concho (P2), so `drumprogramming` is supported.
- **Producer difference**: jacket transcription names *Eduardo Paz producer* and *Gabriel Muñoz co-producer*, while SongSuey (P3) describes Paz alone as main producer. Both are retained with Paz first, Muñoz second; explicit producer credit is not a mere arranger role. This should be checked against a scan of the original album.
- **Lyrics**: love/heartbreak follow title list and album's romantic material; stronger thematic references are sparse, no other themes assigned. `voice`, `sounds_like` and `influenced_by` empty. **Low confidence**; not a defining album (`landmark=0`).

## Pies Descalzos (1995) — shakira-piesdescalzos
- **Originality/date**: Apple Music and Qobuz original 6 October 1995; some discography pages give 1996 for foreign market releases, not original.
- **Genres**: Apple editorial D1 explicitly names rock, dancehall, bossa nova, bachata and house ("Estoy Aquí"); also situates work in Latin pop-rock. Birchmeier/TiVo on Qobuz D2 describes dance-pop, ballads and rock. Genre evidence CSV scopes bachata to "Te Necesito", house to "Estoy Aquí"; dancehall and bossa nova are secondary palette elements, not album-wide dominants. `worldmusic` from Qobuz retail category is listed **last** given lack of specificity.
- **Scales**: energy 3 / tempo 3: alternates energetic dance-oriented singles with intimate ballads; electronic 2: real rock-band instrumentation plus credited drum programming and house-inflected song; groove 3: some clear rhythmic/dance songs but not constant dance music. Mood 3: longing and heartbreak coexist with social defiance and upbeat hooks. Theatrical 3: assertive rock-frontwoman presentation alongside confessional tracks. Experimental 2: album changes styles but retains conventional pop song structures. Polish 3: finished studio-pop arrangement, without evidence for maximal electronic gloss (D1/D2). These are cautious editorial judgments, not measurable audio facts.
- **Voice**: `powerful|raspy` is an editorial reading of Apple Music's description of her assertive/ululating delivery at this stage; a separate specialised vocal analysis would improve this field.
- **Instruments**: Qobuz 1995 track credits D2 identify Ochoa on acoustic/electric guitars, bass, keyboards, harmonica and percussion; Camilo Montilla on piano; Gonzalo Vásquez drums and drum programming; Samuel Torres percussion; Eusebio Valderrama trumpet; Ochoa cello on "Un Poco de Amor"; these are album-credited, though cello/trumpet occur less prominently. No specific electronic instrument **models** identified.
- **Production**: Gonzalo Vásquez specifically credited drum programmer, Ochoa credited programmer (D2); record `drumprogramming|programming`. Do not infer Pro Tools, tape or a machine from the recording year.
- **Producers**: Shakira and Luis Fernando Ochoa both credited by MusicBrainz D3. Qobuz's track metadata also lists Tim Mitchell as a producer on "Estoy Aquí". This discrepancy could reflect an edited version or metadata inheritance. Because the base album producer credit in D3 is Shakira/Ochoa, **Tim Mitchell omitted pending original-edition clarification**. Not an executive producer assumption.
- **Lyrics**: love/heartbreak: "Antología" and "Estoy Aquí"; introspection/identity and social critique: title track's social conformity theme, per D1. Language Spanish.
- **Documented influences**: unusually, Apple Music's album-specific editorial D1 says Shakira was **inspired by Aterciopelados and Alanis Morissette** when shaping the new project. These artists are listed in influenced_by, not guessed from a similar sound. Aterciopelados is sometimes misspelled Aterciopleados in secondary texts; correct group name retained.
- **Landmark**: 1, album that established her Latin American career (D1/D2). **High confidence** for core musical profile but provisional voice and individual genre prominence.

## ¿Dónde Están los Ladrones? (1998) — shakira-dondeestanlosladrones
- **Date/edition**: Apple/AllMusic 29 September 1998; avoid treating a retailer's alternate 25 Sept entry as an independent edition. Arabic sung on "Ojos Así" (D1), so `es|ar` reflects **one song** and its original version, not a bilingual album throughout.
- **Genres**: Latin pop and pop-rock primary (Apple D1, TiVo L2, Rolling Stone L4); track-specific house/mariachi "Ciega, Sordomuda", hard-rock "Si Te Vas", bachata "Moscas en la Casa", ballad "Sombra de Ti" (D1). `rockandroll` is the TiVo critic's rock & roll rhythm description, texture only. `worldmusic` is generic storefront classification and low-priority last.
- **Scales**: energy 3, tempo 3, groove 3 because dance single and rhythmically lively rock coexist with slow introspective material. Electronic 2 from Mendez's credited programming and Jupiter-8 but guitars/drums prominent. Mood 2 because critic notes sustained melancholy tempered by hope (D1). Theatrical 4 because heightened dramatic belting/ululating presentation and wide arrangements; experimental 2 because genre blending occurs in recognisable pop songs; polish 4 for detailed professional multi-studio arrangements and meticulous credits (L2/L3), distinct from 5 ultra-glossy pop. Values remain editorial, not equipment-derived.
- **Voice**: `powerful|raspy`: album's emphatic vocals and distinctive ululating vibrato are described in D1. Raspy vocal timbre is an editorial, weaker claim than the core genre evidence.
- **Instruments**: L2 and L3 identify acoustic/electric guitar, bass and fretless bass, drums, percussion, keyboards, piano, accordion, strings/violin, trumpet, cello, and **oud** (AllMusic album personnel Joe Zeytoonian/Michelle Massad). Several are sparse arrangement details, not necessarily featured on every track. Apple D1 mentions doumbek on "Ojos Así"; `percussion` is retained because source names the exact drum but track personnel does not justify a single standardized physical subtype on its own. `oud` is not yet in repository instrument list; add before validation.
- **Production**: L2 track-level Lester Mendez/Javier Garza programming; AllMusic L3 credits Lester Mendez string arrangements and Jupiter-8. `programming|stringarrangements|jupiter8`. No unsupported specific DAW, drum machine or microphone names.
- **Producer audit**: Qobuz track metadata lists **Shakira, Luis Fernando Ochoa, Lester Mendez**, plus **Pablo Flores** and **Javier Garza** explicitly as producer on "Ojos Así" (L2). Qobuz also credits **Pablo Florez** only as programmer and **Emilio Estefan Jr. as executive producer**. Thus *Pablo Flores* is the producer spelling (not Florez), and Emilio is **excluded** despite a narrative Apple editorial describing him as co-producer; track-role-specific credits take precedence. `producers` does not include engineers or additional-only contributors.
- **Artist sonic parallels**: TiVo L2 compares the album to post-Alanis approaches and Sheryl Crow-like eclecticism. These qualify as `sounds_like`, **not** proof of direct album influences, so `influenced_by` is blank despite the artist's broader formative influences.
- **Lyrics**: theft and social disillusion (album title and "Octavo Día"), loss/heartbreak ("Ciega", "Moscas"), romantic introspection ("Inevitable") per Apple D1; no generic blanket attribution to all tracks.
- **Landmark**: 1, consensus breakthrough and Rolling Stone retrospective L4. High confidence on genres, credits and role distinctions.

## Common caveats
1. Two former Sony/Columbia albums have thin firsthand, publicly readable credit scans. Secondary sleeve transcriptions should be checked during human import rather than upgraded to certainty.
2. Genretoken `bachata` and instrument `oud` are new. The repository validator currently rejects unknown tokens until Claude adds them. All other tokens and column values are from the guide.
3. Album-wide scales are intentionally absent on the early albums where sources do not distinguish timbre/mix across the full record. A blank **is not zero**.
4. `worldmusic` is a generic merchant categorisation included only because all named genre labels were requested. Its position last prevents it dominating recommendation weight.
5. Catalog is incomplete beyond this phase: `MTV Unplugged` (2000, exception: award-winning landmark live), `Sale el Sol` (2010), and `Shakira` (2014), among others, must be added when reached.
