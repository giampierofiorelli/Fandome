# Fandome roadmap: decided, to do later

Things we agreed on but deliberately postponed. Check this list before planning new work.

## Waiting on the owner (accounts, keys, details)

- **Apple Music in the listening party.** Use MusicKit on the Web: Apple Music subscribers log in with their Apple ID and full tracks play inside Fandome, synced like Spotify (non-subscribers get 30s previews). Unlike Spotify Connect it plays in the browser tab, not in the Music app; on iPhone the page has to stay open.
  - Needs an Apple Developer account ($99/year) to create a MusicKit identifier and private key.
  - The developer token (JWT, max 6 months) can be generated in the nightly GitHub Action from a repository secret and shipped with the site; no server needed.
  - Load Apple's MusicKit script only after the person taps "Connect Apple Music" (GDPR: no data to Apple before that).
- **Spotify Client ID** for the listening party: paste it into `src/config.ts` (`SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID`). Development mode allows 25 users.
- **Ticketmaster API key** for live concert dates: add the `TICKETMASTER_API_KEY` repository secret (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions). The nightly job in `scripts/fetch-concerts.ts` does the rest.
- **Contact email only, for now (owner, Oct 2026):** no personal name or address while Fandome is a non-commercial fan site; the pages say "the Fandome project". Needed: one email made for Fandome (not a personal one) in `CONTACT_EMAIL` (`src/config.ts`), for privacy and DSA requests. Name, address (`OWNER_NAME`, `OWNER_ADDRESS`) and VAT only if it becomes an economic activity: to evaluate then, with the lawyer. Until the email is set, the pages show "[contact email, to be added]": a dedicated address such as fandome.contatti@gmail.com is enough and exposes nothing personal. Note (Claude, not legal advice): with everything stored in visitors' browsers and no server, the obligations are minimal, but the lawyer review below stays before opening to the public.
- **MusicBrainz / ListenBrainz account** (for the recommendation data, when we get there): reading MusicBrainz needs no account (just our app name and max 1 request/second). Steps: 1) create an account on musicbrainz.org and confirm the email; 2) sign in on listenbrainz.org with the same account; 3) copy the personal token from ListenBrainz settings and add it as the `LISTENBRAINZ_TOKEN` repository secret (never paste it in chat); 4) only if Fandome becomes commercial: commercial licence/supporter plan on metabrainz.org, needed for MusicBrainz tags, genres and ratings (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial); core data (artists, albums, recordings, relations, areas) is CC0 and free for any use.
- **Lawyer review** of the privacy policy, terms, moderation and ticket-board wording before opening to the public.
- **Official artist profile photos (owner's decision).** Replace the temporary Wikimedia Commons photos with the profile image each artist currently uses on Spotify / Apple Music, the one they chose to represent themselves now. Owner's position: Fandome is for fans but above all to promote and support the artists; the owner takes responsibility for using these images.
  - Source: Spotify Web API (`/v1/artists/{id}` → `images`) or Apple Music API (artist artwork), fetched in the nightly GitHub Action and saved with the site, so visitors' browsers don't contact Spotify/Apple.
  - Write the statement in the right places, starting with the site footer and the Legal notice: images belong to the artists and their rights holders, used to promote and support them, removed on request.
  - Then remove the Commons credits (`src/data/artistPhotos.json`, `PhotoCredit`, the Legal notice list) and the temporary photos in `public/artists/`.
  - Until then the Commons photos stay, as a demo (sombr, Muse, Tina Turner and Michael Jackson have none yet).

## Product, later

- **"Fandome's data shows" (done, Oct 2026; owner: findings said automatically and claimed as ours).** `findingOf` in `src/lib/legacy.ts`: a pioneer (it had the sound before most albums of its area, and its followers sound more like it than like its contemporaries), at least 100 albums described in the 6 years before (so 1960s–early 70s records that only look new because their predecessors are not described stay out), a sound new to the whole catalogue (at most 3% of all earlier albums), spread by 25+ points, and not already brought by an earlier album of the same artist. The album page says it in "What it left behind" (labelled "Fandome's data shows", with the numbers and how it is measured); `/findings` lists them all, marked "Critics agree" or "Our finding". Computed at build time into `src/data/findings.json` (`npm run findings`; a test fails when it is stale). First run: 22 findings, 13 ours (e.g. Do It Good 1974 disco 0→83%, Rettore Brivido divino synth-pop 0→37%, Tozzi, Talking Heads, Bilingual). Next: findings for other kinds of influence (artists, scenes), and a share card for a finding.
- **Method audit (owner, Oct 31 2026):** are we building an algorithm or tuning? Every number of the trace and findings audited in [`docs/research/findings-method.md`](docs/research/findings-method.md) (two of them, 12 points and 30% coverage, had been tuned on the press); research on methods in progress; then the story of a sound (first appearance, becomes main, spreads from) and a published sensitivity analysis.
- **An algorithm that grows with the catalogue (owner, Oct 2026).** We keep adding albums, so the data will get better and show more: every threshold of the trace and of "Fandome's data shows" must stay right at 1,500 albums and at many thousands (shares rather than counts, minimum samples per window, confidence that grows with the albums behind a figure). Research done: `docs/research/influence-research.md` (how scholars and critics judge influence, repeating patterns, visualisation; proposals mapped to our fields). Step (1) done, Oct 2026: every spread carries a 90% Wilson margin (`Spread.sure`: lowest share after minus highest share before; it shrinks to the plain gain as the catalogue grows) and a trait counts only when the gain survives it; findings need the 6 years before described at 30% of our best-described 6 years (relative, not 100 albums: after 2005 we have only 72–93 albums per 6 years, so a fixed count shut out every recent album), a gain of 12+ points after the margin, and no remix albums; calibrated on the sourced legacy scores (19 findings: Dummy, Moby, Dare, Nevermind in; Ziggy and Fetus out). Step (2) done, Oct 2026 (revised after wave 1: a link to an artist without a named record is shared among the artist's three earlier albums that sound most like the later one, weighted by likeness and career weight, so one record does not take every link; the album page counts only links of weight 0.25+): the influence network (`src/lib/influence.ts`, stored in `src/data/influence.json` by `npm run findings`): links from later to earlier albums (influence named 1, critic 0.5, shared producer 0.5, career-wide influence 0.25; a named record gets the link, else the artist's earlier album that sounds most like it; never between albums sharing a billed artist), PageRank, standing among albums within 5 years, disruption (opened a path vs carried a line). Album page: "Drawn on by later albums" when 5+ draw on it and it is in the top tenth of its time. It finds the classics the trace misses (Revolver, Pet Sounds, Kind of Blue, Velvet Underground). Steps (3)–(5) done, Oct 2026 (`src/lib/patterns.ts`, stored by `npm run findings`, shown on `/findings`): recognised late (6+ albums whose artists or critics name it, at most one in its first 10 years, 70%+ after 15: Rumours, Blue, Kind of Blue, Jolene, Scott 4, Suicide, Violator…), revivals (a genre that peaks, halves, and comes back mostly through artists who started after the first peak; median gap 33 years over 28 revivals), niche to mainstream (median 9 years over 22 genres, from 10% of albums with mainstream 0–2 to 10% of those with 4–5; genres already there when our coverage starts are left out). Still to do: (6) scene revolutions; revivals within an area rather than the whole catalogue (shares follow which artists we have described). Next proposals: (1) thresholds that scale (confidence intervals on shares, a relative coverage gate instead of 100 albums, rank-based results such as "top 2% of its decade", a precomputed year × area × trait table); (2) an influence graph from later to earlier albums (influenced_by, sounds_like, shared producers) scored like PageRank and normalised by year, kept separate from innovation; (3) delayed recognition ("sleeping beauties") on trait curves; (4) longevity (+13 to +30 years), spread to other areas, revivals (a sound that fades and returns, crediting who brought it back); (5) niche-to-mainstream lag using the reception mainstream score; (6) scene "revolutions" (years when a scene's mix changes fastest). Pitfalls: famous albums are described first; newer albums carry more labels; critics' comparisons already reflect reputation.
- **Cover every era (owner, Oct 2026).** The numbers of the trace, the findings and the patterns are not final: they are recomputed at every build and correct themselves as albums are added, fastest where coverage is thin. `npx vite-node scripts/coverage.ts` shows albums per decade and per area; choose the next artists to describe from its thinnest cells. At Oct 2026 (1,420 studio albums): the 1980s are the best covered (384); the 1990s 84%, the 1970s 65%, but the 2000s only 37%, the 2010s 39%, the 2020s 26%, the 1960s 14% and the 1950s 4%. By area: rock, alternative and indie after 2000; soul, funk and disco after 1990 (their revival is measured on very few albums); hip-hop in the 2000s; the 1960s in every area.

- **Singles (owner, Oct 2026: "dobbiamo rappresentarli").** Artists known only for singles (Italo disco's Mr. Flagio, Casco; 1950s–60s Italian stars like Claudio Villa or Nilla Pizzi where no album is named) get their key singles in the catalogue as releases of type `single`, described like albums (one song: genres, scales, instruments, production) and listed apart under "Singles" on the artist page; they count in suggestions and in the data like any release. - **Sources from labels and estates (owner, Oct 2026):** a label, manager or artist's estate speaks for the artist: what it says the artist drew on, the genres it gives, credits and dates count.
